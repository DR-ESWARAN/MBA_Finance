Span of Control

Maximum number of subordinates a single manager can effectively supervise

Definition & Ideal Numbers Wide vs Narrow Span Flat vs Tall Structures Importance (6) Determining Factors (5) Graicunas Theory
📐

Definition & Ideal Numbers

Spriegal: "The number of people reporting directly to an authority. No single executive should have more people looking to him than he can reasonably be expected to serve."
5–8
Executive Level
15–25
First-line Supervision
Analogy: Like patients per ICU intensivist — too many → errors; too few → wasteful resources. Optimal span = quality + efficiency.
🏗️

Wide vs Narrow Span — Flat vs Tall Organisations ⭐

🌐 FLAT Organisation (Wide Span)
✅ Pros
Encourages delegation; subordinates take responsibility
Agile — faster communication & decisions
Cost-effective — fewer levels, fewer managers
Empowerment & autonomy → prevents disengagement
❌ Cons
High managerial workload
Role confusion more likely
May cultivate distrust of management
🏢 TALL Organisation (Narrow Span)
✅ Pros
Rapid communication within small teams
Groups easier to control and manage
Greater specialisation & division of labour
More opportunities for employee promotion
❌ Cons
Communication slow → hampers decisions
Silos develop → prevents cross-functional problem solving
Key relationship
Flat = Wide span = Fewer levels Tall = Narrow span = More levels

Importance of Span of Control (6)

⏰
Time Limitation
Head can't personally supervise all employees in a day
⚡
Capacity Limit
Human energy has limits; can't manage everyone alone

Span of Attention
Humans can only focus fully on a limited number of people

Communication

🧠

📈
Growth Chances
Clear roles + specific reporting → better planning and improvement

Factors Determining Span of Control ⭐

#FactorEffect
1Type of Org & ManagementClear plans, policies, standing procedures → fewer decisions for manager → span ↑

3Staff Help AvailableMore qualified staff assistance → manager handles more → span ↑

Graicunas Theory — 3 Types of Relationships ⭐ FORMULA

V.A. Graicunas (French management consultant) — mathematical formula for superior-subordinate relationships. Shows that relationships increase EXPONENTIALLY as subordinates increase.
1
Direct Single Relationships
Between superior and each individual subordinate. Simple one-to-one. If n=3 → 3 direct single relationships.
Formula: n
2
Direct Group Relationships
Between superior and each possible combination of subordinates. Consulting, conferring, advising any group combination. If n=3 → 3 direct group relationships.
Formula: n(2n-1 − 1)
3
Cross Relationships
Mutual relationships among subordinates themselves — peers consulting each other under the same superior. These arise from coordination needs.
Included in total formula
📊 Total Relationships Formula
Total = n(2n/2 + n − 1)
where n = number of subordinates
Conclusion: Reasonable span = 5 to 6 subordinates
Criticisms of Graicunas
Mathematical precision debatable Ignores frequency of relationships & strain Leaves out some possible relationships Fails to identify determining factors
Significance: Even if formula is imperfect, Graicunas proved that adding even one subordinate creates a disproportionate jump in total relationships — justifying the concept of limiting span.
