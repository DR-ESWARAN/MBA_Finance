1. Early Pioneers (Pre-Classical)

Thinker Key Contribution
Robert Owen HR pioneer — workers' welfare; 10.5 hr workday; no child labour under 10; improved living conditions
Charles Babbage "Father of modern computing"; division of labour; work specialisation; profit-sharing plan (basis of Scanlon Plan)
Andrew Ure Academic training for factory managers; advocated "automatic plan" for harmony
Charles Dupin Influenced Fayol; pioneered industrial education in France; time study + workload balancing
Henry Robinson Towne Systematic management practices (1870); "gain sharing" to boost productivity; credited Taylor as scientific mgmt pioneer

2. Classical Approach ⭐

A. Taylor's Scientific Management — 4 Principles

Problem Taylor solved: "Soldiering" — workers deliberately working slow due to fear of job loss, faulty wages, outdated methods.

Principle Meaning
1. Science, Not Rule of Thumb Replace guesswork with scientific methods; experiment to find best techniques
2. Harmony, Not Discord Create "Mental Revolution" — management & labour see each other as indispensable
3. Cooperation, Not Individualism Joint standard-setting by manager + workers; shared responsibility
4. Development of Each Person Scientific selection + training; assign work per ability & interest

Taylor's Implementation Tools:

  • Time, Motion & Fatigue Studies → determine fair day's task
  • Functional Foremanship → 8 specialist foremen (Route Clerk, Instruction Card Clerk, Time & Cost Clerk, Shop Disciplinarian, Gang Boss, Speed Boss, Repair Boss, Inspector)
  • Differential Piece-Rate System → Higher rate for meeting/exceeding standard; lower rate for under-performance

Note: Functional foremanship VIOLATES Fayol's Unity of Command — worker reports to 8 bosses.

Merits: More production, job satisfaction, personality development, higher standard of living Criticism: Unsuitable for small firms; causes unemployment; treats workers as machines; ignores social needs

B. Fayol's 14 Principles of Management ⭐⭐ (MUST KNOW ALL 14)

Henri Fayol = Father of Modern Management. Divided management into 6 activities: Technical, Commercial, Financial, Security, Accounting, Managerial (most important for top managers).

# Principle One-liner
1 Division of Work Specialisation → increased efficiency
2 Authority Right to command + responsibility must go together
3 Discipline Rules must be upheld; methods may vary
4 Unity of Command One employee → one direct supervisor only
5 Unity of Direction One plan, one manager for same objective team
6 Subordination of Individual to General Interest Group interest > individual interest
7 Remuneration Fair pay — financial + non-financial
8 Centralisation Balance between central & employee decision-making
9 Scalar Chain Clear chain of command from top to bottom
10 Order Right place for everything; clean, tidy, safe workplace
11 Equity Fairness + kindness to all staff
12 Stability of Tenure Minimise turnover; plan personnel needs
13 Initiative Give employees freedom to create and carry out plans
14 Esprit de Corps Promote team spirit and unity

Clinical analogy: Like cardiac cath lab protocols — each team member has defined roles (Division of Work), one lead operator (Unity of Command), team spirit essential (Esprit de Corps).

C. Weber's Bureaucratic Theory — 7 Principles

Max Weber = Father of Bureaucratic Management Theory. Believed bureaucracy = more rational & efficient than traditional systems.

Principle Key Point
Formal Hierarchy Structure Ranked positions; each level answers to level above
Administrative Class Paid full-time employees; selected on competence; no ownership interest
Formal Rules & Norms Strict rules for all; stability, continuity, predictability
Division of Work Clearly defined area of competence per official; no role overlap
Impersonal Relationships Decisions based on rational factors, not emotions or personal bias
Official Records All decisions formally documented; filing system; legal evidence
Principle of Equality Selection & promotion based on skills/experience — no favouritism

Criticisms of Bureaucracy:

  • Excessive rules → red tape + delayed decisions
  • Kills creativity
  • Rigid — doesn't adapt to change
  • Employees feel like machines; no belongingness
  • One-way communication (top to bottom) → resentment
  • Ignores informal organisation

3. Behavioural / Human Relations Approach ⭐

A. Mary Parker Follett — "Mother of Modern Management"

  • Management = "art of getting things done through people"
  • Power WITH (co-active), not power OVER → shared power, not hierarchy-based
  • Integration — find solutions acceptable to all group members
  • Group power > personal power
  • 4 Coordination Principles: Direct Contact | Early Stages | Reciprocal Relationship | Continuous Process
  • Advocated participation of all; influenced modern motivation, leadership & teamwork theories

B. Elton Mayo — Hawthorne Experiments ⭐

Mayo = Father of Human Relations Movement. Studies at Western Electric's Hawthorne Plant, Chicago (1927–1933)

4 Experiments:

Experiment Finding
1. Illumination Productivity ↑ not just from physical environment but from informal social relations
2. Relay Assembly Test Room Productivity ↑ even when improvements withdrawn → socio-psychological factors key (recognition, participation, informal groups)
3. Mass Interviewing Program Informal relations + social/psychological needs impact behaviour
4. Bank Wiring Observation Room 14 workers — informal production norms & informal group relations observed

7 Key Conclusions:

  1. Factory = techno-economic + psycho-social unit
  2. Workers form informal groups spontaneously — norms influence performance
  3. Interpersonal relations & supervisory attitude > physical conditions
  4. Workers react as group members, not individuals
  5. Workers are not just economic men — respond to recognition, participation
  6. Informal leaders set and enforce group norms
  7. Managers must understand inter-personal and group relations

Criticisms of Hawthorne Studies:

  • Lacks validity (controlled conditions — Hawthorne Effect)
  • Over-importance to human aspects; ignores technology
  • Overemphasises group decision-making; ignores individual decisions
  • Excess freedom to workers; undervalues supervisory role

C. Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs

Needs are hierarchically ranked — lower must be met before higher.

         ┌──────────────────┐
         │ Self-Actualization│  ← Full potential; new skills; lifelong
        ┌┴──────────────────┴┐
        │    Esteem Needs     │  ← Respect, recognition, mastery
       ┌┴────────────────────┴┐
       │  Love / Belonging    │  ← Friendships, belonging, attachments
      ┌┴──────────────────────┴┐
      │     Safety Needs       │  ← Shelter, protection from danger
     ┌┴────────────────────────┴┐
     │   Physiological Needs    │  ← Air, food, water (MOST BASIC)
     └──────────────────────────┘

Analogy: Like triage in ED — can't address psychological wellbeing (esteem) if patient has haemodynamic instability (physiological). Fix basics first.

D. McGregor's Theory X & Theory Y ⭐

Douglas McGregor (1960) — two contrasting views of employees

Dimension Theory X (Negative / Pessimistic) Theory Y (Positive / Optimistic)
Work attitude Dislikes work; avoids it Sees work as natural, relaxing
Motivation Needs threats, coercion, punishment Self-directed if committed
Responsibility Avoids it; needs formal direction Learns to seek responsibility
Ambition Little or none; values job security Capable, creative, resourceful
Management style needed Dictatorial, close supervision Participative, empowering
  • Theory X → based on physiological + safety needs (Maslow lower levels)
  • Theory Y → based on social + esteem + self-actualisation (Maslow higher levels)
  • McGregor preferred Theory Y — encouraged cordial relations, stimulating jobs, participation

E. Chris Argyris — Maturity–Immaturity Theory

  • Maturity–Immaturity: Development is a continuum from immature → mature
  • Mature person = active, independent, self-confident, self-controlled
  • Immature person = passive, dependent, lacks confidence, needs control
  • Solution: Expand job responsibilities, add task variety, allow participation
  • Model I org = manipulative, risk-averse employees (closed learning)
  • Model II org = open to learning, informed choices, willing to take risks → managers should aim for Model II

4. Quantitative Approach

Post-WWII military analytical methods applied to business.

Branch Key Tools / Use
Management Science Mathematical models (PERT, CPM, linear programming, simulation, probability theory) — decision-making in planning & control
Operations Management Production process management; inventory analysis, materials planning, quality control
MIS (Management Information System) Converts raw data → information; supports decision-making, coordination, control

Management Science used in: Capital budgeting, cash flow, production scheduling, HR planning, inventory optimisation

5. Modern Approaches

A. Systems Theory

Type Description
Open System Interacts with environment (all organisations)
Closed System Relatively self-contained; little external interaction (e.g. assembly line)

4 Components of an Organisational System:

  • Inputs → money, materials, men, machines
  • Transformation Process → managerial & technical abilities convert inputs
  • Outputs → products, services, profits
  • Feedback → information about outcomes relative to environment

B. Contingency Theory (Situational Theory)

  • No single "best way" to manage all situations
  • Optimal management action is contingent on internal & external situation
  • Manager's task: identify which decision best fits the particular situation & time
  • Integrates management theory with complexity of real organisations

Analogy: Like personalised medicine — no one-size-fits-all treatment protocol. Management must adapt to the clinical (organisational) context.
