Early Pioneers (Pre-Classical)

RO
Robert Owen
HR Pioneer — 10.5 hr workday; no child labour under 10; improved worker living conditions; proposals called "radical"
CB
Charles Babbage
"Father of computing"; division of labour; work specialisation; profit-sharing plan (→ basis of Scanlon Plan)
AU
Andrew Ure & Charles Dupin
Industrial educators; Ure = "automatic plan" for factory harmony; Dupin influenced Fayol — time study, workload balancing
HT
Henry Robinson Towne
Systematic management from 1870; "gain sharing" to boost productivity; credited Taylor as scientific mgmt pioneer
⚙️

Taylor — Scientific Management ⭐

Problem: "Soldiering" — workers deliberately slow
Fear of job loss Faulty wages Outdated methods
1
Science, Not Rule of Thumb
Replace guesswork; experiment for best techniques
2
Harmony, Not Discord
"Mental Revolution" — mgmt & labour are indispensable to each other
3
Cooperation, Not Individualism
Joint standard-setting; shared responsibility
4
Development of Each Person
Scientific selection + training; assign work per ability
Tools: Time+Motion+Fatigue Study · 8 Functional Foremen · Differential Piece-Rate
Note: Functional Foremanship (8 bosses) VIOLATES Fayol's Unity of Command.

Fayol's 14 Principles of Management MUST KNOW ALL 14 ⭐⭐

1
Division of Work
Specialisation → increased skill & efficiency
2
Authority
Right to command + responsibility must go together
3
Discipline
Rules must be upheld; methods may vary
4
Unity of Command
One employee → one direct supervisor only
5
Unity of Direction
One plan, one manager for teams with same objective
6
Subordination of Individual
Group interest > individual interest (incl. managers)
7
Remuneration
Fair pay — financial + non-financial
8
Centralisation
Appropriate balance — neither extreme centralised nor decentralised
9
Scalar Chain
Clear chain of command top to bottom
10
Order
Right place for everything; clean, tidy, safe workplace
11
Equity
Fairness + kindness to all staff
12
Stability of Tenure
Minimise turnover; plan personnel needs
13
Initiative
Freedom for employees to create and carry out plans
14
Esprit de Corps
Promote team spirit and unity
Fayol = Father of Modern Management. Divided org into 6 activities — Technical, Commercial, Financial, Security, Accounting, Managerial (most important at top level).

Weber's Bureaucracy — 7 Principles

1
Formal Hierarchy
Ranked positions; each level answers to level above
2
Administrative Class
Paid full-time; selected on competence; no ownership interest
3
Formal Rules & Norms
Strict rules for all → stability, continuity, predictability
4
Division of Work
Clearly defined area of competence; no role overlap
5
Impersonal Relationships
Rational decisions — not influenced by emotions or personal bias
6
Official Records
All decisions formally documented; filing system; legal evidence
7
Principle of Equality
Selection & promotion based on skills/experience — no favouritism
Criticisms
Red tape & delays Kills creativity Rigid system Ignores human factor One-way communication Ignores informal org

Mary Parker Follett — "Mother of Modern Management"

Core Ideas
Power WITH, not OVER Integration Group Power > Personal Power Participation of all
4 Coordination Principles
Direct Contact Early Stages Reciprocal Relationship Continuous Process
Key quote: "Leadership is not defined by the exercise of power but by the capacity to increase the sense of power among those led."
🔬

Elton Mayo — Hawthorne Experiments ⭐ Father of Human Relations

Western Electric's Hawthorne Plant, Chicago · 1927–1933
Experiment 1
💡 Illumination
Productivity ↑ not just from physical conditions but from informal social relations among workers
Experiment 2
🔧 Relay Assembly Test Room
Productivity ↑ even when improvements withdrawn → socio-psychological factors key: recognition, participation, informal groups
Experiment 3
🗣️ Mass Interviewing
Informal relations + social/psychological needs directly impact worker behaviour
Experiment 4
🏦 Bank Wiring Observation
14 workers observed → informal production norms set by workers; informal group relations exist
7 Key Conclusions
Factory = psycho-social unit Workers form informal groups Interpersonal relations > physical conditions Workers react as group members Workers ≠ purely economic men Informal leaders set norms Managers must understand group relations
Criticisms
Lacks validity (Hawthorne Effect) Over-importance to human aspects Overemphasises group decisions Excess freedom to workers

Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs ⭐

🌟
Self-Actualisation
Full potential; new skills; lifelong process; only small % achieve it
▲ must satisfy lower before higher ▲
🏆
Esteem Needs
Respect, recognition, mastery, validation from others
❤️
Love / Belonging
Friendships, social bonds, lasting attachments
🛡️
Safety Needs
Shelter, protection from danger, uncertain future
🍎
Physiological Needs
Air, food, water — most basic; if unmet, nothing else matters
Analogy: Like triage — fix haemodynamic instability (physiological) before addressing psychological wellbeing (esteem).
⚖️

McGregor — Theory X & Theory Y ⭐ 1960

Theory X — Pessimistic
Dislikes work; avoids it
Needs threats & coercion
Avoids responsibility
Little ambition; values security
Resists change
Needs formal direction
Theory Y — Optimistic
Work is natural, relaxing
Self-directed if committed
Seeks responsibility
Creative, capable, resourceful
Loyal if job is rewarding
Self-control & self-direction
Link to Maslow: Theory X → physiological & safety needs | Theory Y → social, esteem & self-actualisation.
McGregor preferred Theory Y — cordial relations, stimulating jobs, participative decisions.

Argyris + Quantitative Approach

Chris Argyris — Maturity-Immaturity Theory
Immature → Passive, dependent, no confidence Mature → Active, independent, self-controlled Model I = manipulative, risk-averse Model II = open learning, informed choices ✅
Quantitative Approach — 3 Branches (Post-WWII)
BranchTools / Focus
Management SciencePERT, CPM, linear programming, simulation — decision-making in planning & control
Operations ManagementProduction, inventory, quality control, scheduling, facilities
MISRaw data → useful information for managers; coordination, control, visualisation

Modern Approaches — Systems & Contingency

Systems Theory
Open System — interacts with environment (all orgs) Closed System — self-contained (e.g. assembly line)
INPUTS
Men, Money, Materials, Machines
→
PROCESS
Managerial & technical transformation
→
OUTPUTS
Products, services, profits
→
FEEDBACK
Info on outcomes vs environment
Contingency Theory (Situational Theory)
No single "best way" to manage Action depends on internal + external situation Manager must adapt to each unique context
Analogy: Like personalised medicine — no universal treatment protocol; management must adapt to the organisational context.
