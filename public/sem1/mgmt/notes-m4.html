Module 4 · Span of Control

Principles of Management · Notes

NotesMind map
  • Span of Control = the maximum number of subordinates that a single executive/manager can effectively supervise
  • Also called: Span of Management / Span of Supervision
  • Spriegal: "The number of people reporting directly to an authority"
  • Ideal ratio (classical theory): 5–8 subordinates at executive level; 15–25 at first-line supervision level

Clinical analogy: Like the number of patients a single intensivist can safely manage in the ICU. Too many → errors; too few → wasteful.

2. Wide vs Narrow Span — Flat vs Tall Organisations ⭐

Wide Span (Flat Org) Narrow Span (Tall Org)
Structure Fewer levels Many levels
Communication Fast, direct Slow, through many layers
Control Looser Tighter
Cost Lower (fewer managers) Higher
Decision speed Faster Slower

Flat Organisation (Wide Span) — Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Encourages delegation; subordinates take more responsibility
  • Agile — faster communication & decision-making
  • Cost-effective — fewer levels, fewer managers needed
  • Empowerment & autonomy → prevents disengagement

Cons:

  • High managerial workload
  • Role confusion more likely
  • May cultivate distrust of management

Tall Organisation (Narrow Span) — Pros & Cons

Pros:

  • Rapid communication within small teams
  • Groups easier to control/manage
  • Greater degree of specialisation & division of labour
  • More opportunities for employee promotion

Cons:

  • Communication slow → hampers decision-making
  • Silos may develop → prevents cross-functional problem solving

3. Importance of Span of Control (6 reasons)

  1. Limitation of time — head of org can't supervise all employees personally
  2. Limitation of capacity — human energy has limits; can't manage everyone
  3. Span of attention — humans can only pay full attention to a limited number of people
  4. Communication — effective communication requires focus on specific individuals
  5. Psychological factors — different personalities need different management approaches; one person can't understand all
  6. Growth chances — clear roles + specific reporting structures → better planning and improvement

4. Factors Determining Span of Control ⭐

Factor Effect on Span
1. Type of organisation & management Clear plans, policies, standing procedures → manager makes fewer decisions → span ↑
2. Nature of work Routine, standardised work → span ↑ (programmed decisions)
3. Staff help available More qualified staff assistance → manager can handle more → span ↑
4. Ability & capacity of subordinates Trained, experienced, self-confident subordinates need less supervision → span ↑
5. Ability of the officer/manager Efficient, capable manager → span ↑

Additional factors:

  • Time available for supervision
  • Degree of decentralisation & delegation (higher degree → wider span)
  • Control practices (written reports = wider span; personal observation = narrower span)

5. Graicunas Theory — 3 Types of Relationships ⭐

V.A. Graicunas (French management consultant) — developed a mathematical formula to analyse superior-subordinate relationships.

3 Types of Relationships:

Type Description Formula
1. Direct Single Between superior and each individual subordinate = n
2. Direct Group Between superior and each possible combination of subordinates = n(2^(n-1) - 1)
3. Cross Between subordinates themselves (peer relationships) Included in total

Total relationships formula:

Total = n(2^n/2 + n - 1) (where n = number of subordinates)

Key finding: Relationships increase EXPONENTIALLY as subordinates increase → Graicunas concluded reasonable span = 5 to 6 subordinates

Example: Adding 1 more subordinate (from 5→6) increases relationships disproportionately.

Criticisms of Graicunas Theory:

  • Mathematical precision debatable — relationships don't increase by exact formula
  • Ignores frequency of relationships and the strain they generate
  • Leaves out certain possible relationships
  • Fails to identify factors that actually determine span of management
