Module 1 · Introduction to Management

Principles of Management · Notes

NotesMind map

Core idea: Getting things done through others by optimising human, material & financial resources to achieve organisational goals.

Author Key Definition (exam-ready)
Harold Koontz Art of getting things done through & with people in formally organised groups
Henry Fayol To forecast, plan, organise, command, co-ordinate & control
F.W. Taylor Art of knowing what you want to do & seeing it done in the best & cheapest way
Peter Drucker Multi-purpose organ — manages a business, a manager, and workers & work
George Terry Distinct process of planning, organising, actuating & controlling to accomplish stated objectives
J. Lundy Task of planning, coordinating, motivating & controlling efforts of others
E.F.L. Breech Social process for effective & efficient planning & regulation of enterprise operations

Clinical analogy: Management = Cardiac ICU. The manager is the intensivist — doesn't do every procedure but coordinates nurses, technicians, residents to keep the patient (organisation) alive & optimal.

2. Importance of Management Principles

  • Improves understanding — tells managers what to do in given situations; makes them efficient
  • Direction for training — identifies areas where managers need development
  • Makes role concrete — defines managerial activities in practical terms; acts as ready reference
  • Guides research — indicates lines of inquiry to make management more effective

3. Need for Management Principles

  • Enhance efficiency — guidelines for operating in different situations → smooth functioning
  • Comprehend nature of management — identify scope, duties & limits of a manager's role
  • Train managers — Fayol: "Principles are necessary to train future managers"
  • Facilitate research — amplify knowledge, generate new strategies
  • Coordinate resources — optimise natural, material & human resources toward common objectives

4. Management: Art or Science?

Management as ART

Element Explanation
Personal skill Uses knowledge & skill to coordinate people
Practical knowledge Applies principles to make things effective
Result-oriented Aims at maximum productivity at lowest cost
Regular practice Perfection through continuous practice
Creativity Getting work done through others via motivation

Management as SCIENCE

Feature Applied to Management
Systematised body of knowledge Built by practitioners over years
Continued observation Principles evolved through ongoing study
Universal application Fundamental truths applicable in all situations
Cause & effect Poor planning → low productivity
Validity & predictability Principles tested & verified

Verdict: BOTH — Science + Art

  • Management is an inexact/social science (like sociology, political science)
  • Deals with human behaviour → cannot be predicted with certainty
  • Dean Teele: "90% art, 10% science today — heading toward 80:20"
  • Science provides the principles; Art applies them in practice

Analogy: Like cardiology — evidence-based science (RCTs, guidelines) + clinical art (reading the patient, experience-driven judgement).

5. Functions of Management — POSDC ⭐

Function Key Point Quote / Author
Planning Deciding in advance — what, when, how Koontz: "bridges gap from where we are to where we want to be"
Organising Bringing together physical, financial & human resources Fayol: "provide everything useful for functioning"
Staffing Putting right man on right job Koontz & O'Donnell: selection, appraisal & development of personnel
Directing "Life-spark" — actuates the organisation Supervision + Motivation + Leadership + Communication
Controlling Measuring accomplishment vs standards; correcting deviations Haimann: checking progress toward objectives

Staffing sub-functions:

  • Manpower planning → Recruitment → Selection & Placement → Training & Development → Remuneration → Performance Appraisal → Promotions & Transfer

Controlling steps:

  1. Set standard performance
  2. Measure actual performance
  3. Compare actual vs standard → find deviation
  4. Take corrective action

Note: Luther Gullick's acronym = POSDCORB (Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, Co-ordinating, Reporting, Budgeting) — know this for MCQs.

6. Levels of Management ⭐

Level Who Focus Key Roles
Top Board of Directors, CEO, MD Planning & Coordinating Sets objectives & policies; strategic plans; appoints middle managers; accountable to shareholders
Middle Branch/Departmental Managers Organising & Directing Executes top-level plans; trains lower level; interprets policies downward; coordinates departments
Lower Supervisors, Foremen, Section Officers Directing & Controlling Assigns tasks; instructs workers; quality & quantity control; discipline; image builders

Analogy: Top = Head of Cardiology dept (strategy) | Middle = Senior Consultant (execution) | Lower = Resident/Fellow (direct patient care & reporting up)

7. Managerial Skills — Katz's Model ⭐

Skill Meaning Most Important At
Technical Ability to perform specific tasks; knowledge of methods & tools Lower level
Human Ability to work with people; lead, motivate & communicate All levels
Conceptual Ability to think abstractly; see the big picture; coordinate full range of activities Top level

8. Mintzberg's Managerial Roles — 10 Roles, 3 Categories ⭐

A. Interpersonal Roles (providing information & ideas)

Role Description
Figurehead Social, ceremonial & legal responsibilities; source of inspiration
Leader Leads & manages team/department performance
Liaison Networks with internal & external contacts

B. Informational Roles (processing information)

Role Description
Monitor Seeks information about environment & team; watches for change
Disseminator Passes useful information to colleagues & team
Spokesperson Transmits organisational goals to outside world

C. Decisional Roles (using information)

Role Description
Entrepreneur Creates & controls change; generates & implements new ideas
Disturbance Handler Manages unexpected roadblocks & mediates disputes
Resource Allocator Decides best use of funding, staff & resources
Negotiator Directs important negotiations within/outside organisation

High-Yield Exam Summary

  • Definitions to know by author: Koontz, Fayol, Taylor, Drucker, Terry, Lundy
  • POSDC — one line per function + controlling's 4 steps
  • 3 levels — who sits where + their focus function
  • Katz's 3 skills — which level needs which most
  • Mintzberg's 10 roles — 3 categories + 10 names (don't mix up)
  • Art vs Science verdict: inexact social science — BOTH
