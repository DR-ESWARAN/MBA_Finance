🔢 8-Step Decision-Making Process ⭐
  1. Identify purpose — Define the problem, who is affected, deadline
  2. Gather information — Collect relevant data from internal & external sources
  3. Set criteria — Define baseline principles for evaluating alternatives (profit, culture)
  4. Brainstorm alternatives — Use Cause-Effect diagrams, Pareto charts to identify root causes
  5. Evaluate alternatives — Compare each for positives & negatives using criteria
  6. Select best alternative — Choose the optimal or most acceptable option
  7. Execute the decision — Convert to a plan or sequence of activities
  8. Evaluate results — Assess outcome; learn and correct for future decisions
🌡️ 3 Decision-Making Conditions ⭐

CERTAINTY

All alternatives, conditions & outcomes known; info accurate & reliable

Routine/repetitive operations

RISK

Incomplete info BUT good idea of probability of each outcome

Launch product with market data

UNCERTAINTY

Future unpredictable; alternatives, risks & consequences unknown

Most strategic decisions
🔍 Modern Approaches Under Uncertainty ⭐
📊 Risk Analysis Quantitative + qualitative assessment of size & nature of risk; trade-off between risk and benefit
🌳 Decision Trees Graphic representation of alternative actions, chance events & probabilities; trace optimum path
■ Square = decision point  |  ● Circle = chance event
🎭 Preference / Utility Theory Individual attitudes toward risk vary — "risk averters" vs "gamblers"; top managers take largest risk but not with own money

👥 Group Decision Making ⭐
Definition: Individuals collectively make a choice — no single person is accountable

✅ ADVANTAGES (8)

  • More diversity of opinions
  • Greater participation & interest
  • More understanding & positivity
  • Less individual bias
  • Team-building opportunity
  • Promotes collective thinking
  • Increases org strength
  • Encourages smart risk-taking

❌ DISADVANTAGES (9)

  • Takes significant time
  • Irrelevant opinions possible
  • Some members stay silent
  • Different group priorities
  • May go against org goals
  • Reduces individual accountability
  • Dominant member overuse
  • Everyone sees themselves as leader
  • Group think — going with flow
🗂️ 3 Types of Group Decision Making ⭐
🤝 Interacting Groups Members meet face-to-face; discuss, argue, agree — most common method
↑ Ideas generated BUT political factors can influence; limits creativity
📋 Delphi Groups No face-to-face; questionnaires sent → summarised → repeated rounds until consensus
Used for forecasting: demand, market trends, future problems
✍️ Nominal Groups Members work independently in silence; write ideas; ideas collected, discussed, voted & ranked
↑ Individual creativity; avoids personality domination
🛠️ Decision-Making Techniques

Standard Financial / Management Tools

Marginal Analysis Extra output from +1 unit input (Paul Samuelson)
Financial Analysis Profitability, payback period, discounted cash flows
Break-Even Analysis Sales level where TR = TC; Profit = 0
Ratio Analysis Interpret financial statements; assess strengths & weaknesses

Operations Research (OR) Techniques

Linear Programming Optimal allocation of scarce resources (linear relationships)
Waiting-Line (Queuing) Balance service vs waiting time
Gaming Theory Competitive strategy selection against rational opponents
Simulation Computer model of real system; test variables
Decision Tree ■ Decision points + ● Chance events → optimum path
