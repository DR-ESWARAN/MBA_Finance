Module 3 · Tabulation

Quantitative Techniques · Notes

NotesMind map
  • Definition: Systematic presentation of numerical data in rows and columns
  • Makes classified data more intelligible and fit for statistical analysis
  • Tuttle: "A statistical table is the logical listing of related quantitative data in vertical columns and horizontal rows of numbers, with sufficient explanatory and qualifying words to make clear the full meaning of the data and their origin."
  • Classification → first step; Tabulation → next step (presents classified data)

2. Objectives of Tabulation (9)

  1. Simplify complex data
  2. Highlight chief characteristics of data
  3. Clarify objective of investigation
  4. Present data in minimum space
  5. Detect errors and omissions
  6. Facilitate comparison
  7. Facilitate reference
  8. Identify trends and tendencies
  9. Facilitate statistical analysis

3. Classification vs Tabulation

Classification Tabulation
Basis/prerequisite for tabulation Follows classification
Divides data into groups/subgroups based on similarity Arranges classified data in rows + columns with heads

4. Main Parts of a Table (8) — HIGH YIELD

# Part Description
1 Table Number Identification; placed at top
2 Title Scope and nature of table — concise and unambiguous
3 Captions & Stubs Captions = column headings; Stubs = row headings
4 Main Body Most important — contains numerical data; columns top→bottom, rows left→right
5 Ruling & Spacing Vertical lines for columns; horizontal lines at top, bottom, and totals only
6 Head-note Below title — indicates unit of measurement (in brackets)
7 Footnote Below last line — abbreviations, explanatory notes
8 Source Required for secondary data — indicates origin of information

5. General Rules for Tabulation (8)

  1. Simple and compact — not overloaded with details
  2. Accordance with objective of investigation
  3. Units of measurement must always be indicated
  4. Captions and stubs arranged systematically
  5. Complete and self-explanatory
  6. Include interpretative figures: totals, ratios, percentages where possible
  7. Entries must be accurate
  8. Table should be attractive to draw reader attention

6. Types of Tables

A. By Purpose of Investigation

Type Also Called Feature
General Purpose Reference table Large data mass; no specific objective; in appendix of reports
Special Purpose Text / Summary / Analytical table Specific problem; smaller; highlights relationships

B. By Nature of Presented Data

Type Feature
Primary (Original) Data in original collected form
Derivative Derived figures — totals, averages, percentages, ratios, coefficients

C. By Construction

Type Characteristics
Simple Table One characteristic only; also called first-order table
Complex Table 2–3 characteristics; further divided: Two-way / Three-way / Multi-way
Cross-classified Classified row-wise AND column-wise; each category of one can occur with any category of other

7. Methods of Tabulation

Method When Used
Manual Small field of investigation; few characteristics
Mechanical Large data; computers for speed + accuracy

High-Yield Exam Points

  • 8 Parts of a Table — most likely MCQ (Caption vs Stub, Head-note vs Footnote)
  • 9 Objectives — know the list
  • Tuttle's definition — often quoted in theory questions
  • Types of tables by Construction — Simple / Complex / Cross-classified
  • Caption = column heading; Stub = row heading (frequently confused in MCQs)
