2. Characteristics of Good Average 7

1Rigidly defined — preferably by algebraic formula
2Easy to compute
3Easy to understand
4Based on ALL observations
5Capable of further algebraic treatment
6Not unduly affected by extreme observations
7Not much affected by fluctuations of sampling
🫀 Clinical Analogy: Like a good diagnostic criterion — must be reproducible (#1), apply to all patients (#4), usable for further risk scoring (#5), not swayed by one outlier reading (#6).

3. Arithmetic Mean (AM) MOST IMPORTANT

Calculation Methods
Data TypeFormula
Individual obsX̄ = ΣX / n
Short-cutX̄ = A + Σd/n (d = X−A)
Ungrouped freqX̄ = ΣfX / Σf
Grouped freqUse mid-values; same formula
Weighted AMX̄w = ΣwX / Σw
X̄ = (N₁X̄₁ + N₂X̄₂) / (N₁ + N₂)
Combined Mean Formula
7 Properties of AM
1Σ(X − X̄) = 0 (centre of gravity)
2Σ(X − X̄)² is minimum (least squares)
3Algebraically treatable
4Combined mean formula applicable
5Add/subtract constant B → mean shifts by B
6Multiply/divide by b → mean multiplied/divided by b
7Replace obs → mean changes by avg change
Merits vs Demerits

4. Median

Definition

Positional average — value that divides distribution into two equal halves. Depends on position, not magnitude.

Determination
Odd nMedian = (n+1)/2 th value
Even nMean of (n/2)th and (n/2+1)th values
GraphicallyIntersection of Less-than and More-than Ogives → drop perpendicular to X-axis
Merits vs Demerits
MeritsDemerits
Not affected by extremesArrangement needed — cumbersome
Works with open-end classesNot algebraically treatable
Can be located graphicallyNot based on all obs
Best for skewed distributionsMore affected by sampling than mean
Only avg for qualitative + rankable dataWeighted median not defined
📈 Trading Analogy: Median return is better than mean for skewed P&L distributions — one huge losing trade won't drag the median down like it would the mean.
