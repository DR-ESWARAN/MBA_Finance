2. Characteristics of Statistics 6

#CharacteristicKey Point
1Numerical FiguresMust be quantifiable (measured or counted)
2Aggregate of FactsSingle figure ≠ statistics; must be a related set
3Multiplicity of FactorsData influenced by many simultaneous causes
4Enumerated / EstimatedCollected by counting or measurement, with reasonable accuracy
5Systematic & PurposefulHaphazard data → wrong conclusions
6ComparableMust be homogeneous, same units, relatable
🫀 Clinical Analogy: Like lab values — a single BP reading means nothing; a series of readings in context (aggregate, systematic, comparable) gives the diagnosis.

Summarise accounts; sampling techniques for auditing large firms

Banks & Insurance

Cash flow estimation; mortality tables for premium calculation

🫀 Clinical Analogy: Quality Control charts (UCL/LCL) = Cardiac monitor alarms — alerts trigger only when values go outside safe range, not on every fluctuation.

7. Limitations of Statistics 7

#LimitationKey Point
1Numerical facts onlyQualitative traits (beauty, honesty) can't be directly measured
2Groups, not individualsIndividual exceptions are ignored
3True only on averageAvg wage ≠ every worker's wage
4Approximately trueSample-based → never exact
5One set of methods onlyMust be supplemented by other analyses
6Liable to be misusedBiased collection → misleading conclusions
7Experts only"Most dangerous tools in hands of inexpert" — Yule & Kendall
