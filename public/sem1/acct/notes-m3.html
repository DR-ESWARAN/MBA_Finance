Module 3 · Subsidiary Books

Principles of Accounting · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map
  • Definition: Books of prime entry where transactions of a particular/similar category are recorded separately
  • Also called: Special Journals / Day Books / Books of Original Entry / Books of Prime Entry
  • Origin: When business grows → Journal becomes bulky → Journal is sub-divided on basis of nature of transaction → Subsidiary Books

Key distinction: Small business = 1 journal sufficient. Large business = Subsidiary Books needed.

2. Types of Subsidiary Books (5 Main)

# Book Records
1 Purchase Book (Purchase Day Book) Credit purchases of goods only
2 Purchase Return Book (Returns Outward Book) Goods returned to suppliers
3 Sales Book (Sales Day Book / Sales Journal) Credit sales of goods only
4 Sales Return Book (Returns Inward Book) Goods returned by customers
5 Cash Book All cash and cheque transactions

3. Needs for Subsidiary Books (Why Sub-divide?)

  • Saves clerical labour — similar transactions grouped → handled faster
  • Easier ledger posting — specific book → specific ledger entries
  • Internal check system — one clerk's book automatically verified by another
  • Avoids bulky single journal — as business grows, one journal is unmanageable
  • Division of labour — different staff handle different subsidiary books
  • Prompt information — quick access to specific category data
  • Easy error detection — disagreement in trial balance → check relevant book

4. Purchase Book

  • Records credit purchases of goods only
  • NOT recorded here:
    • Cash purchases (→ go to Cash Book)
    • Credit purchases of non-goods (furniture, equipment, stationery → Journal)
  • Written from Inward Invoice (received with goods)
  • Goods at net value = Invoice price − Trade Discount
  • Totalled at end of each month → posted to Purchases A/c (Dr)

Format columns: Date | Supplier Name | Invoice No. | L.F. | Amount

5. Purchase Return Book (Returns Outward Book)

  • Records goods returned to supplier (purchased on credit)
  • Reasons: Defective goods / wrong specification / quality issue
  • Document used: Debit Note (prepared by buyer, sent to supplier)
  • Supplier sends back: Credit Note (to acknowledge return)
  • Totalled periodically → posted to Purchase Returns A/c (Cr)

6. Sales Book (Sales Day Book / Sales Journal)

  • Records credit sales of goods only
  • NOT recorded here:
    • Cash sales (→ Cash Book)
    • Sale of non-goods assets (→ Journal)
  • Written from Outward Invoice (sent with goods to buyer)
  • Goods at net value = Invoice price − Trade Discount
  • Total posted to Sales A/c (Cr); individual customer accounts Debited

7. Sales Return Book (Returns Inward Book)

  • Records goods returned by customers (sold on credit)
  • Document used: Credit Note (prepared by seller/business)
  • Buyer sends: Debit Note (in acknowledgement)
  • Key distinction: Credit Note → prepared by Seller | Debit Note → prepared by Buyer
  • Total posted to Returns Inward A/c (Dr); customer accounts Credited

8. Cash Book

  • Records all cash and cheque transactions
  • Dual nature: Functions as both Journal (book of original entry) AND Ledger (cash account)
  • No need for separate Cash A/c in Ledger — cash book serves that purpose
  • Left side (Debit) = All receipts | Right side (Credit) = All payments
  • Particulars column: Debit side starts with "To" | Credit side starts with "By"

4 Types of Cash Book:

Type Columns Records
Single Column (Simple) Cash only Cash receipts + Cash payments
Double Column Cash + Bank Cash + Bank transactions
Triple Column Cash + Bank + Discount Cash + Bank + Discount allowed/received
Petty Cash Book Analysis columns Small/petty expenses (paid by Petty Cashier)

Single Column Cash Book:

  • Debit side: Date, Receipt No., Particulars ("To"), L.F., Amount
  • Credit side: Date, Voucher No., Particulars ("By"), L.F., Amount
  • Opening balance → "To Balance b/d" on debit side
  • Closing balance → "By Balance c/d" on credit side

Double Column Cash Book:

  • Adds Bank column on each side
  • Bank A/c = Personal account → can have Dr or Cr (overdraft) balance
  • Bank Overdraft = Credit side > Debit side in bank column → shown as "To Balance c/d" on receipt side

Triple Column Cash Book:

  • Adds Discount column on each side
  • Discount allowed (Dr side) = expense | Discount received (Cr side) = income
  • Discount column is totalled but NOT balanced (it is not an account)
  • 7 columns on each side

Petty Cash Book:

  • "Petty" from French word "Petit" = small
  • Maintained by Petty Cashier for small expenses (stationery, postage, refreshments)
  • Amount advanced by main cashier at start of period
  • Types: Simple Petty Cash Book | Analytical Columnar Petty Cash Book

9. Important Documents Summary

Book Document Used
Purchase Book Inward Invoice
Purchase Return Book Debit Note (by buyer) / Credit Note (by supplier)
Sales Book Outward Invoice
Sales Return Book Credit Note (by seller) / Debit Note (by buyer)
Cash Book Receipts, Cash memos, Vouchers, Pay-in-slips, Cheque counterfoil

High-Yield Exam Points ⚡

  1. Subsidiary Books = Sub-division of Journal based on nature of transactions
  2. 5 main types — Purchase, Purchase Return, Sales, Sales Return, Cash Book
  3. Purchase Book → Credit purchases only (not cash, not non-goods)
  4. Sales Book → Credit sales only (not cash, not non-goods)
  5. Cash Book = Journal + Ledger (dual purpose)
  6. Debit Note → by Buyer | Credit Note → by Seller
  7. 4 types of Cash Book — Single, Double, Triple, Petty
  8. Triple column → Discount column totalled not balanced
  9. Petty = from French "Petit" = small
  10. Opening balance in Cash Book → "To Balance b/d" on receipt (debit) side
