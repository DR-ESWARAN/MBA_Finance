1. What are Final Accounts?

3 Components
#AccountShows
1Trading AccountGross Profit or Gross Loss
2Profit & Loss A/cNet Profit or Net Loss
3Balance SheetFinancial Position on a specific date
Primary Objectives
Know profit earned / loss suffered Know assets and liabilities Know owner's capital/equity
🫀 Clinical Analogy: Trading A/c = Gross revenue from cath lab procedures. P&L = Net income after hospital overheads. Balance Sheet = Your net worth statement — assets vs liabilities on a given date.

HIGH YIELD
Purpose

Shows result of buying and selling of goods. Finds difference between Selling Price and Cost Price → Gross Profit or Gross Loss.

Advantages of Trading Account
✅ Clearly shows result of purchases and sales
✅ Gross Profit % to Sales → helps determine pricing
✅ GP to Direct Expenses ratio → eliminate waste
✅ Year-on-year comparison → better policy decisions
Key Distinction
Direct expenses → Trading A/c Indirect expenses → P&L A/c

3. Profit & Loss Account

HIGH YIELD
Starts with Gross Profit (from Trading A/c) → reveals Net Profit / Net Loss
Debit Side — 4 Groups of Indirect Expenses
GroupExamples
Selling ExpensesCarriage outwards, Travelling, Advertising
Office ExpensesOffice salaries, Rent, Tax, Postage, Stationery
Maintenance ExpensesRepairs & Renewals, Depreciation
Financial ExpensesInterest on loan, Discount allowed
Balancing P&L A/c
Cr > DrNet Profit → increases Capital A/c
Dr > CrNet Loss → reduces Capital A/c
🏦

4. Balance Sheet

HIGH YIELD
Definition

"A statement which sets out the Assets and Liabilities of a business firm and which serves to ascertain the financial position of the same on any particular date."

Key Points
Statement — NOT an account Prepared on a specific DATE (not period) Both sides must always be EQUAL
Structure
LEFT SideRIGHT Side
Liabilities + CapitalAssets
Equations
Assets = Liabilities + Capital
Capital = Assets − Liabilities
Liabilities = Assets − Capital
5 Objectives of Balance Sheet
1Shows accurate financial position
2Gist of all transactions at a given date
3Shows if assets are sufficient to repay liabilities
4Verifies accuracy of final accounts
5Shows profit/loss carried from P&L A/c
📑
