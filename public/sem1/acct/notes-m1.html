Module 1 · Introduction to Accounting

Principles of Accounting · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map
  • Accounting = Art of recording, classifying, summarising financial transactions in monetary terms and interpreting the results
  • Called "Language of Financial Decisions" — not just "Language of Business" (business has non-financial aspects too)
  • Output of accounting = Financial Statements (Income Statement + Balance Sheet)
  • Continuous cycle: Measure → Record → Report → Decide

Clinical analogy: Accounting is the ECG of a business — it records the financial pulse continuously and lets you diagnose health at any point.

2. Branches of Accounting

Branch Focus Output
Financial Accounting External reporting of all transactions Income Statement, Balance Sheet
Cost Accounting Cost per product/process Cost Sheet, Cost Analysis
Management Accounting Internal decision-making (drawn from FA + CA) Budgets, Forecasts, Performance reports

Financial Accounting — Limitations:

  • Consolidated (no department-wise breakup)
  • Historical (past-focused, not predictive)
  • Doesn't distinguish fixed vs variable costs
  • Not useful for tactical decisions (buy vs make, add/drop product)

Management Accounting — Key Features:

  • Tailor-made for decision-makers
  • Forward-looking (future-oriented)
  • No rigid principles — flexible format
  • Reports can be for whole entity or segments

Financial vs Management Accounting — Comparison Table:

Basis Financial Accounting Management Accounting
Primary Users External (banks, creditors, govt) + managers Internal (business managers only)
Decision Criterion GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) Cost-benefit of proposed action
Time Focus Past Future
Reports Summary — whole entity Detailed — parts of entity
Behavioural Focus Adequacy of disclosure How reports affect employee behaviour

3. Users of Accounting Information

Internal Users:

  • Owners — assess business performance
  • Managers — set goals, evaluate progress, corrective action
  • Employees — wages, bonus, profit-sharing decisions

External Users:

  • Investors & Creditors — evaluate profitability and solvency
  • Banks — lending decisions
  • Government & SEBI — tax computation, regulatory compliance
  • Consumers — check if being overcharged
  • Researchers — economic analysis

Trading analogy: Investors read financial statements like you read a company's balance sheet before buying its stock — same information, same purpose.

4. Basis of Accounting

Cash Basis

  • Revenue recorded when cash received; expenses when cash paid
  • ✅ Simple, fewer adjustments, objective
  • ❌ Does NOT give true profit/loss — ignores outstanding & prepaid items
  • Suitable for businesses with mostly cash transactions

Accrual / Mercantile Basis (preferred & exam-important)

  • Revenue recognised when earned (not when cash received)
  • Expenses recognised when incurred (not when cash paid)
  • ✅ Complete picture of profit/loss; reflects true financial position
  • ❌ More complex; requires multiple adjustments

Illustration: | Item | Cash Basis | Accrual Basis | |---|---|---| | Cash Sales ₹3,90,000 | ✅ Included | ✅ Included | | Credit Sales ₹1,60,000 | ❌ Excluded | ✅ Included | | Expenses Paid ₹1,90,000 | ✅ Included | ✅ Included | | Outstanding Expenses ₹80,000 | ❌ Excluded | ✅ Included |

5. Classification of Accounts

Type Description Examples
Personal Accounts Accounts of persons (natural, artificial, representative) Debtor A/c, Bank A/c, Capital A/c, Outstanding Salary A/c
Real Accounts Accounts of assets — tangible or intangible Cash A/c, Building A/c, Goodwill A/c, Patent A/c
Nominal Accounts Accounts of expenses, losses, income, gains Salary A/c, Rent A/c, Interest Received A/c

Golden Rules (from Study Guide — also covered in Module 2 in depth):

  • Personal: Debit the receiver, Credit the giver
  • Real: Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out
  • Nominal: Debit all expenses & losses, Credit all incomes & gains

6. Accounting Concepts (GAAP — Concepts)

These are fundamental assumptions underlying financial statements.

# Concept Core Idea
1 Business Entity Business ≠ Owner. Transactions recorded from business's perspective. Owner's capital = liability of business
2 Money Measurement Only transactions expressible in money are recorded. Non-monetary facts (MD's health, employee morale) excluded
3 Dual Aspect Every transaction has two effects — basis of double-entry. Assets = Liabilities + Capital
4 Going Concern Business assumed to continue indefinitely — justifies depreciation, long-term asset valuation
5 Accounting Period Business life divided into periodic intervals (usually 1 year) for reporting. Enables matching
6 Cost Concept Assets recorded at historical purchase price (not market value). Cost = acquisition + transport + installation
7 Matching Expenses must be matched with the revenue of the same period they helped generate
8 Accrual Income/expense recognised when earned/incurred, not when cash moves
9 Realisation Revenue recognised at point of sale (when property passes to buyer & legal liability to pay arises)

Clinical analogy:

  • Matching concept = Attributing the cost of a stent to the procedure it was used in, not the month it was purchased
  • Going concern = Assuming your hospital will keep running when planning 5-year capital expenditure
  • Dual aspect = Every intervention has a risk and a benefit — two sides always

7. Accounting Conventions (GAAP — Conventions)

These are customs/traditions guiding statement preparation.

Convention Meaning
Consistency Same accounting methods used year-to-year for comparability
Conservatism (Prudence) Anticipate losses, don't anticipate gains — be cautious
Materiality Disclose items significant enough to influence decisions; trivial items need not follow strict rules
Disclosure All relevant information must be disclosed to users

8. Accounting Equation

Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity (Capital)

  • All assets are either claimed by owners or outsiders — always balanced
  • Basis of the Balance Sheet
