Module 2 · Demand Analysis

Managerial Economics · Notes

NotesMind map
  • Demand = consumers' willingness + ability to purchase a good at a given price
  • Demand Analysis = study of the relationship between price and quantity demanded to measure forces influencing sales volume

6 Objectives of Demand Analysis:

  1. Forecasting sales and revenues
  2. Guidance for manipulation of demand
  3. Analysing market influences on products
  4. Appraising salesman's performance / setting sales quotas
  5. Matching competitive strength of the firm
  6. Planning inventory control + assessing working capital requirements

🫀 Clinical analogy: Like pre-procedure planning — you need to know expected patient volume (demand) before sizing your cath lab (capacity).

2. Determinants of Demand (5)

Determinant Effect on Demand
Price of product ↑ Price → ↓ Demand (inverse; Law of Demand)
Income of consumers ↑ Income → ↑ Demand (normal goods); ↓ Demand (inferior goods)
Prices of related goods Complements: ↑ Price of A → ↓ demand for B; Substitutes: ↑ Price of A → ↑ demand for B
Consumer expectations Expected ↑ price → buy more now; expected ↓ income → buy less
Number of buyers More buyers → higher total market demand

Law of Demand: Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded → downward sloping demand curve.

3. Income & Substitution Effect

When price of a good increases, two effects occur:

Effect What happens
Substitution Effect Good is now relatively expensive → consumers switch to cheaper alternatives
Income Effect Higher price → reduced real disposable income → less of the good demanded

Application — Wages (work vs leisure):

  • ↑ Wages → work more profitable (substitution effect dominates early)
  • ↑ Wages → can maintain income with fewer hours (income effect dominates later)
  • Result: backward-bending labour supply curve

Application — Interest rates & saving:

  • ↑ Interest → saving more rewarding (substitution effect → more saving)
  • ↑ Interest → more income from existing savings (income effect → more spending)

4. Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity = responsiveness/sensitiveness of demand to a change in a determinant (price, income, cross-price)

A. Price Elasticity of Demand (Ed)

Formula: Ed = % ΔQ / % ΔP

Type Value Meaning Example
Perfectly Elastic Ed = ∞ Infinite response to any price change Horizontal demand curve
Elastic Ed > 1 % ΔQ > % ΔP Luxury goods, substitutes available
Unitary Elastic Ed = 1 % ΔQ = % ΔP Rectangular hyperbola curve
Inelastic Ed < 1 % ΔQ < % ΔP Necessities, salt, medicines
Perfectly Inelastic Ed = 0 No response to any price change Vertical demand curve

Key numerical examples (from ebook):

  • Price changes 10%, quantity changes 20% → Ed = 20/10 = 2 (elastic)
  • Price changes 30%, quantity changes 10% → Ed = 10/30 = 1/3 (inelastic)
  • Price changes 30%, quantity changes 30% → Ed = 30/30 = 1 (unitary)

⚠️ Slope ≠ Elasticity. Steep curve ≠ inelastic. Elasticity measures % change, slope measures absolute change.

B. Income Elasticity of Demand (Ey)

Formula: Ey = % ΔQ / % ΔY (Y = income)

Type Ey Value Good Category
High (Ey > 1) Income rises → demand rises more than proportionately Luxury goods (cars, jewellery)
Unitary (Ey = 1) Income rises → demand rises proportionately —
Low (0 < Ey < 1) Income rises → demand rises less than proportionately Necessities (water, electricity)
Zero (Ey = 0) Demand unchanged despite income change Essential staples
Negative (Ey < 0) Income rises → demand falls Inferior goods (margarine, coarse grain)

Numerical example: Income falls 20% (50k→40k), car demand falls 50% (10k→5k) → Ey = -50% / -20% = 2.5 (luxury good — highly income sensitive)

C. Cross Elasticity of Demand (Exy)

Formula: Exy = % ΔQx / % ΔPy

Relationship Exy Value Example
Substitutes Positive (+) Price of coffee ↑ → demand for tea ↑
Complements Negative (−) Price of coffee ↑ → demand for coffee stir sticks ↓
Unrelated Zero (0) Price of tea ↑ → demand for cars = unchanged

Strong vs weak substitutes: Same product category (green tea brands) → high positive Exy. Different categories (tea vs coffee) → low positive Exy.

Managerial use: Products with no substitutes (Exy = 0) can be priced higher. Printers sold at loss → drives high-margin ink sales (complement strategy).

D. Uses of Price Elasticity in Managerial Decisions

  1. Price Discrimination — charge higher price to inelastic segment, lower to elastic segment (e.g., monopolist)
  2. Public Utility Pricing — railways, water, postal: inelastic users charged more
  3. Joint Supply — wool + mutton: price wool higher if wool demand is inelastic
  4. Super Market pricing — charge lower prices on elastic-demand goods to attract footfall

🫀 Trading analogy: Like bid-ask spread strategy — wider spread (higher price) where buyer demand is inelastic (captive), narrower where competition is stiff (elastic).

5. Revenue Concepts

Concept Formula Meaning
TR (Total Revenue) TR = Price × Quantity Total income from all sales
AR (Average Revenue) AR = TR / Q = Price Revenue per unit = always equals price
MR (Marginal Revenue) MRn = TRn − TRn-1 Additional revenue from one more unit
MR (alternate) MR = ΔTR / ΔQ When multiple units change
TR from MR TR = ΣMR Sum of all marginal revenues

Key relationships:

  • AR = Price always (proved algebraically: AR = P×Q/Q = P)
  • AR curve = Demand curve of the firm
  • TR = ΣMR (Total revenue is the summation of all marginal revenues)

Numerical examples (from ebook):

  • 10 chairs × ₹160 = TR = ₹1,600; AR = 1600/10 = ₹160
  • MR of 11th chair = TR₁₁ − TR₁₀ = 1780 − 1600 = ₹180
  • MR when units change from 10 to 14: (2200−1600)/(14−10) = 600/4 = ₹150

TR–Elasticity relationship:

  • Ed > 1 (elastic): ↓ Price → ↑ TR
  • Ed < 1 (inelastic): ↓ Price → ↓ TR
  • Ed = 1 (unitary): TR is maximised; MR = 0

6. Demand Forecasting

Definition: Determining the future level of demand for a product to guide business planning.

Objectives of Demand Forecasting (10)

  1. Formulation of production policy
  2. Regular supply of raw materials
  3. Maximum utilisation of machines
  4. Regular availability of labour
  5. Price policy formulation
  6. Proper control of sales (territory-wise targets)
  7. Arrangement of finance
  8. Deciding production capacity (plant size)
  9. Labour requirement planning
  10. Long-term finance planning

Methods of Demand Forecasting (7)

Method Basis Best For Limitation
Survey of Buyer's Intentions Direct consumer survey Short-term; bulk producers Costly; consumers can't foresee choices
Sales Force Composite Salesperson estimates New products; short-term Subjective; not for long-term
Trend Projection Past data + least squares; O = TSCI Long-term; stable trends Breaks at turning points
Executive Judgment Top management opinion No data available No factual basis; inaccurate
Economic Indicators Link demand to indicators (construction contracts, personal income, etc.) Products with lagged demand Hard to find right indicator
Controlled Experiments Vary price/advertising in test markets Measuring price elasticity Expensive; risky; difficult to control
Expert's Opinions External specialist views New products; no past data Subjective; no segment estimates

Trend Projection formula: O = T × S × C × I

  • O = Observed data
  • T = Secular trend
  • S = Seasonal factor
  • C = Cyclical element
  • I = Irregular movement
