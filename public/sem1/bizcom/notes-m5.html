What is Negative News?

  • Something an audience does not want to hear or receive
  • In emergencies: rumors spin out of control; emotions run high; relationships at risk
  • Delivery method influences response — some prefer direct; others prefer less direct with softer language
  • Feedback (qualitative or quantitative) is a helpful tool to expose negative news and avert problems

7 Goals When Delivering Negative News

# Goal
1 Be clear and concise so additional clarification is not required
2 Help the receiver understand and accept the news
3 Maintain trust and respect for the organization and for the receiver
4 Avoid legal liability or admission of guilt or culpability
5 Maintain the relationship even if a formal association is being terminated
6 Reduce anxiety associated with the negative news to increase comprehension
7 Achieve the designated business outcome

⚡ All 7 goals are MCQ targets — know each label

2. Crisis Communication Plan — 4 Elements

Element Description
Crisis communication team + contact info Members decide actions, carry them out, offer expertise; prevents indecisiveness
Designated spokesperson One person listens to everyone and speaks the common point; prevents egocentric behavior and chorus talking
Meeting place / location Undisturbed location comfortable and reachable for all team members
Media plan with procedures Prevents rumors; enables spreading of correct and confirmed news only

3. Intercultural & International Business Communication

Why It Matters

  • Nation to nation and culture to culture — business is conducted differently
  • Common ground must be found for effective cross-cultural communication
  • Business improves when people from different cultures combine perspectives to solve old problems

Ethnocentrism

  • Term for problems in cross-cultural communication when one culture cannot understand culturally determined differences
  • Definition: Belief that one's own cultural group is innately superior to others
  • Deceptive: members perceive their own behavior as logical since it works for them
  • People tend to believe the values of the culture around them are absolute values

5 Factors Affecting Cross-Cultural Business Communication

Factor Key Issue
Language Most often cited barrier; accent and dialect biases; sociolinguistics reinforces class/national distinctions; use a good translator
Environment and Technology Climate, topography, population, natural resources affect logistics; cultures have divergent views of technology's role
Social Organization and History Nepotism, class structure, gender differences, religious ties, job status, racism; difficult to communicate without judgmental bias
Conceptions of Authority Each culture has its own authority distribution; affects how messages are sent based on rank and status of sender/receiver
Nonverbal Communication Body language, clothing choices, eye contact, touching behavior, personal space — meaning varies across cultures

4. Group Communication

Definition

  • When more than two individuals are involved in communication
  • Group communication = interaction and exchange of information between culturally, geographically, or linguistically alike people who are members of a group
  • In an organization: mode by which employees, employers, and team members communicate

Group vs Crowd vs Organization vs Interpersonal

Groups Crowds Organizations Interpersonal
Common goals ✅ ❌ ✅ ❌
Know each other ✅ ❌ ❌ ✅
Informal rules ✅ ❌ ❌ ✅

Verbal & Non-Verbal Group Communication

  • Both equally important
  • Through both: group members participate in determining goals, making decisions, solving problems

Quality Group Communication

  • Requires: sense of belonging + mutual respect + common goals
  • Quality of messages also determines roles within the group

Group Norms

  • Standards, customs, and behavioral expectations
  • Self and group imposed expectations
  • Emerge from members as a group forms

Group Psychologies

  • The more a group discusses → the more it polarizes into two extreme opinions
  • Group takes either cautious or risky decisions
  • Members may shift their stand to align with the group's favored position (groupthink tendency)

Advantages of Group Communication

  • Primary purpose: share information
  • Manage conflicts; make decisions to overcome difficult circumstances
  • Generation of ideas — different viewpoints create a valuable knowledge pool
  • More comprehensive solutions
  • Group interacting with superior authorities achieves more favorable outcomes than individuals

Disadvantages of Group Communication

  • If a few dominate → sharing of actual/accurate viewpoints does not take place
  • Personally related members may deviate into personal discussions → deviation from company goals

5. Teamwork & Leadership

Teamwork

  • Definition: Collaborative effort of a group to achieve a common goal or complete a task effectively and efficiently
  • Teams = form of group dedicated to production or problem solving
  • Group communication is the base of teamwork
  • Andrew Carnegie: "Teamwork is the ability to work together toward a common vision... It is the fuel that allows common people to attain uncommon results."

10 Considerations for Setting Up a Good Team

Select team members wisely · Select a responsible leader · Promote cooperation · Clarify goals · Elicit commitment · Clarify responsibilities · Instill prompt action · Apply technology · Ensure technological compatibility · Provide prompt feedback

Leadership

  • Definition: Art of motivating a group of people to act toward achieving a common goal
  • Leaders are: appointed, elected, or emerge into the role
  • "We are not born leaders but may become them if the context or environment requires our skill set"

3 Ways Leaders Take on Role

Type Description
Appointed leader Designated by authority regardless of group's wishes; group may not accept them
Democratic leader Elected/chosen by group; involves group in decision-making; open and free discussions
Emergent leader Grows into role through group interaction over time; most often the natural leader

5 Types/Styles of Leaders

Type Description
Autocratic Self-directed; establishes norms; controls all decisions; little input from group
Laissez-faire "Live and let live"; gives professionals space and tools; balances control with freedom
Leader-as-technician Has skills others do not; role occurs when specialized skill is needed
Leader-as-conductor Brings people together for a common goal; central coordinating role
Leader-as-coach Teacher + motivator + goal-keeper; combines many talents; can be autocratic at times

6. Writing Effective Routine & Positive Messages

Definitions

  • Routine message: Reader will be pleased, interested, or neutral; provides who/when/what/where/why/how of daily workplace happenings
  • Positive message: Provides good news, acceptance, or congratulations in the workplace

3-Step Process

Step Action
1. Planning Analyze situation, gather information, select right medium
2. Writing Adapt to audience; open with request/main idea; give details in body; close with cordial request for specific action
3. Completing Revise, produce, proofread, and distribute

Strategy for Routine Replies & Positive Messages

  • Structure: Opening → Body → Close
  • Place main idea in the opening
  • Use body to explain all relevant details
  • Close cordially
  • Use the direct approach for positive messages
  • Look for ways to present any negative information in a positive context
  • Make sure audience understands what to do next and how that action benefits them
