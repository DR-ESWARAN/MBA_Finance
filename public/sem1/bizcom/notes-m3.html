2. Cover Letter — Structure & Format

6 Parts of a Cover Letter

Part Content
Header Your name, address, phone, email + employer's contact info + date
Salutation "Dr./Mr./Ms. Last Name" — if unknown: "Dear Hiring Manager"
Introduction State job applied for; where you heard about it; brief skills preview
Body Why interested + why excellent candidate; specific examples (show, don't just tell)
Closing Restate fit; request interview; state follow-up action; thank employer
Signature Complimentary close + handwritten signature + typed name

Formatting Rules

  • Same font as resume (Arial, Calibri, Georgia, Verdana, Times New Roman); size 10–12pt
  • Space between each section (header, salutation, paragraphs, closing, signature)
  • Maximum one page
  • If emailing: contact info goes after typed name at the end

Proofreading

  • Verify correct employer and company names — easy to mix up when writing multiple
  • Print out and read aloud to catch small errors
  • Double-check spelling of contact's name

3. Business Proposal — 9-Step Structure

Step Content
1 Title page — Your name, company name, date, client name
2 Table of contents — Clickable if electronic
3 Executive summary — Why you're sending it; why your solution is best (value proposition)
4 State the problem or need — Show you understand the client's issue
5 Propose a solution — Customized; include deliverables, methods, timeframe
6 Share qualifications — Case studies, awards, accreditations
7 Include pricing options — Optional fee table; don't under or over-price
8 Terms and conditions — Timeline, pricing, payment schedules
9 Signature space — Client signs; prompt for questions

Business Proposal Tips

  • Start with an outline — stay focused
  • Keep it simple — quality over quantity; avoid jargon
  • Stay on brand — let company personality show
  • Include data and visuals — charts, graphs for compelling evidence
  • Use a call-to-action — tell reader what to do next
  • Create a sense of urgency — set a deadline to act
  • Quality control — proofread before sending

4. Sales Message — 4 Strategies

  1. Start with your greatest benefit — Use in headline; audiences remember beginning and end most
  2. Take baby steps — One product/service at a time; too many options = lost sale
  3. Know your audience — Research buyer; individualize message to their wants and needs
  4. Lead with emotion, follow with reason — Drama/humor/novelty to grab attention → facts for credibility → call to action

5. Report Writing

Definition

A report is a testimonial or account of some happening — based on observation and analysis. Crucial in corporate world for planning and control.

7 Steps to Write an Effective Report

  1. Determine objective — identify the problem
  2. Collect required material (facts)
  3. Study and examine the facts gathered
  4. Plan the facts for the report
  5. Prepare an outline — draft the report
  6. Edit the drafted report
  7. Distribute draft to advisory team for feedback and recommendations

Essentials of Effective Report Writing

  • Be focused — know your objective
  • Analyze the target audience — purpose, data needed, implications
  • Decide the length
  • Disclose correct and true information only
  • Discuss all sides reasonably and impartially
  • Concentrate on structure — use vivid sentence structure
  • Present neatly and document carefully
  • Highlight and recap the main message
  • Encourage feedback — incorporate if useful
  • Use graphs, pie-charts for numerical data
  • Margins: top and sides minimum 1 inch; bottom can be 1.5x

Report Structure (from Study Guide Slides 12–13)

Preliminary Parts: Cover → Title Page → Copyright Notice → Acknowledgements → Authorization Letter → Table of Contents → List of Illustrations → Synopsis/Executive Summary

Main Text: Introduction → Body → Conclusions → Recommendations

Supplementary Parts: Appendixes → List of References → Bibliography → Glossary → Index

6. Memorandums (Memos) — 5 Characteristics

Characteristic Description
Audience Orientation Always consider audience needs; use terms known to all — not just management
Professional, Formal Tone Linear (org → employees); may have legal standing; reflects policies/procedures
Subject Emphasis Subject line = clear and concise; be specific (e.g., "Thanksgiving weekend schedule" not "holiday observance")
Direct Format Always direct — purpose clearly announced upfront
Objectivity Facts only; no personal bias, preference, or interest

7. Developing Business Presentations — 5 Tips

  1. Tell a Story — Connect with audience; take them on a journey; get them emotionally involved
  2. Keep it Simple — 3 to 5 main points only; simple and straightforward
  3. Know Your Stuff — Be ready to answer questions; review pertinent information beforehand
  4. Speak Naturally — Conversational style; approachable and competent; no jargon
  5. Don't Put Everything on Slides — Display a few points; slides show images/figures; you tell the information

A business presentation is about ideas — describe them and how to turn them into reality

8. Overcoming Presentation Obstacles

Obstacle Description
Language Jargon and slang create barriers; find common way to interact; adapt basic language
Perception Others' perceptions differ; cultural values and role identities cause stereotypes and prejudice
Ethnocentrism Conviction of knowing more / being better than other cultures — leads to egoism, discrimination
Physical Barriers Background noise, faulty equipment; overcome by checking all in advance + have backup plan

9. Non-verbal Delivery — Movement in Speech

9 Non-verbal Tips (from Study Guide Slides 19–20): Smile · Don't Slouch · Assume a Power Pose · Make Use of the Space · Don't Forget Facial Expressions · Speak Clearly · Don't Be Afraid to Gesture · Maintain Eye Contact · Remember to Breathe · Learn From Other Presenters

  • Non-verbal communication is irreversible — once expressed, cannot be taken back
  • Non-verbal is continuous during a presentation — always occurring
  • Non-verbal reveals thoughts and feelings before you are even aware of them

10. Non-verbal Strategies & Organization

Non-verbal Strategies

  • Visual aids — Support document/presentation; do not replace it
  • Signposts — Keywords alerting audience to topic change or conclusion ("on the one hand", "in conclusion")
  • Internal Summaries & Foreshadowing — Review what was covered; preview what is coming next

Organizing Principles (for documents)

Principle Description
Chronology First → Next → Now → Future
Comparison One product/argument vs another
Contrast Highlights differences between items
Cause and Effect Establishes relationship between two events
Classification/Categorical Establishes categories
Space (Spatial) Parts of something and how they fit the whole
Elimination Outlines all possibilities and eliminates
Example Vivid, specific examples to support main points
