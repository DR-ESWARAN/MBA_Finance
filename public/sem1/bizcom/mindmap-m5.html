Negative News — 7 Goals

What is Negative News?
Something audience does NOT want to hear Rumors spin out of control in emergencies Delivery method influences response
7 Goals When Delivering Negative News
1 Be clear and concise — no additional clarification needed
2 Help receiver understand and accept the news
3 Maintain trust and respect for organization and receiver
4 Avoid legal liability or admission of guilt TRAP
5 Maintain the relationship even if formal association ends
6 Reduce anxiety to increase comprehension
7 Achieve the designated business outcome
💉 Clinical Bridge: Breaking bad news (SPIKES protocol) maps perfectly — Goals 2, 3, 5, 6 are the heart of compassionate disclosure. "Avoid legal liability" = document carefully, like cath lab notes.

Crisis Communication Plan — 4 Elements

ElementPurpose / Description
Crisis Team + Contact InfoMembers decide actions, carry them out, offer expertise; prevents indecisiveness
Designated SpokespersonOne person listens to everyone and speaks the common point; prevents egocentric behavior and chorus talking

Intercultural & Cross-Cultural Factors

Ethnocentrism — Definition
Belief that one's own cultural group is innately superior Deceptive — members see own behavior as logical People treat cultural values as absolute values
5 Factors Affecting Cross-Cultural Communication
FactorKey Issue
LanguageMost cited barrier; accent/dialect bias; use a good translator
Environment & TechnologyClimate, topography, natural resources; divergent views of technology's role
Social Organization & HistoryNepotism, class, gender, religion, racism — hard to communicate without bias
Conceptions of AuthorityEach culture distributes authority differently; affects rank-based messaging
Nonverbal CommunicationBody language, eye contact, touching, personal space — meaning varies by culture
Business improves when different cultures combine perspectives

Leadership — 3 Types + 5 Styles

Definition
Art of motivating a group to act toward achieving a common goal Leaders are appointed, elected, or emerge into role
3 Ways Leaders Take on Role
TypeDescription
Appointed LeaderDesignated by authority regardless of group's wishes; group may not accept them
Democratic LeaderElected/chosen by group; involves group in decision-making; open and free discussions
Emergent LeaderGrows into role through group interaction over time; most often the natural leader
5 Types/Styles of Leaders
StyleDescription
AutocraticSelf-directed; establishes norms; controls all decisions; little input from group
Laissez-faire"Live and let live"; gives professionals space and tools; balances control with freedom
Leader-as-TechnicianHas skills others do not; role occurs when specialized skill is needed
Leader-as-ConductorBrings people together for a common goal; central coordinating role
Leader-as-CoachTeacher + motivator + goal-keeper; combines many talents; can be autocratic at times
💉 Clinical Bridge: Cath lab HOD = Autocratic when needed in emergencies. Laissez-faire = giving senior interventionalists independence. Leader-as-Technician = the only one who can do complex bifurcation PCI. Leader-as-Coach = training junior cardiologists. Know ALL 5 styles for MCQ.

Group Adv/Disadv + Positive Messages

Advantages of Group Communication
Primary purpose: share information Manage conflicts; overcome difficult circumstances Generation of ideas from different viewpoints More comprehensive solutions Group achieves more favourable outcomes than individuals with superior authorities
Disadvantages of Group Communication
Few dominate → actual viewpoints not shared Personally related members may deviate into personal discussions
Routine vs Positive Message — Definitions
Routine: reader pleased/interested/neutral; who/when/what/where/why/how Positive: good news, acceptance, or congratulations
3-Step Process for Positive/Routine Messages
1 Planning — Analyze situation, gather info, select right medium
2 Writing — Adapt to audience; open with main idea; details in body; close cordially
3 Completing — Revise, produce, proofread, and distribute
Strategy
Opening: main idea first (direct approach) Body: explain all relevant details Close: cordially; tell audience what to do next Any negative info → present in positive context
