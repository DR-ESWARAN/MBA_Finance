2. Effective Business Communication — 8 Ways to Improve

# Strategy Core Idea
1 Stop Assuming Unspoken rules create misunderstandings — communicate needs openly
2 Teamwork Put aside differences; work toward common goal
3 Listen First Don't plan your reply while others speak
4 Ask Questions Clarify, confirm, and obtain more info
5 Emotional Intelligence Connect emotionally but stay respectful
6 Non-verbal Cues Eye contact, silence, body language matter
7 Reinforce Positive Behaviours Positive reinforcement > negative consequences
8 Be Patient Communication is about strengthening relationships, not winning

⚡ Exam tip: Any of these 8 can appear as an MCQ — know the label + core idea

3. Delivering Your Message — 5 Key Points

  1. Be clear on the message — Simple, inspiring, easy to relate to
  2. Prioritize — Limit facts; use them to back your message, not overwhelm
  3. Set context and expectations — Label the conversation (tell audience what's coming)
  4. Appearance — Complements message; affects credibility
  5. Effective body language — Eye contact, facial expression, gestures, posture, movement

"How we communicate is just as important as what we communicate" — Alan Greenspan

4. Principles of Verbal Communication

4a. Language Has Rules — 3 Types

Rule Type Governs
Syntactic Structure/word order/grammar of sentences
Semantic Meaning of words; how to interpret them
Contextual Meaning based on context and social custom

4b. Properties of Language

  • Reality shaped by language — Language determines how we perceive our world
  • Arbitrary & Symbolic — Words have no inherent meaning; humans assign meaning
    • Same thing, different sounds across languages (dog = Kutta / Kutra / Kukur)
    • Sign = Signifier (word/sound) + Signified (symbol/concept) — no logical link between them
  • Abstract — Words represent concepts not directly experienced (e.g., "amazing beach" vs "hot beach")
  • Organizes & Classifies Reality — Categories: male/female, young/old, tall/short
  • Obstacle to Communication — Language barrier = linguistic differences block message

4c. Barriers to Communication (10 Types)

Barrier Description
Language Different linguistic skills in same workplace
Psychological Stage fear, phobia, depression, speech disorders
Physical Background noise, poor lighting, faulty equipment
Cultural Different cultural meanings for same values/gestures
Organizational Structure Misinformation, lack of training/supervision
Attitude Introversion, social anxiety, mood extremes
Perception Same message interpreted differently by different people
Physiological Poor eyesight, hearing issues, voice disorders
Technological Inability to keep up with new tech tools
Information Overload Too many details in short timeframe
Assumption/Jumping to conclusions Not listening fully before interpreting

5. Emphasis Strategies (4)

Strategy Purpose
Visual Communication Graphical representation to create meaning — charts, diagrams
Signposts Keywords that guide listener through the presentation ("on the other hand", "in conclusion")
Internal Summaries & Foreshadowing Summaries = what was covered; Foreshadowing = what is coming next
Repetition Direct: same message; Indirect: alternative ways of saying the same point

6. Improving Verbal Communication — 10 Tips

  1. Think before you speak
  2. Be clear and concise
  3. Speak with confidence
  4. Vary your vocal tone (avoid monotone)
  5. Be a good listener
  6. Aware of non-verbal cues
  7. Think from audience's perspective
  8. Don't rush — speak each word clearly
  9. Make eye contact
  10. Use their name in conversation

7. Understanding Your Audience — 4 Strategies

Strategy What it means
Research Know audience beforehand via review of existing data
Specificity Identify specific group with specific goals to target
Need Know their needs — not just the message you want to convey
Strategy Audience determines your communication strategy + timing

A message aimed at everyone often appeals to no one

8. Differences in Perception

  • Perception = processing, interpreting, selecting, and organizing of information
  • Different backgrounds → different interpretation of same phenomenon
  • We focus on aspects that interest us, are familiar, or feel important
  • Individual differences driven by: physical characteristics, psychological state, cultural background
  • Perceptual set = attitudes, beliefs, values that shape how we see the world

9. Effective Business Writing — 9 Principles

# Principle Key Point
1 Know your purpose Clarify goal before writing
2 Know your audience Familiar with topic? Expert or layman?
3 Keep it simple Short sentences, short paragraphs
4 Logical structure One point flows to next
5 Format it Headings, tables, bullets, bold
6 Get the tone right 3 Fs: Formal (reports), Friendly (emails), Familiar (colleagues)
7 Plain and simple Plain English; avoid long sentences
8 Spelling & Grammar Errors signal incompetence
9 Proofread Print it out; read twice (meaning + errors); get second opinion

⚡ "3 Fs of Tone" is a likely MCQ: Formal / Friendly / Familiar
