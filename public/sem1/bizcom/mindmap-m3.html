1. Resume Writing — 9 Tips

1
Keywords from job postings — Match employer's "Requirements" / "Qualifications" language
2
Review industry examples — Simple, brief, includes metrics; do NOT copy
3
Professional font — Arial or Times New Roman; size 10–12pt
4
Most relevant info first — Hiring managers spend only ~6 seconds per resume
5
Active language — Power words: "achieved," "earned," "completed," "accomplished"
6
Call attention to achievements — Top 3–4 per role; include measurable metrics
7
Only necessary subheadings — No empty sections; combine if fewer than 2 bullets
8
Appropriate margins — 1-inch all sides; single spacing
9
Proofread and edit — Multiple rounds; print and review; ask trusted colleagues
✉️

2. Cover Letter — 6 Parts

PartContent
HeaderYour name, address, phone, email + employer's contact info + date
Salutation"Dr./Mr./Ms. Last Name" — unknown: "Dear Hiring Manager"
IntroductionState job applied for; where you heard about it; brief skills preview
BodyWhy interested + why excellent candidate; specific examples — show, don't just tell
ClosingRestate fit; request interview; state follow-up; thank employer
SignatureComplimentary close + handwritten signature + typed name
Format Rules
Same font as resume Max one page Space between each section Verify correct employer name Print + read aloud to proofread

3. Business Proposal — 9 Steps

⭐ Sequence MCQ
1
Title page — Your name, company, date, client name
2
Table of contents — Clickable if electronic
3
Executive summary — Why sending; why your solution is best (value proposition)
4
State the problem/need — Show you understand the client's issue
5
Propose a solution — Customized; include deliverables, methods, timeframe
6
Share qualifications — Case studies, awards, accreditations
7
Pricing options — Optional fee table; don't under or over-price
8
Terms and conditions — Timeline, pricing, payment schedules
9
Signature space — Client signs; prompt for questions

4. Sales Message & Report

Sales Message — 4 Strategies
1
Start with greatest benefit — Audiences remember beginning and end most
2
Baby steps — One product at a time; too many options = lost sale
3
Know your audience — Research buyer; individualize message
4
Lead with emotion, follow with reason — Drama/humor → facts → call to action
Report — Full Structure
PartComponents
PreliminaryCover · Title Page · Copyright · Acknowledgements · Authorization · TOC · Illustrations · Synopsis
Main TextIntroduction · Body · Conclusions · Recommendations
SupplementaryAppendixes · References · Bibliography · Glossary · Index
7 Steps to Write a Report
1. Determine objective2. Collect facts3. Study facts 4. Plan facts5. Draft outline6. Edit draft 7. Distribute for feedback

5. Memorandums — 5 Characteristics

CharacteristicKey Point
Audience OrientationConsider audience needs; use terms known to ALL — not just management
Professional, Formal ToneLinear (org → employees); may have legal standing; reflects policies
Subject EmphasisSubject line = specific: "Thanksgiving weekend schedule" NOT "holiday observance"
Direct FormatAlways direct — purpose clearly announced; no indirect format in memos
ObjectivityFacts only — no personal bias, preference, or interest
🫀 Clinical Analogy: A memo = a clinical protocol circular. Direct, formal, objective — sent to all staff. Not a discussion — an announcement. No personal opinion, just institutional policy.
🎤

6. Developing Business Presentations

5 Tips
1
Tell a Story — Connect emotionally; take audience on a journey
2
Keep it Simple — 3 to 5 main points only
3
Know Your Stuff — Be ready for Q&A; review info beforehand for confidence
4
Speak Naturally — Conversational; approachable and competent; avoid jargon
5
Don't Put Everything on Slides — Display a few points; you tell the information
4 Obstacles in Presentations
ObstacleOvercome by
LanguageFind common way to interact; adapt basic language; avoid jargon/slang
PerceptionRecognize others' perceptions differ; beware of stereotypes from cultural differences
EthnocentrismConviction of superiority → egoism, discrimination; avoid assuming your way is best
Physical BarriersCheck all equipment in advance; always have a backup plan
