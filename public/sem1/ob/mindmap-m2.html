OB Module 2 — Diversity & Motivation

DY Patil MBA · Organizational Behaviour · Exam: 15 May 2026

1. Workforce Diversity
Definition
Similarities & differences among employees in: age, culture, race, gender, religion, disability, sexual orientation
Makes workforce heterogeneous → necessity + challenge
3 Key Drivers
Women in Workplace — highest-ever participation; dual-income & single-mother families
Organizational Restructuring — downsizing/outsourcing → fewer people, more work
Legislation — equal opportunity laws; both employer & employee rights defined
Benefits
Mutual respect → synergistic teams
Economic empowerment of marginalized workers
Reduces litigation; boosts creativity, recruitment, business image
Diverse firms → better financial performance (research)
Negative barriers: Prejudice, stereotyping, discrimination → harm morale + invite lawsuits. Never use in hiring/termination.
2. Managing Diversity
Structured Interview (15 Characteristics)
Same questions for all · Job analysis basis · Multiple interviewers
Anchored rating scales · Detailed notes · No discussion between interviews
Statistical prediction · Training for interviewers
Similarity bias = preferring candidates similar to oneself → mitigated by all 15 characteristics
Diversified Mentoring
Mentor & mentee differ in race, gender, class, disability, sexual orientation
Mutually beneficial → both gain knowledge, empathy, cross-group skills
Diversity Training Evolution
1964: Civil Rights Act → compliance-only
1980s–90s: Women & minorities focus (often backfired — singled out Whites)
Now: (1) Cultural competencies (2) Valuing differences (3) Better business decisions
Perspective-taking + goal-setting activities → pro-diversity attitudes persist months later
3. Managerial Decision Making — 5 Steps (EDIAE)
#StepKey Point
1Establish ObjectivePrivate → profit max · Public → benefit-cost criterion
2Define ProblemIdentify ROOT CAUSE (e.g., why are profits falling?)
3Identify AlternativesList all possible solutions; consider variable relationships
4Evaluate AlternativesRegression, linear programming, cost-benefit; pick optimum under constraints
5Implement DecisionMonitor continuously; corrective action if results diverge
Constraints: Legal · Financial · Infrastructure/Raw materials · Technological
4. Barriers to Effective Decision Making
BarrierMeaning
Bounded RationalityBrain can't process all info; non-programmed decisions especially vulnerable
Escalation of CommitmentContinuing a bad decision despite worsening outcomes (sunk cost trap)
Time ConstraintsPressure → heuristics → suboptimal choices
UncertaintyCannot know outcome before choosing; must commit blind
Personal Bias"Similar to me" bias; Confirmation bias = seeking info that confirms existing beliefs
ConflictDiscomfort avoidance → skip necessary hard decisions
Clinical analogy: Escalation of commitment = surgeon persisting with failing procedure instead of converting — sunk cost in the OR.
5. Improving Decision Quality
Good Decision = Right Person + Best Evidence + Sound Process
7 Strategies
Learn from experience — record & reflect on past decisions
Entertain doubt — test against "what if 100% wrong?" scenarios
Give options — optimal 3–4 alternatives; fewer = dilemma, more = paralysis
Argue it out — assign devil's advocate; appoint red team
Understand context — go to base data, not bullet summaries
Experiment / Pilots — win-win: success = confidence; failure = prevent disaster
Pre-mortem (Gary Klein) — "Pretend it already failed. What caused it?"
Listening > Talking: Leader who speaks first biases group discussion. Hold opinion until others have fully spoken.

7. Process Theories of Motivation
Focus: WHY behaviours are initiated — how we choose a target & exert effort toward it
TheoryCore IdeaKey Phrase
Operant Conditioning Do what's rewarded; avoid what's punished "Law of Effect"
Equity Theory Motivation driven by perceived fairness compared to others (input/output ratio) Unfairness destroys satisfaction instantly
Goal Theory People attempt to achieve goals they INTEND to achieve; goals → greater effort & direction Strongest theory in OB
Expectancy Theory Exert effort to achieve valued outcomes. Asks: (a) which alternative to choose? (b) how motivated to pursue? Most comprehensive theory
Clinical analogy (Equity): Junior registrar paid same as senior consultant for same work → senior's motivation collapses. Perceived inequity = instant disengagement.
8. Performance Appraisal
Arguments FOR
Direction — aligns individual goals with org goals
Feedback — formal one-to-one performance discussion
Training needs — surfaces areas of weakness
Reward linked to performance — motivates & retains high achievers
Motivating goals — challenging but achievable = stretch performance
Arguments AGAINST (Deming)
Performance equation: Effort + Ability + System Capability + Training = Performance → system factors ignored in individual appraisals
Halo Effect — reputation biases appraiser's judgment
Personal bias — appraiser's likes/dislikes color scores
Lack of reproducibility — same person rated differently by different managers
Targets unworkable: Too low → caps aspiration · Too high → cheating/blame-shifting · Negotiated targets → "aiming for mediocrity"
Deming's verdict: Measurement should improve the SYSTEM, not just evaluate individuals. Using targets to judge people = negative reactions that undermine performance.
