Module 4 · The Companies Act, 2013

Business Law & Corporate Governance · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map
  • Section 3(1)(i), Companies Act 1956: "a company formed and registered under this Act or an existing company"
  • Chief Justice Marshall (USA): "A company is a person, artificial, invisible, intangible, and existing only in the contemplation of the law"
  • Lord Justice Lindley: "A company is an association of many persons who contribute money or money's worth to a common stock and employ it in some trade or business, and who share the profit and loss arising therefrom"
  • Haney: "Joint Stock Company is a voluntary association of individuals for profit, having a capital divided into transferable shares, the ownership of which is the condition of membership"
  • Companies Act 2013 replaced Companies Act 1956 — comprehensive provisions for all listed and unlisted companies

2. Characteristics of a Company

Characteristic Key Points
Incorporated Association Created on registration under Companies Act; association >10 persons (banking) or >20 persons (other) must register — else deemed illegal association
Artificial Legal Person Exists in eyes of law; cannot act on its own; acts through board of directors ("the board are the brains and the only brains" — Bates v Standard Land Co.)
Separate Legal Entity Independent of members; creditors can recover only from company property; company not liable for individual member debts
Perpetual Existence Life not dependent on death/insolvency/retirement of shareholders or directors; law creates it, law alone dissolves it; "members may come and go but company can go on forever"
Common Seal Substitute for company's signature (company cannot sign physically); name engraved on seal; affixed by authority of board resolution in presence of at least 2 directors + secretary
Limited Liability Members liable only to unpaid value of shares (limited by shares) OR fixed guarantee amount (limited by guarantee)
Transferable Shares In public company, shares freely transferable — statutory right; absolute restriction on transfer = ultra vires
Separate Property Company owns property in its own name; members have no ownership in company assets individually or jointly
Delegated Management Actual management delegated to elected directors; shareholders decide general policy by majority; democratic lines
  • Clinical analogy: a company's separate legal entity = like a hospital trust — staff come and go, but the institution and its obligations persist independent of individuals

3. Types of Companies

A. By Mode of Incorporation

Type Description Examples
Chartered (Royal Charter) Companies Created by Royal Charter (monarch's special order); do NOT exist in India East India Company, Bank of England, BBC
Statutory Companies Incorporated by Special Act of Parliament (Central or State); formed for national importance; NO memorandum or articles RBI (RBI Act 1934), LIC (LIC Act 1956), SBI, Unit Trust of India
Registered/Incorporated Companies Formed under Companies Act; come into existence only after certificate of incorporation; most popular mode Google India Pvt Ltd

B. By Number of Members

Type Min Members Max Members Key Features
Public Limited Company 7 No limit Shares freely transferable; min 3 directors; needs certificate of commencement; must issue prospectus
Private Limited Company 2 50 Transfer restricted; cannot invite public; min 2 directors; no prospectus needed; starts business on certificate of incorporation
One Person Company (OPC) 1 1 NEW under Companies Act 2013; similar to sole proprietorship but with limited liability

C. By Liability of Members

Type Liability
Limited by Shares To extent of face value of unpaid shares
Limited by Guarantee To fixed guaranteed amount promised in memorandum in event of winding up
Unlimited No limit — personal assets at risk; very uncommon today

D. By Control

  • Holding Company (Sec 4(4)): Company that controls another company by: (a) holding >50% of equity capital, OR (b) holding >50% voting rights, OR (c) securing right to appoint majority of directors
  • Subsidiary Company (Sec 4(1)): Company whose control is exercised by holding company

E. By Ownership

  • Government Company: Not less than 51% paid-up capital held by Central/State government; auditors appointed by government on advice of Comptroller and Auditor General; annual report placed before both Houses of Parliament
  • Non-Government Company: All other companies

F. By Nationality

  • Indian Companies: Registered in India; registered office in India; nationality of members irrelevant
  • Foreign Companies (Sec 591(1)): Incorporated outside India but has established place of business in India

4. Private Company vs. Public Company — Key Differences

Feature Private Company Public Company
Min members 2 7
Max members 50 No limit
Directors Min 2 Min 3
Transfer of shares Restricted Freely transferable
Public invitation Prohibited Permitted
Prospectus Not required Required
Commencement Certificate of incorporation only Certificate of commencement also required
Quorum 2 members 5 members
Managerial remuneration No restriction Cannot exceed 11% of net profits (Sec 198)
Name Must use "Private Limited" "Limited"

When Private Company Becomes Public:

  1. By default (fails to comply with Sec 3(1)(iii) requirements)
  2. If subsidiary of a public company
  3. By operation of law — Section 43-A

5. Certificate of Incorporation

  • Certificate of incorporation = "birth certificate of a company"
  • Company comes into existence from date mentioned in the certificate
  • Date in certificate is conclusive evidence — even if wrong
  • Conclusive evidence that all requirements of the Act have been fulfilled
  • Cannot be cancelled even if it contains irregularities
  • Company can only be dissolved through winding up provisions of the Act

6. Memorandum of Association (MOA)

  • Definition: Main document of the company; defines objects and fundamental conditions upon which the company is allowed to be formed
  • Also called the "charter of the company"
  • Governs the company's relationship with the outside world
  • Defines the scope of activities
  • Enables shareholders, creditors, and those dealing with the company to know the permitted range of activities

Clauses of MOA:

Clause Content
Name Clause Company name (must be unique; not undesirable); "Limited" or "Private Limited" must be added if liability is limited
Registered Office Clause Name of state where registered office is situated; fixes domicile of company
Object Clause Objects for which company is formed; extent of powers to achieve objects
Capital Clause Amount of share capital; division into fixed-amount shares
Liability Clause States that liability of members is limited
Association/Subscription Clause Subscribers declare desire to form company; agree to take shares; min 7 subscribers (public), min 2 (private)

Forms of MOA (Schedule I):

  • Table B: Company limited by shares
  • Table C: Company limited by guarantee (no share capital)
  • Table D: Company limited by guarantee (with share capital)
  • Table E: Unlimited company

Alteration of MOA:

  • Strict restrictions; detailed formalities required
  • Alterations must NOT be prejudicial to members or creditors
  • Must NOT increase liability of members or creditors
  • Some conditions cannot be altered except with sanction of Central Government

7. Articles of Association (AOA)

  • Definition: Rules, regulations and bye-laws for governing the internal affairs of the company
  • Describes powers of directors, officers, and shareholders
  • Lays down mode and manner of conducting business
  • Must NOT go beyond powers in MOA
  • Must NOT violate requirements of Companies Act
  • Clauses ultra vires the MOA or Act = null and void
  • Must be printed, numbered, and signed by each MOA subscriber

Contents of AOA:

  • Share capital, classes of shares, allotment, lien, calls, forfeiture, certificates, warrants
  • Transfer and transmission of shares
  • Alteration of share capital; borrowing powers
  • Rules regarding meetings; voting rights; notices to members
  • Dividends and reserves; accounts and audit
  • Directors: appointment, remuneration, managing director, secretary
  • Common seal; winding up

Model Forms (Schedule I):

  • Table A: Regulations for management of company limited by shares
  • Public company may adopt Table A in full, exclude it entirely, or frame own articles + adopt part of Table A
  • If Articles do not expressly exclude Table A provisions → Table A automatically applies

8. MOA vs. AOA — Key Distinctions

Feature MOA AOA
Nature Charter of company; supreme document Internal regulations; subordinate to MOA
Purpose Governs relationship with outside world; defines scope Rules for internal management
Status Supreme Subsidiary to MOA
Ultra vires act Wholly void; cannot be ratified even by all shareholders Can be confirmed by shareholders if intra vires MOA
Mandatory Every company must have own MOA Company limited by shares may adopt Table A instead
Alteration Strict restrictions; some require Central Govt sanction Can be altered by special resolution (must not conflict with MOA or Act)

9. Prospectus

  • Prospectus = document issued by company inviting public/investors to subscribe for its securities
  • Informs investors about risk of investing
  • Required only after incorporation of company
  • Only public listed companies can issue prospectus
  • Private companies CANNOT issue prospectus (prohibited from public invitation)
    • Exception: Private company converted to public company may then issue prospectus

Contents of Prospectus:

  • Date of issue; company name and registered office
  • Central Govt consent / SEBI compliance
  • Voting rights, dividend, expenses on issue
  • Name of stock exchange; punishment for fictitious applications
  • Refund if 90% minimum subscription not received
  • Names and addresses of lead managers
  • Issue of allotment letter or refund within 10 weeks with interest
  • Date of opening and closing of issues
  • CRISIL credit rating (Credit Rating Information Services of India Limited)
  • Terms of underwriting; risk factors; capital structure
  • Terms of issue; restriction on transfer and transmission

10. Shares — Key Sections

Section Rule
Sec 44 Shares/debentures = movable property, transferable as per Articles
Sec 45 Every share must be distinguished by its distinctive number (not required for depository-held shares)
Sec 46 Share certificate under common seal = prima facie evidence of title to shares

Share Allotment:

  • Creation and issuing of new shares by a company
  • Can be issued to new or existing shareholders
  • May affect existing shareholders' share proportion
  • New shares typically allotted to bring in new business partners
