Module 3 · The Sale of Goods Act

Business Law & Corporate Governance · Notes

NotesMind map
  • Contract of sale of goods = "a contract whereby the seller transfers or agrees to transfer the property in goods to the buyer for a price"
  • May be between one part-owner and another
  • May be absolute or conditional

Sale vs. Agreement to Sell:

Term Definition
Sale Property in goods is transferred from seller to buyer immediately — executed/absolute contract
Agreement to Sell Transfer of property to take place at a future time or subject to condition — executory contract
  • An agreement to sell becomes a sale when the time elapses or the conditions are fulfilled
  • Trading analogy: Sale = immediate delivery of shares; Agreement to sell = futures contract — transfer at future date on specified conditions

2. Subject Matter and Types of Goods

  • Subject matter must be goods (movable property — excluding actionable claims and money)
  • Contracts for services are NOT contracts of sale
  • Immovable property is governed by Transfer of Property Act (NOT Sale of Goods Act)
Type Description
Existing Goods Owned/possessed by seller; ready for transfer
Future Goods Not yet available; transfer after a time
Contingent Goods Sale may or may not take place depending on contingency

3. Six Essential Elements of a Contract of Sale

  1. Goods — every kind of movable property except actionable claims and money
  2. Price — money consideration for sale; can be partly money + partly goods; payment NOT required at time of contract
  3. Two Parties — seller and buyer; cannot buy one's own goods
  4. Transfer of Ownership — ownership must change; if not = NOT a sale; transfer does NOT occur in agreement to sell
  5. All Essentials of a Valid Contract — offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, lawful object, legal formalities
  6. Includes both sale and agreement to sell — "contract of sale" is generic term

4. How a Contract of Sale is Made

  • By an offer to buy or sell for a price + acceptance of such offer
  • May provide for: immediate delivery, immediate payment, delivery/payment by instalments, or postponement of either
  • May be made in writing, by word of mouth, or implied from conduct of parties

5. Goods Perishing — Key Sections

Section Rule
Sec 7 (Perishing before contract) If specific goods perish WITHOUT seller's knowledge before contract is made → contract is void
Sec 8 (Perishing after agreement to sell) If goods perish after agreement to sell, WITHOUT fault of either party, before risk passes to buyer → agreement is avoided

6. Ascertainment of Price

  • Sec 9: Price may be fixed by contract, agreed method, or course of dealing
  • If no price fixed → buyer shall pay a reasonable price (question of fact)
  • Sec 10: If price to be fixed by third party valuation and third party fails → agreement is avoided; but if goods already delivered and appropriated → buyer pays reasonable price

7. Condition and Warranty

Term Definition Consequence of Breach
Condition Stipulation essential to the main purpose of contract Right to treat contract as repudiated (reject goods)
Warranty Stipulation collateral to main purpose of contract Right to claim damages only (cannot reject goods)
  • Whether a stipulation is condition or warranty depends on construction of the contract
  • A stipulation may be a condition even if called a warranty in the contract

When Condition Treated as Warranty (Sec 13):

  • Buyer may waive the condition
  • If contract is not severable and buyer has accepted goods → breach of condition treated as breach of warranty (cannot reject goods) unless express/implied term allows otherwise
  • Clinical analogy: Condition = essential cardiac function; Warranty = supplementary monitoring — breach of condition = cardiac arrest (major); breach of warranty = mild arrhythmia (compensable)

8. Transfer of Property — Specific/Ascertained Goods (Sections 19–22)

Section Rule
Sec 19 Property passes at time parties intend it to pass; determined from terms, conduct, and circumstances
Sec 20 Specific goods in deliverable state → property passes at time of contract (even if delivery/payment delayed)
Sec 21 Specific goods NOT yet in deliverable state → property passes only when seller puts them in deliverable state AND notifies buyer
Sec 22 Specific goods in deliverable state but seller must weigh/measure/test to ascertain price → property passes only after this is done AND buyer is notified

9. Transfer of Property — Unascertained Goods (Section 23)

  • Sec 23(1): For unascertained goods by description → property passes when goods of specific description are appropriated to contract with consent of the other party (consent can be express or implied)
  • Sec 23(2): If seller delivers goods to buyer/carrier/bailee for transmission to buyer without reserving disposal rights → seller has unconditionally appropriated the contract

10. Unpaid Seller

  • Unpaid seller = seller who has not been paid the whole price, OR received conditional payment (bill of exchange) and that condition has not been fulfilled
  • "Seller" includes agents of seller, consignors/agents who have paid the price

Rights of Unpaid Seller:

Right Condition
Lien on goods While in possession; for the price
Stoppage in transit After parting with possession; if buyer is insolvent
Right of re-sale As limited by Act
Right of withholding delivery If property has NOT yet passed to buyer

11. Negotiable Instruments Act — Meaning and Nature

  • Negotiable instruments = written contracts whose benefit can be passed from original holder to new holder
  • Documents that promise payment to the assignee or specified person
  • Transferable signed documents — promise payment to bearer on demand or at a future time
  • Once transferred → holder obtains full legal title
  • Trading analogy: like a tradeable financial instrument — bearer is entitled to claim value

12. Types of Negotiable Instruments

Instrument Definition
Promissory Note Written promise by an entity/individual to pay holder a certain sum by a pre-decided date; shows amount owed + interest rate + date
Bill of Exchange Legally binding written document that instructs a party (payer) to pay a predetermined sum to another party (payee/seller); signed by party who owes money
Cheque Instrument in writing containing unconditional order to a banker, signed by account holder, requiring banker to pay sum on demand to bearer or specified person

Bill of Exchange — Additional Points:

  • Issued by financial institutions = bank draft
  • Issued by individual = trade draft
  • Used in international trade as promissory note between exporter and importer; banks act as third-party guarantors

13. Negotiation vs. Assignment

Feature Negotiation Assignment
Mode of transfer Delivery only Written document signed by transferor
Consideration Always presumed Must be proved
Notice to debtor Not necessary Must be given
Title of transferee Takes free of defects if holder in due course Takes subject to all defects of transferor's title

14. Holder in Due Course

  • "Holder in due course" = person who for consideration became possessor of a promissory note/bill of exchange/cheque, before amount became payable, and without sufficient cause to believe any defect existed in title of person from whom derived title
  • Enjoys full legal rights to the instrument free of prior defects

15. Dishonour and Discharge of Negotiable Instruments

Dishonour — Two Types:

(I) Dishonour by Non-Acceptance (Bill of Exchange):

  1. Drawee defaults in acceptance (48 hours required)
  2. Presentment for acceptance excused but bill not accepted
  3. Drawee is incompetent to contract
  4. Drawee makes qualified acceptance
  5. Drawee is fictitious person or cannot be found

(II) Dishonour by Non-Payment (Sec 92):

  • Maker, acceptor of bill, or drawee of cheque defaults in payment when duly required to pay
  • Also dishonoured if presentment is excused expressly by maker/acceptor but note/bill remains unpaid

Effect of Dishonour:

  • Holder becomes entitled to sue the parties liable
  • Holder MUST give notice of dishonour to all parties against whom he intends to proceed

Notice of Dishonour:

  • Formal communication of the fact of dishonour
  • Enables notified person to protect himself against prior parties
  • Must be given by holder or party to the instrument remaining liable
  • Any party receiving notice must transmit to all prior parties
  • Only one person needs to give notice; duly authorized person can also give notice
  • If no notice given → party (other than maker of note, acceptor of bill, drawer of cheque) is discharged from liability

16. Noting and Protest

  • Noting = dishonoured instrument noted by Notary Public; must specify: (i) date of dishonour, (ii) reason for dishonour, (iii) notary's charges
  • Protest = formal certificate issued by notary public to holder on demand (noting = mere record; protest = formal certificate)

17. Discharge of Negotiable Instrument

An instrument is discharged (becomes completely useless) when:

  1. Payment in due course made to holder
  2. Acceptor at or after maturity holds bill of exchange that has been negotiated
  3. Party primarily liable becomes insolvent
  4. Holder cancels the instrument intending to release the primarily liable party

Note (Supreme Court — M/s Sri Krishna Agencies v. State of A.P.): Arbitration proceedings do NOT prevent criminal proceedings for cheque bounce. Both can proceed simultaneously.

High-Yield MCQ Targets — Module 3

  • Sale vs. Agreement to Sell — key difference (immediate vs. future transfer)
  • Types of goods: existing, future, contingent
  • Condition = essential to contract (breach = repudiation); Warranty = collateral (breach = damages only)
  • Sec 7 and Sec 8: goods perishing — void vs. avoided
  • Sec 20: specific goods in deliverable state → property passes at contract formation
  • Unpaid seller's three rights: lien, stoppage in transit, right of re-sale
  • Negotiable instruments: 3 types — promissory note, bill of exchange, cheque
  • Bank draft = bill issued by financial institution; trade draft = issued by individual
  • Holder in due course: took for consideration, before maturity, without notice of defect
  • Dishonour: by non-acceptance (5 circumstances) and by non-payment (Sec 92)
  • Notice of dishonour must be given; failure = party discharged
  • Negotiation vs Assignment: negotiation = delivery only; consideration presumed; no notice required
  • Noting = Notary Public record; Protest = formal certificate
