BLCG Module 3: The Sale of Goods Act

DY Patil MBA Finance | Exam: 15 May 2026

Contract of Sale — Basics

  • "Contract whereby seller transfers or agrees to transfer property in goods to buyer for a price"
  • Goods = movable property (NOT actionable claims or money)
  • Immovable property = Transfer of Property Act (not SGA)
  • Services = NOT contract of sale
SaleAgreement to Sell
Immediate transfer of ownershipTransfer at future time or on condition
Executed / AbsoluteExecutory / Conditional
Agreement to sell → becomes Sale when time/condition fulfilled—

Types of Goods & Sections

  • Existing: owned/possessed; ready for transfer
  • Future: not yet available; transfer after time
  • Contingent: depends on contingency
SectionRule
Sec 7Goods perish before contract (no seller knowledge) → contract is VOID
Sec 8Goods perish after agreement to sell, no fault → agreement is AVOIDED
Sec 9No price fixed → buyer pays reasonable price
Sec 103rd party valuation fails → agreement avoided

Condition vs. Warranty

ConditionWarranty
NatureEssential to main purposeCollateral to main purpose
BreachRight to repudiate contract (reject goods)Right to damages only
  • Stipulation may be a condition even if called a warranty in contract
  • Sec 13: Buyer may waive condition; if goods accepted + contract not severable → breach of condition = warranty only

Transfer of Property (Secs 19–23)

SectionRule
Sec 19Property passes at time parties intend it to pass
Sec 20Specific goods in deliverable state → passes at time of contract
Sec 21Not in deliverable state → passes when seller makes ready + notifies buyer
Sec 22Must weigh/measure/test → passes after done + buyer notified
Sec 23Unascertained: passes on appropriation with consent of other party

Unpaid Seller

  • Whole price NOT paid/tendered
  • OR conditional payment (bill of exchange) dishonoured
  • 3 Rights:
  • Lien on goods while in possession
  • Stoppage in transit if buyer is insolvent
  • Right of re-sale as limited by Act
  • If property NOT passed → also right to withhold delivery

Negotiable Instruments

TypeKey Feature
Promissory NoteWritten promise to pay holder a certain sum on date/on demand
Bill of ExchangeWritten order to payer to pay payee; bank = bank draft; individual = trade draft
ChequeUnconditional order to banker to pay bearer/specified person on demand
  • Holder in due course: for consideration, before maturity, no notice of defect → takes free of prior defects

Dishonour, Noting & Discharge

  • Dishonour by Non-Acceptance (BoE): drawee defaults; excused but not accepted; incompetent; qualified acceptance; fictitious drawee
  • Dishonour by Non-Payment (Sec 92): maker/acceptor/drawee defaults in payment when required
  • Must give notice of dishonour to all parties or they are discharged
  • Noting = Notary Public record (date, reason, charges)
  • Protest = formal certificate from Notary
  • Negotiation vs Assignment: Negotiation = delivery only; consideration presumed; no notice needed
  • SC: Arbitration proceedings do NOT stop cheque bounce criminal case

★ High-Yield Exam Targets

  • Sale = immediate transfer; Agreement to sell = future/conditional transfer
  • Sec 7 = goods perish before contract → VOID; Sec 8 = perish after agreement → AVOIDED
  • Condition = breach gives repudiation; Warranty = breach gives damages only
  • Sec 20: specific goods in deliverable state → property passes at contract formation
  • Unpaid seller's 3 rights: lien, stoppage in transit, re-sale
  • Bank draft = BoE by bank; trade draft = BoE by individual
  • Holder in due course: consideration + before maturity + no notice of defect
  • Notice of dishonour must be given; failure = party discharged
  • Noting = Notary record; Protest = formal Notary certificate
  • Negotiation: delivery only; consideration presumed; no notice to debtor required
