BLCG Module 2: Moral Values & Ethics

DY Patil MBA Finance | Exam: 15 May 2026

Values — Definitions

  • Haralambos (2000): "a value is a belief that something is good and desirable"
  • R.K. Mukerjee (1949): "socially approved desires and goals internalized through conditioning, learning or socialization"
  • Types:
  • Intrinsic Values — goals of life; ultimate/transcendent; HIGHEST in hierarchy
  • Instrumental Values — means to achieve goals; incidental/proximate values

Formation of Values

  • Confluence of personal experiences + culture
  • Imposed from family in childhood
  • Reinforced through culture and life experiences
  • Culture provides: community, shared reality, prescriptions for conduct
  • Values ebb and flow with culture (varies by country)

Management Ethics

  • Definition: "discipline dealing with what is good/bad, right/wrong, with moral duty and obligation; standard of behavior guiding managers"
  • Business Ethics (John Donaldson): "systematic study of ethical matters pertaining to business"
  • 3 Steps to Develop Ethics:
  • 1. Make expectations clear — write them down
  • 2. Enforce your policies — breaches must have consequences
  • 3. Be your own change agent — walk your talk

Business Ethics & Dilemmas

  • Ethical requirements codified in law: environmental regulations, minimum wage, insider trading restrictions, collusion restrictions
  • Ethical dilemma = 2 options, neither absolutely acceptable from ethical perspective
  • Extremely complicated; not easily solved
  • Stakeholders: when only shareholder wealth maximization → imbalance → business ethics problem
  • Ethics failures: Enron, Adelphia; excessive CEO pay

BRICS

  • Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa
  • BRICS Summit = convened annually
  • NOT a formal organization — summit between supreme leaders
  • ~3 billion people (~half of world population)
  • Fastest growing + largest emerging market economies
  • Goldman Sachs: by 2050, BRICS = most important; USA = 5th place
  • Primarily an investment category

CSR & Sustainability

  • CSR = corporate citizenship; socially accountable to itself, stakeholders, public
  • Example: Starbucks — 99% ethical coffee, green buildings, refugee hiring
  • Sustainability addresses 2 categories:
  • 1. Effect on environment
  • 2. Effect on society
  • ESG = Environmental, Social, Governance metrics
  • High ESG = lower cost of debt and equity
  • Spiritual values: positive consensus on spirituality-ethics relationship
