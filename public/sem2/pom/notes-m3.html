1. Need for facility location planning

  • Facility location = selecting a suitable site where the plant or facility is installed and starts functioning.
Type of firm Location focus (Ebook)
Industrial Minimise cost
Retail / professional service Maximise revenue
Warehouse Cost + speed of delivery

  • Objective of location strategy: maximise the benefit of location to the firm. Main driver of new facilities: the global economic boom with worldwide capacity growth.

  • Ebook: 5 other reasons to change or add locations: (1) cost/availability of labour, raw material, resources changes · (2) market shifts geographically · (3) firms split, merge or get acquired · (4) new products · (5) political, economic, legal requirements (friendlier environment or labour laws).

  • Quick Notes: 8 reasons: expansion · cost savings · finding resources · extra needs · combining businesses · political & social change · increased demand · tax benefits (rural area incentives).

2. Nature of location decisions

  • One of the most important long-term cost and revenue decisions; fixes both fixed and variable costs.

  • Transport cost alone can be 25% of selling price. Location choice can alter total production and distribution cost by as much as 10%, "the easiest 10% savings management ever makes".

  • Once committed, many costs are locked in (high energy cost region, poor labour) and hard to reduce.

  • Quick Notes: matters for new and established firms, manufacturing and non-manufacturing. Low-cost strategy → near customers, local labour (barber, bank, sweet shop). Quality strategy → skilled labour, top raw material (car makers, IT services).

3. Types of facilities (Ebook)

Type Examples Key location factor
Heavy manufacturing Auto plants, steel mills, oil refineries Construction & land cost, transport (rail), near raw material, utilities, waste disposal, environment. Near-customer less important
Light industry TVs, computers, tool & die, breweries, pharma Skilled labour pool, community, air travel, regulation. Land/raw material less crucial
Warehouse / distribution Store, handle, ship Transport & shipping cost (most important); near customers. Raw material, utilities, waste: almost no importance
Retail & service Restaurants, banks, hotels, clinics, groceries Proximity to customers (single most important); site cost high ("location is everything")

Clinic: A cath lab is "retail & service" for location: it must sit where patients and referring doctors are. A stent warehouse is "warehouse": what matters is courier speed, not footfall.

4. Four levels of facility planning

Level What it decides
1. Global Site location
2. Macro Site planning: number, size, location of buildings; roads, rail, water, energy. Most critical / strategic
3. Micro Facility and building layout: functional layout and physical arrangement of economic activity centres
4. Sub-micro Workstation design
  • Progress global → sub-micro in sequence; each phase is "frozen" by consensus. Benefits: settle global issues first · smooth progress without revisiting · details don't overwhelm the project.

5. Factors: manufactured products

  1. Location of markets: bulky/heavy, fragile, perishable goods; transport cost, transit time, service promptness, selling price. Assembly-type and custom products locate near market.

  2. Location of materials: important if transport is the major part of unit cost, material is regional, bulky, loses bulk in processing, or perishable (processing raises shelf life). Transport cost is not only distance (Delhi-Patna costs more than Delhi-Mumbai).

  3. Transportation facilities: road, rail, water, air. Bulk of freight goes by rail (low cost, flexibility, speed). Ports matter for heavy, low-value-per-ton import/export goods.

  4. Labour supply: manpower is the most costly input. Rule of thumb: area should have 4 times as many permanent job applicants as needed; industry vs commerce mix about 50/50. Check skills, unionisation, labour relations history, turnover, absenteeism. India: software (Microsoft, TI, Cisco, Oracle), Hyundai small-car hub.

  5. Location of other plants and warehouses: whole-system view; minimise total system cost; watch competitors' plants.

  6. Climate: Hurricane Katrina threatened Houston petrochemical plants; Japan's seismic zones.

  7. Government controls and regulations: taxes, environment rules, incentives. World Bank "Doing Business 2006": India 116 of 155; New Zealand 1st, Singapore 2nd; starting a business in India = 11 procedures, ~72 days; contract enforcement ~40 procedures, 425 days; taxes paid 59 times a year.

6. Factors: service products

  • In services, capacity to deliver must be decided first (a hotel room in another city is useless to the customer). 8 parameters:

  1. Purchasing power of customer drawing area

  2. Service and image compatibility with its demographics

  3. Competition in the area · quality of competition

  4. Uniqueness of firm's and competitors' locations

  5. Physical qualities of facilities and neighbouring businesses

  6. Operating policies · quality of management

  • Example: Karim's (Delhi) outlets in Delhi, Noida, Gurgaon; malls with M&S, McDonald's, Tissot, Nike.

8. Site selection: country → state → plant

  • Pool of sites starts global (proximity to customers is not key for a factory); narrowed methodically. GM Saturn plant, Spring Hill, Tennessee (1985): took several years and hundreds of sites.

  • Country: US firms moved abroad for cheap labour (1970s-80s) but missed that the real edge was quality management; shipping cost, skill gaps, forex and unstable governments often cancel savings. Mercedes-Benz now builds in the US for lower labour cost.

  • State/region (India): Western and Central regions most preferred; Eastern least (labour relations).

  • Plant/site: within a community; for service/retail, customer concentration and cost matter most.

Case: P&G third plant Existing plants: Mandideep (MP) for Ariel and Tide; Kundaim (Goa) for Whisper and Vicks. Shampoos (H&S, Pantene, Rejoice) imported from Thailand/ASEAN. Evaluating Himachal Pradesh for backward-area benefits and income tax rebates; VAT and MRP-based excise make outsourcing less attractive. PGHH sales Rs 577.24 cr (+30%), profit Rs 92.17 cr (+35%), year to June 2004.

9. Macro level: procedures for location decisions

  • Macro planning has the greatest strategic impact. It includes: (1) facility master plan (multi-year) · (2) impact planning · (3) evaluation · (4) layout, space allocation, capacity · (5) space standards.

Facility master plan

  • Plans the right services (mission, strategy, market) · right size (demand, staff, equipment) · right location (access, efficiency, building) · right financial structure.

  • Covers 12 areas: land-use planning · site evaluation · zoning analysis · traffic impact analysis · site engineering analysis · architectural programming · needs assessment survey · interior space planning · adaptive reuse study · building design · site design · landscape design.

Phase Work
Phase I Information gathering and analysis: development history, local/regional context, current and projected conditions, baseline data (market, workload, space, issues)
Phase II Synthesis into a master plan: big-picture issues (image, identity), review strategies and investments, phasing plan = "road map" for multi-year investment; immediate, short and long-range projects

Impact planning

  • Every facility leaves an ecological footprint (should be minimised). Impact planning = integration of commercial and practical environmental objectives for optimum benefit to business and environment.

  • Protect: vegetation/tree cover · wetlands, swamps, mangroves · protected areas · lakes · rivers and creeks · sea coast. Also check topography, soil, drainage (avoid low-lying land). Example: IOC depot oil seepage in Bihar polluted the water table.

  • Sahara Mall (Gurgaon): shoppers' traffic, diesel generators (unreliable power), groundwater use. Mall uses AVR, 4 DG sets in noise-proof rooms, 2 bore wells, rooftop rainwater harvesting, cooled garbage room cleared nightly.

10. Site evaluation

  1. Size of site: plant + utilities, waste/water treatment, parking, support, room to expand

  2. Utilities: power limits, peak-hour cost, summer water, water hardness, connection cost

  3. Transportation: rail and road nearby; carrier reliability, frequency, airport distance

  4. Land cost: non-recurring, of little importance in the location decision

City Industrial estate / area Interior greenfield
Highly skilled staff; quick contact with customers and suppliers Far from employees' homes Large land need
Plant size limited; multi-floor Tax exemptions; free of city zoning rules Suits dangerous or objectionable processes
Small-quantity transport; urban utilities Infrastructure not a concern; environment met cheaply Needs large volumes of pure water; tax exemptions
Start with minimum investment (rent) Close to transport and population Few skilled workers; must invest in infrastructure, housing
  • Location analysis is periodic: a location may not stay optimal forever. Community: plant must be safe and acceptable; climate, wind/earthquake loads, capital grants, tax rates, zoning and building codes.

11. Micro level planning

  • Decisions on functional layout and physical arrangement of economic activity centres (teller window, waiting space, machine, workbench, stairway, aisle, cafeteria, storage).

  • Good micro planning: easy flow of materials and information · efficient use of labour and equipment · customer convenience and retail sales · safer working conditions · morale · communication.

  • Questions: what each centre includes · how much space · how configured · where located (relative location vs absolute location).

12. Techniques of locational analysis

Factor rating method (Ebook example)

  • Auto ancillary supplying Maruti; options Jammu (promoters, tax incentives), Chandigarh (land already owned), Gurgaon (next to Maruti; Maruti invests as JV partner).

  1. Choose important factors; rate each factor 1 to 5 (5 = most important)

  2. Give each site a location score 1 to 10 (10 = best)

  3. Composite score = factor rating × location score; add up per site

  4. Highest total wins: Gurgaon

  • Limitation: assumes cost differences are insignificant or already reflected (Gurgaon land may be very costly; Chandigarh land historic cost). Use along with a quantitative model. Quick Notes: covers tangible and intangible factors.

Centre of gravity method

  • Quantitative method to place a facility such as a warehouse at the centre of movement, using weight and distance. Grid map on a Cartesian plane; site i at (xi, yi) shipping annual weight wi.

  • x = Σ(xi·wi) ÷ Σwi ; y = Σ(yi·wi) ÷ Σwi. Assumes minimising weight-distance minimises cost; transport cost ∝ distance; demand/supply points fixed (Quick Notes).

Worked example (illustrative, not in Ebook): Hospitals at (2,4) needing 100 boxes, (6,2) needing 300, (8,8) needing 100. x = (200+1800+800) ÷ 500 = 5.6; y = (400+600+800) ÷ 500 = 3.6. Put the stent depot near (5.6, 3.6), pulled towards the biggest user.

Least cost method (Weber)

  • Locate where total cost is theoretically lowest, not where revenue is highest; agriculture and industry close to market to cut transport cost.

  • A. Weber assumptions: markets fixed at points · transport cost ∝ weight × distance · perfect competition · economic man. Raw materials and markets "pull" the plant. High material index → towards raw material; low material index → towards market. Then labour cost deflects the least-cost point.

13. Quick Notes: location strategy and procedure

5 steps of location strategy 4-step location procedure
1. Define objectives 1. Assessment (internal SWOT: relocate, expand, new)
2. Identify influencing factors 2. Exploration (domestic vs international)
3. Gather information 3. Examination (detailed comparison)
4. Design the location system (timetable) 4. Final selection (govt policy, pros and cons)
5. Execute the move (review, tests, feedback)

Misconceptions: Location decisions affect new and established firms · not only proximity to customers · frequent relocation is impractical (big investment in building and equipment) · affect manufacturing and non-manufacturing · location is a multi-step process.

14. Case: Sahara Mall

  • Sahara India Pariwar: started 1978, Gorakhpur; assets ~US$10.87 bn; 200+ townships planned. Mall on Mehrauli-Gurgaon Road, 2,37,000 sq ft, designed by W.S. Atkins, built by L&T ECC. 1,000-car parking, CCTV, 5 elevators. Anchor: Big Bazaar.

  • India pattern: over 60% of visitors watch a movie and eat out; only 20-30% shop. Attractions: good anchor, kids' centre, disciplined parking. Decision: add a multiplex (37,000 sq ft)? (Case text later says 40-45% shop: a figure inconsistency in the Ebook.)
