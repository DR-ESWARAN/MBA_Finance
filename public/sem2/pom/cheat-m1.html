Module 1 · Introduction to Production & Operations Management

Production & Operations Management · Cheat sheet

NotesCheat sheetMind map

DEFINE — OM = management of productive resources / production system converting inputs to products and services. Production = 6 Ms (men, machines, materials, money, methods, management) → output via transformation. Agriculture = first production. Production = heart of the organisation.

3 SCHOOLS — Classical (scientific mgmt + process mgmt: plan-organise-control) · Behavioural (human relations, behavioural science) · Modelling (decision-making, systems, mathematics).

TRANSFORM (SN) — Disintegration (steel → spoons, knives) · assembling (many → one) · service (TV after-sales).

OBJECTIVES — Right quality, quantity, time, cost; minimise resources and total cost; maximise utilisation. Ebook: quality goods via optimal resources and SQC.

IMPORTANCE (SN 5) — Objectives · goodwill · new products · supports other functions · face competition.

FUNCTIONS (SN 9) — Product & design · process · capacity · planning (routing = path/sequence; scheduling = timing) · control · location (long-term) · inventory · work study (method/motion + measurement/time) · maintenance.

SCOPE (Ebook) — System design (tools, equipment, location, layout, material handling) + analysis/control (production planning = short-term schedules + stock records; control of inventory, WIP, quality via process control).

INTERFACES — Marketing (gatekeeper; conflicts on line width, delivery time, quality) · finance (capex, cost data) · design (clock-speed, simultaneous engg) · HR · IS (ERP, CIM).

Automation Key Examples
Fixed Sequence set by equipment; long runs Transfer lines, assembly machines
Programmable Program changes sequence; batches; set-up time NC tools, robots, PLCs
Flexible Wide range, almost no downtime (not zero) Mixed schedules

AUTOMATION FACTS — Ford, late 1940s (Ebook) · transfer lines since ~1924 (SN) · pros: tedious, dangerous, beyond-human tasks · cons: tech limits, unpredictable cost, high initial cost.

GOODS vs SERVICES — Tangible/stored/forecast-driven vs intangible/consumed as created/demand-driven. Perishability → yield management. Service quality = satisfaction, timeliness, personalisation, service recovery. Lean for manufacturing; agile for services.

PORTER — Low cost or differentiation × scope → cost leadership, differentiation, focus (cost focus / differentiation focus). Resource-based view = Prahalad & Hamel.

SN STRATEGY — Competitiveness = relentless pursuit · operational excellence = efficiency + quality + innovation · cost leadership (economies of scale) · differentiation · flexibility & responsiveness · globalisation.

PRODUCTIVITY — Output ÷ input; attitude of mind. Labour = single factor; multifactor; partial; total factor (most accurate). Green Productivity: APO Tokyo 1994 after Rio 1992. OMS = output per man-shift; national = per capita income; agri = per hectare; SMH ÷ AMH.

WASTIVITY — 1 ÷ productivity. Example: 630 ESH ÷ 756 AMH = 83.33%; wastivity 16.67%. 1% less scrap ≈ 10-15% more sales effort.

IMPROVE — Technology/automation · learning curve (aircraft) · job design & motivation (Hawthorne, Mayo) · benchmarking · KPIs · Lean/Kaizen · Six Sigma DMAIC · PDCA (Deming). Solow = productivity paradox.

CASE — TI Cycles (1949, vertical integration, customer disconnect) vs Hero (Munjal, outsourcing, JIT, low cost). TI productivity 2.45 → 5.78 cycles/man/day.

TRAPS: Maths = Modelling · behavioural ≠ continuous process (False) · production planning = short-term schedules + stock records · labour productivity = single factor · flexible automation does not eliminate downtime · cost leadership ≠ only lowest price · production mgmt also covers services.
