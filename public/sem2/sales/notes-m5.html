Module 5 · Sales Force Management

Sales Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: The Ebook gives a classic 4-step recruitment process (quantity → qualities → sources → selection) and a selection order (screening → psychological test → references → medical → final interview → appointment order). The Study Notes give an HR-led process (hiring manager requisition → requisition number → HR meetings → sources → resume review → phone pre-screen; then interviews → evaluation form → offer → verbal offer → reference and background checks → job marked filled). Learn both and match the wording of the question. SN also adds the 7-step training process and the 3 benefits of motivation. QB Q2 changes salesman to customers: the Ebook says the salesman is the corner-stone, so it is False.

1. Sales force management: meaning

  • Ebook: SFM is a sub-system of marketing management. Sales management translates the marketing plan into marketing performance, so it is called "the muscle behind marketing management". Modern sales managers are customer-oriented and profit-directed.

  • SN: building a strong sales team = planning how they will work, teaching ways to sell, and making sure they use these methods to meet sales goals. A good plan is important for making money; the team represents your brand.

  • SN importance (3): enhanced sales operations (organised teams → happy customers, strong friendships) · greater market understanding (stay relevant in competitive markets) · creating a sales strategy (one plan does not fit all; each salesperson adapts to customer groups).

Misconception: SFM is not mainly about selling products. It covers recruitment and selection, training, motivation, compensation and performance evaluation.

2. Recruitment and selection (Ebook)

  • Recruitment = searching for prospective candidates and encouraging them to apply (QB Q1 True). Vacancies finalised, advertised, applications collected.

  • Selection = choosing the most suitable candidates out of many; scrutiny, tests and interviews.

  • Salesman is the important corner-stone upon which the sales organisation is built; success or failure depends on the salesman selected. It is the duty of the Sales Manager to select salesmen.

  • Why selection is a challenge (4): selling jobs more difficult (complex products, multiplicity of channels; QB Q5 True) · highly competitive markets · selling not fully accepted as a career, so few qualify · absence of institutions that teach salesmanship.

Step Recruitment process (Ebook)
1 Deciding the quantity of sales force: how many, based on expansion and attrition (retirement, resignation)
2 Characteristics and qualities: job description/analysis (about the job, not the individual) and man specification (exact requirements). Ask: Can he do the job? Will he do the job?
3 Tapping sources of recruitment
4 Careful selection and finalising employment

  • Qualities: general (personality, sound health, intelligence, honesty, sociability, consistency, observation) · particular (education, experience, knowledge of product/customers/market, languages) · technical (chemical or mechanical aspects of product, legal implications).

  • Sources (9): own staff (promotion) · competing firms (not ethical; higher salary) · employment exchanges · educational institutions · "Situation Wanted" columns · "Situation Vacant" ads · casual applicants · recommended candidates · placement agencies.

  • Selection order: screening of applications → psychological test → reference check → medical examination → final interview → appointment order (clear on designation, salary and allowances, mode: temporary or permanent).

SN: HR-led recruitment and selection

Recruitment (SN) Selection (SN)
1. Hiring manager submits completed requisition to HR 1. Timely interviews; HR guides techniques
2. Requisition number for tracking in HR 2. Candidate evaluation form per interviewee
3. HR + hiring manager meetings on strategy 3. HR + hiring manager create the offer (position, compensation)
4. Sources: website, internal postings, job platforms, social media, publications, retained agency, temp agency, employee referrals 4. Verbal offer; written offer after checks
5. HR + hiring manager review resumes 5. Reference checks and background screening by HR
6. HR telephone pre-screen, then in-person interviews 6. Candidate accepts and passes checks → HR marks job filled

Misconception: Recruitment is not just posting job ads. It includes defining roles, HR + hiring manager collaboration, many sources, review and interviews, and cultural fit.

3. Training of sales force (Ebook)

  • Old belief: "Salesmen are born and not made." Selling a refrigerator to an Eskimo is rare. Ebook conclusion: "salesmen are not born, nor made, but they are born and made". Salesmanship is a systematic utilisation of one's personality.

  • Need: new salesman lacks product, customer and selling knowledge; even experienced salesmen need training for new products, competitors, improved techniques or a refresher.

  • Extent of training depends on (4): difficulty of the selling job · education and previous training · previous selling experience · type of buyer (expert buyer needs more product knowledge).

Training scheme (7) Content
1. Basic principles of salesmanship Buying motives, selling points, approach, objections; AIDA = Attention, Interest, Desire, Action (QB Q4 "Interview" = False)
2. Knowledge of the firm History, set-up, policies, channels; injects sense of pride
3. Knowledge of the product Nature, production methods, materials, packing, uses; good appearance alone will not sell
4. Knowledge of customers Customer types (silent, talkative, suspicious, nervous, argumentative...); buying motives; customer psychology
5. Knowledge of competitors Their products and policies
6. Day-to-day work Reports, letters, orders and bills, accounts, display and demonstration
7. Knowledge of self Partly personal, partly self-training; reading, communication, public speaking

  • Advantages (6 main): greater sales volume · reduces cost of production · early selling maturity (training reduces time spent with each customer; QB Q6 True) · lowers supervision cost · lowers turnover of sales force · better customer relations (does not oversell).

  • Other advantages: high-margin items sold easily · fewer bad debts · lower selling cost · more sales per call · fewer complaints · fewer calls per order · better demonstration · full line sold · minimum number of salesmen · satisfies buyer's demand for product knowledge.

Methods of training

SN: 7 steps to conduct sales training

  1. Establish clear objectives (clear, measurable, achievable, relevant, deadline).

  2. Assess sales performance against goals (leads, sales data).

  3. Develop the curriculum: participant expertise · learning preferences · training depth · time constraints · delivery format.

  4. Prepare learning materials (books, online lessons, or own).

  5. Determine the format: classroom, online, or hybrid.

  6. Release the training (live or self-paced online).

  7. Evaluate and adjust using feedback, surveys and KPIs.

Misconception: Sales training is not a one-time event; it is ongoing and customised to learning styles.

4. Compensation / remuneration

  • "Man is a wanting animal"; remuneration is an evergreen challenge, more so in a sales organisation.

  • Why pay well (Ebook 4): attract best salesmen · keep sales force contented · build loyalty ("pay well and keep the employee") · sound employer-employee relations. Study Guide adds: "cheap is costlier"; compensation level lies between what the company intends to pay and what the sales force expects.

Element of a good plan (8) Hook
Adequate income Decent standard of living vs same line
Simplicity Understood without accountancy/statistics; else suspicion, low morale
Flexibility New product → commission; regular product → salary
Incentive oriented Bonus or commission for extra effort
Low administration cost Few accountants/clerks needed
Fairness Fair to firm and salesmen; no favour
Prompt payment Delayed bonus kills enthusiasm, induces turnover
Promotion Salary rise on promotion; reward long service

  • SN: sales compensation = salary + commissions + bonuses and other rewards. The plan is a roadmap; it tells the team what to achieve and what they get. Effective plan: boosts motivation · everyone does their best · pushes top performers · focuses on important deals · helps find and keep talent.

  • Study Guide: no compensation plan is perfect; it is a compromise between low sales cost/higher profit and attractive earnings with security.

5. Supervision and motivation

  • Supervision = overseeing employees at work; high degree needed for sales force constantly on the move. Involves direction (motivation) and control, plus development of abilities; tailored to the individual.

  • Supervision consists of (5): observing, monitoring and reporting performance (QB Q9 True) · counselling and coaching · information on plans and policies · feedback and solving business and personal problems · motivating through non-financial incentives (egoistic needs).

  • Satisfies needs for security, opportunity, self-expression, respect, good working conditions; two-way communication. Under democratic style, consultative direction; personal communication is most effective, correspondence only supplementary.

  • Morale = mental readiness of the sales force to co-operate with management. If morale is high, output is also high (QB Q10 = High).

  • Motivation (Ebook): ability to work ≠ will to work. You can buy a man's time but not his willingness, enthusiasm or loyalty. Motivation ignites the will to work; it is financial or non-financial. Examples: sales contests, conventions, conferences.

  • SN benefits of motivation (3): enhanced profits (higher closure rates) · increased team morale · being strong against any situation (market ups and downs).

Misconception: Motivation is not only financial incentives; recognition, growth and supportive environment matter. So SN KC "motivation primarily relies on external factors" = False.

  • SN supervision: too much or too little both cause problems. Warning signs: high sales personnel turnover · many accounts lost · more complaints · sudden jump in mail/phone orders · few orders despite many calls · oddly high or low calls · rising selling cost without more sales · unhappy staff. Supervision must change as the sales team changes; small experienced teams need little head-office involvement.

6. Control of sales force

  • Control = checking whether everything occurs as charted in the plans.

  • Need for control (4): enhance efficiency (compare actual vs targets) · co-ordination (salesman works far from manager) · nature of sales force demands it (some "die for the organisation", some make the unit die) · sound public relations. Kotler: "Of all the assets, customers are the most valuable one."

How control is exercised Key points
Reports and records Daily/weekly/monthly reports (calls, sales, new and lost customers, expenses, credit, competition). Record = summary prepared from reports. Reports help action and build self-analysis habit
Sales quota Amount of business fixed for each salesman; year, quarter, half-year or month. Objectives: basis for remuneration · most feasible measure of individual effectiveness · stimulates effort · plan, control, competition
Sales territory Geographical area assigned. Based on demand, competition, transport, customer types, salesman capacity. Objectives: coverage · compare performance · fix responsibility · minimise expenses · motivate. Avoids duplication and overlap
Authority Catalogues and price lists limit it; may allow credit, discounts, concessions, services
Field supervision Needed when business is spread over a vast area (e.g. thousands of medical representatives in Pharma)

7. Evaluation of sales performance

  • Based on belief in human resource development; sound evaluation assures superior performance.

  • Three reasons: measure performance against planned objectives · distribute rewards · guide development of sales force.

  • Usual targets: sales quotas, expense control, personal development objectives. Evaluation must be forward-looking, not fault-finding; humanistic touch at every stage; use MBO where possible.

  • Measures: sell satisfactory volume · sell at profit · sell at low cost · plan and organise time · knowledge of products, policies, customers, competition · attract and hold customers. Reports, records and quotas are used.

  • SN evaluation = a report card comparing actual vs expected. If not good, three choices: align performance with standards · revise policies, plans or implementation · adjust objectives, standards or measurement criteria.

Clinical analogy: Field coaching is the cath-lab fellow scrubbing in beside the consultant before doing cases alone. Sales territory with quota is like assigning each fellow a ward with a defined case load: coverage, accountability and fair comparison. Follow-up training is your CME: the field keeps changing.
