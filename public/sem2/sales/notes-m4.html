Module 4 · Salesmanship

Sales Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: The Ebook classifies salesmen by who employs them (manufacturer's: missionary, merchandising, dealer-servicing, sales promotion, technical; wholesaler's; retail: indoor and outdoor; specialty). The Study Notes classify by personality (11 types: relational, passive, closer, scripted, opener, networker, scorekeeper, specialist, communicator, relentless, strategist). Sales manager qualities also differ: Ebook gives 10 skills + 10 attributes; SN gives 5 (optimism, motivational prowess, goal-oriented, corrective action, encouraging success). SN adds five sales approaches (instant buddy, guru, consultant, networker, hard seller). The Ebook has many prospecting and psychology statistics; they are listed for recall.

1. Salesmanship: meaning

  • Personal selling is the broader concept; salesmanship may or may not be part of it and is never all of it. Personal selling brings the right products to the right customers and makes ownership transfer.

  • Stroh: salesmanship is "a direct, face-to-face, seller-to-buyer influence" that communicates facts or uses the psychology of persuasion to form a buying decision.

  • Salesmanship is seller-initiated effort that informs and persuades. A salesperson is a psychologist, a human computer, an adviser and a friend, adjusting personality on every call. Advertising = "salesmanship in print". Every man is a salesman in his own walk of life.

  • Labourer = hands · craftsman = hands + head · artist = hands + head + heart · salesman = hands + head + heart + feet.

  • Salesmanship is the ability to persuade people to want the things which they already need (QB Q1 True); converts needs into wants; handles people and products.

Author Definition (key words)

  • Modern concept: old salesman showed goods and waited for orders; the modern one is creative, creates needs and converts them into wants; customer satisfaction and mutual profit.

  • SN: salesmanship is associated with personal sales; persuasion matters even in the virtual (online) area; sellers switch styles between people.

2. Importance and duties

  • Salesman is the connecting link between sellers and buyers at every step (QB Q2 True), from raw materials to finished goods. Large-scale production is in anticipation of demand, so distribution needs better salesmanship.

  • To producers: push products, capture new markets, more volume and profit; salesmen are the "eye and ear" of manufacturers; creator of demand → employment and income.

  • To consumers: educates and guides, right selection; raises rate of turnover, reduces unsold stock, minimises economic stagnation.

  • Duties (12): make sales · travel as assigned · collect bills · report (sales, calls, services, customers lost, competition) · settle complaints peacefully · attend sales meetings · supply information · good customer relations · help selection · goodwill · cooperative habits · periodic inventories of stock.

3. Qualities of a successful salesman

  • Reid's 9 characteristics: good relationships with a variety of people (so QB Q7 "successful without good relationships" = False) · learn quickly, adapt · plan and manage time · work hard for long-term service, not get-rich-quick · communicate clearly in speech and writing · think analytically · produce consistently in quality and quantity · persist · high moral character.

  • Personality = "the personal distinction or dynamic force felt by everybody who comes within the radius"; the sum of impressions made on others.

  • R. G. Walters, J. W. Windate, Russel: three major factors = personality, knowledge of the product, knowledge of customers and their buying motives.

5. The sales manager

Ebook: 10 qualities and skills

Communication (QB Q10 True) · managing performance (own and team) · team leader · pleasing personality (front face of the company) · fair decision making · resilience · motivation (most difficult to learn; smiles and appreciation beat speeches) · delegation · passion · positive attitude.

Ebook: 10 attributes

Integrity (vital besides passion) · loyalty (no false promises or figures) · availability · listening (most neglected, most vital) · continuous learning · innovative · confident (calm under stress) · able to coach (mentor) · leads by example · strategic (consistent, not always >100%; new incentives and metrics). QB Q4: a successful manager takes every action that brings the team gains = True.

SN view

  • A sales manager is like a team captain; reports to the sales boss in big firms, to the top boss in small ones; sets goals and quotas, hires, trains, fires, assigns territories, reports upward.

  • Five qualities: optimism · motivational prowess (gift cards, bonuses, contests, days off, trips) · goal-oriented perspective · effective at corrective action (both wrong and right behaviour, e.g. lateness, personal calls) · committed to encouraging success (mentor, certifications).

6. Prospecting

  • First step of the personal selling process = locating potential customers. Sources: observation, social contacts, trade shows, commercial databases and mail lists, cold calling.
Term Meaning
Suspect May need your product but may not be aware of you
Prospect A suspect you have contacted who confirmed possible interest (10-year-old car with 200,000 miles; husband before wife's 40th birthday)
Customer A prospect who has spent money with you

  • Tactics: phone calls, automated voicemail, email, direct mail. Goal = qualify a recipient as a prospect or not, so marketing money goes to likely buyers.

  • Commit daily time: 81.6% of top performers spend 4+ hours a day on sales activities.

10 prospecting tips Key statistic or point
1. Ideal prospect profile 50% of prospects are not a good fit; problem-solution thinking; account-based marketing
2. Where to meet prospects B2B buyers on LinkedIn; warm up via mutual connection
3. Work call lists Cold, warm, lost lists. 69% of buyers accepted a call; 11–14 questions → 74% greater success; call back in 6 weeks or 6 months
4. Personalised email 80% of buyers prefer email (Rain Group); 26% higher open rate than mass mail; mobile-optimised 15% higher CTR
5. Ask for referrals 91% of B2B buyers influenced by word of mouth; referral close 50–70%; 40.4% of salespeople rarely ask; 83% happy to refer; ask right after the sale
6. Become a know-it-all 51% of top performers seen as experts, 7% as salespeople; 54% of prospects want to see how the product works on the first call
7. Social media 91% B2B buyers active; 84% senior executives; 65% social sellers fill pipeline; win rate +5%, deal size +35%; 82% check vendors on LinkedIn
8. Relevant content 69% want relevant research data; 89% favour content that shows ROI; Demand Gen B2B survey
9. Video 7 in 10 B2B buyers watch video; 96% consumers find it helpful; 85% more likely to buy; video in email lifts replies 8×
10. Follow up If no reply in 24 hours, follow up: 21% chance of reply to the second email
  • SN prospecting: identifying and reaching potential customers to generate new business (cold email, cold calls, LinkedIn). Steps: research → qualification (lead scoring) → outreach. Good prospectors got conversations with 52 of 100 contacts vs 19 of 100.

7. Pre-approach and approach

  • Pre-approach = preparation for the sales presentation: research the prospect (market research), plan and practise the presentation. So QB Q5 "prospecting involves preparation for the sales presentation" = False.

  • Importance: saves time and effort · more confidence and enthusiasm · know needs in detail · fewer mistakes (financial status, likes, tastes) · plan the sales talk systematically.

  • SN customer analysis in pre-approach: age, likes and dislikes, education, lifestyle, social circles, cultural influences. Example: luxury car seller knows age and income, prepares guarantees, prices, performance data.

  • Approach = initial contact; a few minutes of small talk; goal is to find specific needs and let the prospect relax.

SN sales approach Style
Instant buddy Personal connection, friendly questions
Guru Facts and reason over feelings; product superiority
Consultant Caring expert guiding the whole purchase
Networker Connections even with competitors' staff
Hard seller Forceful; customers feel pressured

8. Selling and sales sequence

  • Selling = exchange of a good or service for money, and the process of persuading. Buying gas needs no persuasion; buying a car does.

  • Requirements (6): build long-term relationships one at a time · listen · tenacity (follow-up system) · self-motivation (no blaming the economy) · self-promotion · investing in the community.

Type of selling Meaning
High-pressure "Buyers are liars", don't take no; usually fails or causes regret
Transactional Quick sale, no relationship; fine for gas or an outfit
Consultative (relationship) Ongoing relationship; learn needs and wants
Collaborative One step further: partnership, high buyer trust

  • Sales sequence = sales cadence: a scheduled series of touchpoints (calls, emails, social messages, SMS) at pre-defined intervals. Goal: start conversations with qualified prospects and set meetings. Used by sales development reps and account executives. Removes guesswork; sets standards; can be triggered by a content download, webinar, ad click or list addition.

  • SN: sequence usually has five to seven steps; benefits: no forgotten follow-ups; components: first hello, credibility, engagement, conversion and post-purchase messages. SN summary also calls it a sales campaign. Selling plan usually has seven steps; B2B (professional buyers: wholesale, supply, service) vs B2C (faster; laptop vs life insurance).

9. Psychology of customers

  • Liking is one of the six principles in the "Science of Persuasion". Sales psychology = understanding buyer psychology. Buying gives the buyer ("Kevin") a sense of authority and ego.
6 techniques Key point
Quick response = you care Call within minutes; calling back within 3 minutes improved conversion 391% (1,000 inquiries, 2019)
Don't call me at this hour Time calls to interest; Wednesday and Thursday best days
That's consistent 72% of calls unanswered; 8 attempts to reach; 50% of sales after the 5th attempt
I don't trust that number Use local (e.g. US) virtual phone numbers; Software Advice study
I don't care about your targets Prospects only care whether you solve their problem
More options, more confusion Liraz Margalit (Psychology Today): more options → less likely to decide; give 2–3 choices
5 closing tips Key point
FOMO Fear of missing out = loss aversion; limited-time 10% discount email
What others say Reviews raise conversion 270% (Spiegel); use reviews from the prospect's industry
Did you say free? People like gestures; give valuable content (e-books) → reciprocation
Don't diss competitors Suggest comparing both (Way 2) → respect
Mystery matters Perfume brand (since 1984) with no samples; let prospects explore, but stay guided
  • SN customer psychology: combines social behaviour, advertising effects and decision-making; looks at age, personality, lifestyle, usage, brand loyalty; what customers say often differs from what they do. Seller's attitude affects reactions to ads, offers and price changes. Brand relationships go beyond look, price and packaging.
