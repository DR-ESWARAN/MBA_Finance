Module 1 · Sales Management and Organisation

Sales Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: Module 1 has two parts in the LMS: Part 1 Sales Management and Part 2 Sales Organisation. The Ebook and the Objective QB use the classic text (AMA definition, four branches, 7-point nature, cultural aspects, 16-point scope, process of selling, AIDAS vs buying formula, 7 principles, line / line-and-staff / functional / committee). The Study Notes (which the LMS practice assignment usually follows) add B2C / B2B / enterprise sales, a 12-point nature, three key aspects (operations, strategy, analysis), sales management systems, and a different list of organisation types (territorial, product, functional, market-based, account size). Both are covered below and labelled.

1. Meaning and definitions

  • Sales management = sales + management. Sales is "the art of planning in the mind of another a motive which will induce favourable action".

  • AMA committee, selling: "the personal or impersonal process of assisting and/or persuading a prospective customer to buy a commodity or a service or to act favourably upon an idea that has commercial significance to the seller."

  • AMA, sales management: "the planning, direction and control of personal selling including recruiting, selecting, equipping, assigning, routing, supervising, paying and motivating as these tasks apply to the personal sales force." (QB Q3 blank = Personal Selling.)

  • Originally meant only direction of the sales force; later broadened to all marketing activities (advertising, sales promotion, marketing research, physical distribution, pricing, product merchandising).

  • Patrick Forsyth: sales management is "the function responsible for creating and maintaining a suitable sales activity through management and supervision of the sales team, and hence achieving through them, the required sales results".

  • Study Guide: sales management is a segment of the organisation's marketing mix.

  • SN definition: overseeing and organising all tasks related to making sales successful: planning, coordinating, executing, overseeing and evaluating sales performance; "from onboarding the sales team to managing their targets".

  • Sales is the only function that generates revenue. Salespeople are often the best-paid people and sales is often the fastest route to top management. Sales management operates within the periphery of marketing management.

  • Sales managers are involved in both strategy (planning) and people (implementation) aspects, plus evaluating and controlling.

Four major branches (Ebook, all interrelated)

Branch What it does
1. Top Level Policy Planning Establishes a framework of policy within which the sales objectives may be achieved (QB Q4)
2. Line and Staff Operational Planning Procedures established in advance, against which quality and quantity of work are controlled
3. Organization Structure of responsibilities: charting, assigning responsibility, delegating authority, tracing accountability
4. Administration Meets objectives through guidance and evaluation: sales training, motivation, coordination, execution

  • Order-getting vs order-taking: the soft-drink driver-salesman is mainly an order-taker (product presold, dealers reorder automatically). The aluminium-siding salesman calling on homeowners is an order-getter (demand stimulation). Heavy advertising → more layers of middlemen and order-taking salesmen; advertising used only to back up selling → fewer layers, more order-getting.

  • Why it is hard: field staff work alone with little supervision, irregular hours, broken appointments, different territories. So the sales manager's most important functions are to find productive self-starters, train them, assign territories, motivate them and keep track. Sales management is a people-oriented profession.

SN: sales department strategies and types

  • Five strategies for an efficient sales team: feasible sales strategies (ambitious yet attainable targets), competent team (competency-based recruitment), continuous sales development (review last six months of reports), rewarding performance, monitoring key metrics (quota attainment, deal size, close rates, call durations, pipeline velocity).
Type (SN) Feature
B2C Direct selling to consumers; leads through assertive marketing
B2B Selling to other businesses; higher-value products, longer sales cycles
Enterprise Intricate goods to large corporations; multiple specialised teams (sales engineers, inside, outside sales)

3. Key aspects of sales management

Ebook view

  • Marketing management: in many firms the sales manager handles all marketing. Sales and marketing depend on each other. Eastern Transport Corporation (Quebec): marketing staff attend every sales meeting; nine salespeople lunch with a marketing employee monthly. D. A. Stuart Company uses an intranet to keep salespeople updated. Technology (web sites, database marketing, EDI) raises the need for cooperation.

  • Cultural generalisation: "When in Rome, do as Romans do." Germans: technically exacting, but a 30-hour manufacturing week and guard free time. Koreans: top-down, authoritarian, need job definition; Japanese: bottom-up consensus. New Zealand salespeople more committed and satisfied than Australians; Australians prefer larger salaries, NZ prefer travel incentives. Tool: Hofstede's five cultural dimensions.

  • People management (most obvious and crucial role). Qualities of a sales manager (7): desire to be a manager; ability to lead and motivate (so QB Q5 "not important" is False); organising and planning; control and administration; understanding of finance; recruit, train, motivate, develop; accept that the computer is here to stay. The list has no direct reference to marketing skills; people management covers five of six main categories.

  1. Recruitment: single most important task, yet most neglected. Stages: generation of prospects → short list → final selection → persuasion. Recruitment is itself a sales process. Continuous recruiting costs less than constant high turnover. Best salespeople often found on campus.

  2. Motivation and compensation: even above-average salespeople rarely work at more than 60% of capacity. Reasons: lack of contact, complexity of role, entrepreneurship. Electrolux Mexico (250 people) moved from pure commission to salary + commission after the peso devaluation (45% sales fall). Commission is not publicly acceptable in many countries; Japan collectivist; Koreans paid on seniority. Non-financial: recognition, titles, perquisites, travel. Konica: 350 dealers, 16 trips, sales of targeted copiers +26%.

  3. Leadership: the manager's personal qualities are probably the most important motivator: enthusiasm, courage, self-confidence, integrity, interest, sense of humour, charisma, delegation, communication.

  4. Control: performance measured mostly by sales volume, compared with past sales, other salespeople and targets. Dubinsky: role ambiguity and conflict harm salespeople everywhere. Greater formalisation → negative response where power distance low and individualism high (USA); positive where power distance high and individualism low (India). Kraft Foods reps serve a single chain, ~30% more time per store.

  5. Ethical perceptions: in the US a bribe admits the product cannot compete; elsewhere it is a privilege. Dubinsky study: Americans saw fewer ethical problems than Japanese and Koreans; Japanese saw gift giving as more unethical than US; Koreans less. US salespeople wanted more explicit company policies.

SN / Study Guide view: three key aspects

Aspect Focus
Sales operations Team building: right roles, training, goals and quotas, CRM for coaching
Sales strategy Defining the sales process / pipeline, lead generation to closing
Sales analysis Monitoring and reporting. Four funnel metrics: deal volume, average deal size, % successful deals, sales velocity (average lifecycle of a sale)
  • Sales management system (SN): centralised repository of sales data → (1) team collaboration and productivity, (2) time-efficient operations, (3) automation of workflows, (4) real-time monitoring of the pipeline, (5) surpassing sales goals.

4. Scope of sales management

Sales management emerged from marketing management; marketing is the broader concept. Ebook / Study Guide list (16 areas):

Area Key point
Sales planning / forecasting Plan ahead by anticipating future sales
Sales budgeting Estimate expenses of sales activities
Structure and size of sales organisation Size, composition, structure of the department
Human resource planning Estimate sales personnel requirement
Hiring Recruitment and selection
Training and development Orientation and skills
Salesperson's objectives Achievable goals for each
Sales quotas Monthly, quarterly, yearly; volume or value
Sales territories Region given to each salesperson
Motivation Review and drive performance
Compensation Salary, allowance, commission, benefits
Controlling Monitor performance
Branding, labelling, packaging Salespeople gather customer feedback on packaging
Distribution channel Track channels, fill loopholes
Sales promotion Advertising and promotional tactics
Organising and support; after-sale service Support salespeople; customers judge a firm by after-sale service

  • SN scope (7 + planning): sales planning and forecasting, budgeting, structuring the team, recruitment, training, sales area allocation, team management, compensation.

  • Credibility crisis: salesmen seen as pushy; compared to a tailor who misses the promised date. Glib-tongued salesmen are on their way out; the new breed assists the sale. Seth Godin: sales is "the transfer of emotion".

  • Why sales is a specialised area: geography (away from HQ), isolation (alone in the field), task (skills in a social setting). Salespeople's three-dimensional job: communicating, persuading, differentiating; selling skill itself can be a differentiator.

5. Process of selling (Ebook)

Personal selling = oral presentation in conversation with one or more prospects. Buyer and seller are both sender and receiver.

Step Key points
1. Pre-sale preparation Know product, market, company, competition, buying motives. Background knowledge is its essence
2. Prospecting Locate prospects. Prospect = unsatisfied need + ability to buy + willingness. Sources: present customers, other salesmen, directories, cold canvassing. Qualify: need, purchasing power, inclination, buying authority. Similar to the seeking function of marketing
3. Pre-approach and approach Pre-approach: study the qualified prospect. Approach = obtaining an interview + first contact; avoid being dismissed
4. Sales presentation Follows AIDAS. Objections handled in interest and desire stages. Close = psychological / reaction moment; initiative must come from the salesman
5. Follow-up Write order, delivery, credit, reassure; reduces dissonance (post-purchase anxiety); source of feedback

  • A good presentation is complete, clear, removes competition, wins confidence. Objections are welcome: a prospect who objects is easier to satisfy than one who shows no interest. Empathy = sensing the reaction you produce in another.

  • "A sale is made not in the mind of the salesman, nor over the counter, but in the mind of the buyer."

Theory Orientation Core idea
AIDAS Seller-oriented Attention → Interest → Desire → Action → Satisfaction
Buying formula Buyer-oriented, modern, problem-solving Solution = product/service + brand (source); must feel adequate and give pleasant feelings (the defence of buying habit → repeat sale)

6. Importance and objectives

  • Ebook importance (10): realises organisational objectives, manages sales force, better planning, sales maximisation (higher but attainable targets), builds relationships, optimises distribution, aids top management decisions (expansion, closure), improves profitability, develops personnel, product development.

  • SN importance (4): enhancing product development, optimising distribution (online thrives, retail lags → retailer training), informed financial decisions (replace low-margin items), enhancing staff quality.

  • Three basic objectives: sales volume, contribution to profits, continuing (long-term) growth. Planning precedes goal setting. Cost of sales is affected only indirectly by sales management.

Other objectives (Ebook, 8) Note
1. Revenue generation Sales dept "solely responsible to bring in the money" (QB Q1 = Revenue)
2. Increase sales volume Production facilities not idle
3. Sustained profits Increase sales, reduce costs
4. Organisation growth Gain market share
5. Market leadership From volume and profits
6. Converting prospects to customers An art and a science (QB Q6)
7. Motivate the sales force Incentives and rewards
8. Complement marketing activities Support marketing functions (QB Q2)
  • SN objectives (5): boosting revenue, setting sales targets, enhancing production and distribution, sustaining profits amid expenditures (sales process analyst, monetary limits), motivating the team.

7. Sales organisation (Part 2)

  • SN definition: revolves around the sales force, which promotes and sells through interactions with customers, distributors and recommenders; works with the marketing strategy.

  • Characteristics (SN, 9): integral part (a segment of the enterprise), engaged personnel, common objectives, formal and informal relationships, defined roles, departmentalisation, efficiency and resources, unified efforts, managerial control.

Purposes (Ebook and SN, 5)

Purpose Key line
Development of specialists Specialisation = division of labour, chief means of organisation; assignments = delegation of authority
All necessary activities performed Assign someone to customer relations when executives lose contact
Coordination and balance Team effort; dominant sales executives can disrupt
Define authority Line = require execution · Staff = advise, no power · Functional = specialists enforce within a limited field
Economise on executive time Delegate routine, focus on planning
  • Ebook criteria for setting up: specify functions, allocate resources for customer satisfaction and target ROI, a structure that covers the market and controls the cost of selling.

Seven principles of setting up

Principle Key point
1. Span of control Number of subordinates a manager can supervise. Narrow → tall structure, more layers. Wide → flat
2. Unity of command No subordinate responsible to more than one supervisor; too rigid for multi-speciality hospitals, IT firms (SN: hospitals and hotels)
3. Stability and continuity Assign jobs by talent and preference (retail-selling expert → dealers; institutional → banks, hotels)
4. Centralisation / decentralisation Pure forms rarely exist. Decentralised → more freedom, more motivated; centralised → more coordination. Specialisation and electronic communication favour decentralisation
5. Line and staff positions Line = vertical chain of command. Staff = no authority to issue orders; recommendations go to top executives. Marketing research manager and sales training manager are staff; MR manager gives market feedback to line authority (QB Q10 True)
6. Coordination and integration Traditional = one-way; modern = two-way between sales and R&D, production, finance (budget, credit), marketing (advertising, distribution, price with finance)
7. Specialisation Financial firm needs accounting knowledge; tech firm needs technical staff; improves performance

Types of sales organisation

Two different lists: Ebook = Line, Line and Staff, Functional, Committee. SN / Quick Notes = Territorial, Product-centric, Functional, Market-based (industry / vertical), Customer / Account size. Read the question wording: "line and staff" or "committee" points to the Ebook list; "vertical" or "account size" points to the SN list.

Ebook type Key features
Line Oldest, small firms, small sales force, narrow line, limited area. One boss each; clear lines. Not for large staff
Line and staff Large and medium firms, diversified lines, wide area. Staff specialists advise; cannot issue orders
Functional Each person has as few distinct duties as possible; salespeople get instructions from several executives. Not feasible for small/medium firms
Committee Executive group formulates policy; individuals implement. e.g. sales training committee
SN type Plus Minus
Territorial / geographic Local knowledge, easy evaluation Limits wider expertise
Product-centric Explains value and uses Dwells on features not benefits
Functional Efficient specialists Coordination, duplication, errors
Market-based (industry / vertical) Sector expertise Logistics across locations
Customer / account size Enterprise vs small business Constant communication and resources

  • Importance of sales organisation (SN): like the factory for mass production, the sales department creates mass markets. Like a power station, energy is lost en route to consumers, so sound organisation reduces distribution wastage. Reasons: creation of demand, effective advertising, order execution, customer support, debt collection, production planning, market analysis.

  • Territory (SN keyword) = geographical area assigned to a salesperson.

Exam traps: AMA = control of personal selling · four branches start with Top Level Policy Planning · AIDAS is seller-oriented, buying formula is buyer-oriented · objections handled in interest and desire stages · close = psychological moment · three basic objectives = volume, profit, growth · span of control counts subordinates · unity of command = one supervisor · staff cannot issue orders · line = oldest · functional = several bosses · narrow span = tall.

Trading analogy: Prospecting and qualifying is your stock screener (need = setup, ability to buy = liquidity, authority = the right timeframe). The close is the entry trigger at the "psychological moment"; follow-up is trade management after entry, where you reduce dissonance instead of panic-exiting.
