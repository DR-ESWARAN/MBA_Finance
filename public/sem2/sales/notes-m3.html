Module 3 · Sales Operations

Sales Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: Sources disagree on several definitions here. Sales budget: Ebook = a financial plan for allocating resources (expenses) to achieve forecasted sales; SN = a roadmap of anticipated total revenue (and says it differs from the sales expense budget). Budget methods: Ebook 3 (affordable, rule of thumb, competitive), Study Guide 5 (affordable, % of sales, competitive parity, objective and task, zero base). Quota types: Ebook/SG = direct and overlay; SN = revenue, activity, volume, cost-based, forecast, combination. Sales meeting: Ebook = internal meeting of the sales force; SN = meeting with a buyer (features → evidence → benefits → agreement). Match the answer to the wording of the question.

1. Sales operations

  • Ebook: sales ops is a key function of any sales team (QB Q1). Purpose: streamline sales processes and raise efficiency as the main link between sales strategy and sales reps; empower reps with tools, insights and resources to close more deals in less time.

  • Sales ops teams administer sales data and technology, normally through a CRM solution (QB Q6 True); manage the pipeline and analyse key metrics.

  • SN: functions that keep frontline teams efficient; handles administrative and technical tasks so reps get more customer time; like a supply chain operations team working in the background; implements sales technology and manages sales compensation.

Benefit (Ebook, 6) Key point
Accurate revenue forecasting Manage and analyse the pipeline
Faster growth Firms spending at least 3 hours a month on each rep's pipeline grow 11% faster
Formal processes and accountability Data fields filled, timely follow-up of new leads
Data-driven decisions Predict revenue so leaders devote resources where needed (QB Q7 True)
Easier scalability Define territories, adjust territory mapping, keep data healthy
Cultivation of new talent Vet applicants, onboard and train hires

2. Sales budget

  • Ebook: a financial plan showing how resources should be allocated to achieve forecasted sales (QB Q2). Purpose: maximum utilisation of resources and forecast sales. Best information source = the salesperson. Other departments' budgets depend on the sales budget.

  • Objective: plan for and control expenditure of resources (money, material, facilities, people) to achieve the sales objective (QB Q3); leverage and maximise profit.

  • Roles: planning tool · sets standards · instrument of coordination (sales, finance, production, advertising) · tool of control (actual sales above budget = favourable). Study Guide adds: variance analysis improves future budgets.

Method Key point
Affordable (Ebook, SG) Firms dealing in industrial goods, firms that do not value budgeting, small operations; judgmental (QB Q4)
Rule of thumb (Ebook) Based on market share vs competition, a fixed % of forecast sales, or what is left after other costs
Competitive / competitors' parity Tough competition; needs knowledge of competitors' resource allocation (SG: large firms)
Percentage of sales (SG) Specified % of current or anticipated sales; mass-selling goods, finance-dominated firms
Objective and task (SG) Identify objectives → tasks → cost each task → total
Zero base (SG) Start from zero every year, justify all spending; elaborate and time-consuming

  • Firms use a combination of methods. Preparation follows bottom-up planning: e.g. an automobile company prepares budgets district-wise → regional/zonal → division (product- or market-wise) → central sales department finalises.

  • Items: salaries, administrative, marketing expenses. Direct selling expenses: boarding, lodging, food, travel, commission, employee benefits, office expenses. Advertising/promotional materials: selling aids, contest awards, samples, catalogues, price lists. Review past budgets to narrow actual-vs-budget gaps.

SN view of the sales budget

  • A financial roadmap of anticipated total revenue in a timeframe, estimating quantity and price of products sold. It differs from sales forecasting (projecting revenue) and from the sales expense budget. A good budget can inform the forecast.

  • Sets achievable targets: team aims +50% subscriptions, budget shows a conservative +20% (the extra is a bonus). Even a 1% efficiency gain is a valid objective.

  • Components: income statement, balance sheet, cash flow statement.

  • Seven steps: choose a time period (monthly for steady sales, quarterly for seasonal) → take inventory and assess prices → analyse past sales (August grew <2%, so 10% is unrealistic) → benchmark with industry (US Bureau of Labor Statistics) → engage reps and customers → consider market trends and events (pandemic) → construct the budget.

3. Sales territories

  • Idea: to match sales opportunities with selling effort (QB Q5). A territory is more homogeneous than the market; aids planning, control and appraisal.

  • Study Guide definition: a group of present and potential customers assigned to a salesperson, group, branch, dealer, distributor or marketing organisation at a given time; the area that can be effectively and economically served by a single salesman.

  • Goals (7): cover the market · deploy salespeople · service customer groups · evaluate reps · higher productivity · control selling expenses · coordinate personal selling and advertising.

  • 12 steps of territory planning: research the area → divide (population, accessibility, potential) → study consumer behaviour (QB Q8 True) → assess revenue potential → analyse hurdles → define suitable products → probe needs → plan with quotas → appoint salespeople → monitor → review → avoid overlapping territories (causes conflict).

Size of territory: what makes it larger or smaller

Smaller territory when Larger territory when
Perishable products Consumer durables with long shelf life
Huge demand; high population density and potential Selling through wholesalers (intermediaries)
Industrial bulk buying, or firm handles retailers High allotment to selling expenses
Rural India (poor transport) Better, cheaper, faster transport
Highly competitive market needing close relationships Experienced, well-trained salespeople
Recession Limited products with high profit goals; boom
  • Factors determining size (8): prospect density · ground to cover · possible sales volume · frequency of visits · intensity of effort per call · ease of travel · inventory turnover at retail · ease of selling. Smaller territories are serviced intensively; salespeople prefer large ones.

Allocation factors (Gauss, Whiteman and Bates, 10)

Even distribution (optimum size, QB Q9 True) · eliminate duplication · equal opportunity · flexibility (but avoid frequent change) · controllable at minimum cost (QB Q10 says "at no cost" → False) · capable of comparative study · uniformity in income · economical (minimum travel cost) · efficient performance · new salesmen paired with experienced ones.

  • Advantages: company (responsibility, evaluation, study competitors), salesmen (freedom, rewarded effort), customers (after-sales service, quick complaint disposal).

  • Objectives (12): effective planning · cover the market · assign responsibility · evaluation · reduce selling cost · coordination · marketing research · fair competition among salespeople · customer relations · match salesmen to territory · independent work area · compete effectively.

Designing territories

  • Establish a geographic control unit (country for an MNC; region, state, city, district; or a natural trading area) → sales potential of each unit → combine into tentative territories → adjust → decide number of territories.
Approach How it works
Market build-up Estimate present and potential users and their consumption → total market potential → desired share = sales potential → break down territory-wise. Sonography example: 5,000 units, Maharashtra 1,000 = 20%; hospitals A, B, C with call frequencies → calls needed → number of reps
Workload (W. J. Talley) Group customers → optimum call frequency per group → locate customers → customers × call frequency = total calls → control unit giving adequate workload per rep
Shape When
Wedge Urban and non-urban; starts from a thickly populated urban centre; neighbouring wedges balance calls
Circle Scattered customers; salesman at the centre; optimises call frequency
Clover leaf Randomly located accounts; each leaf = a week's work, home every weekend; common in industrial marketing

  • Territorial adjustment = redistricting; seeks equal additional sales per rupee of expense. Still, Cundiff and Govoni: seven steps: customers and locations → time per call → travel time → call frequencies → calls possible → correct for desired frequencies → correct using salespeople's feedback.

  • Assigning salesmen: match opportunity and talent; extremes are "no-transfer" status quo (territories built around salesmen) and constant shuffling; aim for a golden mean.

  • Routing and scheduling: route plan on a map; adding call frequency gives scheduling; reduce wasted time, better order book, allow time-cushioning; also control tools for movement and expenses.

  • Territory performance: major determinant = market potential; plus workload, salesman characteristics, company standing, company effort (promotion, feedback, supervision, distribution).

  • SN: six methods of dividing territories: geographic location · population (city of 500,000 → about 50,000 per team) · account size (experienced reps get large accounts) · niche (education vs law firms) · products · client name (alphabetical).

4. Sales quotas

  • Ebook / SG: a sales goal, target or minimum sales level for a team or individual; time-sensitive (monthly, quarterly, yearly); measured in money, units, new customers or activities such as demonstrations.
Ebook quota type Meaning
Direct quota For front-line reps who can influence it. e.g. new business quota (historic average selling price and conversion rates), existing customer quota (bottom-up, renewals plus incremental)
Overlay quota Sits on top of direct quotas; sum of all direct quotas below (manager, then VP)

  • Importance: the big visible number; draws the line in the sand for on-target earnings at risk. Bad quotas → channel conflict, ownership confusion, disengagement.

  • Five quota tips: match quotas to sales activities (retail staff cannot control gross margin) · reinforce positive behaviour · build together, not hand down (sales, sales ops, finance) · leave yesterday's quota with yesterday ("it worked last year" is not a strategy) · yesterday's quota should not determine today's. Sales Performance Management (SPM) technology tracks history.

SN quota type Example
Revenue / profit Furniture firm: Rs 20,000 revenue per rep per month
Activity Calls, meetings, mail; met regardless of actual sales
Volume Quantity sold, not value (car dealership: number of cars)
Cost-based Resources spent per sale (electronics store: 20 minutes per sale)
Forecast From history, for team or territory (clothing store +10% next quarter)
Combination Several metrics (recreational vehicle dealer)

5. Control of sales

  • Ebook definition: analysing and measuring sales force performance, comparing with standards, noticing deviations and causes, and taking corrective steps. Bases: time, sales volume, discipline, expenses, activities. Control is not fault-finding; too liberal or too strict both harm.

  • Why important (4): salesmen work independently at a distance · transparency so negative deviations are corrected · direct effort to profitability · keep customers on top priority.

Step Key point
1. Set standards Realistic, numerical, short period (annual $12,00,000 → $1,00,000 per month); revise for festivals
2. Measure actual performance Both quantitative and qualitative; watch rejections and sales returns
3. Compare with standards Minor deviations ignored; strict action on major ones
4. Correct deviations, follow up Major deviations to top management: critical point control and management by exception

  • Methods of controlling the sales force (9): territories · quotas · maintaining contact · defining authority and rights · salesman's reporting (most used) · complaints and objection notes · analysing expenses · field trips at least once in six months · sufficient sales tools (manuals, order forms, visiting cards, videos).

  • SN definition: strategies, processes and measures to oversee, regulate and optimise sales activities. SN control tools (4): sales budget (overarching constraint), sales programs (training, company values), sales audit (impartial review of objectives, policies, structure, methods, procedures, personnel), sales analysis (sales volume data for sales and profit trends).

6. Sales meetings and contests

Sales meeting (Ebook)

  • Session to share ideas and experiences; important for communication and motivation; motivates through group pressure; management uses it to raise standards. Also called sales conferences; can be informal, one-on-one or online (Zoom, Skype, Webex).

  • Five planning decisions: define training aims → content → methods → how to hold it → how to evaluate.

  • Purposes: motivate, recognise top performers, set goals, product updates, identify problems, overcome deficiencies in sales figures, strategies, client knowledge. Meetings are costly and cut productivity, so hold them sparingly. A technical expert may join a pitch.

Sales meeting (SN)

  • A forum where reps engage potential buyers to explore leads, discuss quotations or finalise orders; also post-sale reviews. Avoid flooding the buyer with irrelevant features.
FEBA approach Meaning
Features Distinctive features that meet market demand
Evidence Data, energy ratings, comparative studies (AC example)
Benefits Tangible gain, e.g. lower electricity bill
Agreement Close or advance the cycle (price quote request)
  • Types (SN): on client's premises · virtual · event/conference · review meetings with existing clients (upselling, cross-selling). Important for B2B and high-involvement products.

Sales contest

  • Ebook: a motivational program giving rewards based on sales/results; "a special selling campaign offering incentives beyond the compensation plan". Types (3): direct competition (one winner), team competition, goal (more than one person can win).

  • Theory: Herzberg → achievement and recognition (motivators); Maslow → esteem and self-actualisation (higher-order needs). Also team spirit and morale.

  • Objectives (10): new customers · larger orders per call · push slow-moving, high-margin or new products · overcome seasonal slump · profitable mix · improve distributors' salespeople · seasonal merchandise · more dealer displays · reorders · special deals.

  • Running a contest (6 tips): keep it simple (explain in two sentences; one contest at a time) · make it fun · involve the team in choosing prizes · daily/weekly updates (HubSpot live dashboard) · no false promises (2002 waitress promised a Toyota got a "toy Yoda", sued and won) · hand out prizes ASAP (author paid nine months late).

  • SN: a brief competitive event for short-term objectives; rewards are separate from regular incentives; modest investment for the firm.

7. Displays, showrooms and exhibitions

  • Display (SN): in-store exhibition, "we have what you're looking for"; central to retail promotion.

  • Showroom (SN): showcases products in a personalised way; not primarily for direct sales; samples, exclusive items; possible even for services. Goals: brand visibility, attract clients, reinforce image, drive sales.

  • Exhibition (Ebook): the only promotional tool that brings buyers, sellers and competitors together in a commercial setting; very targeted (visitors spend time and money); designed to create mass hysteria; expensive (space, logistics). In industrial buying it is an information source second only to personal selling, ahead of direct mail and print.

  • Objectives (8): reach an interested audience · awareness and relationships · demonstrations · stimulate needs · introduce new product · recruit dealers/distributors · company image · handle service problems.

  • Planning: clear objectives → visitor selection criteria (qualify by purpose) → design strategies (who receives, registers, demonstrates; mock run) → promotional strategies (pre-show direct mail, phone, sales call, trade press) → follow-up (database; prospects to sales, partners to purchase). Participation is not one department's job.

  • Evaluation (11 measures): visitors · key influencers · leads · cost per lead · orders · cost per order · new distributors · competitive intelligence · interest in new products · new relationships · complaint handling.

  • SN exhibitions: public showcase for brand exposure; main aim is to acquire multiple leads; present the organisation as well as it appears online; network with fellow entrepreneurs.

Trading analogy: A sales budget is your risk capital allocation per strategy; quotas are position targets; overlay quota is the desk P&L made of each trader's book. Sales control is your trade journal review: set the standard, measure, compare and correct, managing by exception rather than micromanaging every tick.
