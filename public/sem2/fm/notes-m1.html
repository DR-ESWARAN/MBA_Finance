1. Finance: definitions

  • Ebook: management of the flows of money through an organisation (corporation, school, bank or government agency).
Author Keyword to remember
Howard & Upton Finance = administrative area / set of administrative functions relating to the arrangement of cash and credit so the organisation can carry out its objectives
Bonneville & Dewey Financing = raising, providing, managing of all the money, capital or funds used in the business
Guthmann & Dougall Business finance = planning, raising, controlling and administering of funds used in the business
Peter L. Bernstein (SN) Managing money: acquiring, spending, investing and managing risks
Ezra Solomon (SN) How people and businesses evaluate investments and raise capital: who, what, where, when, why, how
Brigham & Ehrhardt (SN) Business finance = raising, administering and disbursing funds by privately owned units in non-financial fields

Watch out: Guthmann & Dougall (planning, raising, controlling, administering) defines business finance, not finance in general. Bonneville & Dewey uses "raising, providing, managing".

  • Business finance covers sole proprietors, partnerships and companies. Same principles of finance apply to large and small, proprietary and non-proprietary organisations (Ebook).

  • Study Notes: finance studies money, currency, capital, assets, investments. Three actions: creation, management and investment of wealth. Business finance is "more of an art than a science".

Term (SN keywords) Meaning
Money Standard object used to exchange goods and services
Currency System of money used in a particular country
Capital Wealth in the form of money, assets or bonds (currency used to multiply itself)
Assets Anything of value: physical, intellectual, living, human
Investments Giving over money with the intention of returns as profits

2. Types of finance (Study Notes)

Type Flow of funds between Key points
Personal Individuals, households, small businesses Saving, investing, insurance, debts, taxation effects, contingency fund, retirement
Corporate Large firms and corporations Increase firm value. 3 active areas: budgeting, policymaking (dividends), structure and formation. Basis of managerial finance
Public Governments Budgets, taxes, debt, municipal bonds; prepares for calamities, wars; five-year plans; managed mostly by RBI
  • Other types: environmental (green / sustainable) · quantum and computational · international (World Bank, WTO, forex) · behavioural (psychology: fear, panic).

Economics vs finance

Economics Finance
How resources are allocated to satisfy needs and wants How individuals and organisations manage their money
Production, distribution, consumption of goods and services Money, currency, capital, assets, investments
Broader; finance is a part of economics Narrower term
Overall health of the economy; long-term study Monetary activities; both long and short-term
  • Related disciplines: mathematical finance, financial law, financial economics, financial engineering, fintech, business, accounting. Theories that can be tested = experimental finance.

Four key areas of finance

  1. Financial management: managing the company's resources.

  2. Financial accounting: tracking transactions, profit and loss; compliance, budgeting, forecasting.

  3. Investment management: allocation of assets to benefit investors; = asset management; portfolio optimisation, fundamental and technical analysis.

  4. Risk management: credit, market, operational risk; protecting firm value = enterprise risk management; suggests diversification.

Knowledge Check: Avoiding loss more than seeking profit = loss aversion · assets traded in a financial system = financial instruments · lower-value assets have higher volatility · public finance studies political states.

3. Financial management: meaning, scope, objectives

  • Ebook: planning, organising, directing and controlling the investment, financing and dividend decisions to help the business achieve its objectives. Uses statistical, mathematical and economic tools.

  • "Intelligent quest for optimal use of financial and other economic resources." Came out of economics to become a standalone profession. A housewife economising is doing financial management.

  • Scope: cuts across sectors; used by government and NGOs too.

Three decisions (Ebook)

Decision What it covers
Investment Where to put funds
Financing Where to raise funds from
Dividend Distribution of profits: dividend to shareholders vs retained profits, and the form of distribution

Five objectives (Ebook)

  1. Regular and adequate supply of funds.

  2. Adequate returns to shareholders (depends on earning capacity, market price of share, expectations).

  3. Optimum utilisation of funds at least cost.

  4. Safety of investment.

  5. Sound capital structure (balance of debt and equity).

Goals of financial management (Study Notes)

Goal Key point
Wealth vs profit maximisation Wealth maximisation = raise market value / share price; broader than profit maximisation; considers stakeholders; long-term; also called value maximisation
Prevention of bankruptcy Keeps the firm solvent; contingency fund
Resources and marketing optimisation Avoid wastage; efficient capital structure
Dividend decisions How much to pay vs retain; affects reputation and market value

Analogy (memory aid, not from source): Profit maximisation is booking a quick gain this quarter; wealth maximisation is growing the stock's fair value over years. Exams favour wealth maximisation as the broader, long-term goal.

Duties of a finance manager (Study Notes)

  • Two levels in large firms: tactical (observe, record, report; advisory only, no decision power) and strategic (top management, data-driven decisions).

  • Duties (5): money matters (capital management = the most important duty) · document issues (reports, budgets) · compliance (tax, licences, payroll) · strategic planning (M&A, alliances) · relationship management (staff, investors, media).

4. Debt vs equity financing (Study Notes)

Debt financing Equity financing
What Loans from institutions; periodic repayment Selling stock; buyers become part-owners
Plus Lender has no control; relationship ends on repayment; interest is tax-deductible (so usually cheaper) Investors share the loss; no monthly payments; freedom to experiment
Minus Adds monthly expense; hard in recession; may need collateral Dilutes control; must consult and answer to shareholders
Examples Bank loans, credit card, invoice financing Crowdfunding, venture capital, angel investors

Basis Categories and examples
1. Period

Long term (> 5 yrs): equity shares, retained earnings, preference shares, debentures, loans from FIs

Medium term (1–5 yrs): bank loans, public deposits, loans from FIs (text adds lease financing)

Short term (≤ 1 yr): trade credit, factoring, banks, commercial paper
2. Ownership

Owner's funds: equity shares, retained earnings

6. Long-term sources

  • Need (SN): growth and sustainability, lower interest over time, supports CSR, transfers risk to providers (who charge a premium), crucial for M&A and expansion. Generally a higher amount. Tenure = period to return principal.

Shares

  • Share = fractional part of share capital; basis of ownership. Holders = shareholders.

  • Equity (ordinary) shares: part-owners, take maximum business risk, have voting rights, members of the company. Capital raised = ownership capital / owner's funds. Foundation of a company.

  • Preference share: two conditions: (a) preferential right to dividend (fixed amount or fixed rate) before equity; (b) preferential right to repayment of capital on winding up. May participate in surplus per Memorandum/Articles.

Type of preference share Meaning
Cumulative vs non-cumulative Unpaid dividend carried forward to future years vs lost
Participating vs non-participating Share in extra surplus after equity dividend vs no share
Convertible vs non-convertible Can be converted into equity within a period vs cannot
Redeemable vs irredeemable Repaid during company life vs not

Preference vs equity (Ebook table)

Basis Preference share Equity share
Voting No voting except at class meetings Voting rights
Dividend Paid first Paid after preference
Capital repayment Repaid first Repaid after preference
Rate of dividend Fixed May vary year to year
Convertibility Can be converted into equity Non-convertible
Redemption Redeemable during company life Not redeemable during company life
  • Study Notes: shares traded on NSE and BSE; created via IPO; profit paid = dividend. Common shares carry votes; preferred usually do not but have stable dividends.

Debentures

  • Ebook: debt instrument issued by companies and government; issued on reputation; fixed rate of interest; also known as a bond.

  • Four types: secured/unsecured · registered/bearer · convertible/non-convertible · first/second.

  • Study Notes: holder gets periodic interest + principal on maturity; may be redeemable or irredeemable; no voting rights or ownership; paid before shareholders in bankruptcy; main risk = inflation (plus market, credit, interest, liquidity risk).

Term loans

  • Repaid in regular payments over a set period; specific amount; fixed or floating rate; suits an established small business with sound financial statements; may need a substantial down payment.

  • SN: given against collateral; higher credit score = lower interest; repaid as EMIs; penalties on default; govt concessions (farm loans, waivers), World Bank loans.

Lease and hire purchase

Lease Hire purchase
Parties Two: lessor (owner) and lessee (user) Hirer + seller, often a third party finance company
Payment Periodic lease rentals Down payment + instalments (principal + interest)
Ownership No transfer of ownership Passes to hirer after last instalment
Maintenance May or may not be on lessee Hirer bears it
  • SN types of lease: (1) no ownership, return asset · (2) ownership to lessee at nominal price · (3) lessor refunds up to 95% of rent if lessee brings a buyer.

Retained earnings

  • Ebook: profits earned to date less dividends or other distributions. Large balance = financially healthy.

  • Formula: Beginning RE + Profit/loss − Dividends = Ending RE.

  • SN: positive RE over time = accumulated profits; negative = accumulated loss; positive RE raises book value. Uses: expansion, R&D, debt prepayment, buyback.

Bonds

  • Instrument of indebtedness of issuer to holders. Buying a bond = lending to government, municipality or corporation.
Feature (Ebook) Meaning
Face value Price of one unit; also principal, nominal or par value
Coupon rate Interest rate (from paper coupons)
Tenure Period after which bond matures
Credit quality Investors' confidence in the issuer's long-term asset performance
Tradable In the secondary market

  • Advantages (Ebook): stability, indentures (legal guarantee: par value, coupon, tenure, rating), portfolio diversification.

  • SN: YTM = total return to maturity, expressed as an annual rate. Coupon is fixed, unlike dividend.

Bond type (SN) Key point
Government Bills, notes, sovereigns; binding even if ruling party changes
Corporate Cheaper than bank loans for the company
Municipal Local govt; some tax-free
Agency · Foreign Govt-affiliated agencies · global corporations and institutions
Zero-coupon No periodic interest; issued at discount, repaid at face value
Convertible Converts to shares; lower interest
Callable / puttable Callable: company buys back early (benefits company) · Puttable: investor sells back early (benefits investor)
Utility Local govt for public services (roads, sewage, power). Legal document = bond indenture

7. Medium-term sources: public deposits

  • Unsecured deposits invited from the public, mainly for working capital; company advertises in newspapers.

  • Limit: cannot exceed 25% of share capital and free reserves (Ebook). Regulated by RBI (SN).

  • Company side: cheaper than bank loans. Public side: higher interest than bank deposits; prefer well-established companies.

  • SN: types = fixed deposit and recurring deposit; deposit certificate issued; no voting rights, fewer legal formalities. Minus: hard for new companies; unreliable in recession.

8. Short-term sources

  • Ebook: period not exceeding one year. Examples: trade credit, bank loans, commercial paper. Common for seasonal businesses, wholesalers and manufacturers with large inventories or receivables.

  • Four named (Ebook): bank finance · commercial paper · trade credit · bills discounting.

Bank finance

  • Accounts for over four-fifths of total credit by all financial institutions. Types: short, medium, long-term finance.

  • SN: traditional loans vs overdraft (withdraw more than balance; for temporary shortfalls) · revolving line of credit (pre-approved limit; interest only on amount used).

Commercial paper (CP)

Fact Value
Nature Unsecured money market instrument, issued as a promissory note
Introduced in India 1990
Maturity (Ebook) Minimum 7 days, maximum 1 year (not beyond issuer's rating validity)
Denomination Rs 5 lakh or multiples
Who can issue Only firms with high credit ratings

Watch out: Study Notes say CP maturity is "a few days to 270 days" (a US-style figure). The Ebook figure for India is 7 days to 1 year. Use the Ebook figure unless the option says 270.

  • SN risks of CP: market, credit, roll-over, regulatory, liquidity.

Trade credit

  • Credit extended by one trader to another when goods are bought on credit; buy supplies without immediate payment. Most widely used short-term source (Objective QB).

  • SN: terms like "net 30" / "net 60"; improves working capital management; negotiate via strong payment history, financial health, volume commitments, early payment discounts, flexible structures.

Bills discounting

  • Ebook: discount/fee a bank takes from a seller to release funds before the credit period ends; typical with letter of credit purchases.

  • Study Guide: selling a bill of exchange to a financial institution before maturity at less than par; discount depends on time left and risk; "win-win" for buyer and seller.

  • SN: also called invoice discounting or bill negotiation; credit risk transfers to the institution; main cost = discount rate; risks = cost, customer perception, dependence on external funding.

Certificate of deposit (CD) (SN)

  • Document of title to a term deposit. Issued by banks and FIs; Rs 1 lakh or multiples; 7 days to 1 year; usually at a discount; freely transferable; demat form.

9. Internal sources of finance (Study Notes Part 4)

  • Funds from within the organisation, no third party. Convenient, secure, fewer restrictions. Drawback = power crisis: a manager controlling funds may choose safer, lower-return ventures; manager's motive may vary.
Basis Internal External
Procurement Easy Difficult
Size/reputation matters? No Yes
Owned by Internal team Banks, FIs, investors, creditors
Risk · cost Less risky · cheaper More risky · costly (interest, dividends)
Legal Board-approved audits only Lengthy legal procedures
  • Nine types: retained earnings · owner's funds · sale of assets · stock sale · reduction in working capital (early debt collection, delayed payments) · personal loan · sale of investments · discounted sale · delay in salaries (unethical, extreme).

Retained earnings (most common internal source)

  • Profits left after dividends; also called trading profits / earning surplus. No-cost, self-supportive, best emergency fund, best for short and medium projects; needs investor permission at the annual meeting.
Advantages Disadvantages
Continuous, interest-free Not reliable: profits vary
Good for risky projects Shareholder discontent
Raises share price; improves debt-equity ratio Management monopoly in decisions
Prevents dilution of control Not enough for large / long-term projects
Immediately available; higher return (no financing cost) Misuse wastes past effort

  • Underuse = profits collected for long and not used productively; a strategy failure. Invest in high-IRR projects, in smaller units.

  • Appropriated RE: earmarked by the board for a specific use (M&A, R&D, debt repayment, buyback, CSR...). Unappropriated RE = Total RE − Appropriated RE − Dividends.

  • On liquidation, appropriated earnings return to the main retained earnings and are distributed.

Knowledge Check: RE are a zero-cost source = True · RE need lengthy legal process = False · RE cause dilution of control = False · coupons must be paid to use RE = False · a healthy company combines internal and external sources = True.

10. Common misconceptions (LMS)

Wrong belief Correct
Finance is only handling money Shapes social, economic and political activity
Corporate finance = only shareholder profit Also budgeting, policy, structure, sustainability
Public finance = only budgets and taxes Also calamities, wars, technology; RBI manages
Long-term finance only for large firms SMEs need it too
Shares and debentures serve the same purpose Shares = ownership + votes; debentures = debt
Public deposits = bank deposits Private companies, higher interest, reputation-based
All long-term finance transfers ownership Only shares dilute ownership
Bonds are only issued by governments Also corporations, municipalities, international bodies
Bill discounting increases credit risk It transfers credit risk to the institution
Trade credit = bill discounting Trade credit = delayed payment to supplier; discounting = selling receivables for cash
Internal finance has no drawbacks Power crisis; RE fluctuate with profits
Delaying salaries is normal internal finance Extreme and unethical
