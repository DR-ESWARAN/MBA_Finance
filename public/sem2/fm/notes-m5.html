1. Meaning of working capital

  • Ebook: a measure of a company's efficiency and short-term financial health; holding of current assets (cash, receivables, inventory, marketable securities), also called circulating capital.

  • Funds available and used for day-to-day operations. "The less working capital used to attract sales, the higher the likely return on investment."

  • Working capital management = managing current assets, current liabilities and the interrelations between them.

  • SN: working capital = excess of current assets over current liabilities; also called circulating, fluctuating, revolving capital. Like blood in the body.

Author (SN) View
Gerstenberg Excess of current assets over current liabilities (net WC); he called it circulating capital
J. S. Mill Sum of current assets = working capital (gross WC)

2. Two concepts of working capital (Ebook)

Balance sheet concept Operating cycle concept
Meaning Excess of current assets over current liabilities (or sometimes gross current assets) Time from procurement of inventory to collection of cash from receivables
Shows Firm's current solvency in repaying creditors How long resources are tied up
Notes ICAI: net current assets = CA − CL. "Shortage of WC" usually means scarcity of cash. An increase in WC = application of funds 3 activities: purchase, produce, sell. Cash out happens before cash in (unsynchronised). Quicker cycle = less WC needed, better profitability
  • Production / manufacturing cycle = the time involved in manufacturing goods (Objective QB).

Analogy (memory aid): The operating cycle is door-to-balloon time for cash: the shorter the cycle from cash out (raw material) to cash in (collection), the less capital sits in the system.

3. Significance of adequate working capital (Ebook, 9)

  1. Maintains solvency through uninterrupted production.

  2. Prompt payments build goodwill.

  3. Loans on easy terms from banks (good credit standing).

  4. Avail cash discounts on purchases, so lower costs.

  5. Regular supply of raw materials and continuous production.

  6. Regular payment of salaries and wages.

  7. Exploit favourable market conditions.

  8. Face business crisis.

  9. Security, confidence, high morale, overall efficiency.

  • SN: objectives of WC management = maintain the operating cycle, minimise cost of capital spent on WC, maximise return on current asset investments. WC ratio = CA ÷ CL.

4. Types of working capital

Ebook: gross vs net

Gross WC Net WC
Meaning Total investment in all current assets (total WC) Current assets minus current liabilities
Nature Quantitative; focuses on optimising and financing current assets Qualitative concept; can be positive or negative
Reading Positive WC best expressed as CA ÷ CL Greater margin = better liquidity; negative = possible distress

  • Current assets convert to cash within a year (cash, receivables, inventory, prepaid). Current liabilities paid within a year (rent, utilities, payroll, payments toward long-term debt).

  • Ebook lists sources tied to net WC: equity capital (owners' risk capital) · debt capital (loans) · speciality capital ("gold standard", almost no economic cost) · sweat equity (equity to executives on favourable terms for value added).

  • No fixed rule for gross and net WC; depends on the business. Both matter.

5. Adequacy and how to improve working capital

  • Ebook: adequate WC = enough for day-to-day operations under normal conditions; lets the firm face losses and emergencies.

  • Management of WC includes inventory, receivables and payables management.

Ebook: 6 ways to improve WC SN: 5 tactics for adequacy
Borrow long-term / replace short-term debt with long-term debt Organise inventories and purchases
Improve inventory management Enhance receivables handling (credit checks, automation)
Manage expenses to improve cash flow Automate GST returns and ITC claims (GSTR-2B)
Automate business financing processes Automate vendor payments and communication
Work with vendors who give deals and discounts Real-time data analytics to spot anomalies
Track business performance

6. Factors affecting working capital needs

Ebook factor (5) Effect
Nature of business Ebook: trading firms need more WC than manufacturing, yet also says service/trading need low-modest and manufacturing substantial WC
Seasonality Seasonal raw materials bought in bulk → more WC
Production policy Produce to order → less WC; produce in anticipation → more WC
Market conditions Strong competition → more WC
Supply conditions Prompt, predictable supply → small inventory, less WC

Watch out: The Ebook contradicts itself on nature of business. For an MCQ, "manufacturing needs substantial WC; service/trading needs low to modest" is the safer reading. SN: necessities and public utilities need less WC; luxury goods need more.

  • SN 10 factors: nature · size · volume of sales (primary variable; direct relation) · production cycle (longer = more) · business cycle (boom = more) · terms of purchase and sale (liberal sales credit + no purchase credit = more) · credit control (cash sales = less) · growth and expansion · management ability · external factors (easy bank finance = less).

  • Objective QB: rising prices → higher WC needed · WC needs are lower when met through current liabilities · no single WC investment policy suits all firms · WC enables cash discounts · WC ensures regular supply of raw materials.

Knowledge Check (SN): More cash sales needs more WC = False · liberal sales credit without purchase credit needs more WC = True · scarce inventory loses revenue, excess blocks capital = True.

7. Forecasting working capital requirements (Ebook, 6 methods)

Method Key point
Cash forecasting Total cash receipts and disbursements for a period. Direct = receipts and disbursements method (short-term liquidity); indirect = from projected income statements and balance sheets. Running out of cash = insolvency
Balance sheet method Arrive at closing cash and bank balances. Also called the percentage of accounts receivable method (estimates bad debts)
Adjusted P&L method Start from opening cash and bank balances; add/deduct items to reach closing balance; useful for long-term planning
Percent of sales Each line item as a % of sales, applied to future sales; also days of sales, turnover
Operating cycle Time to convert cash back into cash; longer cycle = bigger WC
Regression analysis Statistical average relationship between sales and WC over past years
  • SN: cash flow analysis (projections, timing, seasonality) and operating cycle approach (interplay of inventory turnover and receivables collection). Cash conversion cycle = time to convert investments in inventory into cash; shorter = more efficient.

Estimating WC (SN worked format)

  • Current assets: raw material stock + WIP (raw material 100%, wages and overheads 50% as they accrue evenly) + finished goods (at cost) + debtors (at cost or sale price as stated) + cash. Less current liabilities: creditors + outstanding wages + outstanding overheads. WC = CA − CL.

  • SN Illustration 1 (78,000 units, 52 weeks → 1,500 units/week; cost 90, price 120): CA 37,50,000 − CL 6,90,000 = 30,60,000.

  • SN Illustration 2 (30,000 units → 2,500/month; cost 10, price 12): CA 1,40,000 − CL 25,000 = 1,15,000.

8. Accounts receivable

  • Ebook definition: "debt owed to the firm by customers arising from sale of goods or services in the ordinary course of business." Outcome of credit sales. Also called trade receivable. A current asset; legally enforceable claims, usually invoices.

  • Importance (Ebook): part of profitability; clearest indicator of income; generates cash inflow; affects future cash flow; credit builds customer relations; investors use receivable days to check collection efficiency.

Study Notes: managing receivables

  • Credit policies: assess creditworthiness; stricter terms for high-risk customers; communicate terms clearly.

  • Collection strategies: reminders, escalation, flexible options, early-payment discounts, credit holds, collection agencies; automation + CRM, but human oversight still needed.

  • Credit analysis: financial and non-financial factors; credit limits; risk segments; credit insurance or guarantees.

  • Discount policy: e.g. 2% if paid within 10 days; speeds cash and cuts collection period but can erode profit.

  • WC strategies: conservative, aggressive, moderate: different liquidity and risk.

Knowledge Check (SN): Cash conversion cycle is about fixed assets = False · early-payment discounts may hurt profitability = True · credit analysis only financial = False · discount policy prolongs collection = False.

9. Common misconceptions (LMS)

Wrong belief Correct
WC is only cash on hand Also inventory, receivables, short-term investments
All businesses need the same WC Varies by size, industry, operations
WC matters only in crises Matters every day
WC is only the finance department's job Operations, sales, procurement affect it
More sales always needs proportionally more WC Better inventory and receivables control can avoid it
Cash conversion cycle is only about cash Covers all assets and liabilities in the cycle
Discounts always improve profitability They can erode margins
All WC strategies give similar outcomes Conservative, aggressive, moderate differ in risk and liquidity
