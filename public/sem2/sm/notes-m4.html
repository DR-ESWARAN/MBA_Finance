Strategic Management · Notes

Read this first: The Ebook covers formulation (3 modes, aspects, 6-step process, 3 levels), portfolio tools (ADL matrix, Hofer matrix, Shell Directional Policy Matrix, PIMS), implementation (definitions, Hrebiniak & Joyce 1984, 5 activities, pace), 6 ways to overcome failure, leadership in implementation and SBUs. The QB asks about organisation structures (simple, divisional, matrix, network, hourglass, M-form) that are not in any Module 4 source; see section 8.

1. Strategy formulation

  • Ebook: an analytical process of selecting the best course of action to meet objectives and vision; sets long-term goals to exploit strengths and opportunities; examined through SWOT. SN: setting goals and selecting the best course of action to reach them.
Mode (Ebook) Who / how
Entrepreneurial One powerful individual; focus on opportunities; founder's vision
Adaptive Reactive solutions to existing problems
Planning Analysts; both proactive search for opportunities and reactive solutions

  • Strategic planning relates to the whole enterprise or to SBUs; makes risk-taking (entrepreneurial) decisions; forward looking (product-market posture, profitability, size, innovation).

  • Aspects influencing formulation (Ebook): cultural · political · role of directors and CEOs · role of CEO/MD.

Ebook / Study Guide process Detail
1. Establish organisational objectives Long-term goals first
2. Analyse organisational environment SWOT; S/W internal (controllable), O/T external (not controllable)
3. Form quantitative goals e.g. 30% revenue increase this year
4. Objectives in context of divisional plans Targets for every department
5. Performance analysis Gap between actual and standard performance
6. Selection of strategy Final step: evaluate alternatives, pick the best
SN — 6 steps to execute formulation Detail
1. Develop a strategic mission 3 parts: time (1, 5, 10 years) · core values · business description
2. Establish organisational goals Target market · customers · offerings · adaptation to change
3. Create departmental plans e.g. convert 10+ leads a month, raise income 15%
4. Conduct a performance analysis SWOT
5. Execute a plan of action Website, online marketing, tech edge
6. Revise as needed Monthly, quarterly or yearly reviews

  • SN tips: use SMART goals (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-based) · ask employees for feedback · keep and compare drafts.

  • SN 5 high-level steps: understand your strategy level → internal and external research → build the plan backwards (start with the end in mind) → review progress regularly → take the first step: implement. "Long-range planning without short-term action is a nosedive into failure."

Levels of strategy

Level Ebook SN
Corporate What you want: growth, stability, acquisition, retrenchment; which business to enter How the organisation is set up and units work together; treats units as a portfolio
Business How you compete; SBUs Win over rivals in a niche: positioning, target market, value
Functional How the organisation grows; daily actions, resource allocation Marketing, finance, operations, HR plans supporting corporate strategy

Misconceptions: Formulation is continuous, not one-time · all three levels matter · it is more than goal setting · needs internal and external research · execution is not straightforward.

2. Portfolio and planning tools (Ebook)

  • Strategic planning process: starts with situation analysis, ends with preparation of the business-unit strategic plan (mission, business composition, SBU analysis, objectives in between).

Arthur D. Little (ADL) Strategic Condition Matrix

  • Two dimensions: competitive position and industry maturity (like the PLC; an "industry life cycle").
Competitive position Meaning
Dominant Monopoly or customer lock-in (Microsoft Windows)
Strong Lot of freedom (Apple iPod)
Favourable Strengths in segments of a fragmented market
Tenable May be eroded; no sustainable advantage
Weak Unenviable, but can improve
  • ADL 6 generic strategy categories: market · product · management and systems · technology · retrenchment · operations.

Hofer's Portfolio Matrix

  • Vertical axis = competitive position; horizontal axis = phases of the market life cycle. Circles = size of the branch; shaded section = company's market share.

Shell Directional Policy Matrix

  • A refinement of the Boston Matrix. Horizontal = prospects for sector profitability; vertical = company's competitive capability. Options: double or quit, growth, custodial (milk, like a cash cow), cash generator, phased withdrawal, divest; zones also include leader and try harder.

PIMS (Profit Impact of Market Strategy)

  • Developed by the Strategic Planning Institute (SPI), Cambridge, Massachusetts; data over 20 years by Prof. Sidney Schoeffler (Ebook spells it Schoerffler); 2,200+ companies/SBUs; about 100 items per business; assumptions in two ranges: 1-4 years and 5-10 years. Unit of analysis = a business (division, product line or profit centre).

3. Strategy implementation

  • SN: putting the strategic plan into practice; the fourth step of the strategic management process (Study Guide: after mission/vision/objectives, environmental analysis and formulation; followed by evaluation and control).

  • Ebook definitions: sum of activities for executing a plan · operationalisation · interventions in structures, key personnel actions and control systems · factoring big problems into smaller ones · managerial interventions aligning action with intention (Floyd & Wooldridge, 1992).

  • Term became well known through "Strategy Implementation" (1984) by Lawrence G. Hrebiniak and William F. Joyce. "Strategy execution" links implementation with the Balanced Scorecard.

5 activities (Ebook) Meaning
Articulation Consensus on outcomes; e.g. a Destination Statement
Validation Internal (cascading down) and external (regulators, key customers)
Communication Translate into clear short-term operating objectives, measurable, with milestones
Monitoring Start early; a few high-level measures
Engagement Managerial interventions; regular structured, time-limited meetings

  • ACME framework = Articulate, Communicate, Monitor, Engage. Pace: a slow implementation with small steps usually improves performance; committed teams implement more effectively but more slowly (Dooley et al., 2000).

  • Study Guide factors in implementation: institutionalisation of strategy · proper organisational climate · appropriate operating plans · appropriate organisation structure (structure is a means to an end) · periodic review (major strategies at least once a year).

Importance of implementation (SN, 7)
Achievement of objectives Usage of effort and money
Evaluation of the strategy Identification of loopholes
Taking corrective measures Measuring manager's efficiency
Enhancing competitive capabilities
7 stages of implementation (SN)
1. Set clear goals and define key variables Attainable goals; backup plans
2. Determine responsibilities, roles, relationships Document resources; communication procedure
3. Delegate the work Do not micromanage
4. Execute, monitor, support Check-ins; daily/weekly/monthly reports
5. Take corrective action Implementation is iterative
6. Get closure and agreement on output
7. Conduct a retrospective Learn even from failure

4. Strategy failure

10 reasons for failure (SN)
1. Premature upscaling (beyond breaking point) 6. Too much focus on structural changes
2. Poor managerial skills / lack of leadership 7. Lack of empowerment
3. Zero succession plan 8. Short-term planning, losing sight of goals
4. Overwhelming strategic plan 9. Lack of communication
5. Unrealistic goals: all vision, no direction 10. Inadequate monitoring

  • 6 methods to overcome failure (Ebook and SN): strengthen your support network · manage cash flow (forecast) · take time to reflect · take responsibility · improve through feedback loops · take a step-by-step approach (start small).

  • Study Guide 5 ways: understand the environment · scenario planning and wargaming · be disciplined in planning · develop strategy strategically (involve stakeholders) · minimise friction.

5. Strategic leadership

  • Study Guide: a manager's ability to express a strategic vision and motivate others to acquire it; strategic leaders create structure, allocate resources, express vision.

  • SN objectives: process analysis, strategic efficiency, innovation, environment for productivity, liberty, self-initiative; may use rewards and incentives.

  • Process: understand purpose → design a vision for a future date → plan to move from current to intended state.

6 competencies (SN) Meaning
Anticipate Predict competitor behaviour
Challenge Find root causes from several perspectives
Interpret Open-minded on multiple hypotheses
Decide Balance long-term investment with short-term results
Align Stakeholder motivations and conflicts
Learn Share successes and failures; change course

  • 3 classifications in large firms: production/action-focused (low-level, little influence) · organisational (mid-level, short and medium term) · strategic (senior, long term). Operational leadership ensures daily procedures are carried out correctly.

  • Ebook — leadership in implementation: involve all affected departments · get employee buy-in (sense of urgency) · monitor staff and results (benchmarks, KPIs) · do it in phases with a project manager.

6. Strategic Business Units (SBUs)

  • Ebook: a profit centre focusing on a product offering and market segment; own marketing plan; responsible for its own profitability. GE has 49 SBUs. McKinsey traits: separate competitors and a profitability bottom line.

  • Study Guide: a scientific grouping of the businesses of a multi-business corporation; products in an SBU get the same planning treatment. Examples: P&G, LG.

  • SN characteristics: targets a segment · independently run · shares often listed separately · clients may not know the parent · one manager oversees · reports to the parent.

  • Structure: SBU manager/president → functional departments (marketing, operations, HR, finance, R&D) → teams. Not totally independent; corporate is involved in key decisions.

SBU types (SN) Basis
Product-based Product lines ("magnifying glass")
Market-based Customer segments (government, business, retail)
Geography-based Regions; typical of multinationals
Process-based An organisational procedure (e.g. R&D)
Function-based Business functions (production, sales)
Hybrid Mix (geography SBUs inside product SBUs)
  • SBU planning: situational analysis → SMART objectives → strategies → implement → monitor and control → evaluate and adjust. P&G SBUs: beauty, health care, grooming, baby, fabric & home care, feminine & family care.
Advantages Disadvantages
Higher profitability (targeted segment) Hard to establish (own vision, goals)
Long-term survival Competition among SBUs can hurt the parent
Easier bookkeeping Costly: higher operating costs
Simpler decision making Needs better communication
In-depth R&D for markets Unethical politics, rivalry
