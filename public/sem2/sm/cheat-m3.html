Module 3 · Strategy Analysis

Strategic Management · Cheat sheet

NotesCheat sheetMind map

ANALYSIS — Research the business environment to form strategy; plan B ready. Tools: SWOT, value chain, PESTLE, Porter. Do it regularly (yearly refresh). Focus high-level · look back and forward · involve leaders.

5 STEPS (SN) — Level → team → methods (SWOT/PESTLE, BSC 4 perspectives) → summarise → formal strategy (strategy map).

8 APPLICATIONS — Market sizing · product portfolio (stars) · forecasting · brand perception · market assessment · partner · player · competitive.

SCANNING — Internal = S/W; external = O/T. Appraisal = opportunities and threats. Before scanning: events → trends → issues → expectations.

Technique Key
SWOT Audit tool; S/W internal, O/T external
ETOP Relative importance of external O/T
Ansoff (product/market grid) 2×2 growth + risk
QUEST Monitor change: observe → appraise → report → review
PEST(EL) Political, economic, social, technological (+ environmental, legal)
Ansoff Existing product New product
Existing market Penetration Product development
New market Market development Diversification

PROFILE — Strategic profile = history + products + future plans: background → mission/vision → situation analysis → SWOT (most important). SAP = strengths and weaknesses by functional area; KFS; big firms slower.

MODEL — Mission & goals → environmental scanning → formulation → implementation → evaluation & control (final). Advantage = profit above industry average.

PLANNING MODELS — SAM = IT + business strategy · BSC = financial, customer, internal process, capacity · basic = new firms · Blue Ocean = no competition (red = saturated) · issue-based = large firms, most complex · Porter 1979 = entrants, substitutes, buyers, suppliers, rivalry · gap planning = need assessment, current vs desired.

TRAPS: PEST includes legal and environmental · SAP includes weaknesses · analysis is ongoing · QB: world economy into one market = globalisation · competitors' vision, mission, niche = competitive landscape · superior perceived feature = competitive advantage · "sales fall sharply" = decline (not in options).
