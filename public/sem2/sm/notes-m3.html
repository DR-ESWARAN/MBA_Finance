Module 3 · Strategy Analysis

Strategic Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: The Ebook is short: meaning of strategic analysis, 8 applications, environmental scanning (internal vs external environment, SWOT, events/trends/issues/expectations), strategic profile (4 steps) and the 5-step strategic management model. The Study Notes (LMS) add the 5 steps of a strategic market analysis, 7 advantages, environmental appraisal techniques (SWOT, ETOP, Ansoff, QUEST, PEST), Strategic Advantage Profile (SAP) and planning models (alignment, Balanced Scorecard, basic, Blue Ocean, issue-based, Porter's Five Forces, gap planning, PESTEL).

1. Strategic analysis

  • Ebook: a process of researching the business environment within which the enterprise performs, to formulate strategy. Long-term resource investment; systematic and continuous. Includes predicting future events and substitute strategies if plan A fails.

  • Common factors: identify and evaluate relevant data · define external and internal environments · use analytic tools (SWOT, value chain, PESTLE; Study Guide adds Porter's five forces).

  • Study Guide types: internal strategic analysis (look inward; SWOT is the reputed technique) and external.

  • SN — when doing it: focus on high-level strategy · look backwards and forward · involve company leaders. Do it regularly, refresh the market analysis once a year; faster-changing industries more often.

5 steps of a strategic market analysis (SN) Detail
1. Determine the level Divisional, corporate or functional
2. Gather a team Leadership + finance, operations, HR, sales
3. Use analytic methods SWOT or PESTLE; Balanced Scorecard's four perspectives lead the discussion
4. Summarise findings Report = basis of the plan
5. Devise a formal strategy Priorities, goals, strategy map
  • Advantages (SN, 7): sustainability · funding (credibility) · whole-organisation approach · sound goals · external focus · clear expectations · effectiveness.

Misconceptions: Strategic analysis is ongoing, not one-time · involves many departments, not only top management · environmental appraisal covers internal and external factors · SAP covers strengths and weaknesses · PEST(EL) includes legal and environmental factors.

Application Point

2. Environmental scanning and appraisal

  • Ebook: purpose = identify opportunities and threats for strategic decisions; collect information and forecast the impact of changes.

  • Internal environment (value system, mission, structure, culture, employees, unions, technology) → strengths and weaknesses. External environment → opportunities and threats.

  • SN: environmental appraisal = determining the organisation's opportunities and threats; scanning considers both external and internal factors.

SWOT element Ebook meaning Internal / external
Strength Inherent capacity giving strategic advantage Internal
Weakness Inherent constraint creating disadvantage Internal
Opportunity Favourable condition in the environment External
Threat Unfavourable condition causing damage (e.g. new competitor) External
Factor to consider before scanning (Ebook) Meaning
Events Specific occurrences in internal or external sectors
Trends General course of related events; positive or negative
Issues Concerns arising from events and trends; hard to spot early
Expectations Demands of interested groups based on issues

Scanning techniques (SN)

  • SN says there are four scanning methods: PEST, QUEST, ETOP and SWOT; it then also describes the Ansoff Matrix (FAQ lists 5: SWOT, ETOP, Ansoff, QUEST, PEST).
Technique What it is
SWOT Strengths/weaknesses internal; opportunities/threats external; an auditing tool
ETOP (Environmental Threat & Opportunity Profile) Describes environmental information and relative importance of external opportunities and threats
Ansoff Matrix (Product/Market Expansion Grid) 2×2 grid for growth strategies and their risk
QUEST Supports strategy by monitoring change and its effects: observe events/trends → appraise issues → report → planners review feasibility
PEST Political/legal, economic, social/cultural, technological factors
Ansoff Existing products New products
Existing markets Market penetration (more sales of current products) Product development
New markets Market development (current products into new markets) Diversification (new products, new market)
  • QB: "Selling existing products into a new market through new packaging, distribution channels" → Market development.
PESTEL factor (SN) Examples
Political Stability, policy, trade, monetary and tax systems; Consumer Protection Act, competition law
Economic Interest rates, unemployment, inflation, raw material prices, exchange rates, disposable income
Social Family demographics, culture, education, lifestyles
Technological Innovation rate, AI, R&D, automation
Environmental Climate change, waste, carbon footprint, CSR
Legal Employment, consumer, trade, health and safety laws

3. Strategic profile and Strategic Advantage Profile

  • Strategic profile (Ebook): a portrayal of the firm's history, current products/services and plans for the future. Stating an organisational strategy is the first step. SWOT is the useful tool.
Steps to develop a strategic profile (Ebook)
1 Collect company background (history, origin, past sales, growth) and internal/external factors
2 Compose mission and vision statements
3 Write the situation analysis: general, industry and competitive environments
4 Conduct a SWOT analysis (the most important step)

  • Position (SN): a particular instance of a job inside a company (one "chair"); e.g. HR Administrator job.

  • SAP (SN): outline of benefits and drawbacks in particular areas that could affect future operations; requires diagnosis of each functional area (marketing, finance & accounting, production, personnel/HR, R&D). Big firms are financially strong but slower.

SAP approach Idea
1. Compete on current advantages KFS (Key Success Factors); concentrate resources on one important area
2. Avoid direct competition Use relative advantages where rivals do not compete
3. Head-to-head in a static market Innovative strategy; create new success factors

4. Strategic management model

  • Ebook: a firm has competitive advantage if its profitability is higher than the industry average. Strategic management applies to small and large organisations.
5-step model (Ebook / Study Guide) Detail
1. Mission and goals Establishing organisational direction; goals = desired ends, short-term measurable
2. Environmental scanning (image in Ebook; named in Study Guide)
3. Strategy formulation
4. Strategy implementation
5. Strategy evaluation and control Final step: appraise factors, measure performance, corrective action

Strategic planning models (SN)

Model Key point
Alignment model (SAM) Functional integration + strategic alignment; combines IT strategy with business strategy; 4 aspects: strategy execution, technology potential, competitive potential, service level
Balanced Scorecard (BSC) Shows achievements visually; balances financial indicators and strategy; 4 perspectives: financial, customer, internal processes, organisational capacity
Basic (simple) model For new organisations with no planning experience: mission → objectives → divide work → plan, monitor, report
Blue Ocean strategy Market with little or no competition; red ocean = saturated, price pressure
Issue (goal) based Next step after the basic model; for larger companies; most complex and most popular: SWOT → mission → budget, plan, schedule → review after a year
Porter's Five Forces (1979) Threat of new entrants · threat of substitutes · buyer bargaining power · supplier bargaining power · rivalry among existing firms
Gap planning Also "strategic-planning gap", "need-gap analysis", "need assessment"; gap between current and desired state; finds internal deficiencies; "change agenda"/"shift chart"
PEST / PESTEL Used with the external factors of SWOT; L and E added

Wording note: SN prints Porter's first force as "the risk of being accepted" and second as "alternative services and products present a risk". Read these as threat of new entrants and threat of substitutes.

QB key problems: (1) "Which is not an area identified in major core competencies?" Options: competitor differentiation, customer value, application to other markets. Module 1 Ebook lists all three as the core-competence tests, so the key is unclear in the sources. (2) "Stage of the product life cycle where sales and profit fall sharply because a new product replaces it" = decline, but the options are only introduction, growth, maturity. Expect the LMS key to differ. Driving forces, competitive landscape and product life cycle are not explained in the Module 3 sources.
