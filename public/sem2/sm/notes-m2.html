Module 2 · Strategic Management

Strategic Management · Notes

NotesCheat sheetMind map

Read this first: The Ebook covers meaning, 4 phases of development, objectives, the 5-stage framework, 7-point nature, needs, scope (Glueck, Hofer, Sharplin, Porter, Chandler, Harrison & St. John), importance, 21 functions, roles of strategists (two lists), 5 types of strategy (competitive, corporate, business, functional, operating) and limitations. The Study Notes (LMS) cover definition vs operational/tactical management, history (Drucker, Humphrey SWOT, Porter, RBV/Barney), need, scope, 8 key features, importance, decision-making process, then 12 business strategies, strategies at 3 management levels and 7 limitations.

1. Meaning and definitions

  • Ebook: formulation and implementation of the major goals and initiatives taken by top managers on behalf of owners, based on resources and assessment of internal and external environments. Not static; models include a feedback loop.

  • Strategic planning = analytical, formalised procedures producing data (inputs). Strategic thinking = synthesises the data into strategy. Two major processes: formulation and implementation (iterative).

  • "The art and science of formulating, implementing and evaluating cross-functional decisions that enable an organisation to achieve its objectives." Integrates management, marketing, finance, production, R&D and information systems.

  • Strategic management vs strategic planning: used as synonyms; "strategic planning" more in business, "strategic management" more in academia. Strictly, strategic management = formulation + implementation + evaluation; strategic planning = formulation only.

  • Purpose: exploit and create new and different opportunities for tomorrow; long-range planning instead tries to optimise for tomorrow the trends of today.

  • A strategic plan is a company's game plan (football analogy).

  • SN: strategic management = comprehensive planning, execution and evaluation of long-term objectives. Operational management = day-to-day routine; tactical = short-term planning and coordination; strategic = long-term direction.

Phases of development (Ebook)

Phase Focus
1. Basic financial planning Meet the annual budget; operational control
2. Forecast-based planning Effective plans, environmental scanning, resource allocation
3. Externally-oriented planning Response to markets and competition, situational analysis, strategic alternatives
4. Strategic management Create the desired future; orchestrate all resources for competitive advantage; participative climate

History (SN)

Who / what When Contribution
Peter Drucker Mid-20th century Systematic planning; align internal capabilities with external opportunities
SWOT (Albert Humphrey) 1960s Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats
Porter's Five Forces 1970s (SN Module 3: 1979) External forces on competitiveness
Resource-based view (Jay Barney) 1980s Internal resources and capabilities give sustained advantage
Dynamic capabilities 21st century Ability to adapt and innovate
  • Ebook theorists: Glueck (1984) "a stream of decisions and actions which leads to the development of an effective strategy" · Hofer (1984) organisational renewal and growth · Sharplin (1985) · Lawrence & William (1988) · Porter (1985) · Chandler (1962) strategy needs structure · Harrison & St. John (1998) analyse and learn from environments to satisfy stakeholders.

2. By "strategy" managers mean (Ebook)

  • Large-scale, future-oriented plans for interacting with the competitive environment.

  • An integrated set of commitments and actions to exploit core competencies.

  • A game and action plan of how, when and where to compete, against whom and for what purposes.

3. Objectives (Ebook, 16)

  • Exploit and create new opportunities · give conceptual frameworks (actions, context, performance) · put the firm in a competitive position and sustain it · respond to key stakeholders · find, attract and keep customers · use strengths and exploit competitors' weaknesses · understand strategy, policies, plans, programmes · know the environment · determine mission, objectives, strategies · solve real problems · develop analytical ability, strategic decision-making skills and a creative attitude.

4. Framework — 5 stages (Ebook)

Stage Question Activity
1. Planning & analysis Where are we now? (beginning) Situational analysis = SWOT
2. Strategy formulation Where do we want to be? (ends) Goal setting after vision and mission
3. Alternative selection How might we get there? (means) Strategic alternatives
4. Evaluation Which way is the best? Pick the best alternative in line with SWOT
5. Implementation & control How can we ensure arrival? Implement, keep analysing, repeat

Memory hook: Like planning a PCI: Where are we (angiogram) → where do we want to be (TIMI 3 flow) → how might we get there (wires, balloons, stents) → which way is best (strategy for this lesion) → how do we ensure arrival (deploy, check, follow up).

5. Nature of strategic management (Ebook, 7)

Feature Point
Process Framework to perform processes; embodies general management principles
Top management function Distinct from operational management; shift from operational to strategic
General management approach System frame of reference; criteria = organisation as a whole; synergy (may be suboptimal for some units)
Relating organisation to environment Open systems approach
Long-term issues e.g. R&D investment with no immediate effect
Flexibility Plans may be left, postponed or changed
Innovation New things or new ways of working
  • SN key features (8): forward-looking · comprehensive analysis · integration across levels · alignment with mission and vision · continuous adaptation · holistic perspective · long-term focus · risk management.

6. Need, scope and importance

  • Need (Ebook, 3): keep pace with a changing environment · boost employee efficiency (clear role perception) · strong base for unified decision making. SN: uncertainty, competition, dynamic environments, alignment with mission.

  • Scope (SN): strategic analysis, formulation, implementation, evaluation; dimensions = corporate, business-level, functional-level strategy. Ebook: both a process and a philosophy; allows systematic management of change.

  • Importance (Ebook, 7): proactive not reactive · framework for major decisions · pathfinder to opportunities · avoids costly mistakes · builds core competencies · neutralises threats or turns them into opportunities · adds to reputation.

  • SN importance: informed decisions · resource optimisation · competitive advantage · adaptation · goal alignment · performance measurement · communication · risk mitigation.

  • Functions (Ebook, 21, highlights): set long-term goals · adopt courses of action · allocate resources · creates a fit between organisation and environment · pattern in a stream of decisions · identify strengths and weaknesses vs competitors.

7. Role of strategists

Strategist types by role (Ebook)

Type Role
Competitive-advantage officer ("architect") Fact-based analysis of industry shifts; drives M&A, divestitures, JVs
Mobilizer Capability builder, performance challenger, delivers projects; learning organisations; talent-heavy industries
Trend forecaster Scans for trends and shocks; uses big data for the next growth pocket
Surveyor Spots long-range disruptions; knows government and regulatory strategy
Fund manager Reallocates resources, optimises corporate portfolio (FMCG, conglomerates, telecom)

Strategists at management levels (Ebook)

Strategist Role
Board of Directors Supreme authority; elected by shareholders; guide, review, appoint senior executives
CEO Most important strategist; chief architect; link between company and Board
Entrepreneurs Promoters; Drucker: "always searches for change, responds to it and exploits it" (e.g. Kiran Mazumdar Shaw)
Senior management Top committees; MRF used 5 groups (products & markets, environment, technology, resources, manpower)
SBU-level executives Own strategies; Shriram Fibers planned at SBU and corporate level
Corporate planning staff Supporting/administrative role; not responsible for strategic management, does not initiate it
Consultants For firms without planning departments; McKinsey (change, visioning), Anderson (restructuring, IT), BCG (competitive advantage), KPMG (financial, feasibility)
Middle-level managers Vital link; mainly implementers, "sounding boards"
Executive assistant Data collection, alternatives, briefs; "bread and butter" role = corporate planner
  • SN strategist responsibilities: set vision and mission · environmental analysis · formulate strategies · allocate resources · implement · monitor (KPIs) · adapt and renew. Qualities: strategic flexibility, data-driven decisions, communication, risk management.

Misconception: Strategic decisions are not made only by top executives; strategists exist at all levels. Strategic renewal is not a one-time event.

8. Types of strategies

Ebook (5 levels / types)

Type Key points
Competitive Gain competitive advantage against rivals; contrast, low-cost, focus/niche; has a competitor angle
Corporate Top level of a diversified company ("group of companies"); long-term objectives for all units (Bashundhara buying tissue firms)
Business (business-unit) Competitive position of products; Hill and Jones; product development, market development, diversification
Functional (departmental) Production, marketing, HR, finance; supports competitive strategy (low-cost → cost-cutting production)
Operating Field level; on-hand objectives; annual goals

SN — 12 business strategies

Strategy Meaning
Structuralist Study economy and market shifts, then set apart (cheaper if rivals are high-end)
Differentiation Unique features; can charge more
Price-skimming High launch price, then reduce gradually
Acquisition Buy another business (or rights to some products)
Growth Move into new markets, products, offices
Focus Specific niche (pet owners, vegetarians, a region)
Cross-selling Sell more to existing clients (warranty on appliances)
Operational Streamline internal processes and technology
Transformational Drastic change to the organisation
Sustainability Environment-friendly operations and products
Diversification Increase range of profitable activities (phones → laptops)
Retention Keep present customers (cheaper than new ones)
  • Why know strategy types (SN): unify staff · common objectives · efficient processes · continual optimisation · enhance profit. Managing strategies: identify goals → evaluate strategies → actionable steps → monitor continuously (KPIs).

Strategies at management levels (SN)

Level Who Strategy focus
Top Board, Chairman, MD, COO, President, GM Strategic planning, decision making, leadership development; survival of the organisation
Middle Functional department heads, plant superintendent Tactical planning, team building, performance monitoring; link between top and lower
Lower (first-line) Supervisors Operational planning, task execution, problem solving; report to middle management

9. Limitations of strategic management

Ebook (4) SN adds (3)
Complex process (interrelated components; downturn → layoffs → revise objectives) Poor adaptability (paperwork causes inflexibility)
Time consuming (may hurt day-to-day work, turnover) Limited to a set of rules
Difficult to implement (manager in formulation but not implementation may not feel accountable) Lack of short-term benefits
Requires skilful planning (contingency plans)
  • QB: "In a competitive environment it is difficult to clearly estimate ___ about the firm's strategies" → competitive responses.

QB key note: "Which is not a limitation of strategic management?" Options: time consuming / difficult estimation of competitive responses / costly process. Time consuming is in both sources and competitive responses is implied by the next QB item, so the answer that fits is Costly process. "Costly" is not named in the Module 2 sources, so treat it as the likely key.

Not covered in Module 2 sources: QB items on public enterprises diversifying, government vs non-government strategists, and MNC vs TNC are not explained anywhere in the Ebook, Study Notes, Study Guide or Quick Notes. Market development (existing products into new markets) is explained in Module 3 (Ansoff matrix).
