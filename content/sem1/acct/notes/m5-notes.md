# ACCT Module 5 — Final Accounts
**Source:** ACCT Module 5 Ebook + Study Guide | Exam: 15 May 2026

---

## 1. What are Final Accounts?

- Prepared at **end of accounting year** to disclose financial position and performance
- Prepared on the basis of **Trial Balance**
- Three components:
  1. **Trading Account** → Gross Profit or Gross Loss
  2. **Profit & Loss Account** → Net Profit or Net Loss
  3. **Balance Sheet** → Financial Position on a specific date

> **Clinical analogy:** Trading A/c = Gross revenue from procedures. P&L = Net income after overheads. Balance Sheet = Net worth statement — your assets vs liabilities on a given date.

---

## 2. Objectives of Final Accounts

**Primary:**
- Know **profit earned or loss suffered** during the period
- Know **assets and liabilities** of the business on a specific date
- Know the **capital/equity** of the owner

**Additional:**
- Amount due to creditors and from debtors
- Trading profit, operating profit, abnormal gains/losses
- Compare performance with similar businesses
- Ascertain tax liability (Income tax, Sales tax, Wealth tax)
- Calculate ratios for financial analysis
- Aid future policy decisions

---

## 3. Trading Account

- Shows result of **buying and selling of goods** (trading activity)
- Finds difference between **Selling Price** and **Cost Price**
- Result: **Gross Profit** (if Sales > Cost) or **Gross Loss** (if Cost > Sales)

### Debit Side (Dr) — Cost items:
| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Opening Stock** | Stock on hand at start of year |
| **Purchases** | Total cash + credit purchases |
| **Less: Purchase Returns** | Deducted from Purchases → Net Purchases |
| **Direct Expenses (on purchases):** | |
| → Wages (Productive/Manufacturing wages) | |
| → Carriage Inwards | Carriage to bring goods in |
| → Octroi Duty | Duty for bringing goods within municipal limits |
| → Customs duty, dock dues, import duty | |
| → Fuel, Power, Lighting (production-related) | |
| → Packing charges (to make goods saleable) | |

### Credit Side (Cr) — Revenue items:
| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Sales** | Total cash + credit sales |
| **Less: Sales Returns** | Deducted from Sales → Net Sales |
| **Closing Stock** | Stock at end of period — appears **outside Trial Balance** |

### Balancing Trading Account:
- Cr side > Dr side → **Gross Profit** → transferred to **Cr side of P&L A/c**
- Dr side > Cr side → **Gross Loss** → transferred to **Dr side of P&L A/c**

### Advantages of Trading Account:
- Clearly shows result of purchases and sales
- Gross Profit ratio to Sales easily ascertained → helps determine price
- Gross Profit to direct expenses ratio → helps eliminate unnecessary expenses
- Year-on-year comparison → better administrative policies

---

## 4. Profit & Loss Account

- Starts with Gross Profit (from Trading A/c)
- Shows **Net Profit or Net Loss** for the period
- Records **Indirect Expenses** on Debit side

### Debit Side (Dr) — Indirect Expenses (4 Groups):

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Selling Expenses** | Carriage outwards, Travelling expenses, Advertising |
| **Office Expenses** | Office salaries, Rent, Tax, Postage, Stationery |
| **Maintenance Expenses** | Repairs & Renewals, **Depreciation** |
| **Financial Expenses** | Interest on loan, Discount allowed |

### Balancing P&L Account:
- Cr side > Dr side → **Net Profit** → transferred to Capital A/c (increases capital)
- Dr side > Cr side → **Net Loss** → transferred to Capital A/c (reduces capital)

### Key distinction:
- **Direct expenses** → Trading A/c (related to buying/making goods)
- **Indirect expenses** → P&L A/c (related to running the business)

## 5. Balance Sheet

- **Definition:** "A statement which sets out the Assets and Liabilities of a business firm and which serves to ascertain the financial position of the same on any particular date"
- NOT an account — it is a **statement**
- Prepared on a **specific date** (not for a period)

### Structure:
| Left Side | Right Side |
|---|---|
| **Liabilities + Capital** | **Assets** |

**Both sides must always be equal.**

### Balance Sheet Equations:
- Assets = Liabilities + Capital
- Capital = Assets − Liabilities
- Liabilities = Assets − Capital

### Objectives of Balance Sheet:
1. Shows accurate **financial position** of the firm
2. Gist of all transactions at a given date
3. Shows if firm has sufficient assets to **repay liabilities**
4. Verifies **accuracy of final accounts**
5. Shows profit/loss carried from P&L A/c

---

## 6. Difference: Trial Balance vs Balance Sheet *(MCQ Target)*

| Basis | Trial Balance | Balance Sheet |
|---|---|---|
| Accounts shown | All ledger accounts | Personal + Real accounts only |
| When prepared | After Ledger | After Trading A/c + P&L A/c |
| Objective | Check arithmetical accuracy | Reveal financial position |
| Order of items | Not in specific order | Items shown in proper order |
| Stock | **Opening stock** | **Closing stock** |
| Headings | Debit and Credit | Assets and Liabilities |

---

## 7. Adjustments — Golden Rule

> **Golden Rule:** Items appearing in Trial Balance → entered at **one place** (either Trading A/c or P&L or Balance Sheet). Items in **Adjustments** → treated at **two places**.

**Outstanding Expenses** (mentioned in source):
- Expense incurred but not yet paid
- Treatment: Add to relevant expense in P&L A/c (Dr) + Show as liability in Balance Sheet (Cr)

---

## 8. Flow of Final Accounts

```
Trial Balance
     ↓
Trading Account → Gross Profit / Gross Loss
     ↓
Profit & Loss Account → Net Profit / Net Loss
     ↓
Balance Sheet → Financial Position (Assets = Liabilities + Capital)
```

---

## High-Yield Exam Points ⚡

1. **3 parts of Final Accounts:** Trading A/c (GP/GL) → P&L A/c (NP/NL) → Balance Sheet (position)
2. Trading A/c Dr side = Opening stock + Purchases − Purchase Returns + Direct expenses
3. Trading A/c Cr side = Sales − Sales Returns + Closing Stock
4. Closing stock → appears **outside Trial Balance** (adjustment item)
5. **4 groups of indirect expenses in P&L:** Selling | Office | Maintenance | Financial
6. **Balance Sheet = Statement** (not an account); shows position on a **specific date**
7. Balance Sheet: Left = Liabilities + Capital | Right = Assets
8. A = L + C → always balanced
9. **Direct expense** → Trading A/c | **Indirect expense** → P&L A/c
10. Adjustment items → treated at **two places** (Golden Rule)
