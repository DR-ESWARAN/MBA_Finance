# ACCT Module 2 — Primary Book (Journal)
**Source:** ACCT Module 2 Ebook + Study Guide | Exam: 15 May 2026

---

## 1. What is a Journal?

- **Definition (L.C. Cropper):** "A book employed to classify or sort out transactions in a form convenient for their subsequent entries in Ledger"
- **Origin:** French word **"JOUR"** = Day → records day-to-day transactions
- **Also called:** Book of Original Entry / Primary Book of Entry
- First step in the accounting cycle before Ledger

**Key Characteristics:**
- Records transactions **chronologically** (date-wise)
- Each transaction recorded **separately**
- Shows both **debit and credit** sides of every transaction
- Contains **narration** — brief explanation of each entry

---

## 2. Journalising

- **Journalising** = Process of entering/recording transaction data in the Journal
- Today done via software (e.g., **Tally**) — earlier done in physical books

**A Complete Journal Entry has 3 components:**

| Component | Content |
|---|---|
| **Date** | Date, month, year of transaction |
| **Accounts + Amounts** | Which account is Dr, which is Cr, with amounts |
| **Narration** | Brief explanation (starts with "Being...") |

---

## 3. Format of Journal — 5 Columns

| Column | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Date** | Date of transaction (year written once at top; month + date repeated) |
| **Particulars** | Name of Dr account (written first) + Cr account (prefixed "To") + Narration in brackets |
| **L.F. (Ledger Folio)** | Page number in Ledger where this entry is posted (written in red ink) |
| **Debit Amount (Dr)** | Amount opposite the debited account |
| **Credit Amount (Cr)** | Amount opposite the credited account |

**Writing convention in Particulars column:**
- Dr account → written first, with "Dr" at the end near L.F. column
- Cr account → written next line, starting with **"To"**
- Narration → written below in brackets, beginning with **"Being"**

---

## 4. Casting of Journal

- At end of each page: total Debit = total Credit (arithmetical check)
- **Total C/F** (Carried Forward) → written at bottom of page
- **Total B/F** (Brought Forward) → written at top of next page
- **Grand Total** → cast on the last page of the journal

---

## 5. Opening Entry

- **First ever entry** in a new set of books / new financial year
- Records **opening balances** of all assets and liabilities from previous year
- **Assets → Debited** (assets have debit balance)
- **Liabilities + Capital → Credited** (liabilities have credit balance)
- Closing balance of previous year = Opening balance of new year

---

## 6. Types of Journal Entry

| Type | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple Entry** | 1 Debit + 1 Credit | Cash paid for utility bill → Cash A/c Cr, Utility A/c Dr |
| **Compound Entry** | >1 Debit and/or >1 Credit | Purchase building (part cash + part loan) → Building Dr; Cash Cr + Notes Payable Cr |

> **Key distinction:** Compound entry affects more than 2 accounts; Simple entry affects exactly 2.

---

## 7. Advantages of Journal

- **No omission** — every transaction recorded immediately on occurrence
- **Chronological record** — easy to trace when and why a transaction happened
- **Error minimisation** — Dr = Cr for every entry; mismatch is instantly visible
- **Permanent record** — legally valid document
- **Narration clarity** — reason for every entry is documented
- **Basis for Ledger** — posting from Journal → Ledger → Trial Balance → Final Accounts
- **Ledger stays concise** — details in Journal; Ledger stays clean

---

## 8. Debit and Credit

| Aspect | Debit (Dr) | Credit (Cr) |
|---|---|---|
| **Side of Account** | Left side | Right side |
| **Represents** | Receiver of benefit | Giver of benefit |
| **Increases** | Assets, Expenses | Liabilities, Revenue, Equity |
| **Decreases** | Liabilities, Revenue, Equity | Assets, Expenses |

---

## 9. Steps to Determine Dr and Cr for Any Transaction

1. **Identify** the two accounts involved in the transaction
2. **Classify** each account — Personal, Real, or Nominal
3. **Apply** the Golden Rules for each account type

---

## 10. Golden Rules of Debit and Credit *(High-Yield)*

| Account Type | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Personal** (persons — natural, artificial, representative) | The **Receiver** | The **Giver** |
| **Real** (assets — tangible & intangible) | What **Comes In** | What **Goes Out** |
| **Nominal** (expenses, losses, income, gains) | All **Expenses & Losses** | All **Incomes & Gains** |

**Worked Example — Sunil's Transactions (April 2013):**

| Date | Transaction | Account Type | Dr | Cr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apr 1 | Started business with cash ₹25,000 | Real (Cash) + Personal (Capital) | Cash A/c | Capital A/c |
| Apr 3 | Electricity charges ₹900 | Nominal + Real | Electricity A/c | Cash A/c |
| Apr 15 | Purchased goods from Z ₹10,000 | Real + Personal | Purchases A/c | Z A/c |
| Apr 22 | Machinery for cash ₹8,000 | Real + Real | Machinery A/c | Cash A/c |
| Apr 29 | Salary by cheque ₹1,000 | Nominal + Personal | Salary A/c | Bank A/c |

> **Clinical analogy:** Journal is like an ECG strip — every beat (transaction) recorded in real time, in sequence. Ledger is the summary report (echo/cath report). You need the raw strip to verify the summary.

> **Trading analogy:** Journal = Trade log. Every order execution stamped with time, instrument, quantity, price. You don't go straight to P&L — you verify the trade log first.

## High-Yield Exam Points ⚡

1. Journal = **Book of Original Entry** | From French "JOUR" = Day
2. 5 columns: Date | Particulars | L.F. | Dr Amount | Cr Amount
3. Narration starts with **"Being"**; Cr account prefixed with **"To"**
4. **Casting**: Dr total = Cr total at end of each page
5. **Opening entry**: Assets Dr, Liabilities + Capital Cr
6. Simple (2 accounts) vs Compound (>2 accounts)
7. Golden Rules — **3 account types × 2 rules = 6 rules** (memorise the table)
8. Dr increases: Assets + Expenses | Cr increases: Liabilities + Revenue + Equity
