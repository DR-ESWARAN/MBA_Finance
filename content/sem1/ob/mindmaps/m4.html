DY Patil MBA · Organizational Behaviour · Exam: 15 May 2026

1. What is Power?
Max Weber: "Probability that one actor will carry out his own will despite resistance"
Emerson: "Resistance on the part of B that can be overcome by A"
Working def: One individual/group has ability to cause another to take action not otherwise taken
Involves implied force — not necessarily actual force
Power vs Authority vs Leadership
Power = ABILITY to secure compliance (informal authority)
Authority = RIGHT to seek compliance; backed by legitimacy (legitimate power)
Leadership = elicits responses BEYOND required compliance — voluntary, not forced
Headship (rights) ≠ Leadership (inspiration)
2. Types of Power (Etzioni) & Bases of Power (French & Raven)
Etzioni's 3 Types
Coercive — force to comply → Prison orgs
Utilitarian — performance-reward contingency → Business orgs
Normative — belief in org's right to govern behaviour → Religious orgs
French & Raven's 5 Bases → Employee Response
Referent (Charismatic) — personal admiration → Commitment
Expert — knowledge/expertise in narrow domain → Commitment
Legitimate — right to exert power (= authority) → Compliance
Reward — controls valued rewards (pay, promotions) → Compliance
Coercive (Punishment) — based on FEAR; can fire/demote/transfer → Resistance
Power Dependency Factors
Subordinate's values — if B values what A controls, B is more susceptible
Nature of A-B relationship — peers vs superior; permanent vs temporary
Counterpower — B's own sources of power that buffer A's influence (e.g., union)
Clinical analogy: Referent power = senior cardiologist's influence through respect alone, not hierarchy. Expert power = how a specialist commands authority in a narrow domain over senior administrators.
3. Political Behaviour in Organizations
Definitions
Lasswell: Politics = "who gets what, when, and how"
Pfeffer: "Activities to acquire, develop, and use power and resources to obtain preferred outcomes under uncertainty"
Power vs Politics
Power = property of the system at REST (store of potential influence)
Politics = power in ACTION — exercising power to get things accomplished
Political behaviour is NOT inherently bad; can serve org interests
5 Reasons for Political Behaviour
Ambiguous goals — room for personal gain under guise of org goals
Limited resources — scarcity → need to claim one's share
Changing technology & environment — dynamic environment → ambiguity → politics
Non-programmed decisions — ambiguous conditions leave room for manoeuvring
Organizational change — restructuring → factions fight over territory
Strategic Contingencies (Miles)
"Requirement of activities of one subunit affected by activities of other subunits"
Lawrence & Lorsch study: Successful firms' power aligned to strategic contingencies
Container mfg → Sales & Production · Food processing → Sales & Research
Resource Dependence: Controlling scarce resources → power → more resources → more power ("rich get richer")
4. Resolving Conflicts — 5 Methods
MethodHow
Change StructureAlign goals; both report to same exec; evaluate on shared outcomes
Change Team CompositionSeparate conflicting personalities; change physical seating layout
Common Opposing ForceUnite against a common enemy (competitor, recession)
Majority RuleVote; fair process; don't overuse; follow healthy discussion
Problem SolvingFocus on problem not people; find root cause; both sides partially right
Seating research: Antagonists seated ACROSS each other → more conflict. Seated SIDE BY SIDE → less conflict.
5. Conflict-Handling Styles (5)
StyleAssertive?Cooperative?BehaviourBest When
AvoidanceLowLowDeny conflict; postpone decisions; "I don't care"Issue is trivial
AccommodationLowHighGive in; sacrifice personal goals; "Let's do it your way"Others' interest more important
CompromiseMediumMediumEach side sacrifices something; middle groundTime-pressed; moderate stakes
CompetitionHighLowWin regardless of others' feelings; push for own outcomeStrong moral objection; ethical issue
Collaboration ⭐HighHighBoth argue + listen; challenge ideas not people; WIN-WINMost situations; complex issues
No single "right" style — depends on situation. Collaborative has highest potential. Managers prefer forcing; subordinates prefer avoiding/accommodating/compromising.
Clinical analogy: Avoidance = ignoring a cardiac finding hoping it resolves. Collaboration = multidisciplinary team case discussion — best outcome.
6. Negotiation — Stages & Strategies
4 Stages of Negotiation
1. Non-task time — get acquainted; small talk; relationship-building (especially Mexico, S. Korea)
2. Information exchange — background + general info; sets context (very important in Japan)
3. Influence & Persuasion — promises, threats, questions; effectiveness depends on relative power + cultural norms
4. Closing — agreement or failure
USA: signed contract = deal closed ✅
China: signed contract = beginning of final negotiations 🔄
2 Bargaining Strategies
Distributive — Win-Lose: Fixed resources; maximize own share; short-term relationship (e.g., house buyer vs seller)
Integrative — Win-Win: Both parties try creative solutions that benefit both; mutual problem-solving (e.g., bilateral trade negotiations)
7. Org Environment & Structure
Open vs Closed Systems
Open system — responsive/adaptive to environment; Input → Throughput → Output → Environment (feedback loop)
Closed system — less sensitive to environmental resources and possibilities
Example: US Big 3 auto makers (1980s) acted as closed systems → failed to adapt to Japanese competition
4 Building Blocks of Structure
Centralization — degree to which decision-making authority is at higher levels
Formalization — extent to which rules/procedures are written and articulated
Departmentalization — how jobs are grouped (Functional vs Divisional)
Span of Control — reporting relationships defining hierarchy
6 Types of Org Structures
Functional — grouped by function (marketing, finance, HR, IT)
Divisional — grouped by product, customer, or geography
Matrix — functional + divisional combined; dual reporting
Geographic — separate departments per region/country
Networked Team — cross-functional; less formal hierarchy
Virtual — geographically dispersed; technology-connected
