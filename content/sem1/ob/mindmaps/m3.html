1. What is a Group?
Definition
2+ individuals, interacting & interdependent, come together to achieve particular objectives
Members: motivated to join · perceive as unified unit · contribute variably · reach agreements & disagreements
5 Organizational Functions of Groups
Work on complex tasks too large for one person
Generate new ideas / creative solutions
Serve liaison/coordinating functions across workgroups
Facilitate implementation of complex decisions
Train new employees in methods and group norms

4. Skills for Healthy Group Climate
Openness — willing to know others with different backgrounds; listen + elicit ideas
Trust & Self-Disclosure — share ideas/feelings; mutual trust built on honesty + accountability
Support — team loyalty; collaborators not competitors; help struggling members
Respect — "What can we learn?" over "Who is to blame?"
Individual Responsibility & Accountability — agree on who does what; hold self + others accountable
Constructive Feedback — focus on ideas/behaviours not individuals; positive; suggest improvement; receive openly
5. Work Group Effectiveness — Hackman's Model
3 Criteria for Effectiveness
Productive output — meets/exceeds quantitative + qualitative org standards
Personal need satisfaction — membership facilitates employee need satisfaction
Capacity for future cooperation — social processes maintained; destructive processes avoided
3 Intermediate Determinants
Group effort — amount of effort members exert toward task
Knowledge & skill — skills available for group effort
Task performance strategies — appropriate problem-analysis approaches
Influenced by: Environmental context · Design factors · Interpersonal processes
4 Manager Actions
Managerial Awareness — understand group dynamics + motivation
Sensitivity to Norms — norms can facilitate OR inhibit performance
Understand Conformity — balance dependable role performance vs innovative behaviour
Harness Cohesiveness — align cohesive groups with org goals via reward systems
Social Loafing: Tendency for individual members to reduce effort believing others will cover for them.  |  Status Incongruence: Person holds a status position inconsistent with conventional criteria for that position.
6. Multicultural Teams
4 Cultural Differences (Harvard Business Review)
Direct vs Indirect communication — explicit vs question-based; direct = offensive; indirect = passive-aggressive
Accents & Fluency — non-speakers feel excluded; perceived as less competent
Attitudes toward Hierarchy — strict vs egalitarian; clashes over respect and status
Decision-making norms — slow/analytical vs quick/intuitive; frustration with speed differences
4 Interventions (in order of escalation)
Adaptation — work with/around differences; best when members acknowledge gaps
Structural intervention — reorganize to reduce friction; separate cliques
Managerial intervention — management decides; use sparingly
Exit — voluntary/involuntary removal; last resort
Cultural Intelligence = competency enabling effective functioning in cross-cultural environments. Develops with awareness + behavioural adaptation.
7. Managerial Communication & Corporate Reputation
Peter Drucker: "Managers have to learn to know language… respect for language as our most precious gift."
Communication = the work of managers — speaking, writing, listening, critical thinking
Identity = who your org IS  ·  Reputation = who others think your org IS  ·  Confidence = ability to communicate effectively
"Words do matter. Without words we have no way of expressing strategic concepts." — Eccles & Nohria
8. Leadership — Traits, 21st Century Needs & Behavioural Approaches
Trait Approach
4 decades of research → leaders have "right stuff":
Drive · Self-confidence · Honesty · Integrity
Traits = important but only PART of the story
6 Qualities — 21st Century Leaders (Conger)
Strategic opportunists — identify opportunities before competitors
Globally aware — 80% of orgs face foreign competition
Manage decentralized orgs — speed, flexibility, leanness
Sensitive to diversity — diverse workforce's different needs
Interpersonally competent — multicultural awareness
Community builders — develop sense of ownership in org members
Ohio State Studies (Stogdill)
Consideration — relationship-oriented; supportive, friendly, open comms, recognizes people → addresses GROUP MAINTENANCE needs
Initiating Structure — task-oriented; scheduling, directing, planning, standards → addresses GROUP TASK needs
Leaders can display ANY combination of both
University of Michigan Studies
Job-centered — planning, scheduling, coordinating, providing resources = Initiating Structure
Employee-centered — consideration and support = Consideration
Additional behaviours: support, work facilitation, goal emphasis, interaction facilitation
