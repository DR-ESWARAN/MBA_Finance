## 1. What is a Group?

**Definition:** A collection of individuals who interact such that one person's actions have an impact on others; two or more individuals, interacting and interdependent, who come together to achieve particular objectives.

**Comprehensive definition — Group members:**
- Are motivated to join
- Perceive the group as a unified unit
- Contribute in various amounts to group processes
- Reach agreements and have disagreements through interaction

**Functions of Groups (organizational):**
- Work on complex tasks too large for one person
- Generate new ideas / creative solutions
- Serve liaison/coordinating functions across workgroups
- Facilitate implementation of complex decisions
- Train new employees in methods and group norms

## 2. Types of Groups

| Type | Key Feature |
|------|------------|
| **Formal** | Created to achieve specific organizational objectives; management-defined goals and norms; tend to be permanent |
| **Informal** | Based on personal relationships; satisfy psychological/social needs not linked to tasks |
| **Managed** | Under a named manager; members may not work closely together |
| **Process** | Enact a fixed process (e.g., production line); little interaction or highly prescribed |
| **Semi-Formal** | Power distributed across group; collaborative; negotiation over command |
| **Goal** | Work toward shared objective; no fixed instructions; require intelligence + experience |
| **Learning** | Come together to increase knowledge; collaborative or teacher-led |
| **Problem-Solving** | Address issues; shared purpose; different perspectives can cause disagreements |
| **Friendship** | Form because members share common characteristics (age, ethnicity, interests) |
| **Interest** | Affiliate to attain a specific objective; may cut across formal groups |

## 3. 6 Reasons Individuals Join Groups (SEAPIH)

| Reason | Why |
|--------|-----|
| **Security** | Strongest reason — reduces insecurity, fewer self-doubts, more resistant to threats |
| **Esteem** | Gain self-esteem through high-status group membership; recognition and praise from close relationships |
| **Affiliation** | Enjoy regular company of others with common interests |
| **Power** | Collective strength > individual ("united we stand"); informal leaders exercise power over members |
| **Identity** | Group membership helps answer "Who am I?" — others' praise/admiration shapes self-concept |
| **Huddling** | Informal executive get-togethers to deal with emerging matters; cuts red tape; compensates for lack of leadership |

## 4. Skills for a Healthy Group Climate

- **Openness** — willing to know others with different interests; listen + elicit ideas; balance cohesion with individual expression
- **Trust & Self-Disclosure** — share ideas and feelings; mutual trust built on honesty, respect, and accountability
- **Support** — team loyalty; cheer on group + help struggling members; collaborators, not competitors
- **Respect** — communicate opinions respectfully; "What can we learn?" over "Who is to blame?"
- **Individual Responsibility & Accountability** — agree on who does what; hold self and others accountable
- **Constructive Feedback** — focus on ideas/behaviours not individuals; be positive; offer improvement suggestions; receive openly

## 5. Work Group Effectiveness — Hackman's Model

**3 Criteria for Effectiveness:**
1. **Productive output** — meets/exceeds quantitative and qualitative standards of the organization
2. **Personal need satisfaction** — membership facilitates employee need satisfaction
3. **Capacity for future cooperation** — social processes maintained/enhanced; destructive processes avoided for long-term cohesiveness

---

## 6. Determinants of Work-Group Effectiveness (3 Intermediate Criteria)

| Factor | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **Group effort** | Amount of effort members exert toward task accomplishment |
| **Group knowledge & skill** | Knowledge and skills available for group effort |
| **Task performance strategies** | How the group analyses and attempts to solve problems — appropriate strategies |

- Relative importance varies with **work technology** (routine jobs → effort > skills; complex R&D → skills > effort alone)
- These 3 factors are themselves influenced by: **Environmental context** (reward system, training), **Design factors** (structure, member composition, norms), **Interpersonal processes** (conflict reduction, commitment, knowledge sharing)

## 7. Implications for Group Management (4 Manager Actions)

1. **Increased Managerial Awareness** — understand why people join groups; recognize motivational implications of group dynamics; distinguish healthy vs alienated cohesiveness
2. **Sensitivity to Group Norms** — norms can facilitate OR inhibit performance; company actions can shape norms; use process consultation to develop compatible norms
3. **Understanding Conformity Pressures** — groups punish deviants (ostracism); balance dependable role performance vs innovative spontaneous behaviour
4. **Harnessing Group Cohesiveness** — show how members benefit from working together toward org goals; use reward systems to align cohesive groups with goals

> **Social Loafing:** Tendency for individual group members to reduce effort in the belief others will cover for them.
>
> **Status Incongruence:** One person holds a status position inconsistent with conventional criteria for that position.

## 8. Multicultural Teams — 4 Cultural Differences (Harvard Business Review)

| Difference | Description | Risk |
|-----------|-------------|------|
| **Direct vs Indirect communication** | Some cultures explicit; others indirect + ask questions | Direct = offensive; Indirect = passive-aggressive |
| **Accents & Fluency** | Dominant language leaves non-speakers feeling excluded | Perceived as less competent |
| **Attitudes toward Hierarchy** | Some cultures hierarchy-strict; others egalitarian | Clashes over respect and status |
| **Decision-making norms** | Some cultures slow + analytical; others quick + intuitive | Frustration with speed differences |

**4 Intervention Techniques:**
1. **Adaptation** — work with/around differences; best when members acknowledge cultural gaps
2. **Structural intervention** — reorganize to reduce friction; separate unproductive subgroups
3. **Managerial intervention** — management decides without team; use sparingly
4. **Exit** — voluntary or involuntary removal; last resort

**Cultural Intelligence** = competency enabling individuals to function effectively in cross-cultural environments. Develops with awareness and behavioral adaptation.

## 9. Managerial Communication & Corporate Reputation

- Management communication = movement of information via speaking, writing, listening, critical thinking
- Peter Drucker: "Managers have to learn to know language... respect for language as our most precious gift."
- Communication determines whether companies succeed/fail and whether they are rewarded/penalized for their reputations
- 3 dimensions: **Identity** (who your org IS), **Reputation** (who others think your org IS), **Confidence** (ability to communicate effectively)
- "Words do matter. Without words we have no way of expressing strategic concepts." — Eccles & Nohria

## 10. Leadership Needs in the 21st Century (Conger)

**2 Forces defining leadership needs:**
1. **External environment** — global competitiveness
2. **Growing internal diversity** — changes relationships between org members, work, and organization

**6 Qualities of Effective 21st Century Leaders (Conger):**
1. **Strategic opportunists** — identify strategic opportunities before competitors (organizational visionaries)
2. **Globally aware** — 80% of orgs face foreign competition; knowledge of global markets crucial
3. **Manage decentralized organizations** — high-involvement organizations need speed, flexibility, learning
4. **Sensitive to diversity** — diverse workforce brings different needs and concerns
5. **Interpersonally competent** — highly aware of multicultural expectations
6. **Community builders** — help build org community where members develop sense of ownership

## 11. Approaches to Leadership

**Trait Approach:**
- 4 decades of research → leaders have "right stuff": **drive, self-confidence, honesty, integrity**
- Traits are important but only PART of the story

**Behavioural Approaches:**

### Ohio State University Studies (Stogdill)
Two major leader behaviours identified:

| Behaviour | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Consideration** | Relationship-oriented; supportive, friendly, open communication, recognizes people, respects ideas, shows concern for feelings — addresses GROUP MAINTENANCE needs |
| **Initiating Structure** | Task-oriented; scheduling work, deciding what/how/when, planning, coordinating, problem-solving, maintaining performance standards — addresses GROUP TASK needs |

- Initially thought to be either/or; later shown leaders CAN display any combination of both
- Effects are NOT consistent situation to situation

### University of Michigan Studies
Two similar dimensions:

| Behaviour | Equivalent |
|-----------|------------|
| **Job-centered** | Supervisory functions, planning, scheduling, coordinating, providing resources = same as Initiating Structure |
| **Employee-centered** | Consideration and support = same as Consideration |

- Additional behaviours: **support, work facilitation, goal emphasis, interaction facilitation**
