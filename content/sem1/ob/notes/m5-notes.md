## 1. Human Resource Management & Compliance

**HR Compliance** = area tracing back to the origin of HR function — administrative and regulatory responsibilities.

- HR must navigate laws to keep org compliant and avoid fines, penalties, and reputational harm
- Best practices: **Education & Training, Documentation, Audit**

### 6 Key US Employment Laws (High-Yield)

| Law | Year | Key Protection |
|-----|------|---------------|
| **ADEA** — Age Discrimination in Employment Act | 1967 | Protects individuals ≥40 years from age-based employment discrimination; covers hiring, firing, promotion, compensation, training |
| **ADA** — Americans with Disabilities Act | 1990 | Prohibits discrimination against qualified individuals with disabilities; employer must make **reasonable accommodation** unless it causes "undue hardship" |
| **FLSA** — Fair Labour Standards Act | 1938 | Establishes **minimum wage, overtime pay, recordkeeping, youth employment standards** for public + private sector workers |
| **FMLA** — Family and Medical Leave Act | 1993 | Up to **12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave** per 12-month period for family/medical reasons; applies to employers with 50+ employees |
| **NLRA** — National Labour Relations Act | 1947 | Rights to **organize and bargain collectively**; protects employees from employer and union misconduct |
| **WARN** — Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act | 1988 | Requires **60-day advance notice** of plant closings or mass layoffs; covers employers with 100+ employees |

**ADA defines disability as:** (1) physical/mental impairment that substantially limits a major life activity, OR (2) record of such impairment, OR (3) regarded as having such impairment.

**FMLA applies to:** All public agencies + private employers with 50+ employees in 20+ workweeks in current or preceding calendar year.

**HR Best Practices for Compliance:**
- **Education & Training** — ongoing; laws change yearly; both HR professionals AND managers need training
- **Documentation** — maintain accurate records
- **Audit** — periodic review of compliance practices

## 2. Coping with Work-Related Stress

### When is Stress Too Much?
- **Good stress:** Keeps you focused, energetic, alert — helps meet challenges
- **Bad stress:** Exceeds coping ability → damages mind, body, job satisfaction

### Common Causes of Workplace Stress:
- Fear of being laid off
- More overtime due to staff cutbacks
- Pressure to perform with no increase in job satisfaction
- Pressure to work at optimum levels all the time
- Lack of control over how you do your work

### Stress Warning Signs:
- Feeling anxious, irritable, or depressed
- Apathy, loss of interest in work
- Problems sleeping; fatigue
- Trouble concentrating; muscle tension/headaches
- Stomach problems; social withdrawal; loss of sex drive
- Using alcohol or drugs to cope

### 5 Tips for Managing Work Stress

| Tip | Action |
|-----|--------|
| **1. Reach out** | Share stress with someone; co-worker support; strong friends & family network |
| **2. Exercise & Nutrition** | 30 min aerobic exercise; small frequent healthy meals; minimize sugar/refined carbs; avoid nicotine; moderate alcohol; eat Omega-3s |
| **3. Don't skimp on sleep** | 8 hours; consistent sleep schedule; screens off 1 hour before bed; avoid stimulating activity before sleep |
| **4. Prioritize & Organize** | Balanced schedule; leave earlier; regular breaks; healthy boundaries (no work emails at home); don't over-commit |
| **5. Break bad habits** | Resist perfectionism; flip negative thinking; don't try to control the uncontrollable; use humour; clean/organize your workspace |

> **Clinical analogy:** Stress and cardiac output — optimal stress improves performance (like a well-loaded ventricle), but excessive stress causes decompensation (like heart failure). The Starling curve applies to human performance too.

## 3. Overview of Entrepreneurship

### Definitions by Thinker

| Thinker | Era | Definition |
|---------|-----|-----------|
| **French origin** | 17th century | "Undertakes" a specific project or activity |
| **Jean Baptiste Say** | 19th century | Individuals who **create value by shifting resources from lower- to higher-valued activities** |
| **Joseph Schumpeter** | 20th century | Entrepreneurs are innovators driving **"creative destruction"** — reforming or revolutionizing patterns of production |
| **Peter Drucker** | 21st century | Entrepreneurs always **search for change, respond to it, and exploit it as an opportunity** |

### 7 Elements of Entrepreneurship (Combined View)
1. Recognizing change
2. Pursuing opportunity
3. Taking on risk and responsibility
4. Innovating
5. Making better (higher value) use of resources
6. Creating new value meaningful to customers
7. Doing it all over again and again

> "Entrepreneurship is an attitude and drive to pursue opportunity and create something new and of value."

---

## 4. Entrepreneurial Opportunities

- **Sources of opportunity:** Changes in knowledge, new technology, shifting demographics, political change, changing attitudes/norms
- Entrepreneur must: **Recognize opportunity → Innovate solution → Investigate economic value → Research market**
- True business opportunity = revenues cover costs + promise net revenue above operating costs after start-up investment repaid

**Entrepreneurial Resources required:**
- Capital (financial)
- Talent and know-how (accounting, finance, operations, management, legal)
- Equipment and facilities
- The entrepreneur themselves — most critical factor: ability to identify opportunity, develop creative response, sell, organize, attract investors

**Entrepreneurs must have:** Passion, drive, excitement, and unique capabilities.

---

## 5. Why Do Entrepreneurs Do It?

- **Only consistent predictor:** One or both parents were entrepreneurs
- **Other motivations:**
  - Pursue a passion or deep interest
  - Opportunity to create something new
  - Enhance personal reputation; make an impact
  - Desire for independence — be your own boss
  - Flexible work schedule (lifestyle motivation)
  - Financial rewards

---

## 6. Intrapreneurship

**Definition:** Applying the entrepreneurial mindset — innovation, risk-taking, flexibility — within an **existing organization**.

- Distinguishes from entrepreneurship (starting new business)
- Objective: Enhance established firm's ability to react to market opportunities quickly
- Example: **GE** encourages intrapreneurship to foster innovation, accelerate new product development
- Social entrepreneurship = entrepreneurship focused on value to society

---

## 7. Entrepreneurial Risk, Resilience & Persistence

- Most business start-ups fail before 4 years (US Small Business Administration data)
- Requires broad competence: finance, accounting, strategy, marketing, management, operations, interpersonal skills
- **Internal failure factors:** Limited funding, poor planning/decision-making, bad business idea
- **External failure factors:** Weak economy, changing public policies, market implications
- **Key mindset:** Resilience + persistence — Steve Jobs was removed from Apple before returning to transform it

> First and most important reason to write a business plan: **Test whether an idea is truly an economically promising market opportunity.**

## 8. Optimal Support for Entrepreneurship — Government's Role

### A. Reducing Barriers (Less Discouragement)

| Barrier | How Government Can Help |
|---------|------------------------|
| **Regulations** | Clear, stable rules; reduce unnecessary restrictions; protect intellectual property; enforce property rights |
| **Outdated laws** | Laws lag behind technology (Skype vs FWD example) |
| **Taxes** | Lower taxes → more willingness to risk capital; progressive tax penalizes success; tax exemptions for non-profits |
| **Cultural barriers** | Outlaw discrimination (sex, race, religion); education campaigns; subsidize childcare |
| **Trade barriers** | Tariffs protect incumbents, disadvantage new entrants |

**Skype vs FWD example:** FWD waited 18 months for regulatory clearance in the US; Skype (abroad, free from this) built its user base. FWD went out of business. Government red tape killed competition.

### B. Increasing Positive Incentives (More Encouragement)

- **Technology transfer:** Give government-produced technology to private sector for commercialization
- **Financing:** Government loans, subsidized lending/borrowing; lower transaction costs for funding
- **Venture capital:** Government provides capital where private sector won't risk (risk: props up lower-quality businesses)
- **Training programs:** Formal entrepreneurship courses; business incubators; informal networking events
- **Long-term pipeline:** Financial literacy education; comfort with risk-taking; entrepreneurship culture starting from elementary school
- **Social entrepreneurship support:** Market value of small businesses; moral support for volunteerism; chambers of commerce

> **Risk of government support:** Cronyism — favoring particular industries/regions not best-positioned economically; propping up low-quality ventures.
