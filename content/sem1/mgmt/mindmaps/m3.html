Social Responsibility — For vs Against ⭐

✅ FOR (10 arguments)
Business is part of society
Long-term self-interest of business
Moral justification (take → give back)
Better public image → attracts stakeholders
Avoidance of govt. regulation
Maintenance of society
Balance of power & responsibility
New profit avenues (conservation, recycling)
Best use of business resources
Prevention better than cure
❌ AGAINST (9 arguments)
Contrary to basic function (Milton Friedman)
Conflict with profit motive
Distortion of resource allocation
Imposition of business values
System inefficiency
Operational problems — ambiguous
Excessive costs → higher prices for consumers
Lack of accountability to society
Business lacks skills for social problems
Verdict: Economic & social concerns are NOT opposites — broad overlap area exists. Business DOES have SR; the key is identifying it clearly.

6 Social Stakeholders ⭐

📈 Shareholders
Capital providers; expect good ROI; timely information about functioning
👷 Employees
Biggest asset; income, benefits, health & safety protection mandatory
🛍️ Customers
Product/service quality & value; consumerism growing; keep customers happy
🏭 Creditors & Suppliers
Provide raw materials & capital; management must fulfil obligations to them
🌍 Society
Orgs draw resources from society → must preserve & enhance member well-being
🏛️ Government
Taxes + GDP; provides basic infrastructure; monitors & controls business systems
Jack Ma's priority: Customers → Employees → Investors

Managerial Ethics

2 Types
⚖️ Legal Ethics
Based on laws & regulations. Fair hiring, safe workplace, fair pay, OSHA compliance. Minimum that must be met.
💡 Moral Ethics
Beyond legal minimum. E.g. paternity leave not required by law but offered ethically. How complaints are handled.
5 Factors Affecting Ethics
Stage of moral developmentIndividual characteristics Structural variables (rules)Organisational culture Intensity of the issue
Ethical Guidelines (8)

Importance: People follow what leaders do. Ethics vacuum at top → ↓ morale → ↑ turnover → ↑ HR costs → ↓ customer loyalty.

Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development ⭐ 3 Levels, 6 Stages

Level 1 — Pre-Conventional (External Control)
Stage 1
Obedience-Punishment
Avoid punishment. Wrongness = severity of punishment. Obey rules to escape consequences.
Stage 2
Instrumental Orientation
"What's in it for me?" Self-interest. Limited concern for others — only if it benefits self. Scratch my back / I'll scratch yours.
Level 2 — Conventional (Social Relationships)
Stage 3
Good Boy / Nice Girl
Seek approval of others; avoid disapproval. Be "nice." Emphasis on good behaviour.
Stage 4
Law & Order
Blindly follows rules to maintain social order. Duty to uphold law. MOST active members of society remain here.
Level 3 — Post-Conventional (Abstract Principles)
Stage 5
Social Contract
Laws = social contracts; can be changed if they don't serve the greater good. Basis of democratic government.
Stage 6
Universal Ethical Principles
Abstract principles — equality, dignity, justice. May disobey unjust laws. Acts morally because it is right. Rare to achieve.
Criticism: Carol Gilligan — biased toward male/Western perspective. Women focus more on maintaining relationships, not just justice. Also: culturally biased; doesn't account for inconsistency in moral judgements.
