## 1. Social Responsibility — Definition

- **Social responsibility** = obligation of a business to enhance the condition of society along with its own interests
- Firms are accountable to **6 major stakeholder groups**: Shareholders, Employees, Customers, Creditors & Suppliers, Society, Government
- **Social responsiveness** = ability of a firm to implement policies and take part in activities that benefit both society and the firm

---

## 2. Arguments FOR Social Responsibility ⭐

| # | Argument | Key Point |
|---|----------|-----------|
| 1 | Business is part of society | Subsystem must benefit the whole system |
| 2 | Long-term self-interest | Existence depends on satisfying society's stakeholders |
| 3 | Moral justification | Taking from society → must give back (equity principle) |
| 4 | Better public image | Social involvement → attracts customers, employees, investors |
| 5 | Avoidance of govt. regulation | Socially responsible firms attract less regulatory attention |
| 6 | Maintenance of society | Legal provisions can't keep pace with change; business must fill gap |
| 7 | Balance of power & responsibility | Business has power → must have corresponding responsibility |
| 8 | New profit avenues | Conservation & recycling can generate profit |
| 9 | Best use of business resources | Optimum use of managerial skills + capital for social good |
| 10 | Prevention is better than cure | Businesses can be sources of employment → prevent social unrest |

---

## 3. Arguments AGAINST Social Responsibility

| # | Argument | Key Point |
|---|----------|-----------|
| 1 | Contrary to basic function | Adds cost → raises price → reduces business viability (Milton Friedman) |
| 2 | Conflict with profit motive | Social responsibility ↓ profit margin |
| 3 | Distortion of resource allocation | Social costs divert resources from optimal use |
| 4 | Imposition of business values | Business imposes its values on society → undesirable |
| 5 | System inefficiency | Replaces self-interest (profit motive) → reduces efficiency |
| 6 | Operational problems | Managers unclear on what "social responsibility" means in practice |
| 7 | Excessive costs | Cost passed to consumers as higher prices |
| 8 | Lack of accountability | No mechanism to hold businesses accountable to society |
| 9 | Lack of skills | Business people lack skills to handle social problems |

> **Verdict:** Economic and social concerns are NOT opposites — there is a broad overlap area where both are consistent. Business DOES have social responsibility; the key is identifying it clearly.

## 4. Social Stakeholders — 6 Groups ⭐

| Stakeholder | Stake / Interest |
|-------------|-----------------|
| **Shareholders** | Capital providers; expect good return on investment; need timely information |
| **Employees** | Biggest asset; income, benefits, health & safety |
| **Customers** | Product/service quality and value; consumerism growing |
| **Creditors & Suppliers** | Revenue generation; raw materials & capital inputs |
| **Society** | Health, safety, economic development; firms draw from society → must give back |
| **Government** | Taxes (corporate, payroll, sales), GDP contribution; provides basic infrastructure for business |

> Jack Ma's priority order: Customers → Employees → Investors

## 5. Social Responsiveness

**Elements (5):**
1. Social goals should be incorporated into the overall planning process
2. Firms should assess how other companies are meeting social desires
3. Shareholders must be informed; social goals disclosed in annual reports
4. Managers must cost-benefit-analyse social programmes
5. Managers compute cost of social programmes for viability assessment

**Measurement categories:**
- Contributions | Fund-raising | Volunteerism | Recycling | Valuing Diversity | Direct Corporate Investment | Quality of Work Life | Attention to Consumers | Pollution Control

## 6. Social Audit ⭐

**Definition:** "A systematic study and evaluation of the social, rather than the economic, performance of an organisation"

**Types:** (1) Government-required audits (pollution control, product performance, equal employment) | (2) Voluntary audits

**Objectives (5):**
1. Assess gaps between needs and available resources
2. Create stakeholder awareness of company's services
3. Increase efficiency of corporate development programmes
4. Assess corporate policy decisions in stakeholders' interest
5. Estimate opportunity cost to stakeholders of not accessing public services

**Steps (5):**
1. Framing social programmes
2. Monitoring activities
3. Measuring social performance
4. Evaluating socially responsive activities
5. Framing strategies for future goals

**Merits:** Identifies achievement of social goals | Cost-benefit data | Locates productive areas for social programmes

**Problems:** Attitude of auditor | Poor programme design | Inadequate information | Time lag in impact assessment

## 7. Managerial Ethics

**Definition:** Set of moral principles or beliefs that affect the behaviour of employees

**Two Types:**
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Legal Ethics** | Based on laws & regulations — fair hiring, safe workplace, fair pay, OSHA compliance |
| **Moral Ethics** | Beyond legal minimum — e.g., paternity leave (not legally required but ethically offered); how complaints are handled |

**Importance:** People follow what leaders do. When top leaders ignore ethics → morale drops → turnover ↑ → HR costs ↑ → customer loyalty ↓

**5 Factors Affecting Managerial Ethics:**
1. Stage of moral development (individual's judgement of right/wrong)
2. Individual characteristics
3. Structural variables (formal rules & regulations)
4. Organisational culture
5. Intensity of the ethical issue

---

## 8. Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development ⭐

| Level | Stage | Key Idea |
|-------|-------|----------|
| **Level 1: Pre-conventional** | Stage 1: Obedience-Punishment | Avoid punishment; wrongness = severity of punishment |
| | Stage 2: Instrumental Orientation | "What's in it for me?" — self-interest; limited concern for others |
| **Level 2: Conventional** | Stage 3: Good Boy / Nice Girl | Seek approval; behave to avoid disapproval |
| | Stage 4: Law & Order | Follow rules because they maintain social order; duty to uphold law |
| **Level 3: Post-conventional** | Stage 5: Social Contract | Laws = social contracts; should be changed if they don't serve the greater good; basis of democratic government |
| | Stage 6: Universal Ethical Principles | Abstract moral principles (equality, dignity, justice); may disobey unjust laws |

> Most active members of society remain at **Stage 4**. Stage 6 is rare.
> **Criticism of Kohlberg:** Biased toward Western/male perspective (Carol Gilligan); ignores other values beyond justice; doesn't account for inconsistency in moral judgements.

## 9. Ethical Guidelines for Managers (8)

1. **Recognise ethics is a process** — not just deliverables; reflection & dialogue matter
2. **Bottom line = preferred behaviours** — values must translate to fair actions
3. **Best way is prevention** — codes of ethics minimise unethical behaviour
4. **Make ethics decisions in groups** — diverse perspectives, better quality, more credibility
5. **Integrate ethics with management practices** — embed in strategic planning & HR policies
6. **Use cross-functional teams** — employees feel ownership → better adherence
7. **Value forgiveness** — support people in correcting mistakes; trying is what counts
8. **Trying ethically & making mistakes > not trying at all**
