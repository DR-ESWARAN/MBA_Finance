## 1. Definition & Significance

- **Decision** = an act requiring judgement that is translated into action
- **Decision-making** = selecting a course of action from among alternatives to deal with a specific problem
- *"The quality of managers' decisions is the yardstick of their effectiveness and value to the organisation"*
- Managers are evaluated and rewarded based on the importance and results of their decisions

**4 Characteristics of Managerial Decisions:**
1. Oriented toward long-range organisational objectives
2. Best choice from a set of alternatives
3. Involves organisational change
4. Requires commitment of scarce resources

> **Clinical analogy:** Like deciding between stenting vs. CABG in complex CAD — multiple alternatives, incomplete information, time constraints, irreversible commitment of resources.

## 2. Rational vs Non-Rational Decision Making ⭐

| Dimension | **Rational Model** | **Non-Rational Model** |
|-----------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Nature | Prescriptive; ideal | Descriptive; realistic/practical |
| Approach | Deterministic | Probabilistic |
| Information | Complete, perfect | Incomplete, limited |
| Goal | Optimise — find best solution | Satisfice — find "good enough" solution |
| Basis | Logic, objectivity, facts | Judgment, intuition, personal bias |
| Outcome | Optimal decision | Satisfactory decision |

### Rational Decision Making — Key Features:
- Follows structured, step-by-step process
- Seeks single best/optimal outcome
- Objective and unbiased; based on facts
- Considers future consequences of each alternative
- All rational decision makers → same conclusion given same information

### Limitations of Rational Model:
1. Humans can't gather/process ALL information → impractical
2. People rarely have perfect information
3. Limited cognitive ability and time
4. People often "satisfice" rather than optimise
5. Ignores emotional factors, ethics, loyalty, altruism
6. Risk-avoiding tendency can disrupt rational process

### Non-Rational Models — Key Thinkers:
- **Herbert Simon** — Maximisers vs Satisficers: Maximisers try to optimise (take longer, regret more); Satisficers simplify choices and accept "good enough"
- **Gerd Gigerenzer** — Simple heuristics (experience-based shortcuts) often lead to better outcomes than exhaustive optimization

## 3. Decision-Making Process — 8 Steps ⭐

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| **1** | **Identify purpose** — Define the problem, who is affected, deadline |
| **2** | **Gather information** — Collect relevant data from internal & external sources |
| **3** | **Set criteria** — Define baseline principles for evaluating alternatives (e.g. profit, culture) |
| **4** | **Brainstorm alternatives** — Use Cause-Effect diagrams, Pareto charts to identify root causes, then generate solutions |
| **5** | **Evaluate alternatives** — Compare each for positives & negatives using criteria |
| **6** | **Select best alternative** — Choose the optimal or most acceptable option |
| **7** | **Execute the decision** — Convert to a plan or sequence of activities |
| **8** | **Evaluate results** — Assess outcome; learn and correct for future decisions |

## 4. Types of Decision-Making by Conditions ⭐

| Condition | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Certainty** | All alternatives, conditions, outcomes known; info accurate & reliable | Routine/repetitive day-to-day operations |
| **Risk** | Incomplete info BUT good idea of probability of each outcome | Launching a product with market data |
| **Uncertainty** | Future unpredictable; alternatives, risks & consequences unknown | Most significant strategic decisions |

---

## 5. Modern Approaches to Decision-Making Under Uncertainty ⭐

| Approach | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Risk Analysis** | Quantitative + qualitative assessment of size & nature of risk; trade-off between risk and benefit |
| **Decision Trees** | Graphic representation of alternative actions, chance events & probabilities; decision-maker traces optimum path. Square = decision point; Circle = chance event |
| **Preference / Utility Theory** | Individual attitudes toward risk vary — "risk averters" vs "gamblers"; top managers take largest risk but not necessarily with own money |

## 6. MIS vs DSS

| | **MIS** (Management Information System) | **DSS** (Decision Support System) |
|-|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Function | Converts raw data → useful information for managers | Helps identify problems & finalise decisions |
| Tools | Computer-based; reporting, coordination, control | Communication tech, data, documents |
| Output | Structured reports for routine decisions | Supports semi-structured & unstructured decisions |

## 7. Group Decision Making ⭐

**Definition:** Individuals collectively make a choice — no single person is accountable for the final decision.

### Advantages (8):
1. More diversity of opinions
2. Greater participation & interest
3. More understanding & positivity in team
4. Collective contributions; less individual bias
5. Team-building opportunity
6. Promotes collective thinking
7. Increases organisational strength & resilience
8. Encourages smart risk-taking (risk spread across group)

### Disadvantages (9):
1. Takes significant time
2. Irrelevant opinions & ideas possible
3. Some members stay silent (social pressure)
4. Group may have different priorities than the decision requires
5. Final choice may go against organisational goals
6. Reduces individual accountability
7. Overuse of authority by one dominant member
8. Everyone may see themselves as a leader → confusion
9. "Group think" — going with the flow to avoid conflict

---

## 8. Types of Group Decision-Making ⭐

| Type | Method | Key Feature |
|------|--------|-------------|
| **Interacting Groups** | Members meet face-to-face; discuss, argue, agree | Most common; ↑ ideas but political factors can influence; limits creativity |
| **Delphi Groups** | No face-to-face; questionnaires sent → summarised → repeated rounds until consensus | Used for forecasting; demand, market trends, future problems |
| **Nominal Groups** | Members work independently in silence; write ideas; ideas collected, discussed, voted & ranked | ↑ individual creativity; avoids personality domination |

## 9. Decision-Making Techniques

### Standard Financial/Management Tools:

| Technique | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| **Marginal Analysis** | Extra output from adding one more unit of input (Paul Samuelson) — evaluating alternatives |
| **Financial Analysis** | Estimate profitability, payback period, discounted cash flows |
| **Break-Even Analysis** | Level of sales to cover all fixed costs; at BEP → Total Revenue = Total Cost; Profit = 0 |
| **Ratio Analysis** | Interpret financial statements; define relationship between two variables; assess strengths & weaknesses |

### Operations Research (OR) Techniques:

| Technique | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| **Linear Programming** | Optimal allocation of scarce resources; all relationships must be linear |
| **Waiting-Line (Queuing)** | Balance service provided vs. waiting time; optimal customer service |
| **Gaming Theory** | Competitive strategy selection; rational strategies against opponents |
| **Simulation** | Build model of real system; computer-based; test variables & interrelationships |
| **Decision Tree** | Graphic tool; squares (decision points) + circles (chance events) → trace optimum path |
