## 1. What is Statistics?

- **Plural sense:** Set of numerical figures/data (e.g., sales figures over 10 years)
- **Singular sense:** Scientific method for collection, classification, analysis, and interpretation of numerical data
- R.A. Fisher's book *"Statistical Methods for Research Workers"* (1925) marks the beginning of modern statistics

> **Clinical analogy:** Statistics is to data what an ECG report is to cardiac function — raw numbers become meaningful only when analysed systematically.

## 2. Definitions (Key Authors)

| Author | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| A.L. Bowley | "Numerical facts placed in relation to each other" |
| Yule & Kendall | "Quantitative data affected by multiplicity of causes" |
| Webster | "Classified facts respecting conditions of people, stated in numbers" |

---

## 3. Characteristics of Statistics (6 Points)

1. **Numerical Figures** — must be quantifiable (measurable or countable)
2. **Aggregate of Facts** — single figure ≠ statistics; must be a related set
3. **Affected by Multiplicity of Factors** — data influenced by many simultaneous causes
4. **Enumerated or Estimated with Reasonable Accuracy** — collected by counting or measurement
5. **Collected Systematically for a Predetermined Purpose** — haphazard data → wrong conclusions
6. **Capable of Being Placed in Relation to Each Other** — must be homogeneous and comparable

---

## 4. Statistics as a Scientific Method

### Theoretical Statistics
| Branch | What it Does |
|--------|-------------|
| **Descriptive** | Collection, classification, tabulation, diagrams, averages, dispersion, correlation |
| **Inductive** | Makes generalisations about population from a sample; includes forecasting |
| **Inferential** | Tests hypotheses regarding population characteristics |

### Applied Statistics
- Application of statistical methods to practical problems
- Examples: sample survey design, quality control, business decision-making

### Statistics = Science + Art
- **Science** — systematic, general application
- **Art** — successful use depends on skill and experience of the statistician

## 5. Importance of Statistics

| Area | Key Application |
|------|----------------|
| **State/Government** | Military strength, welfare policy, administration |
| **Economics** | Demand-supply laws, econometrics, price indices |
| **National Income Accounting** | GDP, savings, investment estimation |
| **Planning** | Resource assessment, growth rate feasibility |
| **Business** | Location decisions, production planning, inventory control, quality control, auditing, banking, insurance |

> **Trading analogy:** Just as a trader uses volume + price data to make buy/sell decisions, governments use statistical data to make policy decisions.

**Business applications (6 sub-points):**
1. Location & size decisions
2. Production planning (demand forecasting)
3. Inventory control (optimal stock levels)
4. Quality control (control charts, UCL/LCL)
5. Accounts writing and auditing (sampling techniques)
6. Banks & Insurance (mortality tables, cash flow estimation)

---

## 6. Scope & Functions of Statistics (9 Functions)

1. Present facts in numerical figures
2. Simplify complex data
3. Study relationships between phenomena (correlation)
4. Provide comparison techniques (mean, variance, ratios)
5. Enlarge individual experience
6. Help in policy formulation
7. Help in forecasting (time series, regression)
8. Test hypotheses
9. Decision-making under uncertainty

---

## 7. Limitations of Statistics (7 Points)

| # | Limitation | Key Point |
|---|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | Deals with numerical facts only | Qualitative traits (beauty, honesty) cannot be directly measured |
| 2 | Deals with groups, not individuals | Individual exceptions are ignored |
| 3 | Results true only on average | Average wage ≠ every worker's wage |
| 4 | Results only approximately true | Sample-based → approximate; not exact |
| 5 | Only one set of methods | Must be supplemented by other analyses |
| 6 | Liable to be misused | Biased collection → misleading conclusions |
| 7 | Must be used by experts only | "Most dangerous tools in hands of inexpert" — Yule & Kendall |
