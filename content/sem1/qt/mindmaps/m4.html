1. Advantages of Diagrams 6

1Attractive & impressive — lasting impression; used in advertising, newspapers
2Simplify complex data — intelligible form
3Give more information — reveal hidden facts not visible in tables
4Save time and labour — grasped practically in no time
5Useful in comparisons — across time or place
6Universal applicability — economics, business, administration, social
⚠️

2. Limitations of Diagrams 9

1Give only vague idea — not for expert analysis
2Supplement to tabulation — NOT alternative
3Limited information vs classification + tabulation
4Very low level of precision
5Helpful only for comparisons; no further analysis
6Can portray only limited characteristics
7Liable to be misused — illusory picture possible
8Meaningless when measurements have wide variations
9False base line diagrams must be analysed carefully

TypeKey Point

📈 Trading Analogy: Histogram = distribution of daily returns; Ogive = cumulative returns curve (like equity curve); Frequency Polygon = comparing return distributions of two stocks.

6. Methods of Collecting Primary Data 5

MethodBest ForKey MeritKey Demerit
Direct Personal ObservationNarrow scope, complex, confidentialMost accurate; original dataExpensive; investigator bias
Indirect Oral InterviewLarge area; reluctant respondentsEconomical; confidential info possibleNot homogeneous; lower accuracy
Local Agencies / CorrespondentsVery large area; periodic collectionEconomical in time, money, labourLacks originality; biased correspondents
Questionnaire (by Respondent)Extensive area; educated respondentsFree from investigator bias; originalNon-response problem; inflexible
Schedule (by Investigator)Extensive area; illiterate respondentsDirect contact; cross-questioning possibleVery expensive; investigator bias
⚠️ Questionnaire = filled by RESPONDENT ⚠️ Schedule = filled by INVESTIGATOR Same format — different filler
