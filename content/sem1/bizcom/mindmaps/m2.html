1. Definition & 5 Purposes

Definition

Professional communication tool for internal or external audience. Helps readers know something or do something. Must be: Substantive · Clear · Correct · Easy to scan

Forms
Memos Reports Proposals Emails Presentations
5 Purposes
1
Convey information
2
Deliver news
3
Direct action
4
Explain or justify
5
Influence someone to take action

2. 8 Characteristics of Effective Business Writing

#CharacteristicKey Point
1To the pointState exactly why you're writing upfront
2Everyday words"about" not "concerning"; "expect" not "anticipate"
3Know your audienceAdjust tone to suit intended reader
4Use contractionsIf improves flow — use it; if more persuasive without — don't
5Active verbsActive = faster comprehension over passive
6Write tight"decided" not "made the decision"
7Simple fontsHelvetica / Times New Roman; limit variety
8Don't overuse visualsToo many graphics confuse and detract

4. Ethics, Plagiarism & Sources

Plagiarism — 3 Forms
FormDescription
Verbatim copyWord-for-word reproduction without credit
Paraphrase without creditRewritten in own words but source uncredited
Patch writingCut-and-paste insertion of fragments without crediting sources
Paraphrase vs Summary vs Plagiarism ⭐ MCQ
TermDefinition
ParaphraseSame length as original; rewritten in own words; credit given informally
SummaryCondensed version; highlights main ideas in own words
PlagiarismRepresenting another's work as your own — neither paraphrasing nor summarizing
Evaluating Sources
Reputable + reliable sources How credible is the source? Information literacy = essential skill Business relies on reputation & trust

7. Feedback in the Writing Process

Definition

Receiver's response to a source. The drive that steers the writer through the writing process. Final step in the communication process.

3 Sources of Feedback
1
Peer feedback — Active learning; multiple viewpoints; helps redraft writing
2
Conferencing — Collaborative reflective dialogue; multiple views at same time
3
Written comments — Positive (on track) or negative (error/ambiguity correction)
5 Categories of Feedback HIGH YIELD
CategoryDescription
EvaluativeMarks / scores / grades allotted
InterpretiveOverall judgment provided by interpreting the work
SupportiveMostly positive statements; acknowledges effort
ProbingLeads to questioning for deeper information
UnderstandingExplanation and understanding both occur

8. Quantitative vs Qualitative Research

FeatureQuantitativeQualitative
FocusTesting theories / hypothesesExploring ideas; formulating theory
AnalysisMath & statistical analysisSummarizing, categorizing, interpreting
Expressed inNumbers, graphs, tablesWords
RespondentsManyFew
QuestionsClosed (MCQ)Open-ended
Key termsTesting · Measurement · Objectivity · ReplicabilityUnderstanding · Context · Complexity · Subjectivity
🫀 Clinical Analogy: Quantitative = RCT (numbers, p-values, large sample). Qualitative = patient interview or case study (words, context, small sample). Both valid — different questions need different methods.
