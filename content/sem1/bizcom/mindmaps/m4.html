7 Purposes of Presentation

#PurposeCore Goal
1To InformAccurate explanations, share findings
2To EducateKnowledge-focused; interactive; varied learning styles
3To InstructDirections/orders; audience gains new skill
4To PersuadeAccept proposal or make a sale; logic + emotion
5To Solve ProblemPanel; identify → facts → causes → solutions
6To InspireTED Talk style; create reason for change
7To DecidePersuade audience to carry out requests; interactive
⚡ Trap: Educate = knowledge-focused · Instruct = directions/orders-focused

Speech to Inform — 8 Elements

1 Start with what you know — more enthusiastic delivery
2 Consider audience's prior knowledge — survey/ask
3 Adapt technical terms — define and substitute
4 Use outside information — adds depth and credibility; cite sources
5 Present information ethically — honest even when contradicting personal bias
6 Reciprocity — give first, without requirement in return
7 Mutuality — find common ground; inclusive format
8 Non-judgmentalism — open-minded; negotiate meaning constructively
💉 Clinical Bridge: Like a cath lab briefing — know your audience (junior vs senior cardiologist), cite evidence (cite sources), and never oversell findings.
🧠

6 Persuasion Principles — Cialdini

#PrincipleCore MechanismKey Detail
1ReciprocityGive first without expecting return → indebtednessMust be personalized and unexpected
2Commitment / ConsistencyPeople stick with what they already choseGet small initial voluntary/public commitments (ideally in writing)
3Social Proof / ConsensusPeople look to others' behavior when uncertainUse peer influence to drive action
4AuthorityPeople follow credible, knowledgeable expertsDisplay credentials and diplomas visibly
5LikingPeople say yes to those they likePoint out shared beliefs, interests, language
6ScarcityFewer = more valuableUse loss language (what you'll lose) not gain language
💉 Clinical Bridge: As a specialist, you already use Authority (credentials) and Social Proof (guidelines/evidence). Scarcity = "This window for intervention closes fast." All 6 work in medical sales pitches too.

Sound Bites · Toasts · Meetings

Sound Bite vs Quote
Brief excerpt (word to 2 sentences) Captures attention Clear · Vivid · Easy to repeat · Memorable
Toast vs Roast
TypeDescription
ToastHonor/goodwill; appreciation/congratulation; short speech; attractive and brief
RoastRidicules/criticizes to HONOR someone; for those in power; NOT a mean speech
3 Types of Meetings
Informative — give info/instruction Consultative — group consulted for solutions Executive — decisions announced/finalized
Formal vs Informal Meetings
Formal: set rules + minutes Informal: no rules; no written record required

Intrapersonal vs Interpersonal Communication

FeatureIntrapersonalInterpersonal
DefinitionCommunication with yourself; internal dialogue; self-conceptBetween two or more people via verbal/non-verbal messages
FoundationSelf-awareness — how you view your position in surroundingsSocial expectations and customs (learned or acquired)
Development3 components: self-awareness → self-regulation → motivationBest honed by practice
Key SkillsSelf-talk, self-concept, adjusting reactionsActive listening, negotiation, public speaking
Intrapersonal — 3 Development Components
1. Self-Awareness — monitor reactions; first step to growth 2. Self-Regulation — control habitual adverse reactions; integrity 3. Motivation — internal drive to make changes long-lasting
Improving Interpersonal Communication
Express appreciation for team & support staff Display empathy; moderate disputes quickly Active listening = repeat back speaker's comment to confirm
💉 Clinical Bridge: Self-awareness = recognizing your bias before interpreting an ECG. Self-regulation = staying calm in a cardiac arrest. Motivation = why you keep learning despite long shifts. Intrapersonal skills underpin every doctor-patient interaction.
