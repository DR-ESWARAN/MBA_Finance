# BIZCOM — Module 2: Business Writing
**Source: EBook + Study Guide | Exam: 15 May 2026**

---

## 1. Business Writing — Definition
- Professional communication tool used by corporations to communicate with **internal or external audience**
- A purposeful piece of writing that helps a reader **know something or do something**
- Must be: **Substantive, Clear, Correct, Easy to scan**
- Designed to be read **quickly**
- Forms: Memos, reports, proposals, emails, presentations

---

## 2. Purposes of Business Writing (5)
1. Convey information
2. Deliver news
3. Direct action
4. Explain or justify
5. Influence someone to take action

---

## 3. Characteristics of Effective Business Writing (8)
| # | Characteristic | Key Point |
|---|---------------|-----------|
| 1 | **To the point** | State exactly why you're writing upfront |
| 2 | **Everyday words** | "about" not "concerning"; "expect" not "anticipate" |
| 3 | **Know your audience** | Adjust tone to suit intended reader |
| 4 | **Use contractions** | If it improves flow — use it; if more persuasive without — don't |
| 5 | **Active over passive verbs** | Active = faster comprehension |
| 6 | **Write tight** | "decided" not "made the decision" |
| 7 | **Simple fonts** | Helvetica or Times New Roman; limit font variety |
| 8 | **Don't overuse visuals** | Too many graphics confuse and detract from message |

---

## 4. Business Writing Process — 5 Steps
1. **Preparation** — Objective, Readers, Scope
2. **Research and Investigation**
3. **Organizing**
4. **Drafting**
5. **Review and Revision**

> ⚡ *Exam tip: Know the 5 steps in sequence*

---

## 5. Business Writing Preparation — 3 Pillars
| Pillar | What it means |
|--------|--------------|
| **Objective / Purpose** | Define what the document must achieve; must be specific (= thesis statement for writing); must be realistic and achievable |
| **Readers / Audience** | Who they are and what they need — determines scope and tone |
| **Scope** | What to include and how much — guided by audience understanding |

> *Preparing to write is as important as the act itself*

---

## 6. Research and Investigation — 4 Focus Areas
1. **Narrow down your topic** — Most essential information only
2. **Focus on key points** — Maintain research around them
3. **Plan your investigation** — Know when and where to gather info
4. **Stay organized** — Thorough research = backbone of any successful business document

## 7. Ethics, Plagiarism & Reliable Sources

### Business Ethics
- Laws govern how a company conducts business — including business writing
- Corporate code of ethics applies to written communication

### Plagiarism
- Taking someone else's work and representing it as your own — regardless of format (text, photo, chart)
- Committed whether you copy **verbatim**, **paraphrase**, or represent someone's ideas as your own
- **Patch writing** = cut-and-paste insertion of fragments without crediting sources = plagiarism

### Paraphrase vs Summary vs Plagiarism
| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Paraphrase** | Same length as original; rewritten in your own words; informal credit given |
| **Summary** | Condensed version; highlights main/key ideas in your own words |
| **Plagiarism** | Representing another's work as your own — neither paraphrasing nor summarizing |

### Evaluating Sources
- Use **reputable, reliable sources** (printed + electronic) — reflects on credibility of message and messenger
- Central question: **how credible is the source?**
- **Information literacy** = essential skill in business writing
- Business and industry rely on reputation and trust — source quality matters

## 8. Organizing Principles (14)
| Principle | Core Idea |
|-----------|----------|
| **Unity of Objectives** | Goals defined for org, each dept, and each position |
| **Specialization** | Each person confined to a single job — leads to expertise |
| **Span of Control** | Numerical limit of subordinates one manager can supervise |
| **Exception** | Only exceptionally complex matters go to higher levels |
| **Scalar Principle** | Chain of command — unbroken authority from top to bottom |
| **Unity of Command** | One employee = one boss only; multiple bosses → confusion |
| **Delegation of Authority** | Authority delegated must equal responsibility |
| **Responsibility** | Clearly defined for all; cannot be avoided by delegating authority |
| **Authority** | Clearly defined; must equal responsibility |
| **Efficiency** | Achieve goals at minimum cost; optimum use of resources |
| **Simplicity** | Minimum levels; everyone understands duties and relationships |
| **Flexibility** | Adaptable to changing environment; allows replacement without disruption |
| **Balance** | Reasonable balance in dept size and centralization vs decentralization |
| **Unity of Direction** | One objective + one plan for activities with same objective |

> ⚡ *Exam tip: Scalar Principle = Chain of Command. Unity of Command = one boss. Know the distinction.*

## 9. Revising and Presenting Your Writing

### Revision Checklist
**Content & Structure:** Completeness, Unity & coherence, Key points stand out, Logical flow

**Format & Accuracy:** Facts, Names, Spelling, Punctuation, Grammar, Format

### Evaluating Others' Work
- Understand the assignment → Evaluate how well writing fulfils it → Check assertions and facts → Watch for errors → Deliver evaluation **logically and diplomatically** → Provide **both positive and negative** feedback

---

## 10. Proofreading & Design Evaluation

### Proofreading
- Final stage before publishing/submitting
- Reviews: Grammar, correct language use, format
- Eliminates: Mistakes, imperfections, grammatical errors
- Tips: Make a checklist, print hard copy, read aloud, remove unnecessary words, check punctuation, maintain formatting

### Design Evaluation — 3 Elements
| Element | What it covers |
|---------|---------------|
| **Framing** | Presentation style — margins, line justification, templates |
| **Typefaces** | Design of symbols/letters/numbers; font choice |
| **Paragraphs** | Subsections that make document easier to read |

---

## 11. Feedback in the Writing Process

### Definition
- Feedback = receiver's response to a source
- The **drive that steers the writer** through the writing process
- Final step in the communication process

### 3 Sources of Feedback
1. **Peer feedback** — Active learning; multiple viewpoints; helps redraft writing
2. **Conferencing** — Collaborative reflective dialogue; multiple views at same time
3. **Written comments** — Positive (on track) or negative (error/ambiguity correction)

### 5 Categories of Feedback
| Category | Description |
|----------|------------|
| **Evaluative** | Marks/scores/grades allotted |
| **Interpretive** | Overall judgment by interpreting the work |
| **Supportive** | Mostly positive statements; acknowledges effort |
| **Probing** | Leads to questioning for deeper information |
| **Understanding** | Explanation and understanding both occur |

> ⚡ *Exam tip: All 5 categories are MCQ targets — know label + 1-line description*

## 12. Quantitative vs Qualitative Research
| Feature | Quantitative | Qualitative |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Focus** | Testing theories/hypotheses | Exploring ideas; formulating theory |
| **Analysis** | Math & statistical analysis | Summarizing, categorizing, interpreting |
| **Expression** | Numbers, graphs, tables | Words |
| **Respondents** | Many | Few |
| **Questions** | Closed (MCQ) | Open-ended |
| **Key terms** | Testing, measurement, objectivity, replicability | Understanding, context, complexity, subjectivity |
