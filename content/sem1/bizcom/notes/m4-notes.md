## 1. Presentation — Definition
- Process of showing or presenting a work/project in front of an audience
- Typically a **demonstration, introduction, lecture or speech** meant to inform, persuade, inspire, motivate, build goodwill or present a new idea/product
- Characteristics: adequate but compact, direct and appealing, concise and effective, convincing and comprehensive

---

## 2. Seven Purposes of Presentation
| # | Purpose | Core Goal |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 1 | **To Inform** | Provide clear information; communicate accurate explanations; share new findings |
| 2 | **To Educate** | Audience walks away more knowledgeable; dynamic and interactive; cater to varied learning styles |
| 3 | **To Instruct** | Give specific directions or orders; audience gains new skill; may run longer |
| 4 | **To Persuade & Convince** | Convince audience to accept proposal or make a sale; use logic, evidence, and emotion |
| 5 | **To Solve Problem** | Panel setting; identify problem → present facts → generate causes → uncover solutions |
| 6 | **To Inspire** | Create reason for change and action; TED Talk style; provoke employee action |
| 7 | **To Decide** | Present ideas strongly enough to persuade audience to carry out requests; interactive |

> ⚡ *Educate vs Instruct: Educate = knowledge-focused; Instruct = directions/orders-focused*

---

## 3. Six Presentation Styles
| Style | Used by / When |
|-------|---------------|
| **Coach** | Motivational speakers; very enthusiastic about topic |
| **Connector** | Speakers who form connections; audience feels speaker is "one of them" |
| **Freeform** | Thorough knowledge; no notes needed; talking points memorized |
| **Instructor** | Giving instructions, orders, or conveying a message to audience |
| **Storytelling** | Conference speaking, networking, sales; adequate time for stories |
| **Visual** | Use slides to complement speech; short time to prepare |

## 4. Preparing a Speech to Inform — 8 Elements
1. **Start with what you know** — More enthusiastic delivery; saves time
2. **Consider audience's prior knowledge** — Survey, ask teammates; do homework on the organization
3. **Adapt technical terms** — Define and describe key terms; substitute common terms where appropriate
4. **Use outside information** — Adds depth and credibility; cite sources clearly
5. **Present information ethically** — Avoid prejudiced comments; honest even when it contradicts personal bias; admit when you don't know
6. **Reciprocity** — Mutual exchange and interdependence; give first, without requirement in return
7. **Mutuality (Keep it mutual)** — Find common ground; integrate inclusive, accessible format
8. **Non-judgmentalism** — Open-minded; willing to examine diverse perspectives; negotiate meaning constructively

---

## 5. Principles of Persuasion — 6 (Cialdini)
| # | Principle | Core Mechanism |
|---|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | **Reciprocity** | Give first without expecting return → creates sense of indebtedness; must be personalized and unexpected |
| 2 | **Commitment / Consistency** | People stick with what they already chose; get small initial voluntary/public commitments (ideally in writing) |
| 3 | **Social Proof / Consensus** | People look to others' behavior when uncertain; use peer influence to drive action |
| 4 | **Authority** | People follow credible, knowledgeable experts; display credentials, diplomas visibly |
| 5 | **Liking** | People say yes to those they like; point out shared beliefs, interests, language with audience |
| 6 | **Scarcity** | Fewer = more valuable; use loss language (what you'll lose) rather than gain language |

> ⚡ *All 6 are high-yield MCQ targets — know the name + 1-line mechanism*

## 6. Making an Argument

### Components of an Argument
- A **claim** + **reasons** to support the claim + **evidence** to support the reasons
- Making an argument = expressing a point of view + supporting it with evidence

### GASCAPT — 7 Argument Strategies
| Letter | Strategy | Description |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| **G** | Generalization | What is true of a good sample will be true of the whole population |
| **A** | Analogy | Two situations alike in observable ways will be alike in others (e.g., Alcohol is a drug. So is tobacco.) |
| **S** | Sign | Statistics/facts indicate meaning — look for correlation |
| **C** | Cause | If two conditions always appear together, they are causally related |
| **A** | Authority | What a credible source indicates is probably true |
| **P** | Principle | An accepted or proper truth |
| **T** | Testimony | Personal experience |

### 7 Steps to Build a Strong Argument
1. **Consider the situation** — Know subject, purpose, receiver, context
2. **Clarify your thinking** — Pros and cons of each side diplomatically
3. **Construct a claim** — 1–2 clear, concise sentences
4. **Collect evidence** — Variety of sources
5. **Consider key objections** — What is the counterargument?
6. **Craft your argument** — Use claim + evidence; appeal to reader's needs; answer objections
7. **Confirm your main point** — Restate claim in a new way; connect to real life and future

## 7. Elevator Pitch — 6 Steps
1. Identify the goal
2. Introduce yourself and your product
3. Communicate your proposition
4. Engage with a question
5. Wrap it up
6. Practice

> An elevator pitch = brief, persuasive speech; lasts **20–30 seconds** (one elevator ride); interesting, memorable, succinct; explains what makes you/product **unique**

## 8. Telephone & VoIP Communication

### Telephone Communication
- Transmission of speech over distance via electric signals or radio signals
- One of the most **widespread and expeditious** forms of communication

### VoIP (Voice over Internet Protocol)
- Also called **IP telephony**
- Converts voice into a digital signal for calls via computer, VoIP phone, or data-driven devices
- Delivery of voice + multimedia over IP networks (e.g., Internet)

### Five-Stage Telephone Conversation
| Stage | What happens |
|-------|-------------|
| 1. **Opening** | Both identify themselves; greetings reciprocated |
| 2. **Feed forward** | Purpose and tone established; permission to continue given |
| 3. **Business** | Substance of conversation; parties exchange roles |
| 4. **Feedback** | Signal that business is concluded |
| 5. **Closing** | Both parties say goodbye |

## 9. Sound Bites & Quotable
- **Sound bite** = brief excerpt (single word to a sentence or two) to capture audience's interest and attention
- **Quote** = memorable sayings extracted from written or verbal messages
- Both help in **summarization** and help audience remember your presentation

### Characteristics of Good Sound Bites/Quotes
- Clear and concise
- Vivid and dynamic language
- Easy to repeat
- Memorable

## 10. Celebrations — Toasts & Roasts
| Type | Description |
|------|------------|
| **Toast** | Ritual expression of honor or goodwill; formal expression of appreciation, congratulation or announcement; acknowledges a person or group; like a short speech — must be attractive and brief |
| **Roast** | Modification of toast; public proclamation that ridicules/criticizes someone TO HONOR them; designed for those in positions of power; NOT an opportunity to say something mean |

## 11. Meetings
- Meetings = **group communications in action** around a defined agenda, at a set time, for an established duration
- Can be: effective, ineffective, or a complete waste of time
- Formats: face-to-face, video conferencing, teleconferencing
- Always define purpose in advance with an **agenda**

### Formal vs Informal Meetings
| | Formal | Informal |
|--|--------|---------|
| Rules | Set rules for convening and conducting | No necessarily set rules |
| Record | Written record (minutes of meeting) | No requirement |
| Invitation | Requires notice or invitation | May or may not require invitation |

### 3 Types of Meetings
| Type | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| **Informative** | Give information, instruction, or make announcement |
| **Consultative** | Group consulted for solution to a problem |
| **Executive** | Decisions by those empowered to do so are announced or finalized |

## 12. Intrapersonal vs Interpersonal Communication
| Feature | Intrapersonal | Interpersonal |
|---------|--------------|--------------|
| **Definition** | Communication you have with yourself; internal dialogue; self-concept | Communication between two or more people through verbal or non-verbal messages |
| **Foundation** | Self-awareness — how you view your position in surroundings affects how you interact with others | Social expectations and customs (learned or acquired) |
| **Development** | 3 components: self-awareness → self-regulation → motivation | Best honed by practice |
| **Key skills** | Self-talk, self-concept, adjusting reactions | Active listening, negotiation, public speaking |

### Developing Intrapersonal Communication — 3 Components
1. **Self-awareness** — Monitor reactions; first step to growth; use personal assessment tools
2. **Self-regulation** — Control habitual adverse reactions; demonstrates integrity, reliability, adaptability
3. **Motivation** — Internal drive to make changes long-lasting

### Improving Interpersonal Communication
- Express appreciation for team members and support staff
- Display empathy; moderate disputes quickly; control displays of temper
- **Active listening** = repeat back speaker's comment to confirm true communication
