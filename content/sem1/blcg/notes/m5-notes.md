# BLCG Module 5: Membership — Exam Notes
**Source: DY Patil Ebook + Study Guide | Exam: 15 May 2026**

---

## 1. Membership — Definition

- **Company** is composed of members and/or shareholders
- To become a member: name must be entered in **register of members** OR as **beneficial owner in records of depository**
- **Section 2(55), Companies Act 2013** — 'member' means:
  1. Subscriber to the **memorandum** (deemed to have agreed on registration; entered in register on registration)
  2. Every person who **agrees in writing** to become a member and whose name is entered in register of members
  3. Every person holding shares whose name is entered as **beneficial owner** in records of a depository

---

## 2. Capacity to Become a Member

- **Capacity** = competency to enter into a contract
- Only persons competent to contract under **Indian Contract Act, 1872** may become members
- Subject to provisions of memorandum and articles

| Entity | Membership Rights |
|---|---|
| **Minor** | Cannot become member — contract with minor is **absolutely void** |
| **Company** | Can become member of another company if authorized by its memorandum |
| **Partnership Firm** | **Cannot** purchase shares in its own name — has no legal personality |
| **Fictitious Name** | Person taking shares in fictitious name = liable as member; also punishable for impersonation under **Sec 68-A** |
| **Hindu Undivided Family (HUF)** | Can hold shares in name of its **Karta** |
| **Foreigners/NRIs** | Can become members but require **RBI permission** under FERA, 1973 |
| **Insolvent** | Cannot become member — incompetent to contract |
| **Pawnee** | Does NOT become member — ownership remains with **pawner** (pawnee has possession only) |

---

## 3. Modes of Acquiring Membership

1. **By subscribing memorandum** — persons who agree to purchase at least one share at time of incorporation
2. **By allotment of shares** — company issues shares via IPO or Further Public Offering (FPO)
3. **By transfer** — transferee becomes member when company registers transfer and enters name in register
4. **By transmission** — person entitled to deceased member's shares becomes member when name entered in register on notice of transmission
5. **By becoming beneficial owner** — name entered as beneficial owner in depository records
6. **By estoppel** — person whose name was entered in register, knowing this, does NOT apply for rectification → becomes member by estoppel

---

## 4. Cessation of Membership

A member ceases to be a member when name is removed under these circumstances:
- (a) Transfer of shares to another person
- (b) Sale of shares under lien
- (c) Forfeiture of shares
- (d) Valid surrender of shares
- (e) Death of member
- (f) Insolvency of member
- (g) Rescission of contract to take shares on ground of misrepresentation or fraud
- (h) Winding up of company
- (i) Redemption of redeemable preference shares
- (j) Issue of share warrants in exchange of fully paid shares

---

## 5. Duties and Liabilities of Members

### Duties:
- (a) Subscriber must take shares opposite his name and pay for them
- (b) Take allotted shares and pay per terms of issue
- (c) Pay all valid calls when made
- (d) Abide by decisions of majority (unless majority acts vindictively, oppressively, mala fide or fraudulently)
- (e) Contribute to assets of company in liquidation

### Liabilities:

| Company Type | Liability of Member |
|---|---|
| **Limited by Shares** | Amount **unpaid on shares**; if fully paid = **nil liability** |
| **Limited by Guarantee** | Amount they undertook to contribute on winding up |
| **Unlimited** | **Fully liable** for all debts contracted during their membership period |

## 6. Borrowing Powers

- Every trading company has an **implied power to borrow** for business purposes (unless prohibited by memorandum or articles)
- Can give security by creating mortgage or charge on property
- Sources:
  - **Internal**: equity share capital, accumulated profits
  - **External**: external commercial borrowings, debentures, public fixed deposits, bank loans

### Section 179(3)(c) and (d): Directors' power to borrow
- Directors can pass resolution to borrow money
- Total amount to be borrowed must be stated in resolution
- Power to borrow can only be exercised by passing resolution

### Section 180 — Restrictions:
- Board of directors restricted from borrowing in excess of paid-up share capital + free reserves (apart from temporary loans)
- **Temporary loans** = loans repayable on demand within **6 months** (includes short-term, cash credit, discounting of bills)

---

## 7. Ultra Vires Borrowings

- **Ultra vires borrowing** = company borrows without authority OR beyond amount set in articles
- Such borrowings are **void**; lender cannot sue the company
- Securities given for unauthorized borrowings = **void and inoperative**

### Consequences of Ultra Vires Borrowings:

| Consequence | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Injunction** | Lender can restrain company from using borrowed money; members can apply for court injunction |
| **Subrogation** | Lender entitled to treat loan as intra vires to extent money was actually applied; can sue company by subrogation principle |
| **Liability of Directors** | Directors personally liable for unauthorized borrowings |

### Intra Vires Borrowing but Ultra Vires Directors' Authority:
- Exception to ultra vires — company's borrowing powers are valid but directors exceeded their authority
- This type of borrowing **may be ratified by shareholders**

---

## 8. Types of Borrowings

| Type | Duration | Key Feature |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-term** | 5+ years | For ongoing operations; not due within 1 year |
| **Short-term** | Up to 1 year | Temporary support; working capital needs |
| **Medium-term** | 2–5 years | Between short and long term |
| **Secured** | Any | Against **collateral** (security) |
| **Unsecured** | Any | No collateral; debt is financial obligation only |
| **Syndicated** | Any | Large fund from **group of lenders** under one agreement |
| **Bilateral** | Any | Borrowing from **one financial institution**; single contract |
| **Private** | Any | Bank loans and private financial institution loans |
| **Public** | Any | Freely tradable on a public exchange |

## 9. Company Management

- **Table A, Article 80**: "the business of a company shall be managed by the directors"
- **Lord Cranworth (Aberdeen Railway Co. v. Balkie Bros)**: Directors = "a body to whom is delegated the duty of managing the general affairs of the company"
- **Director** = individual elected by shareholders to manage affairs of the company
- **Board of Directors** = collective body of all directors (defined in Companies Act 2013)

## 10. Meetings

- **Meeting** = gathering or assembly of 2+ persons at a pre-decided date, time, and place for transacting lawful business
- Company management is carried on through meetings
- Shareholders and directors exercise powers through their respective meetings

### Types of Meetings:
1. **Members/Shareholders meetings**:
   - Annual General Meeting (AGM)
   - Extra-ordinary General Meeting (EGM)
   - Class meetings
   - Statutory meeting
2. **Board/Directors meetings**
3. **Debenture holders meetings**
4. **Creditors meetings**

## 11. Accounts and Audits (Sections 128–133, 138–148)

### Books of Account (Sec 128):
- Every company shall prepare and keep at **registered office** (and branches) books of accounts
- May be maintained in **electronic mode**
- Books kept on **accrual basis** and **double entry system**
- Retained for **8 financial years** with relevant vouchers

### Financial Statement (Sec 129):
- **Financial statement** includes: Balance Sheet, P&L Account / Income & Expenditure Account, Cash Flow Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity, explanatory notes
- Must give a **true and fair view** of state of affairs
- Must comply with accounting standards under **Sec 133**
- Prepared in form provided in **new Schedule III**

### Board's Report (Sec 134) must include:
- Extract of annual return; number of board meetings
- Directors' Responsibility Statement; declaration by independent director
- Nomination and remuneration committee details
- Particulars of loans, guarantees, investments
- Related party transactions with justification
- Risk management policy; CSR policy
- Formal evaluation of board's performance

### National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA):
- Constituted by Central Government (Sec 132, 133, 143(10))
- Chairperson: person of eminence with expertise in accounting, auditing, finance, or law
- Up to **15 full-time or part-time members**

### Penalty Provisions (Sec 147):
- Company: fine not less than **Rs 25,000** up to **Rs 5,00,000**
- Officer of company: imprisonment up to **1 year** OR fine not less than **Rs 10,000** up to **Rs 1,00,000** OR both
- Auditor: fine not less than **Rs 25,000** up to **Rs 5,00,000 OR 4 times auditor's remuneration** (whichever is less)

## 12. Prevention of Oppression and Mismanagement

### Oppression:
- **Lord Cooper**: "Oppression is a misdemeanour committed by majority shareholders who, under colour of their majority power, wrongfully inflict upon the minority shareholder any harm or injury"
- **Section 241**: Minority shareholders can apply to **Tribunal** for relief in case of oppression or prejudice to public interest

### Mismanagement:
- Affairs of company conducted in a manner **prejudicial to public interest** or to the interest of the company
- Material change in management/control and company affairs likely to be conducted prejudicially

### Foss v. Harbottle — The Rule:
- Two principles:
  1. **Proper plaintiff rule**: wrong done to company can only be vindicated by the company (separate legal entity)
  2. **Majority rule principle**: if alleged wrong can be ratified by simple majority in general meeting → court will NOT interfere

### Exceptions to Foss v. Harbottle:
- Ultra vires and illegality
- Actions requiring a **special majority**
- Invasion of individual rights
- **Frauds on the minority**

### Key Sections — Oppression/Mismanagement:
| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| **Sec 241** | Minority shareholders' application to Tribunal |
| **Sec 242** | Powers of the Tribunal |
| **Sec 243** | Consequences of termination/modification of agreements |
| **Sec 244** | Right to apply under Sec 241 |
| **Sec 245** | Class Action |
| **Sec 337** | Penalty for frauds by officer |
| **Sec 339** | Liability for fraudulent conduct of business |
| **Sec 340** | Tribunal power to assess damages against delinquent directors |

### Key Cases:
- **Hindustan Co-operative Insurance Society Ltd**: Majority changed objective clause to deny minority compensation → Calcutta HC held = oppression; quashed new clause; ordered payment
- **Rajahmundry Electric Corporation v. A. Nageswara Rao**: VC's group misappropriated funds; company unable to supply electricity → SC appointed 2 administrators for 6 months; divested VC's group of director powers

## 13. Winding Up of a Company

- **Winding up** = end of the life of a company; permanent closing down of business
- Company is creature of law → cannot die naturally → termination affected by law
- **Liquidator** conducts winding up: determines assets and liabilities; sells assets; meets creditors' claims; distributes balance to shareholders proportionally

### Three Modes of Winding Up:
1. **Winding up by Court**
2. **Voluntary winding up**
3. **Winding up subject to court supervision**

#### Grounds for Court Winding Up:
- Special resolution by company
- Default in delivering statutory report or holding statutory meeting
- Delay in commencement of business within **1 year** of incorporation OR suspension for whole year
- Members reduced below minimum (7 for public; 2 for private)
- Failure to pay debt (proved unable to pay)
- Ceases to be a listed company

#### Voluntary Winding Up — Two Types:
- **A. Members' Voluntary Winding Up**: Directors declare company is solvent; pass resolution; appoint liquidators
  - Circumstances: Expiry of period fixed in articles; Special resolution; Declaration of solvency (no debts OR can pay within 1 year); Appointment of liquidators (shareholders fix remuneration; directors' powers cease on liquidator appointment)
- **B. Creditors' Voluntary Winding Up**: Company insolvent; creditors involved in process

## 14. Consumer Protection Act

- **Consumer Protection Act, 1986** (revised 2019): enacted to provide simpler and quicker redressal of consumer grievances
- Covers all goods and services in private, public, and cooperative sectors (unless specifically exempted)
- Consumer courts can impose maximum **3 years imprisonment** and fine of **Rs 10,000**
- **Consumer** = person who buys goods/avails services for **self-use** (NOT for resale or commercial purpose)

### Six Basic Rights of Consumers:
1. Right to be **protected** against hazardous goods/services
2. Right to be **informed** about quality, quantity, standard, price
3. Right to **access** variety of goods at competitive prices
4. Right to be **heard** — interests receive due consideration at forums
5. Right to seek **redressal** against unfair trade practices
6. Right to **consumer education**

### Consumer Redressal Forums — Jurisdiction:

| Forum | Jurisdiction (Claim Amount) | Appeal Time |
|---|---|---|
| **District Forum** | Less than **Rs 5 lakh** | Within 30 days (extendable 15 days) to State Commission (Sec 15) |
| **State Commission** | Rs 5 lakh – Rs 20 lakh | Within 30 days to National Commission (Sec 19) |
| **National Commission** | More than **Rs 20 lakh** | Within 30 days to Supreme Court (Sec 23) |

### Filing Requirements:
- Within **2 years** of buying product / using service
- Must be in writing (registered post, hand delivery, email, fax)
- Complaint should mention: complainant details, opposite party details, problem details, relief demanded
- No compulsion to hire a lawyer

## 15. Cyber Law in India

- **Information Technology Act, 2000** — notified 17 October 2000
- Legal framework for **electronic governance**; defines penalties for cybercrime
- Established **Cyber Appellate Tribunal** for cyber disputes
- Covers: electronic records, digital signatures, e-commerce, monetary transactions
- Also applicable: IPC 1860 provisions (fraud, criminal intimidation, cheating, breach of trust)
- **Article 20(2), Indian Constitution**: No double jeopardy — person cannot be punished twice for same offence
- First-ever cybercrime recorded in **1820**
- IT Act 2000 governs: computer technology, mobile devices, software, internet as medium and target of crimes

### Importance of Right to Information (RTI) Act, 2005:
- Did NOT create new bureaucracy
- Tasked existing officials to shift from secrecy to **openness**
- **Information Commission** = highest authority; can order any office to provide information; can fine non-compliant officials
- RTI = key to strengthening **participatory democracy**
- Empowers poor and weaker sections to demand information about public policies
- Makes government more **accountable**; improves decision-making by removing unnecessary secrecy
