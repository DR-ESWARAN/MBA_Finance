## 1. Values — Definition

- **Values** (Haralambos, 2000) = "a belief that something is good and desirable"
- **Values** (R.K. Mukerjee, 1949) = "socially approved desires and goals that are internalized through the process of conditioning, learning or socialization and that become subjective preferences, standards and aspirations"
- Generally: moral ideas, general conceptions, orientations, interests, attitudes, preferences, needs, sentiments, dispositions

---

## 2. Types of Values

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Intrinsic Values** | Related to goals of life; also called ultimate/transcendent values; determine human rights, duties, virtues; HIGHEST in hierarchy |
| **Instrumental Values** | Means to achieve intrinsic values (goals); also called incidental or proximate values; come after intrinsic values in hierarchy |

- Values can also be classified as **personal** (principles you stand for) vs **organizational** (shared beliefs and standards)

---

## 3. Formation of Values, Ethics, and Behavior

- Value formation = confluence of **personal experiences** and **culture**
- Values are **imposed from family** in childhood and **reinforced through culture** and life experiences
- Culture provides:
  - Community and shared reality
  - Prescriptions for appropriate conduct
  - Meaning, purpose, and significance
- Values ebb and flow with culture (evident when traveling to different countries)

## 5. Managerial Excellence through Human Values

### Management Ethics:
- **Management ethics** = "the discipline dealing with what is good and bad, or right and wrong, or with moral duty and obligation. It is a standard of behavior that guides individual managers in their works"
- **Business ethics** = application of ethical principles to business relationships and activities
- When managers assume social responsibility, they are expected to act **ethically**

### Development of Ethics (3 Steps):
1. **Make expectations clear** — write down what ethical behavior means
2. **Enforce your policies** — ethical breaches must have consequences (tolerating unethical behavior teaches others to be unethical)
3. **Be your own change agent** — "walk your talk"; employees watch leaders for behavioral cues

---

## 6. Ethical Business Decision Making

- Ethical decision-making = a **discipline** and an approach to identifying and resolving business issues
- Requires **awareness/sensitivity** to ethical issues that may arise
- Not simply "doing the right thing" — more complex
- Requires careful discussion among interested parties
- If regularly practiced, managers can apply this discipline in making important decisions based on ethical principles

---

## 7. Business Ethics

- **Business Ethics** (John Donaldson) = "the systematic study of ethical matters pertaining to business, industry or related activities, institutions and beliefs"
- Also = "the systematic handling of values in business and industry"

### Stakeholder Management and Business Ethics:
- When business focuses ONLY on shareholder wealth maximization and **excludes other stakeholders** → creates imbalance in benefits vs obligations → **business ethics problem**
- Long-term: structural business ethics problem — shareholder wealth maximization **endangers reciprocity**

### Business Ethics and the Changing Environment:
- Businesses and governments operate in changing **technological, legal, economic, social & political environments** with competing stakeholders
- Disruptive technologies, increased work stress → pressure on stakeholders
- Examples of ethics failures: **Enron, Adelphia**; excessive CEO pay with poor corporate performance

## 8. BRICS

- **BRICS** = Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa
- BRICS Leaders' Summit convened **annually**
- Does NOT exist as a formal organization — annual summit between supreme leaders of 5 nations
- Collectively represent almost **3 billion people** (~half of world population)
- Goldman Sachs prediction: by 2050, BRICS will be the most important economies — **USA relegated to 5th place**
- These are the fastest growing and largest **emerging market economies**
- Primarily an **investment category**

## 11. Modern Business Ethics and Dilemmas

- **Business ethics** = system of moral and ethical beliefs that guides values, behaviors and decisions of a business organization and individuals within it
- Some ethical requirements are **codified into law**: environmental regulations, minimum wage, restrictions against **insider trading** and **collusion**
- Trading analogy: insider trading = using non-public information to trade stocks — illegal and unethical
- **Ethical dilemma** = "a problem in the decision-making process between two possible options, neither of which is absolutely acceptable from an ethical perspective"
- Ethical dilemmas are extremely complicated; not easily solved
- Every person can encounter ethical dilemmas in personal, social, and professional contexts

---

## 12. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

- **CSR** = self-regulating business model — company is socially accountable to itself, stakeholders, and the public
- Also called **corporate citizenship**
- Operates in economic, social, and environmental dimensions
- Example: **Starbucks** (IPO 1992): 99% ethically sourced coffee; 10,000 refugee hiring goal; green building stores

---

## 13. Sustainability

- Addresses two main categories:
  1. Effect business has on the **environment**
  2. Effect business has on **society**
- Sustainable business strategy = make a **positive impact** on either category
- **ESG metrics** (Environmental, Social, Governance) = used by investors to analyze ethical impact and sustainability
  - Carbon footprint, water usage, community development, board diversity
- Companies with **high ESG ratings** = lower cost of debt and equity
- Trading analogy: ESG ratings are like clinical biomarkers — high ESG = healthy company fundamentals; low ESG = risk factors warranting caution before investing
