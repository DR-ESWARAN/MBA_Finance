# BLCG Module 1: Corporate Governance — Exam Notes
**Source: DY Patil Ebook + Study Guide | Exam: 15 May 2026**

---

## 1. Corporate Governance — Definition

- **Corporate governance** = combination of rules, processes or laws by which businesses are operated, regulated or controlled
- Encompasses internal and external factors affecting interests of stakeholders (shareholders, customers, suppliers, govt regulators, management)
- **Board of directors** is responsible for creating the corporate governance framework
- Clinical analogy: like hospital protocols — governance framework sets rules so every "department" (division) operates safely and transparently

---

## 2. Five Common Issues in Corporate Governance

| Issue | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Conflicts of Interest** | Board member has personal financial interests conflicting with company's objectives (e.g., director of solar company holds oil stocks) |
| **Oversight Issues** | Board fails to adequately monitor executive practices and daily operations |
| **Accountability Issues** | Lack of reporting hierarchy — each level must be accountable to another |
| **Transparency** | Inaccurate reporting of profits/losses to investors; selective disclosure |
| **Ethics Violations** | Executives making decisions NOT in best interest of stockholders or society |

---

## 3. Need for Corporate Governance

- Felt due to increasing **non-compliance with financial reporting standards** by board and management
- Caused **huge investor losses**
- Global examples: **Enron (USA)**, **Satyam (India)** — collapsed due to corrupt practices and bad governance framework
- Trading analogy: poor governance = company with manipulated financials — the kind of stock that destroys your F&O positions overnight

---

## 4. Code of Best Practices for Corporate Governance

- Adopted by **Securities and Exchange Commission** (capital market regulator) and **Corporate Affairs Commission** (company regulator)
- Recommended for all private and public companies
- Directed mainly at **boards of directors**
- Purpose: enhance **corporate discipline, transparency and accountability**

### Board of Directors Responsibilities:
- Strategic planning
- Selection, performance appraisal and compensation of senior executives
- Succession planning
- Communication with shareholders
- Ensuring integrity of financial controls and reports
- Ensuring ethical standards and legal compliance

### Key Rules:
- Non-executive directors appointed for periods **not exceeding 3 years** (except with shareholder approval)
- Reappointment based exclusively on **performance**
- Total emoluments of executive directors must be **fully disclosed**
- Shareholders holding **>20% of issued capital** must be represented on the board
- At least **one director** must represent minority shareholders
- **Remuneration committee** = composed wholly or mainly of non-executive directors

### Audit Committee:
- Must consist of **independent persons** not influenced by any board member
- Members must be free from any business relationship affecting **independence of judgement**

---

## 5. Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

- **CSR** = self-regulating business model that helps a company be socially accountable — to itself, its stakeholders, and the public
- Also called **corporate citizenship**
- Impacts: economic, social, and environmental
- CSR is primarily a strategy of **large corporations**
- Example: Starbucks — 99% ethically sourced coffee, green building, community service
- CSR benefits: stronger employee-corporation bond, improved morale, public brand value

---

## 6. Corporate Social Reporting

- **Social reporting** = division of reporting related to the functioning of the social system
- Measured as community reporting through **annual reports**
- Annual report includes: Chairman Report, Directors Report, Financial Statements, Auditors Report
- Areas covered: pollution control, community relations, consumer complaints, quality, plant safety, human resources
- **Corporate social disclosure** = expansion of financial disclosure, reflects wider societal role of business
- Legitimacy theory: if legitimacy of sector is threatened, organizations respond by reporting **more positive information**
- Three higher purposes (Guthrie & Parker, 1990):
  1. Comprehensive view of organization and its resources
  2. Constraint on socially irresponsible corporate behavior
  3. Positive motivation for socially responsible action

---

## 7. Role of the Board of Directors

- Board's main role = **oversight and planning** (NOT daily operations)
- Should NOT micromanage management
- May delegate powers to **CEO or CFO** under certain circumstances
- **Board committees** = subset of full board; handle specific issues requiring deep focus; call in experts; provide regular reports to board

---

## 8. Corporate Governance Systems Worldwide

- Systems worldwide are **converging** towards a model based on **shareholder primacy and dispersed ownership**
- Driven by corporate scandals across USA, UK, and continental Europe

---

## 9. Corporate Disclosure

- **Corporate disclosure** = communication of information by people inside public firms to people outside
- Legal requirement for public companies, mutual funds, corporate insiders
- Main aim: "to communicate firm performance and governance to outside investors" (Healy & Palepu, 2001)
- Forms of disclosure:
  - **Financial reporting** (IFRS-compliant financial statements)
  - Informal communication (press conferences, website announcements)
- Full disclosure + transparency = reduces information gap, lowers cost of capital, boosts investor confidence
- Trading analogy: disclosure is like the cardiac monitor — shows real-time vital signs to investors

## 10. Investor Protection in India — Companies Act 2013

| Provision | Section | Key Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Acceptance of Deposits** | Sec 73 | No company can accept public deposits except under Chapter V rules |
| **Misstatement in Prospectus** | Sec 34 | Criminal liability for untrue/misleading statements in prospectus |
| **Fraudulent Inducement** | Sec 36 | Punishment for inducing investment through false means — liable u/s 447 |
| **Non-Payment of Dividend** | Sec 123 | Dividend must be credited to investor within **5 days** of declaration |
| **Investor Protection Fund** | Sec 125 | Fund for unpaid/unclaimed dividends; used for investor awareness |
| **Right to Financial Statements** | Sec 136 | Members can demand Balance Sheet + Auditor Reports; default = Rs 25 penalty for company, Rs 5,000 for officer |

## 11. Indian Contract Act — Contracts

### Definition:
- **Contract** = "an agreement enforceable by law" (Indian Contract Act, 1872)
- **Contract = Agreement + Enforceability**

### Essential Elements of a Valid Contract:
1. **Two Parties** — one offering, one accepting
2. **Agreement** — offer + acceptance
3. **Consent** — agree on same thing in same sense at same time
4. **Intention to create legal relationship** — social/household agreements are NOT contracts
5. **Contractual Capacity** — must be: of majority age + sound mind + not legally disqualified
6. **Consideration** — "something in return" — can be cash, kind, or act; past/present/future; must be real and lawful
7. **Free Consent** — not obtained by misrepresentation, fraud, undue influence or mistake
8. **Certainty** — terms must be certain, not vague
9. **Possibility to Perform** — impossible acts cannot be contracts
10. **Legal Formalities** — where law requires: must be in writing, registered, attested

### Unlawful Consideration — when consideration is unlawful:
- Forbidden by law
- Would defeat provisions of any law
- Fraudulent
- Involves injury to person/property of another
- Court regards it as immoral

---

## 12. Void Agreements

- **Void agreement** = "an agreement not enforceable by law" (Section 2(g), Indian Contract Act)
- Parties get NO legal redress in court
- Arise due to non-fulfillment of conditions in **Section 10**
- Clinical analogy: void contract = flatline ECG — never had legal life

---

## 13. Performance of Contract

- **Performance of contract** = both promisor and promisee have fulfilled respective obligations
- Section 27: parties must either perform or offer to perform their promises
- Performance must be **exact and complete** to match contractual obligations
- Obligations bind representatives of the promisor even in case of death (unless contrary intention)

---

## 14. Breach of Contract and Remedies

- **Section 73** — Compensation for breach:
  - Party who suffers breach is entitled to receive compensation for **loss or damage** that naturally arose from the breach
  - Compensation NOT given for **remote and indirect** loss
  - Means to remedy inconvenience must be considered when estimating loss
- Clinical analogy: breach of contract = MI — requires immediate remedy/intervention; compensation = treatment to restore the patient (aggrieved party) to health

---

## 15. Quasi Contract

- **"Quasi"** = pseudo/resembling
- Law implies a promise and imposes obligations even when basic contract elements are absent
- Courts recognize and enforce these like a contract

### Features:
- Usually a right to **money** (generally a liquidated sum)
- Right is **imposed by law**, not by agreement
- Right is against a **specific person(s)** only (resembles contractual right)

### Sections (ICA 1872) for Quasi Contracts:
| Section | Circumstance |
|---|---|
| **Sec 68** | Necessaries supplied to persons incapable of contracting (minors, lunatics) — reimbursement from their property |
| **Sec 69** | Payment by an interested person on someone else's legal obligation — entitled to reimbursement |
| **Sec 70** | Obligation of person enjoying benefits of a non-gratuitous act |
| **Sec 71** | Responsibility of finder of goods |
| **Sec 72** | Liability of person to whom money is paid by mistake |
