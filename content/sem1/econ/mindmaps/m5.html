3 Profit Formulas
Accounting Profit = TR − (W + R + I + M)
W=Wages, R=Rent, I=Interest, M=Materials | Also = Gross Profit
Economic Profit = TR − (Explicit + Implicit costs)
Implicit = own land rent, owner salary, own capital interest
Pure Profit = Accounting Profit − (Opportunity Cost + Bribes)
🧠 5 Theories of Profit HIGH YIELD
Walker's Rent Theory
Profit = differential ability like land fertility
Most efficient entrepreneur earns profit above normal
Limitation: only measures, doesn't explain nature
Clark's Dynamic Theory
Profit arises only in dynamic economy (changing pop, capital, wants, tech)
Static economy = only normal profit
Criticism (Knight): only unforeseen changes yield profit
Hawley's Risk Theory
Profit = reward for risk-taking (1893)
Greater risk → higher expected profit
Criticism: profit doesn't always match risk; superior managers reduce risk
Knight's Uncertainty Theory
Profit = reward for non-calculable uncertainty (NOT insurable risk)
Calculable risks = insured by insurers
Uncertainty = market shifts → only entrepreneur bears
Key: Risk ≠ Uncertainty
Schumpeter's Innovation
Profit = reward for innovation
New products, methods, markets, raw materials
Criticism: ignores uncertainty and risk
Knight's Key Distinction
Calculable (Insurable) risks: fire, theft → insured → entrepreneur doesn't earn profit for these  |  Non-calculable uncertainty: demand shifts → entrepreneur bears → EARNS PROFIT
🎯 Profit Max & Drucker's Functions EXAM STAPLE
Peter Drucker — 3 Functions of Profit
  • Measure performance — high profit = efficient management
  • Cover costs — replacement, technical, uncertainty costs
  • Secure future capital — retained profits → expansion, innovation
Profit Maximisation — 2 Rules
1. MC = MR  |  2. MC cuts MR from BELOW
π(Q) = R(Q) − C(Q) → Maximise
9 Criticisms of Profit Maximisation
Profits uncertain No internal relevance No perfect knowledge Evidence vague Don't use MC/MR Avg cost principle used Static theory Not for oligopoly Varied objectives
Hall & Hitch: businessmen haven't heard of MC/MR | Berle & Means (1932): managers ≠ shareholders
📊 Break-Even Analysis HIGH YIELD
Key Formulas
BEP (units) = Fixed Cost / (P − AVC)
Contribution Margin = P − AVC
TCM = TNP + TFC = TR − TVC
P/V ratio = CM / Price = (P − AVC) / P
BEP (₹ sales) = FC / P/V ratio
BEP (% capacity) = BEP units / Max capacity
At BEP: ANP = 0 → AFC = ACM (average contribution margin)
Indian Airlines Example (Ebook)
ItemValue
Capacity10,000 passengers/month
Fare (P)₹500
Variable cost (AVC)₹100/passenger
Fixed Cost₹3,00,000/month
Contribution Margin₹500 − ₹100 = ₹400
BEP3,00,000 / 400 = 750 passengers

📈 Inflation — Types & Measurement HIGH YIELD
3 Causes / Types
TypeCauseMechanism
Demand-Pull Excess demand > production capacity Demand gap → prices rise; also: ↑ money supply → ↑ spending → inflation
Cost-Push Rise in production input costs Higher wages/raw materials → higher finished product prices
Built-In Adaptive expectations (wage-price spiral) Prices ↑ → workers demand ↑ wages → costs ↑ → prices ↑ → self-perpetuating
3 Inflation Indexes
IndexMeasuresPerspective
CPIConsumer basket (food, transport, medical) — retail pricesBuyer's perspective; US BLS monthly since 1913
WPIPrices at wholesale/producer levelPre-retail; used in India
PPIAverage selling prices received by domestic producersSeller's perspective; USA variant of WPI
Inflation Formula & Example
Change in Inflation = Final CPI / Initial CPI
Sept 1975 CPI=54.6 → Sept 2018 CPI=252.439
Ratio = 252.439/54.6 = 4.6234 (462.34%)
$10,000 in 1975 → $56,234.25 in 2018
⚖️ Inflation Effects & Hedging TRADING RELEVANT
Effects on Income Distribution
GroupEffect
Debtors/BorrowersGAIN — repay in cheaper rupees
Creditors/LendersLOSE — receive cheaper rupees
Bond/Debenture holdersLOSE — fixed interest eroded
Equity investorsGAIN — stock prices rise with inflation
Salaried/fixed incomeLOSE — wage hikes lag price rises
Speculators/profit-earnersGAIN — flexible incomes outpace prices
Effects on Growth
  • Mild inflation → incentivises investment → higher output (multiplier)
  • High inflation → uncertainty → less investment → lower growth
  • Hyperinflation (≥50%/month) → capital flight, savings collapse, balance of payments crisis
Inflation Hedges (Best to Worst)
Stocks (best) TIPS Gold REITs Commodities Cash (worst) Fixed bonds (worst)
Control Mechanism
Monetary policy — central bank raises interest rates, tightens money supply | Targets: USA 2%, India 4%, Brazil 4.25%
