Income & Substitution Effect

When price ↑ — Two effects occur
EffectMechanism
SubstitutionGood more expensive → switch to cheaper alternatives
IncomeHigher price → less real income → buy less of the good
Application: Wages (Work vs Leisure)
↑ Wages → work more (substitution effect early) ↑ Wages → work less later (income effect dominates) Result: Backward-bending labour supply curve
Application: Interest Rates & Saving
↑ Interest → saving more rewarding (substitution → more saving) ↑ Interest → more income from savings (income → more spending)

Price Elasticity of Demand EXAM HIGH-YIELD

Ed = % ΔQ / % ΔP
TypeEd ValueMeaningCurveExample
Perfectly Elastic Ed = ∞ Infinite response to tiny ↑ price Horizontal Theoretical — competitive market
Elastic Ed > 1 % ΔQ > % ΔP Flatter Luxury goods, coffee (substitutes)
Unitary Ed = 1 % ΔQ = % ΔP Rectangular hyperbola TR maximised; MR = 0
Inelastic Ed < 1 % ΔQ < % ΔP Steeper Salt, medicines, necessities
Perfectly Inelastic Ed = 0 No response to any price change Vertical Life-saving drugs (insulin)
Numerical Examples (Ebook)
Ed = 2 (Elastic)Price ↑10%, Qty ↓20% → 20/10 = 2
Ed = 1/3 (Inelastic)Price ↑30%, Qty ↓10% → 10/30 = 0.33
Ed = 1 (Unitary)Price ↑30%, Qty ↓30% → 30/30 = 1
⚠️ Slope ≠ ElasticitySteep curve ≠ inelastic. Ed is % change, not absolute.
TR–Elasticity Relationship
Ed > 1: ↓ Price → ↑ TR Ed < 1: ↓ Price → ↓ TR Ed = 1: TR maximised, MR = 0
4 Uses of Price Elasticity in Management
Price discrimination — charge inelastic segment more Public utility pricing — railways, water, postal services Joint supply — price inelastic good higher (wool vs mutton) Supermarket — lower prices on elastic goods for footfall

Income Elasticity of Demand

Ey = % ΔQ / % ΔY
5 Types + Good Classification
Ey ValueTypeGood Category
Ey > 1HighLuxury (cars, jewellery, premium)
Ey = 1UnitaryProportional change
0 < Ey < 1LowNecessity (water, electricity, tobacco)
Ey = 0ZeroEssential staples — demand fixed
Ey < 0NegativeInferior goods (margarine, coarse grain)
Numerical Example (Ebook)
Income: $50k → $40k (↓20%) | Cars: 10k → 5k (↓50%) Ey = 50% / 20% = 2.5 → Luxury good
📊 Trading analogy: During market downturns, luxury stock demand crashes (high Ey) while FMCG/pharma holds (low Ey necessity goods).

Cross Elasticity of Demand

Exy = % ΔQx / % ΔPy
Interpretation
ExyRelationshipExample
Positive (+)Substitutes↑ Price of coffee → ↑ demand for tea
Negative (−)Complements↑ Price of coffee → ↓ demand for stir sticks
Zero (0)UnrelatedPrice of tea has no effect on car demand
Strong vs Weak Substitutes
Same category (green tea brands) → high Exy Different categories (tea vs coffee) → low Exy
Managerial Use
No substitutes (Exy=0) → can price higher Printers at loss → drives high-margin ink sales (complement strategy)

Revenue Concepts — TR, AR, MR EXAM HIGH-YIELD

ConceptFormulaKey RelationshipExample (10 chairs @ ₹160)
TR (Total Revenue) TR = P × Q TR = ΣMR 10 × ₹160 = ₹1,600
AR (Average Revenue) AR = TR / Q = Price AR Curve = Demand Curve 1600 / 10 = ₹160 = Price
MR (Marginal Revenue) MRn = TRn − TRn-1 MR = ΔTR / ΔQ TR₁₁=₹1780; MR₁₁ = 1780−1600 = ₹180
Key Proofs & Relationships
AR = Price (always)AR = TR/Q = PQ/Q = P ✓
TR = ΣMRSum of all marginal revenues = Total revenue
MR (multi-unit)ΔTR/ΔQ = (2200−1600)/(14−10) = ₹150
Ed=1 → MR=0TR is maximised at unitary elasticity
📊 Trading analogy: MR = marginal profit per additional trade — when MR falls to 0, you've hit peak total return. Same logic as Ed = 1 point.

Demand Forecasting — 7 Methods

MethodBasisBest ForKey Limitation
Survey of Buyer's Intentions Direct consumer survey Short-term; bulk goods Costly; consumers can't foresee choices
Sales Force Composite Salesperson estimates New products; short-term Subjective; not for long-term
Trend Projection Past data + least squares; O=TSCI Long-term; stable trends Fails at turning points
Executive Judgment Top management opinion When no data exists No factual basis; inaccurate
Economic Indicators Link demand to indicators (income, contracts) Products with time-lagged demand Hard to find right indicator
Controlled Experiments Vary price/ads in test markets Measuring price sensitivity Expensive; risky; hard to control
Expert's Opinions External specialist views New products; no past data Subjective; no segment estimates
Trend Projection Formula: O = T × S × C × I
T = Secular Trend S = Seasonal Factor C = Cyclical Element I = Irregular Movement
