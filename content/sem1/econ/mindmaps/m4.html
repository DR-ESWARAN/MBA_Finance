📐 Pricing Methods 6 METHODS
MethodBasisBest ForLimitation
Full Cost Plus All costs (direct + indirect) + margin Long-term contracts; regulated industries Ignores demand; circular reasoning if volume changes
Manufacturing Cost Plus Mfg cost only + higher margin (covers non-mfg too) Standard product pricing Doesn't include admin/selling in base
Conversion Cost Plus Labour + overhead (customer provides material) Customer-supplied materials Narrow applicability
Marginal Cost Plus Variable cost only + markup Special orders; spare capacity; new markets Fixed cost recovery not guaranteed
Differential Cost Plus Incremental total cost (variable + some fixed) + markup One-time extra orders Limited to marginal volume decisions
Standard Costs Pre-determined efficient cost + markup Quick pricing; avoids absorbing inefficiency Outdated standards = wrong prices

1
Develop product satisfying customer needs
2
Choose target price based on perceived value + competitor prices
3
Derive target cost = Target Price − Desired Profit
4
Estimate actual cost of the product
5

Life Cycle Costing

🏪 Market Structures — Price & Output Decisions 4 STRUCTURES
Perfect Competition
  • Many sellers, homogeneous products
  • Price TAKER
  • P = MR = MC at equilibrium
  • Demand curve = horizontal (perfectly elastic)
  • Price = intersection of market demand & supply (Marshall's scissors)
  • No barriers to entry/exit
Monopoly
  • Single producer; no close substitutes
  • Price MAKER
  • Equilibrium: MR = MC
  • AR (demand) curve slopes downward
  • MR < AR; MR falls 2× rate of AR
  • MR = AR[(e−1)/e]
  • MC = 0 → equilibrium at unitary elasticity
  • 📊 Q=44, P=120, Profit=₹20,844
Monopolistic Competition
  • Many firms, differentiated products
  • Price MAKER (limited)
  • Low barriers to entry/exit
  • Demand highly elastic
  • SR: positive profit
  • LR: profit → zero (firms enter)
  • Heavy advertising
Oligopoly
  • Few large firms; mutual interdependence
  • Sticky prices (change rarely)
  • Non-collusive: independent; price war possible
  • Collusive: joint profit max; cartel
  • Price leadership: Low-cost / Dominant / Barometric firm leads
  • Price settles between monopoly (upper) and competitive (lower) limits
📉 Economic Approach & Indifference Point
Economic (MR=MC) Approach
  • Profit maximised where MR = MC
  • Produce as long as MR > MC
  • Two practical problems: (1) Hard to estimate demand curve; (2) Demand depends on quality, ads — not just price
Price Indifference Point
  • Sales volume where net income is same under 2 pricing options
  • Expected volume > indifference point → accept price change
  • Expected volume < indifference point → reject price change
  • Used in short-term decisions
