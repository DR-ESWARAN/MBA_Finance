**1. Financial statements**

- Ebook: **formal records** of the financial activities and position of
  a business, person or other entity. Summarised reports giving
  **operating results and financial position**; useful to judge
  operational efficiency and financial soundness.

- Often **audited** (government agencies, accountants, firms) for
  accuracy, tax, financing or investing.

- General-purpose set (4): **balance sheet · income statement ·
  statement of owner's equity · statement of cash flows**.

- SN: two significant statements = **Balance Sheet (statement of
  position)** and **Profit & Loss Account (income statement)**; goal =
  present an **annual report**.

**Features of financial statements (Ebook, 5)**

1.  Prepared at the **end of the accounting period**.

2.  Disclose both **facts and opinions**.

3.  Prepared on **going concern** value.

4.  Recorded facts based on **historical cost**.

5.  Greatly affected by the **personal judgement** of accountants.

- Income statement = the report that reviews **profitability**
  (Objective QB).

**2. Financial statement analysis**

- Ebook: **critical evaluation** of the financial information in
  financial statements to understand and make decisions about the firm's
  operations.

- Financial analysis = **analysis** (simplifying data by **methodical
  classification**) + **interpretation** (explaining **meaning and
  significance**).

| **Author**           | **Definition**                                                                                                                       |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Metcalf & Titard** | Evaluating the **relationship between component parts** of a financial statement to understand a firm's **position and performance** |

- SN: analysis without interpretation is meaningless; interpretation
  without analysis is impossible. Time-series = over time;
  **cross-sectional** = against other firms. Main goal: best **estimates
  and forecasts** (a judgemental process).

- Objective QB: interpretation of accounts = art and science of
  translating figures **and** knowing strengths and weaknesses (both).
  The process of explaining meaning, significance and relationship
  between two factors = **interpretation**. First step = **specify the
  objectives** of the analysis.

> **Analogy (memory aid):** *Analysis is arranging the echo measurements
> in a table; interpretation is saying "moderate LV dysfunction, needs
> therapy". One without the other is useless.*

**3. Users of financial statement analysis**

| **User**                   | **Interest (Ebook)**                                                                              |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Creditors**              | Ability to repay debt; focus on **cash flow** measures                                            |
| **Investors**              | Ability to keep paying **dividends**, generate cash flow, keep growing                            |
| **Management**             | Controller's ongoing analysis with internal metrics (cost per delivery, profit by product)        |
| **Regulatory authorities** | Securities and Exchange Board checks conformity with accounting standards (listed firms)          |
| **Others**                 | Bankers, FIs, employees, government, trade associations, economists, researchers, tax authorities |

- SN significance by user: **finance manager** (operational and
  managerial effectiveness) · **top management** (resource use) ·
  **trade payables** (short and long-term obligations) · **lenders**
  (long-term viability; principal and interest) · **investors**
  (bankruptcy risk, security of investment).

**Importance (Ebook)**

- Identifies **strengths and weaknesses** by relating balance sheet and
  P&L items · reviews periodic performance · shareholders decide to
  **hold or sell** · management evaluates past actions · creditors
  decide to **extend loans** and at what rate · prospective investors
  decide to invest.

**4. Objectives of analysis**

**Ebook (10)**

1.  Assess **earning capacity / profitability** (firm and departments).

2.  Assess **operational efficiency and managerial effectiveness**.

3.  Assess **short and long-term solvency**.

4.  Identify reasons for change in profitability and financial position.

5.  **Inter-firm comparison**.

6.  **Forecast** future prospects.

7.  Assess **progress** over time.

8.  Help in **decision making and control**.

9.  Guide **dividend** action.

10. Information for **granting credit**.

- SN (7): support planning · estimate earnings · support investment
  decisions · judge effectiveness · data on available resources ·
  changes in net resources from operations · other user needs.

- Objective QB: main objective of a financial statement = know
  **solvency and debt capacity** (all of the above).

- SN limitation (FAQ): statements give **absolute numbers** but not
  earning potential, liquidity or financial stability on their own.

**5. Tools of analysis**

- Ebook: **comparative statements** and **common-size statements**. SN:
  comparative, common-size, **ratio analysis** (quantitative), **trend
  analysis**; also cash flow analysis.

|              | **Comparative statement**                       | **Common-size statement**                                                                                                   |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Also called  | **Horizontal analysis**                         | **Vertical analysis**                                                                                                       |
| What it does | Figures of **two or more periods side by side** | Each item as a **% of a common base**                                                                                       |
| Shows        | Trend and direction of position and results     | Composition / structure of the statement                                                                                    |
| Base         | Previous year                                   | Balance sheet: **total assets** (or total funds = 100) · Income statement: **revenue / sales = 100** (total assets for FIs) |
| Best for     | Same firm across periods                        | Firms of **different sizes** in the same industry; intra-firm and inter-firm                                                |
| Condition    | Same accounting principles each period          |                                                                                                                             |

**Comparative statements (horizontal)**

- May show (4): **absolute figures** · **changes** in absolute figures ·
  absolute data as **%** · increase or decrease in **%**.

- Types: **comparative balance sheet** (trend of same items on different
  dates; SN: main goal = assess **short and long-term solvency**) ·
  **comparative income statement** (progress over time; profitability).

- **Absolute change = Current year − Previous year**. **% change =
  Absolute change ÷ Previous year × 100** (SN).

- SN comparative balance sheet format: Sources of funds (**owned** +
  **borrowed** funds) vs Application of funds (fixed assets +
  investments + working capital).

**Common-size statements (vertical)**

- Brings numbers to a **common base**, so they compare easily with
  previous years or other firms.

- SN example: building 4,00,000 ÷ total funds 16,00,000 × 100 = **25%**.

- SN advantages: compares profitability and position of 2+ firms ·
  standardises item sizes · links each item to total assets /
  liabilities or to sales.

> **Trap (Objective QB Q1):** *False statements: "all balance-sheet
> figures as % of total = **horizontal**" (it is **vertical**) and
> "vertical analysis is **dynamic**" (vertical is static; horizontal,
> across years, is dynamic). So answer = **Both A and C**. Statement B
> ("several years analysed = vertical") is marked true by the LMS key;
> the Ebook calls multi-period comparison horizontal, so treat this key
> with care.*
>
> **Knowledge Check (SN):** *Comparative statements show 2+ years side
> by side = **True** · comparative analysis is also called vertical
> analysis = **False** (horizontal) · absolute change = current −
> previous = **True**.*

**6. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Wrong belief**                                       | **Correct**                                            |
|--------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Statements are only historical; useless for the future | Trend and ratio analysis help **forecast**             |
| Only for large corporations                            | Useful for **all sizes**                               |
| Only finance professionals analyse                     | Management, investors, lenders, trade payables too     |
| Once prepared, no analysis needed                      | Preparation is the first step; ongoing analysis needed |
| Analysis is only about profitability                   | Also liquidity, solvency, efficiency                   |
