> and discounting (present value) with formulas. **Capital budgeting**:
> meaning, factors, nature, significance and **5 techniques** (payback,
> ARR, IRR, NPV, PI) with merits and demerits. The **Study Notes** add

**1. Time value of money (TVM)**

- Ebook: money available **now is worth more** than the same amount in
  the future, because of its **potential earning capacity**.

- Provided money can earn interest, any amount is worth more **the
  sooner it is received**. Linked to the later concept of **time
  preference**. "A dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow."

- Study Notes: value changes over time because of **inflation,
  opportunity cost, strike price and money supply**. TVM is also called
  **present discounted value**. Discounted cash flow analysis = TVM
  (Objective QB).

> **Analogy (memory aid):** *A patient seen today versus the same
> patient seen in six months: the earlier intervention is worth more
> because it can start compounding benefit now. Same logic for a rupee.*

**Five components of TVM (Study Notes)**

| **Component**          | **Point**                                                               |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Rate of interest**   | Fixed at start; inflation is volatile                                   |
| **Time period (n)**    | Longer period makes TVM diminish; short/moderate = predictable          |
| **Present value (PV)** | Current value of a future amount: **PV = FV / (1 + r)^n**               |
| **Future value (FV)**  | Value later of money held now: **FV = PV × (1 + r)^n**                  |
| **Instalments (PMT)**  | Periodic loan payments: **PMT = P × r × (1 + r)^n / \[(1 + r)^n − 1\]** |

- Uses of TVM (SN, 7): investment calculation · discounting in capital
  budgeting · **loans and mortgages** · personal/retirement plans · real
  estate · insurance · **value and volatility** (volatility measured by
  **VIX**; lower-value assets have **higher** volatility, an inverse
  relation).

**2. Compounding and discounting**

|                  | **Compounding**                                             | **Discounting**                                    |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Direction        | Present → **future value**                                  | Future → **present value**                         |
| Idea             | Interest on principal **and accrued interest** (reinvested) | Apply a **discount rate** to future cash flows     |
| Single cash flow | **Amount × (1 + R)^n**                                      | **Amount × 1 / (1 + R)^n**                         |
| Annuity          | **Amount × \[(1 + R)^n − 1\] / R**                          | **Amount × \[(1 + R)^n − 1\] / \[R(1 + R)^n\]**    |
| Series           |                                                             | PV = FV1/(1+R)^1 + FV2/(1+R)^2 + ... + FVn/(1+R)^n |

- n = number of years; R = rate of return (compounding) or discount rate
  (discounting).

- SN: compound interest differs from **simple interest** (simple =
  interest on initial principal only). Compounding frequency: annual,
  biannual, quarterly, monthly.

> **Source error (Study Notes):** *The Study Notes say "the formula for
> discounting is the same as FV" and "compounding is the same as PV".
> That is **reversed**. Compounding gives **FV**; discounting gives
> **PV** (Ebook, Study Guide and Objective QB all agree).*
>
> **QB key conflict:** *Objective QB Q1 marks "**Higher than**" for
> "value of money received in the future is \_\_\_ the same amount
> today". Every definition in the Ebook says money today is worth
> **more**, so future money is worth **less**. If this exact question
> appears, the concept answer is "lower than"; be aware the LMS key may
> expect "higher than".*

**3. Inflation (Study Notes)**

- Fall in **purchasing power** of money due to a rise in prices.
  Calculated annually as a %. Measured by **CPI** (consumer view) and
  **PPI** (producer / seller view).

- **Inflation = (Price Year 2 − Price Year 1) / Price Year 1 × 100**.

- Hits salaried more than businesspeople; **pensioners worst hit**;
  redistributes wealth.

| **Type**           | **Meaning**                                                       |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Moderate**       | **Healthy**; RBI targets a predetermined rate                     |
| **Hyperinflation** | Prices rise out of central bank control; can collapse the economy |
| **Deflation**      | Sustained **fall** in prices; as harmful as hyperinflation        |

- Factors (8): **panic buying** (COVID) · **familiarity bias** ·
  **confirmation bias** · **recency (experiential) bias** · **loss
  aversion** · demand \> supply · cost of production · cost of labour.

- Capital (SN) = money needed for a business's initiation, expansion and
  growth (not only cash: machinery, materials, labour, technology).
  Banking began in **Babylon ~3000 BC** (temple storage).

**4. Capital budgeting: meaning and factors**

- Ebook: process in which a business **determines and evaluates
  potential large expenses or investments** (new plant, long-term
  venture).

- Study Guide: capital expenditure (fixed assets, repairs, R&D,
  expansion) + budgeting (setting targets). Also called **investment
  appraisal**.

**Factors affecting capital budgeting (Ebook, 6)**

| **Factor**                | **Point**                                                                       |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Technological changes** | Study cost and efficiency of new vs old equipment                               |
| **Demand forecast**       | Long-period demand analysis                                                     |
| **Competitive strategy**  | Follow competitor's high-capacity, cost-effective machinery                     |
| **Type of management**    | **Innovative** management invests more than conservative                        |
| **Cash flow**             | Cash budget shows **when** to invest                                            |
| **Other**                 | Fiscal policy (tax concessions, rebates), political stability, global situation |

**Nature (Ebook, 7)**

- Exchange of **current funds** for **future benefits** · benefits over
  a **series of years** · funds in **non-flexible, long-term**
  activities · long-term effect on profitability · **huge funds** ·
  **irreversible** · **strategic** decisions with high risk.

**Significance (Ebook, 5)**

- **Long-term commitment** (greater risk, greater need) ·
  **irreversible** (disposal causes heavy loss) · **large investments**
  (demand for funds exceeds resources) · **difficulty** of decisions
  (uncertainty; loss of flexibility and liquidity) · **long-term effect
  on profitability**.

- SN: significant for owners, shareholders, organisation, **nation**,
  public, employees, government. Unlimited wants, limited resources.
  Finance manager follows **safety, liquidity, profitability**.

**Study Notes 5-step procedure**

- Capital management → forecasting → budgeting → financial analysis →
  **reporting**.

**5. Techniques of capital budgeting**

| **Technique**               | **Formula / rule**                                                                                     | **Uses TVM?** |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------|
| **Payback period**          | Years to recover original outlay. Constant inflows: **outlay ÷ annual inflow**. Shorter is better      | **No**        |
| **ARR**                     | **Average income after tax ÷ average investment**. Uses accounting profit                              | **No**        |
| **IRR**                     | Rate at which **NPV = 0**; equates PV of inflows with PV of outflows. Accept if \> required rate       | Yes           |
| **NPV**                     | **PV of inflows − PV of outflows**, discounted at the **cost of capital**. Accept if positive          | Yes           |
| **PI (benefit-cost ratio)** | **PV of cash inflows ÷ initial outlay**. \> 1 accept, \< 1 reject, = 1 indifferent. Net PI = gross − 1 | Yes           |

- Traditional (non-discounting): payback, ARR. Discounted cash flow:
  IRR, NPV, PI.

**Payback period**

- Most **popular, widely recognised traditional** method. Also called
  **payout** period.

| **Advantages**                                           | **Disadvantages**                                     |
|----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------|
| Shorter payback = favourable short-run effect on **EPS** | Ignores cash inflows **after** payback                |
| Handles **risk**: shorter period guards against loss     | Not a measure of **profitability**                    |
| Shows **liquidity** of the project (early recovery)      | Ignores **pattern** (magnitude and timing) of inflows |
|                                                          | Hard to fix the maximum acceptable period             |

**Accounting rate of return (ARR)**

| **Advantages**                       | **Disadvantages**                                              |
|--------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Simple to understand and use         | Uses **accounting profits, not cash flows**                    |
| Calculated from accounting data      | Ignores **TVM**                                                |
| Uses the **entire stream** of income | Ignores length of project life; ignores reinvestment of profit |

**Internal rate of return (IRR)**

- Called "internal" because it depends **solely on the project's outlay
  and proceeds**, not on any rate determined outside.

- Equation: C = A1/(1+r)^1 + A2/(1+r)^2 + ... + An/(1+r)^n, i.e. 0 = Σ
  At/(1+r)^t − C.

| **Advantages**                                         | **Disadvantages**                                                                                                |
|--------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Considers TVM                                          | **Complicated** computation                                                                                      |
| Cash flows over the **entire life**                    | May give **no unique answer**: negative or **multiple** rates                                                    |
| Users like a **rate of return** figure                 | Assumes reinvestment **at the IRR** (NPV assumes reinvestment at **cost of capital**, which is more appropriate) |
| **Cost of capital not needed** in advance (unlike NPV) |                                                                                                                  |
| Compatible with maximising owners' welfare             |                                                                                                                  |

**Net present value (NPV)**

- NPV = \[A1/(1+k)^1 + A2/(1+k)^2 + ... + An/(1+k)^n\] − C, where **k =
  cost of capital**, C = initial outlay.

| **Advantages**                                 | **Disadvantages**                                                          |
|------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Recognises TVM                                 | Difficult to use                                                           |
| All cash flows over project life               | Presupposes **discount rate (cost of capital) is known**; hard in practice |
| Consistent with **maximising owners' welfare** | Unsatisfactory when projects have **different investment sizes**           |

**Profitability index (PI)**

- Ratio of PV of future cash benefits (at required rate) to initial cash
  outflow. Also called **benefit-cost (BC) ratio**. May be **gross** or
  **net** (net = gross − 1).

- Advantages: considers TVM · more computation than traditional methods
  but **less than IRR** · can rank **mutually exclusive** projects via
  incremental BC ratio.

- SN: a project with PI 2 is more viable than one with PI 1.5.

> **Illustration (my numbers, for method only):** *Outlay 1,00,000;
> inflow 25,000 a year for 5 years. Payback = 1,00,000 ÷ 25,000 = **4
> years**. If PV of the five inflows at the cost of capital were
> 1,10,000: NPV = **+10,000** (accept), PI = 1,10,000 ÷ 1,00,000 =
> **1.1** (accept).*
>
> **Analogy (trading):** *NPV is your expected edge in rupees after
> discounting; IRR is the return % at which the trade only breaks even;
> payback is how fast you get your margin back, which tells you about
> liquidity, not profit.*
>
> **Knowledge Check:** *Capital budgeting is also called **annual**
> appraisal = **False** (it is **investment** appraisal) · payback
> recovers initial investment **with** profits = **False** · human wants
> are limited = **False** · project profitable only if PI \> 1 =
> **True**.*

**6. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Wrong belief**                       | **Correct**                                                          |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Capital means only cash                | Also machinery, raw materials, labour, technology                    |
| TVM is only about interest rates       | Also time, inflation, opportunity cost                               |
| Inflation hits all classes equally     | Middle class more than businesspeople; **pensioners most**           |
| All inflation is harmful               | **Moderate** inflation is healthy; hyperinflation and deflation harm |
| Capital budgeting is only about profit | Also strategic planning, forecasting, budgeting, analysis, reporting |

**7. High-yield exam anchors (Objective QB)**

| **Question**                                       | **Answer (LMS key)**                                                    |
|----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Future money is \_\_\_ the same amount today       | **Higher than** (key; concept says lower, see conflict box)             |
| TVM must be considered in outlay decisions because | **Cash inflows and outflows occur at different points**                 |
| Money has time value because                       | **Possibility of earning a risk-free return on money invested today**   |
| TVM compares cash flows at different times by      | **Discounting or compounding to a common point** (either)               |
| Basic rule of TVM                                  | **A rupee today is worth more than one promised later**                 |
| PV of a future amount                              | **Always less than FV and calculable if rate and periods known** (both) |
| Future or present value depends on                 | **Interest rate and number of periods** (all)                           |
| FV from PV is called                               | **Compounding**                                                         |
| Rate of return an investment provides              | **Internal rate of return**                                             |
| Discounted cash flow analysis is also called       | **Time value of money**                                                 |

- Lock these: FV = PV(1+r)^n · PV = FV/(1+r)^n · payback = outlay ÷
  annual inflow · ARR = avg income ÷ avg investment · IRR: NPV = 0 · NPV
  at **cost of capital** · PI \> 1 accept · payback and ARR **ignore
  TVM** · IRR may give **multiple rates** · NPV struggles with **unequal
  project sizes**.
