**1. What is e-commerce?**

- Ebook: e-commerce = exchange executed using **ICTs**, between
  companies, companies and customers, or companies and public
  administration. It includes **electronic trading of both goods and
  electronic material** (QB answer: "electronic").

- QB: e-commerce is "a **systematic and organized network** for the
  exchange of goods between producers and consumers".

| **Author**             | **Definition (key words)**                                                                                                          |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Greenstein and Feinman | Use of electronic transmission media (**telecommunication**) to exchange products and services, transported physically or digitally |
| Turban and others      | **Buying and selling** (or exchanging) products, services and information via computer networks incl. the Internet                  |
| Rayport and Jaworski   | **Technology-mediated exchanges** between parties + intra/inter-organisational activities that facilitate them                      |
| WTO                    | Production, distribution, marketing, sale or delivery of goods and services **electronically**                                      |

- Everyday uses: card authorisation, travel reservation, wire transfers,
  POS, e-banking, e-insurance, online education, auctions. **Caution:**
  e-commerce and e-business are **not** the same thing.

- **Disintermediation** = removing the middleman (better profits, lower
  prices). **Reintermediation** = creating new middlemen, e.g. escrow
  services and eBay connecting buyers and sellers.

- Before the Web, firms exchanged documents through proprietary **IOIS**
  such as EDI over leased **VANs** (costly). Internet **e-procurement**
  systems and B2B marketplaces are **open** systems.

- SN benefits: global reach, 24/7 availability, lower costs,
  personalisation, data insight. Challenges: **security and privacy**,
  logistics, competition, trust.

**2. E-commerce models**

| **Model** | **Ebook meaning**                                                                                    | **Examples**                                                     |
|-----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| B2C       | Businesses serving **end consumers** with products/services (QB)                                     | Amazon (books, reviews, gift wrap); buying shoes from a retailer |
| B2B       | Businesses selling goods and services to **other businesses**; the **largest** segment of e-commerce | Safetylogic.com, Extensity, StaplesLink, Alibaba (SN)            |
| C2B       | Consumers use an **online agent** to look for a product or service that suits them                   | **Priceline.com**, ShopBot, AutobyTel                            |
| C2C       | Businesses that **act as agents between consumers** with goods/services to sell                      | **eBay**, Yahoo! Auctions, Excite classifieds                    |
| P2P       | Exchange of information (files, money) between PCs or hand-held devices                              | **Napster**, PayPal, ProPay, Ecount                              |

> **QB trap:** *"**Consumer to Business** act as agents between
> consumers with goods and services to sell" = **False**. That sentence
> is the Ebook definition of **C2C**.*

- SN business models: **subscription** (Netflix, Spotify),
  **aggregator/marketplace** (Uber, Airbnb), **freemium** (Dropbox),
  plus B2C (Amazon) and B2B (Alibaba).

- B2C activities include online ordering, payment and **customer
  reporting** = giving statements of account and order status to
  customers online (QB: **True**).

**3. Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)**

- Developed in the **early 1960s** to speed up shipment and transport
  documents; widely used since the **1980s** in automotive, retail and
  international trade.

- Standardised messages for structured data between computer
  applications: orders, invoices, payments. Used for **regular repeat
  transactions**, not search or negotiation. Part of **JIT** and
  quick-response supply. Mature EDI enables **mass customisation**.

| **Source**                            | **Definition**                                                                                                                                 |
|---------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ANSI (Accredited Standards Committee) | Transmission, in a **standard syntax**, of unambiguous information of business significance between computers of **independent organisations** |
| UN/EDIFACT Training Guide             | Interchange of standard formatted data between trading partners' application systems with **minimal manual intervention**                      |

- SN standards: **ANSI X12** and **EDIFACT**. LMS answer: EDI =
  exchanging data **in a standardised format between businesses**.

- **VAN (Value Added Network)** = private network provider hired to
  facilitate EDI. Collects forms in an electronic mailbox, translates
  and forwards them; sold by subscription (pay per data moved). Good for
  firms without network expertise.

**4. Intranets**

- **Intranet** = private network contained within an enterprise.
  Connected to the Internet, it sits behind a **firewall**; if it allows
  access from the Internet it becomes an **extranet**.

- Uses: **event-driven publishing** (catalogues, handbooks, directories
  updated instantly), **group collaboration** (U.S. West Global
  Village), functional applications in finance, sales/marketing,
  manufacturing and HR.

- **Ford** opened its intranet to suppliers: seats arrive in the colour
  sequence needed, so every car is customised while costs fall.

- SN security: **VPN**, **multi-factor authentication**. LMS answer: an
  intranet gives employees a **secure internal communication network**.

**5. Telecommunication and payments**

- **Telecommunication** = transmission of information from one point to
  another through a communication medium (QB). Model: **source → encoder
  → channel → decoder → destination**. The **channel** is the path from
  source to destination (QB).

- **Videoconferencing** over ISDN or IP lines; builds familiarity with
  people you may never meet. SN: Zoom, Teams. Not exclusive to large
  firms.

- **Wireless payment** card-based types: **dual-slot**, **dual-chip**,
  **one-chip** (SIM + financial function). The system has two parts:

  **Initialization process** = one-time preparation: download from the
  GVM server, set password, enter card, server fetches certificate, card
  details saved on the device (8 steps; card change restarts at step 4).

  **Transaction process** = the procedure of payment for purchases
  through the mobile device **on and off line**; authentication server
  returns an admission number.

> **QB trap:** *"The **initialization** process is a procedure of
> payment for purchases through the user's mobile device on and off
> lines" = **False**. That describes the **transaction** process.*

- SN: NFC and **mobile wallets** (Apple Pay, Google Pay); **VoIP**;
  **RFID** works without line of sight.

**6. Bandwidth and transmission media**

- **Bandwidth** = data rate supported by a network connection or
  interface (QB); measured in **bits per second**; the primary measure
  of network speed. Dial-up modem: **56 Kbps** peak but **53 Kbps** in
  practice.

| **Medium**                    | **Key facts (Ebook)**                                                                                                      |
|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| UTP (unshielded twisted pair) | 8 copper wires; Cat 1-6 (Cat 4 = Token Ring 16 Mbps, Cat 5 = 100 Mbps); **RJ-45**; max **100 m**; cheap but prone to noise |
| STP (shielded)                | Shield reduces interference; max 100 m                                                                                     |
| Coaxial                       | 10-100 Mbps; max **500 m**; **Thicknet** (backbone, vampire tap), **Thinnet** (10BASE2); **BNC** connectors                |

**7. Networks**

| **Network** | **Key facts**                                                                                                             |
|-------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| LAN         | Within a building/campus; share resources; technologies **Ethernet (Xerox)**, Token Ring, **FDDI** (backbone)             |
| MAN         | City-sized, **5 to 50 km**; high-speed sharing of regional resources                                                      |
| WAN         | State or country; leased circuits; banking, airline, brokerage networks; Network Service Providers form the Internet core |
| VPN         | Private network configured **within a public network**; Internet VPNs give secure encrypted links (run by ISPs)           |
| VAN         | Hired private provider for EDI and other services                                                                         |

- SN network design: **redundancy** (backup paths), **load balancing**
  (spreads traffic across servers so performance stays seamless),
  **CDNs** (cache content near users, cut latency, **speed up page
  loads**), security.

- SN infrastructure: **routers direct data traffic between networks**;
  **switches** connect devices inside a LAN; servers store and serve.

**8. Protocols and internet services**

- **Protocol** = the set of rules endpoints use when they communicate.
  Ebook list: **TCP/IP, HTTP, FTP, UDP, IRP, ICMP, IGMP, SMTP**.

| **Protocol / service** | **Role**                                                                                           |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| TCP/IP                 | Most widespread; open; interoperability across all hardware                                        |
| HTTP / HTTPS           | Web pages ("404 Not Found"); **HTTPS adds SSL/TLS encryption = secure transaction data** (SN, LMS) |
| FTP                    | Oldest; file transfer between hosts; control + data connections                                    |
| SMTP                   | E-mail                                                                                             |
| DNS                    | Domain name → IP address (and supports other services)                                             |
| DHCP                   | Assigns IP addresses dynamically and manages connections                                           |
| NAT                    | **Maps private to public IP addresses** (LMS)                                                      |
| Proxy                  | Intermediary between users and the Internet                                                        |

- LMS: protocols matter because they make online transactions **secure
  and reliable**.

> **Misconceptions (LMS):** *DNS is not only for websites · load
> balancing is not only crash prevention, it optimises resources · DHCP
> does more than assign IPs · network design is not only bandwidth
> (redundancy, security) · DNS, DHCP and load balancing are **not
> interchangeable**.*

**9. The digital organisation (SN Part 2)**

- **Digital organisation** = **integration of digital tools and
  technologies** into all aspects of the business, not just a sleek
  website.

- **Digital transformation** enables **agile and flexible structures**,
  faster decisions and collaboration. It is a mindset change and helps
  firms of every size (Misconceptions).

- **5G** enhances real-time communication; also edge computing, IoT,
  **SDN/NFV**, convergence of voice, data and video.

| **Tool**                                | **Best for**                                                                |
|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| E-mail                                  | Formal, documented communication                                            |
| Messaging apps (Slack, Teams, WhatsApp) | **Real-time, informal** communication                                       |
| Collaborative platforms                 | Shared documents and projects                                               |
| Unified communication systems           | **Integrate diverse channels** (voice, video, chat, e-mail) in one platform |
