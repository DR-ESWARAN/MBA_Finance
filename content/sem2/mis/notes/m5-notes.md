**1. The wireless revolution and business value**

- Mobile phones are now platforms for photos, video, music, Internet
  access and **payments**. Firms use wireless to cut costs, increase
  flexibility and create new products.

- Universal access needs: low-cost terrestrial wireless, local
  micro-enterprises offering value-added services, and **supportive
  public policy** (spectrum licence exemptions).

| **Ebook business value of wireless** |                                                                                                               |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1                                    | **Wireless communication** helps businesses easily stay in touch with customers, suppliers and employees (QB) |
| 2                                    | Higher worker productivity: less time to reach people and information                                         |
| 3                                    | Savings on wiring offices, moving and network changes                                                         |
| 4                                    | New products, services and sales channels (OnStar, Starbucks hot spots)                                       |

- SN adds: efficiency, **cost-effectiveness**, **mobility**, real-time
  communication, better customer engagement. Misconception: wireless is
  not only about personal devices.

**2. Wireless network basics (Ebook)**

| **Link**                                   | **Analogy**                                 | **Term**              |
|--------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Point-to-point (tower A to B)              | Spotlight, focused beam                     | **Wireless backhaul** |
| Point-to-multipoint (tower B to buildings) | Theatrical light, broad beam from a **hub** | **WMAN**              |
| Access point inside a building             | Bare light bulb, lights the room            | **WLAN**              |

- The **access point** inside the building gives the **WLAN** connection
  (QB). WLANs and point-to-multipoint WMANs let multiple subscribers
  **share the same hub** (QB). More focused beams travel further with
  less interference and power.

**3. WiMAX**

- **WiMAX = Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access** (QB);
  **IEEE 802.16**; meant for wireless **metropolitan area networks**.
  WiMAX Forum formed June 2001.

- Range: up to **30 miles (50 km)** fixed, **3-10 miles (5-15 km)**
  mobile. Wi-Fi (802.11) only **100-300 feet (30-100 m)**.

- With WiMAX, Wi-Fi-like data rates are easily supported **and
  interference is lessened**. Works on licensed and unlicensed
  frequencies.

> **QB trap:** *"With WiMAX ... the issue of interference is
> **increased**" = **False**. The Ebook says **lessened**.*

- WiMAX (and Wi-Fi) are **certification marks**, not technologies. Uses:
  link Wi-Fi hotspots, **last-mile broadband** alternative to cable/DSL,
  4G mobile data, business continuity backup, nomadic connectivity.

- Limits: 70 Mbit/s and 70 miles are **not simultaneously** true; high
  bandwidth **or** long reach, like DSL. Bandwidth is shared within a
  radio sector.

- SN: Wi-Max uses **point-to-multipoint** architecture and **OFDM**;
  base stations talk to subscriber units; benefits are high-speed
  access, extended coverage, scalability, last mile. Misconception:
  Wi-Max and Wi-Fi are **not** the same (Wi-Max = long range).

**4. EVDO and CDMA**

- **EV-DO** (Evolution-Data Optimized / Data Only) = telecom standard
  for wireless data through **radio signals**, typically broadband
  Internet (QB). Standardised by **3GPP2** in the CDMA2000 family; one
  of two major **3G** standards (rival: **W-CDMA**, preferred in
  Europe).

- Developed by **Qualcomm**; uses CDMA and TDMA; **packet-based** (IP)
  so no bandwidth used when silent; theoretical **2.4 Mbps** (Rev 0),
  3.1 Mbps (Rev A); channel 1.25 MHz. Uses existing CDMA frequencies, so
  cheaper. Deployed by Verizon, Sprint, Korea.

- **CDMA (Code Division Multiple Access)** uses advanced mathematical
  techniques so **multiple wireless devices transmit simultaneously on
  the same frequency** (QB True); each device has a unique mathematical
  signature.

- EVDO advantages over Wi-Fi: always on with seamless roaming, no 300-ft
  limit, corporate VPN anywhere, real-time video, service beyond DSL
  areas.

**5. M-commerce**

- **M-commerce** = buying and selling of goods and services through
  **wireless handheld devices** such as cell phones and PDAs.
  "Next-generation e-commerce"; based on **WAP (Wireless Application
  Protocol)**; stronger in Europe.

> **QB trap:** *"M-commerce is the buying and selling of goods and
> services through **agent**" = **False**. It is through wireless
> handheld devices.*

- Industries: financial services (mobile banking, brokerage), telecom,
  service/retail, information services.

| **Future features**                   | **Specific advantages**                           |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **Ubiquity** — location independent   | Context-specific (location-based) services        |
| **Immediacy** — real-time             | Time-critical situations                          |
| **Localization** — GPS-based offers   | Spontaneous decisions (small purchases)           |
| **Instant connectivity** — GPRS       | Efficiency increase (use "dead spots" in the day) |
| **Simple authentication** — SIM + PIN |                                                   |

- SN: mobile wallets (**Apple Pay**, Google Pay, Samsung Pay), mobile
  banking, in-app purchases, platforms using **NFC** or QR codes.
  Security: encryption, **biometric authentication**, MFA.

| **SN sector** | **Example apps**     |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Retail        | **Amazon**, Walmart  |
| Banking       | Chase Mobile, PayPal |
| Travel        | Airbnb, Uber         |
| Food          | Starbucks, Uber Eats |

- Misconception: m-commerce is **not limited to retail** (banking,
  ticketing, healthcare, education, entertainment).

- Ebook "killer app": ordering a book takes about **140 clicks on a PC
  vs 350 on a WAP phone**; SMS is the only popular app (10-12% of ARPU).
  Success = the **killer process**, and each customer picks a personal
  killer app.

**6. CRM (Ebook) and wireless CRM (SN)**

- **CRM** = a multifaceted process, mediated by IT, creating **two-way
  exchanges** with customers for intimate knowledge of needs, wants and
  buying patterns. Keyword: the philosophy, policy and coordinating
  strategy connecting players to create valuable customer experiences.

- Failures come from software-only installs, unrealistic vendor claims
  and **data quality**. When gathering **data** for CRM, firms must
  consider **customer privacy and data security** with respect to legal
  and cultural environments (QB).

- Applications: **sales force automation** (contact and lead
  management), **eCRM** (self-service, survey management), customer
  service (call centre, help desk), **partner relationship management**
  (contract and distribution management).

| **Four principles for mobile customers** |                                                                                         |
|------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1                                        | **Target** customers to build critical mass: find **innovators** and **talkers** first  |
| 2                                        | **Match products** to customers; seed the market with basics                            |
| 3                                        | Make **acquisition** positive: awareness → relevance → purchase                         |
| 4                                        | **Develop customers one at a time**: understand, permission, educate, reinforce, direct |

- **Web-based (on-demand, SaaS) vs hosted CRM:** SaaS (Salesforce,
  NetSuite) suits small or short-lived firms; key limitation = **lack of
  customisability**.

- SN **wireless CRM**: **real-time access** to customer data, better
  communication, mobility, data sync. Tools: mobile CRM apps, **cloud
  CRM (remote access from anywhere)**, mobile-friendly platforms,
  offline access. Benefits: personalised responses, 360-degree view,
  omni-channel, real-time sales insight, better forecasting.

- Misconception: wireless CRM helps strategy and resource allocation
  too, not only customer-facing teams.

**7. Supply chain**

- **SCM** = planning, implementing and controlling supply chain
  operations as efficiently as possible; spans raw materials, WIP and
  finished goods from origin to consumption. Loosely coupled network =
  **Extended Enterprise**.

- Challenges: distribution network configuration, distribution strategy,
  information, inventory, cash flow. Activities at **strategic, tactical
  and operational** levels. Models: **SCOR**, GSCF. **Reverse
  logistics** = inbound flow of secondary goods ("aftermarket customer
  services").

- Mobile SCM captures goods movements accurately and lets users react
  quickly.

- SN wireless SCM: real-time data, mobile devices, **RFID and GPS**
  tracking, VoIP. Benefits: **reduced lead times**, better forecasting,
  **real-time data sharing for collaboration**, proactive issue
  resolution, **dynamic routing** (traffic, weather), **route
  optimisation**, **just-in-time** inventory.

- Misconception: wireless SCM is more than real-time data (devices,
  asset tracking, communication).

**8. Healthcare**

- Ebook: healthcare is the **largest service industry**. INET wireless
  healthcare **portal** gives one point of contact for patient data,
  medical knowledge, guidelines and reimbursement rules. Four goals:
  **improve patient care, reduce transaction costs, increase quality,
  enhance teaching and research**.

- SN: **real-time patient monitoring**, efficient communication, EHR
  access at the point of care, patient portals. **Telemedicine** =
  **remote consultations**. Remote monitoring for chronic or
  post-surgical patients. **Wearables** (smartwatches, fitness trackers)
  track activity, heart rate and **sleep patterns**. Smart pill
  dispensers aid adherence. Predictive analytics for disease management.
