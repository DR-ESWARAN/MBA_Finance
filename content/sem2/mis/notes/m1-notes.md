**1. Information and information systems**

- MIS is an **old management tool** used for better management and
  scientific decision-making. **Information** is the **most critical
  resource** of an MIS (Ebook).

- Information systems are as old as the hills: kings, the church,
  **Tainali Rama and Akbar** in India, and the **merchants of Venice**
  all ran their own information systems.

- **Ebook definition:** an information system is "a collection of
  elements that **capture data**, convert it into **information** and
  **disseminate** it to the decision-makers in an organisation."

- **SN definition:** an integrated set of components that collect,
  process, store and disseminate data and information to support
  decision-making and control. IS = **people, processes, data and
  technology**.

- An IS differs from other systems: its objective is to monitor or
  document the operations of another system, the **target system**. It
  cannot exist without one (production scheduling system → production;
  HRIS → human resources; even a vending machine has an IS sub-system).

- The IS triangle: **hardware, software and "liveware" (people)**. Input
  → processing → output → **feedback**. Feedback is the most important
  step and the one most often overlooked. Hardware + software alone =
  **computer literacy**; add liveware and feedback = **information
  literacy**.

| **Component (SN)** | **What it is**                                                         |
|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hardware           | Computers, servers, storage, networking devices                        |
| Software           | Operating systems, databases, specialised applications ("heart" of IS) |
| Data               | **Raw material** of IS: text, numbers, images                          |
| Procedures         | Rules and methods that guide use and control of IS                     |
| People             | Users, developers, administrators; the most important part             |

**Why business needs IT (Ebook, 3 reasons)**

1.  Support of **business operations**

2.  Support of **managerial decision making**

3.  Support of **strategic competitive advantage**

- Strategic importance of IS (SN): **efficiency**, **competitive
  advantage**, **decision support**, **global reach**, **security and
  compliance**.

- Ebook drivers of the need for IS: the **competitive business
  environment**, **emergence of the global economy** (no "Webmaster" job
  existed in 1991) and **transformation of the business enterprise**
  (job cuts moved from blue-collar in the 80s to white-collar management
  in the 90s).

**2. Types of information systems (SN)**

| **System**                           | **Who / what**                                             | **Key words for MCQs**                                                                             |
|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **TPS** Transaction Processing       | Operational level; backbone of retail, finance, healthcare | **Routine, repetitive daily transactions**; capture, store, process; accuracy                      |
| **MIS** Management Information       | **Middle managers**                                        | **Summarised**, structured reports drawn from TPS; monitor performance and trends                  |
| **DSS** Decision Support             | Managers facing complex choices                            | **Semi-structured and unstructured** decisions; analytical models; **scenarios**                   |
| **EIS** Executive Information        | **Top executives**                                         | Strategic information, KPIs; summarised, user-friendly interface                                   |
| **ERP** Enterprise Resource Planning | Whole organisation                                         | **Integrated** software for finance, HR, production, supply chain; data sharing across departments |

- **Data vs information:** sales figures = data; a report that analyses
  which products sell best = information.

- Information as a **strategic resource**: competitive advantage,
  informed decisions (HR uses an **HRIS** for promotions), strategic
  planning (ERP consolidates data), risk management (fraud detection).

- IS improves **decision quality** through **timeliness** (real-time
  access), **accuracy**, **accessibility** (cloud, mobile, remote),
  **data analysis** and **decision support** (DSS).

**3. Digital convergence and the changing business environment**

- **Ebook / QB definition:** digital convergence is "an approach by
  which all types of media and communication will be **digitized**
  allowing them to be used through a **single worldwide network**."

- **SN view:** merging of technologies, devices and industries into a
  **unified digital environment**. Key aspects: **integration of
  technologies** (mobile, Internet, cloud, analytics, AI, IoT),
  **breaking down industry silos** (wearables and telemedicine in
  healthcare; connected cars), **enhanced connectivity**, **improved
  user experiences** (Netflix), **data integration**.

- **Interactive multimedia** and the "**information appliance**": one
  central device for TV, music, phone and computing. Telephone and cable
  TV mergers point to convergence of TV and the Internet.

- **Disintermediation** = eliminating the middleman from transactions
  (the music industry worry).

- **Impact of digital technologies on business (SN):** efficiency and
  productivity (ERP in manufacturing), customer-centricity (product
  recommendations), innovation and new business models (**Uber, Lyft**),
  global reach, **data-driven decision-making**, enhanced security.

**New organisational forms (Ebook)**

- To compete, firms adopt **less hierarchical, more flexible
  structures**, combining low cost, high quality, flexibility and quick
  response. Few firms can build their whole value chain alone.

- **Network** = "a specific kind of relationship joining a particular
  group of people, objects, or events" (QB). Two factors: a **group of
  elements** and **specific relationships** among them. In a network
  model, **juridically independent companies** form cooperative
  long-term links. A network is "a dynamic structure that allows
  consolidating the competitive position of its members", not a purpose
  in itself.

- **Interorganisational Information Systems (IOS)** improve the
  efficiency of a **group** of enterprises (traditional IT served
  internal processes). IT changes the nature, punctuality and detail of
  shared information, **reduces transaction costs** and **reduces
  co-ordination costs**.

- IT is **necessary but not enough** for competitiveness; management
  will and capability matter. **McKesson** (drug distributor helping
  small stores against big pharmacy chains) and **American Hospital
  Supply** show the network's value came from relationships along the
  value chain, not from computer links.

- Shared attributes in a network: objectives, experience, labour,
  decisions, responsibility, trust, reward. Operative IOS benefits: less
  paperwork, **lower stock**, faster material flow, standard procedures,
  faster demand information, **lower telecom costs**. Tools: **EDI**,
  shared databases, e-mail, video conferences.

**Changing business environment (Ebook)**

- Changes are classed as **political, social, economic and
  technological**. Customers are the focal point.

- Organisations have **five components**: IT; structure and culture;
  management and business processes; strategy; individuals and roles.
  They sit in **equilibrium** until a significant change makes the
  system unstable.

- By adopting IS a business can compete in **three ways**: change
  industry structure and the rules of competition; create competitive
  advantage; spawn new businesses.

- Most studies show IT **without matching organisational change** gives
  little gain.

**4. Business transformation: the Four R's (Ebook, QB favourite)**

**Definition:** "The orchestrated redesign of the **genetic
architecture** of the corporation, achieved simultaneously, although at
different speed, along the four dimensions of reframing, restructuring,
revitalization and renewal." It is a **biological model**.

| **R**              | **Meaning**                                                                                                         |
|--------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Reframing**      | Shifting the company's **conception of what it is** and what it can achieve; **new visions and new resolve** (mind) |
| **Restructuring**  | "Girding of corporate loins"; reach competitive performance; being **lean and fit** (body)                          |
| **Revitalization** | **Igniting growth** by linking the corporate body to the **environment**                                            |
| **Renewal**        | The **people side** and the **spirit** of the company; new skills and purposes so the company regenerates itself    |

> **QB trap:** *"Revitalization deals with the people side of
> transformation and the spirit of the company" is **False**. That is
> **Renewal**.*

**Five levels of IT-induced reconfiguration**

Plotted on two axes: **degree of business transformation** and **range
of potential benefits from IT**. Levels 1-2 are **evolutionary**
(incremental); levels 3-5 are **revolutionary** (fundamental change).

| **Level** | **Name**                            | **Focus**                                                                   |
|-----------|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1         | Localized exploitation (automation) | IT within single business functions                                         |
| 2         | Internal integration                | Technical + organisational integration on a common IT platform              |
| 3         | Business process redesign           | Reconfigure the business with IT as the **central lever**                   |
| 4         | Business network redesign           | Reconfigure scope and tasks of the business **network**                     |
| 5         | Business scope redefinition         | Enlarge the mission and scope, or shift the business with IT-enabled skills |

**5. Information and knowledge economy**

**Ebook: information work, OAS and KWS**

- **Information work** is the **"art"** of creating and processing
  information.

- **Data workers** process and distribute information; **knowledge
  workers** create knowledge and information. Ask: do they create
  original ideas, make their own decisions, set their own procedures?

- **Office Automation Systems (OAS)** distribute knowledge. **Document
  imaging systems** convert documents into digital form; documents not
  in use sit on an optical disk system called a **jukebox**; the **index
  server** locates them. Example: banks keep digital images of cheques.
  The gain comes only if workflows are redesigned.

- **Knowledge Work Systems (KWS)** create knowledge: they keep the
  organisation **up to date in knowledge**, serve as **internal
  consultants** and act as **change agents**.

**SN: the knowledge economy**

- An economy where generating and exploiting **knowledge** drives
  production. Features: **intellectual capital** valued above tangible
  assets, **ICT** as enabler, **innovation**, a **highly skilled
  workforce** (lifelong learning), **globalisation**.

- Role of knowledge in organisations: innovation, decision-making,
  customer understanding, competitive advantage, learning,
  collaboration, digital transformation.

| **Level**       | **Meaning**                                             | **Temperature example (SN)**                   |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| **Data**        | Raw facts without context                               | 32°F, 68°F, 89°F                               |
| **Information** | Processed, structured data with meaning                 | "New York was 68°F at 2:00 PM"                 |
| **Knowledge**   | Information + understanding, experience, interpretation | 68°F in December in New York is unusually warm |

- **Managing data:** collection → storage → processing → analysis.
  **Leveraging data:** data-driven decisions, personalisation,
  performance optimisation.

- **Managing knowledge:** creation, **capture** (so it is not lost when
  staff leave), sharing. **Leveraging knowledge:** problem solving,
  innovation, competitive advantage.

**Intellectual property (SN)**

| **Tool**     | **Protects**                                       | **Term**                      |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Patent       | New and useful **inventions**                      | Typically **20 years**        |
| Copyright    | Original literary, artistic, musical **works**     | Creator's life + **70 years** |
| Trademark    | Symbols, names, slogans that identify products     | n/a                           |
| Trade secret | Confidential processes, customer lists, strategies | As long as kept secret        |
| Contracts    | **NDAs** and licensing agreements                  | n/a                           |
| DRM          | Digital content against copying and modification   | n/a                           |

- Digital-age issues: **digital piracy**, controversial **DRM**, **open
  source and Creative Commons**, international issues (**WIPO**),
  evolving laws (**DMCA** in the US, **GDPR** in the EU).

**6. Contemporary approach to IS (Ebook, QB)**

| **Approach**        | **Core idea**                                                                                   | **Disciplines / notes**                                                         |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Technical**       | **Mathematically based models**; physical technology and formal capabilities. "2 + 2 = 4"       | **Computer science, management science, operations research**                   |
| **Behavioural**     | Human issues: strategic integration, design, implementation, use, management. "2 + 2 = maybe 4" | Cannot be studied with technical models                                         |
| **Socio-technical** | Combines both; IS belongs to everyone                                                           | Technology may be "**de-optimized**" to fit organisational and individual needs |

- Computer science = theories of computability, computation, efficient
  storage. Management science = models for decision-making. Operations
  research = mathematical optimisation of transport, inventory,
  transaction costs.

**7. Management challenges (Ebook, QB)**

| **Challenge**                  | **Core point**                                                                                                                                                        |
|--------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Strategic business**         | Technology bought but unused. **Creating a digital firm and obtaining benefit is a long and difficult journey**; organisations may need redesign, not just automation |
| **Globalization**              | **A good MIS meets both domestic and foreign opportunities and challenges**; needs global standards, cross-cultural reporting, transnational processes                |
| **Information architecture**   | Decide the business, core competencies and goals first; these drive technology. Brittle IT platforms constrain strategy                                               |
| **IS investment**              | Managers see only hardware/software cost; how to get a payoff and corporate value                                                                                     |
| **Responsibility and control** | Humans drive technology; build systems people can control and understand                                                                                              |

- **IT architecture:** a conceptual design for implementing IT in an
  organisation: hardware, software and network platforms, data
  resources, application portfolio and IS organisation.

**Digital-era challenges (SN)**

- **Emerging MIS trends:** **big data** (3 Vs = **volume, velocity,
  variety**) and analytics; **cloud computing** (AWS, Azure; scalable,
  lower capex, remote access, but security and privacy concerns);
  **mobile and BYOD** (cost savings, flexibility, but data security).

- **Management challenges:** cybersecurity and data protection
  (breaches, ransomware, phishing; **GDPR** compliance), privacy
  concerns and regulations (**GDPR, CCPA**, data governance, data
  protection officers), change management (resistance, skills gap,
  strategic alignment).

- **Ethical issues:** privacy, security, intellectual property
  (plagiarism, piracy), **accessibility** (inclusive systems for all
  abilities), cyberbullying, **AI ethics (bias, discrimination,
  accountability)**.

- **Social issues:** **digital divide** (unequal access to technology
  and skills), job displacement, addiction and mental health,
  misinformation, e-waste, surveillance, cultural impacts.

**8. E-commerce and IoT (SN)**

- E-commerce roots: early **1990s** (Amazon, eBay); late-1990s **dot-com
  boom** and bubble. Models: **B2C, B2B, C2C, C2B**; SN gives **Airbnb**
  as a C2C example. M-commerce and social commerce; AI chatbots and AR.

- **E-business** is broader: buying and selling **plus internal
  processes**. Models: process integration, e-commerce,
  **e-procurement** (streamlines procurement: e-catalogues, supplier
  negotiation, online purchase orders), e-government, e-learning, online
  banking, e-marketing, social media management.

- **IoT:** network of physical objects with sensors and software
  exchanging data **over the Internet**. Concept: interconnected
  devices, data collection, communication, central control,
  **automation** (smart thermostat), scalability.

- IoT in business: supply chain (GPS on containers), smart manufacturing
  (**Industry 4.0**, predictive maintenance), asset tracking, retail
  (**smart shelves track inventory**, in-store beacons), healthcare
  (wearables), smart buildings, fleet management.
