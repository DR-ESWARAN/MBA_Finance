**1. Utility and the purpose of business**

- **Utility** = want-satisfying power of a good or service. Four kinds:

| **Utility**                | **Meaning**                    | **Example (Ebook)**                 | **Created by** |
|----------------------------|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------|----------------|
| **Form**                   | Raw materials → finished goods | Pizza from ingredients              | **Production** |
| **Time**                   | Available **when** wanted      | Pizza in 30 min; 24×7 online retail | Marketing      |
| **Place**                  | Available **where** wanted     | Doorstep delivery; vending machines | Marketing      |
| **Ownership (possession)** | Transfer of title to buyer     | Pizza sale for cash/card            | Marketing      |

- **Peter Drucker** (*The Practice of Management*): the one valid
  purpose of a business is **to create a customer**; that purpose lies
  outside the business, in society.

- **Guiltinan and Paul**: creating a customer = identify needs → find
  which needs can be served profitably → develop an offering that
  converts potential buyers into customers.

- Activities of marketing managers: identify needs · design
  goods/services · communicate · make available at the right time and
  place · price (costs, competition, ability to buy) · after-sale
  service and follow-up.

**2. What is marketing**

| **Point**      | **Selling**                | **Marketing**                    |
|----------------|----------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Starting point | Factory                    | Marketplace                      |
| Focus          | Existing products          | Customer needs                   |
| Means          | Selling and promoting      | Integrated marketing             |
| End            | Profits through **volume** | Profits through **satisfaction** |

- Customer quote: *"Don't tell me how good your product is, but tell me
  how good it will make me."*

- **AMA definition**: process of planning and executing the
  **conception, pricing, promotion and distribution** of ideas, goods
  and services to create **exchanges** that satisfy individual and
  organisational objectives.

- Simply: **marketing = delivery of customer satisfaction at a profit**.
  The **essence of marketing is the exchange process** (Objective QB:
  True).

- Market conditions in exchange: buyers outnumber sellers; each buyer
  weaker than each seller, but a fraction of buyers can make or break
  sellers; sellers compete and keep modifying behaviour.

- SN definition: creating, communicating, delivering and **exchanging
  value**; right product to the right customer at the right time and
  place.

- SN importance of marketing (9): customer understanding · creating
  awareness · competitive advantage · revenue generation · long-term
  relationships · market expansion · innovation · adaptation to change ·
  measurement and improvement.

- SN role in the economy (10): resource allocation · job creation ·
  price determination · innovation · consumer choice · market
  competition · economic growth · global trade · tax revenue · market
  information.

**3. Evolution of marketing**

**Ebook: 5 eras**

| **Era**                    | **Attitude**                                                                 | **Slogan**                                                  |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Production**             | Products available and affordable; production efficiency, economies of scale | "Availability and affordability is what the customer wants" |
| **Product**                | Most quality, performance, innovative features                               | "A good product will sell itself" (better mousetrap)        |
| **Sales**                  | Consumers buy only if promoted/sold                                          | "Creative advertising and selling will overcome resistance" |
| **Marketing**              | Needs of target markets; satisfy better than competitors                     | "The consumer is king! Find a need and fill it"             |
| **Relationship marketing** | Superior value; long-term ties with customers and partners                   | "Long-term relationships lead to success"                   |

- Shift from **seller's market** (shortage) to **buyer's market**
  (abundance) created the marketing concept. Keyword = **customer
  orientation**.

| **Characteristic**   | **Transaction-based** | **Relationship**       |
|----------------------|-----------------------|------------------------|
| Time orientation     | Short term            | Long term              |
| Goal                 | Make the sale         | Customer **retention** |
| Customer service     | Relatively low        | Key component          |
| Customer contact     | Low to moderate       | Frequent               |
| Commitment           | Low                   | High                   |
| Basis of interaction | Conflict manipulation | Cooperation, trust     |
| Source of quality    | Production            | Companywide commitment |

**Study Notes: historical milestones**

- Barter and early trade (prehistoric–19th c) → Industrialisation (19th
  c: mass production, **urbanisation**) → Product-oriented (early
  20th c) → Sales-oriented (**1920s-30s**) → Marketing concept
  (**1950s-60s**) → Relationship marketing (late 20th c) → Digital
  marketing and globalisation (21st c) → Sustainability and ethical
  marketing (contemporary).

**Study Notes: 5 marketing concepts (LMS favourite)**

| **Concept**            | **Core idea**                                                                                |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Production**         | Make many products cheaply; customers like available, low-cost products (economies of scale) |
| **Product**            | Quality, features, innovation; customers will buy great products                             |
| **Selling**            | Aggressive selling and promotion push products; ignores lasting relationships                |
| **Marketing**          | Customer first: find needs via research, then meet them                                      |
| **Societal marketing** | Customer needs **plus** welfare of society; honest, responsible marketing                    |

**4. Marketing framework and the 4Ps**

- Marketing strategy = (1) **target market** + (2) **marketing mix**
  (product, price, place, promotion). The mix is **controllable**; the 5
  environments (outer circle) are largely **uncontrollable**:
  competitive, political-legal, economic, technological,
  social-cultural.

- **IMC (Integrated Marketing Communications)** coordinates all
  promotion so the consumer gets one unified, consistent message.

| **Market**      | **Industrial (B2B)**                                   | **Consumer (B2C)**                                       |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Market          | Geographically concentrated; fewer buyers              | Dispersed; mass markets                                  |
| Product         | Technical, customised                                  | Standardised                                             |
| Service         | Critical                                               | Somewhat important                                       |
| Buyer behaviour | Cross-functional teams; rational; stable relationships | Family; physiological/social/psychological; non-personal |
| Channel         | More **direct**, fewer intermediaries                  | Indirect, many layers                                    |
| Promotion       | **Personal selling**                                   | **Advertising**                                          |
| Price           | Competitive bidding, negotiation                       | List price / MRP                                         |

- **Services**: 4 characteristics → intangibility (sell a promise),
  inseparability (produced and consumed together), heterogeneity (no two
  providers alike), perishability (no inventory). Answer = 3 extra Ps:
  **Process** (service blueprint, solves heterogeneity), **Physical
  evidence** (tangibilise the intangible), **People** (providers are
  part of the service).

- International marketing: extra environmental factors; dilemma =
  **standardise or differentiate** the mix. Non-profit marketing: must
  serve **multiple publics** (clients and sponsors).

- Ebook match-the-following: product mktg = electric bulbs · service =
  ayurvedic massage centre · consumer = ice creams to adults ·
  industrial = iron ore to a steel maker · international = Disney park
  in Hong Kong · non-profit = AIDS awareness campaign.

**5. Functions of marketing (8 universal)**

- Marketing costs = **40-60%** of product cost (time, place, ownership
  utilities).

| **Group**                 | **Functions**                                                                        |
|---------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Exchange**              | Buying · Selling                                                                     |
| **Physical distribution** | Transporting · Storing                                                               |
| **Facilitating**          | Standardising and grading · Financing · Risk taking · Securing marketing information |

- Case: Amazon.com (Jeff Bezos, July 1995): click-and-buy model; online
  supermall; first full-year profit 2003.

**6. Core marketing concepts (Ebook lesson 2)**

1.  **Need** = state of felt deprivation (physical, social, spiritual).

2.  **Want** = form a need takes, shaped by culture and personality
    (thirst → water, soft drink, juice).

3.  **Demand** = want backed by **buying power**.

4.  **Product** = anything offered to satisfy a need or want (goods,
    services, places, persons, ideas); also called market offering or
    satisfier.

5.  **Value and satisfaction**: customer picks the product giving most
    benefit per rupee (closest to the zero-cost ideal).

6.  **Exchange** = obtaining a desired object by offering something in
    return. Conditions: ≥2 parties, each has value for the other, each
    wants to deal, free to accept/reject, can communicate and deliver.
    Exchange creates value like production does.

7.  **Transaction** = marketing's **unit of measurement**; monetary or
    **barter**.

8.  **Market** = set of actual and potential buyers. Physical =
    **marketplace**; virtual = **marketspace**. Self-sufficiency →
    decentralised exchange → centralised exchange (merchant cuts number
    of transactions).

- SN: needs = basics to live (food, clothes, shelter); wants go beyond
  needs (designer clothes). **Customer value** = perceived benefit vs
  cost; value above expectation → satisfaction → repeat buying and word
  of mouth.

**Marketing in a connected world (Mohanbir Sawhney)**

| **Hither-to: marketing as hunting**                                                                                                        | **Hence-forth: marketing as gardening**                                                                                                                  |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Market as jungle, customers as targets, marketers as hunters, segmentation as rifle vs shotgun, products as mousetraps, loyalty as lock-in | Relationships as a garden, marketer as gardener, partners in an ecosystem, loyalty as roots, lifetime profits as harvest, process = seed, feed and yield |

- Shift from information **asymmetry** (scarce info, monologue,
  command-and-control) to **information democracy** (ubiquitous info,
  conversations, connect-and-collaborate). **Cluetrain Manifesto**:
  "Markets are conversations".

**7. Marketing process**

**Ebook: 6 parts**

| **Part**                                              | **Time horizon**      | **Lateral connection**         |
|-------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------|--------------------------------|
| 1\. **Strategy formulation** (broadest, longest-term) | Long                  | Corporate strategy             |
| 2\. **Marketing planning**                            | Medium, **2-5 years** | Business plan                  |
| 3\. **Programming, allocating and budgeting**         | **One year**          | Operating programs and budgets |
| 4\. **Implementation** (execution; people-oriented)   | Short                 | Operating activities           |
| 5\. **Monitoring and auditing**                       | Short                 | Business audits                |
| 6\. **Analysis and research**                         | Ongoing               | Business analysis and research |

- Allocating = deciding what **not** to do; marketers tend to be
  optimists. Budgets feed production ("load the factory") and finance.
  Audit = (1) performance vs quantitative goals + (2) processes and
  qualitative aspects; monitoring = day-to-day, mostly external data.

- **Situation analysis** = environmental assessment, marketing research,
  market size/growth estimates.

- **5 Cs** of analysis: **Customers, Company, Competitors,
  Collaborators, Context** → target market and positioning → 4Ps. Value
  created (segment, positioning, product, place, promotion),
  **captured** by price, **sustained** by acquiring and retaining
  customers.

**Study Notes: 6-step process**

1.  **Market research and analysis** (first step, the foundation)

2.  Setting marketing objectives and goals (clear, measurable,
    time-bound)

3.  Developing marketing strategies (product, price, distribution,
    advertising; USP)

4.  Implementation of marketing tactics (campaigns, content, social
    media, launches)

5.  Monitoring and controlling (**KPIs**: traffic, conversion, sales,
    comments)

6.  Evaluation of marketing performance (ROI, feedback loop)

- SN Knowledge Check: market research is the **first**, not the final,
  step.

**8. Marketing environment**

- **Environmental scanning** = collecting information about the
  **external** marketing environment to identify and interpret trends
  (opportunities or threats). **Environmental management** = attaining
  objectives by **predicting and influencing** the 5 environments.

**Competitive environment (Ebook)**

- 3 types of competition: (1) **direct** (similar products: insurer vs
  insurer) · (2) **substitutes** (low-cost airline vs train/bus) · (3)
  **all organisations** competing for limited discretionary buying
  power.

- 3 questions: Should we compete? · In what markets? · How should we
  compete? **Time-based competition** = develop and distribute faster
  than competitors.

**Political-legal environment**

- Laws that set competitive conditions and protect consumer rights.
  Issues include foreign policy, government ideology, trade barriers,
  **anti-dumping duties**, IPR violations, bans on comparative ads and
  on advertising to children, price regulation, mandatory ingredient
  labels. Ignorance of law is no excuse.

**Economic environment**

- Consumer spending ≈ **two-thirds** of economic activity. Forces:
  business cycle, inflation, unemployment, resource availability,
  income.

| **Cycle stage** | **Marketer response**                                                                                         |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Prosperity**  | Expand lines, promotion, distribution; raise prices                                                           |
| **Recession**   | Consumers buy basic, low-price products; cut prices, drop marginal products, improve service, raise promotion |
| **Depression**  | Lowest spending                                                                                               |
| **Recovery**    | Ability to buy rises but caution restrains willingness; may save                                              |

- **Inflation** devalues money through persistent price increases (QB).
  Outcomes: buy now, alter patterns, postpone. **Unemployment** =
  without jobs and actively looking. A rise in income = potential for
  **increasing** sales (QB: "decreasing" is False); key = **disposable
  income**. Shortage response = **demarketing** (reduce demand to what
  the firm can supply).

**Technological and social-cultural**

- Technology creates products and whole industries, can make products
  obsolete fast, gives competitive advantage, improves service.

- Social-cultural: values, demographics; matters more in international
  markets. **Consumerism** = social force protecting buyers via legal,
  moral, economic pressure. Consumer rights: **choose freely, be
  informed, be heard, be safe** (QB: "all of the above").

- Case: **Euro Disney** (Marne-la-Vallée, France, opened 12 April 1992;
  "cultural Chernobyl"; price ~50% above Disney World → 15% attendance
  fall for 10% price rise).

**Study Notes: macro vs micro (LMS)**

|            | **Macro environment**                                                                            | **Micro environment**                                                                                                                    |
|------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Meaning    | **Broader societal forces**; outside the firm's control                                          | **Immediate and controllable** factors close to the business                                                                             |
| Components | Economic · socio-cultural · technological · political and legal · environmental (sustainability) | Competitors (SWOT) · suppliers · customers (CRM) · intermediaries (distributors, retailers) · **publics** (media, government, community) |
| Link       | Factors interconnected (economy ↔ spending; tech ↔ culture)                                      | Factors interconnected (customer shift → distributor tactics)                                                                            |

- Economic factors decide **purchasing power**. Adapting = flexibility
  in strategy, innovation and creativity, **agile** decision-making,
  collaboration and partnerships.

> **Source conflict:** *Ebook treats all environmental forces as largely
> uncontrollable. Study Notes call micro factors "immediate and
> controllable". LMS questions follow the Study Notes.*

**9. Buyer behaviour**

- Definitions: **Mowen** (buying units and exchange processes:
  acquiring, consuming, disposing) · **Loudon and Della Bitta**
  (decision process and physical activity) · **Jacob Jacoby** (totality
  of decisions on acquisition, usage and disposition of goods, services,
  **time and ideas** by decision-making units over time).

- **Kurt Lewin**: B = f(P, E) → for consumers **B = f(I, P)**:
  interpersonal influences (culture, family, friends) + personal factors
  (attitudes, learning, perception). Generic model =
  **stimulus-response**.

| **Interpersonal**                                                                                                                                                                    | **Personal**                                                                                                                                        |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Culture** (broadest determinant; subcultures) · **Groups** (norms; **Asch phenomenon**; reference groups; opinion leaders; social class) · **Family** (most important determinant) | **Needs and motives** · **Perception** (5 senses) · **Attitudes** (enduring evaluations) · **Learning** (change from experience) · **Self-concept** |

| **Family role**      | **Meaning**                           | **Example**         |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------|---------------------|
| **Autonomic**        | Equal number of independent decisions | Personal-care items |
| **Husband-dominant** | Husband decides most                  | Insurance           |
| **Wife-dominant**    | Wife decides most                     | Children's clothing |
| **Syncratic**        | Joint decisions                       | Vacation            |

**Consumer decision process (5 steps)**

1.  **Problem recognition** (actual vs ideal state)

2.  **Information search** (internal memory / external sources) →
    **evoked set** = brands actually considered

3.  **Evaluation of alternatives** (evaluative criteria)

4.  **Purchase decision and act**

5.  **Post-purchase evaluation** (satisfaction or **cognitive
    dissonance** = post-purchase anxiety)

- High-involvement vs low-involvement; thought-based (cognitive) vs
  feeling-based (emotive).

**Study Notes additions (LMS)**

| **Buying decision** | **Involvement**            | **Typical item**                |
|---------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Routine**         | Low, habitual              | Low-cost, frequently bought     |
| **Limited**         | Moderate                   | Moderately priced or infrequent |
| **Extensive**       | High; research and compare | Cars, homes                     |

- Factors: **cultural** (culture, subculture, social class) · **social**
  (reference groups, family, roles, status) · **personal** (age, job,
  income, lifestyle, personality) · **psychological** (motivation,
  perception, learning, beliefs, attitudes).

- Organisational vs consumer buying: nature of buyer, more complex
  decisions, long-term relationships, **higher volumes**.

- **Buying centre** roles: **Initiator** (spots the need) ·
  **Influencer** (experts, consultants) · **Decider** (final authority)
  · **Buyer** (negotiates, paperwork) · **User**.

- Organisational buying factors: **economic** (cost, conditions) ·
  **technological** (efficiency, compatibility) · **organisational**
  (culture, structure).

- Market research data methods: surveys/questionnaires · interviews ·
  observational research · focus groups · data analytics and big data.

**10. Segmentation, targeting, positioning**

- **Segmentation** = dividing a heterogeneous market into smaller,
  relatively homogeneous groups. Benefits (Ebook, 4): better targeting ·
  avoids trial-and-error · implements the marketing concept · data for
  long-range planning.

- Criteria for useful segments (Ebook): **internally homogeneous ·
  identifiable · accessible · effective demand**.

- Segmentation analysis (7 steps): define purpose → analyse total market
  data → develop segment profiles → evaluate segmentation → select
  target segment(s) → design strategy → reappraise.

| **Consumer base**      | **Variables**                                                                                                |
|------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Geographic**         | Region, culture, mobility; climate (SN); oldest, rarely enough alone                                         |
| **Demographic**        | Age, sex, income, education, social status; used for media decisions                                         |
| **Psychographic**      | Personality, perceptual style, attitudes, reference groups, social roles, **lifestyle and values**           |
| **General life-style** | Demographic + psychographic; activities and interests                                                        |
| **Market history**     | Product usage (heavy/medium/light/non-users) · product benefit · decision process (price-sensitive, impulse) |
| **Behavioural** (SN)   | Buying patterns, usage, brand loyalty                                                                        |

- Industrial segmentation: macro then micro; **Shapiro and Bonoma nested
  approach**: demographics · operating variables · purchasing approaches
  · situational factors · personal characteristics.

| **Targeting approach**      | **Meaning**                                                                                      |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Undifferentiated (mass)** | One product, one mix for all (Ford Model T "any colour as long as it is black"); scale economies |
| **Differentiated**          | Several products, different mixes for several segments; higher cost, more sales                  |
| **Concentrated (niche)**    | One segment; suits small firms; risk = dependence on that segment                                |
| **Micromarketing**          | Postal code, occupation, individuals = "segments of one"; internet → mass customisation          |

- SN: segment attractiveness criteria = size, growth rate,
  profitability, accessibility, fit with resources. SN segmentation
  challenges = **overlapping segments**, data accuracy, cost, dynamic
  markets, resistance to change.

**Positioning**

- Designing the offer to occupy a distinct, valued place in target
  customers' minds. **Ries and Trout**: positioning is not what you do
  to the product, but what you do to the **mind** of the customer.

- Statement: Product X is positioned as offering (benefit) to (target)
  with the competitive advantage of (advantage) based on (basis).

- Bases: attribute · benefit · competitor · product category ·
  quality/price.

| **Positioning error** | **Meaning**                                  |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| **Under**             | Unique but unimportant attribute highlighted |
| **Over**              | Claims exceed performance                    |
| **Confused**          | Customer categorises product wrongly         |
| **Doubtful**          | Claims hard to believe                       |

- **Positioning map** = graph of perceptions (e.g. price vs quality).
  **Repositioning** example: Nestle **Milkmaid** (milk substitute →
  sweets ingredient → dessert ingredient). Case: HLL Kwality ice cream
  repositioned as adult indulgence.

- 4 positioning identities: **Who am I?** (origin, credentials) · **What
  am I?** (functional capabilities) · **For whom am I?** (target
  segment) · **Why me?** (differential advantage).

- Differentiation: hypermarket 40,000 SKUs, family uses 150; 1,600 ads a
  day, 80 noticed, 12 provoke reaction. Nike levels: **cognitive ·
  normative · wired-in**. Dimensions: product, service, personnel,
  channel, image. **MacMillan and McGrath**: differentiate across the
  whole **consumption chain** (need awareness → disposal).

- SN: **USP** = distinct message answering "why choose this product over
  others?". Repositioning = update USP, new segment, adjust messaging,
  new features.

**11. Marketing mix overview**

| **P**         | **Sub-elements (Ebook)**                                                                                |
|---------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Product**   | Design, positioning, branding, packaging/labelling, line breadth and depth, service, warranty, NPD, PLC |
| **Price**     | Selling prices, terms, bidding, discounts, new-product pricing (**skim vs penetration**)                |
| **Promotion** | Advertising, sales force, direct marketing, PR, price promotions, trade shows                           |
| **Place**     | Direct vs indirect, channel length, channel breadth (exclusive/selective/intensive), franchising        |

- 3 degrees of interaction in a program: **consistency** (no poor fit) ·
  **integration** (positive harmony) · **leverage** (each element used
  to best advantage). Then check program/customer, program/company and
  competitive/program fit.

- Total product: generic → **expected** (satisfaction) → **augmented**
  (delight) → **potential** (dream). Item decisions: pruning, balancing,
  modernisation, filling, extension.

- NPD sequence: opportunity identification → design → testing →
  introduction → life cycle management. PLC: introduction, growth,
  maturity, decline.

- 8 channel functions: product information, customisation, quality
  assurance, lot size, assortment, availability, after-sale service,
  logistics. You can eliminate a layer but **not the tasks**.

- **6 Ms** of communication: Market, Mission, Message, Media, Money,
  Measurement. Sales promotion types: consumer, trade, retail. PR =
  non-paid space. **Push** (induce intermediaries) vs **pull** (consumer
  demand pulls product through channel).

- Price is the **only revenue element**; easily copied; cost = floor;
  perceived value = ceiling. Penetration suits price-sensitive buyers,
  scale economies, spare capacity, threat of competition. Price
  customisation: product line, controlled availability, buyer
  characteristics, transaction characteristics.

- SN 4P details: pricing strategies (cost-based, value-based,
  competitive, dynamic), price elasticity, **psychological pricing** (Rs
  9.99), content marketing, social media.

- Extended 7Ps: **Process** (flow, service script, customer involvement)
  · **Physical evidence** (facility, ambience, signage, dress) ·
  **People** (employees, customers).

- SN integrating the mix: coordinate the 4Ps for one consistent message
  (premium product → premium price, matching place and promotion) and
  adjust the mix to market changes.
