**1. What a marketing channel is**

- Ebook (QB answer): a **marketing channel** consists of the **people,
  organisations and activities necessary to transfer the ownership of
  goods from the point of production to the point of consumption**. Also
  called a **distribution channel**.

- SN: a set of intermediaries facilitating ownership transfer from
  **manufacturer to end user**. Primary function = product distribution.

- Major role (Ebook): make products available at the **right time, right
  place, right quantities**. Channel decisions are **long-term
  commitments**: it is easier to change price or promotion than
  channels.

- **Dual distribution**: manufacturer or wholesaler uses **more than one
  channel** at once (own website + retailer). Example given:
  franchising.

**Roles of channels in marketing strategy (QB: "All of the above")**

1.  Links producers to buyers.

2.  Influences the firm's pricing strategy.

3.  Affects product strategy through branding, policies, willingness to
    stock.

4.  Customises: install, maintain, offer credit.

**Utilities created**

| **Utility**          | **Created by**                                          |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **Time**             | Product available **when** the customer wants it        |
| **Place**            | Product available **where** the customer wants to buy   |
| **Possession**       | Customer has access to use or store the product         |
| **Form** (sometimes) | Channel members assemble, prepare or refine the product |

- **Exchange efficiency** (QB): **marketing intermediaries** reduce the
  cost of exchanges by efficiently performing services. Critics call
  wholesalers parasitic, but **wholesalers can be eliminated; their
  functions cannot**.

- **Supply chain management** (QB): **long-term partnerships** among
  channel members to cut inefficiencies, costs and redundancies and to
  develop innovative approaches to satisfy customers. Broader than the
  channel: includes facilitating agencies (parts suppliers, shippers,
  communication firms). Starts with the customer.

**2. Channel levels (Ebook)**

| **Level**               | **Path**                                                    | **Notes**                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Zero-level** (direct) | Producer → consumer                                         | Peddling, **mail order**, **manufacturer-owned stores** (Singer). Close customer contact, no middleman margin, faster delivery; yet \< 3% of consumer sales. Internet is reviving it (software) |
| **One-level**           | Producer → retailer → consumer                              | Best for **shopping goods** (clothes, shoes, furniture, toys); retailers bring assortment and brand-loyal networks (Walmart, Target)                                                            |
| **Two-level**           | Producer → wholesaler → retailer → consumer                 | Wholesaler buys big lots, resells small lots, stores goods. Each margin raises end price. **5 makers × 100 retailers = 500 deliveries; via a wholesaler = 105**                                 |
| **Three-level**         | Producer → agent/broker → wholesaler or retailer → consumer | Agents speed market entry (**perishables**); brokers bring buyer and seller together, earn commission, cannot change prices                                                                     |

- **Distributor** (QB): the **representative of the manufacturer**,
  performing functions on its behalf for distribution from producer to
  wholesaler or retailer; studies the market, advertises, stocks
  inventory, builds a dealer network; rarely sells to consumers.

> **Source conflict:** *Ebook two-level text says consumers can buy in
> bulk directly from wholesalers. SN says wholesalers **do not** deal
> with end consumers (KC2: "wholesalers connect directly with consumers"
> = **False**). For the LMS, follow the SN.*

**3. Types of channels (Study Notes)**

|          | **Direct**                                                                                     | **Indirect**                                                            |
|----------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Path     | Manufacturer → consumer, no intermediaries                                                     | Through wholesalers, retailers, agents                                  |
| Strength | **Greater control** (pricing, experience), direct communication; niche or specialised products | **Broader reach** via established networks; maker focuses on production |

- Choice depends on **nature of product, target market, producer's
  resources**. Misconception: direct does not "always" give more control
  in a useful sense; indirect can give reach and efficiency.

| **Intermediary** | **Role**                                                                      |
|------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Wholesaler**   | Buys in bulk from makers, sells to retailers; negotiates deals, holds storage |
| **Retailer**     | **Final link**; interacts with consumers; physical or online                  |
| **Agent**        | Facilitates deals **without taking ownership**; earns **commission**          |

- **Offline** (brick-and-mortar, face-to-face; perishables, high-touch
  items) vs **online** (digital platforms, global reach). Not mutually
  exclusive: **hybrid / omnichannel**.

**Channel functions (SN)**

| **Function**                  | **Detail**                                                                                |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Information flow**          | Inventory, trends, preferences, specs shared; **EDI** enables real-time data              |
| **Promotion and advertising** | Manufacturers inform intermediaries; trade promotions; **co-branded** campaigns           |
| **Sorting**                   | Categorise by size, quality, type (fruits by size)                                        |
| **Grading**                   | Assign a quality level (agriculture, manufacturing)                                       |
| **Packaging**                 | **Protects** in transport and storage and **builds brand image**, differentiation, appeal |

**4. Channel structure and length (SN)**

- **Structure** = arrangement and relationships among producers,
  intermediaries and consumers (KC2: **True**).

- **Channel length** = **number of intermediaries** (KC2: "physical
  distance" = **False**). Shorter when direct communication matters;
  longer for broad-appeal products.

**5. Channel design: six steps (Ebook)**

5.  **Recognise the need**: new product/line, new target market (B2B →
    consumer), change in mix (e.g. lower price → discounters),
    environmental change, new geographic area.

6.  **Set and coordinate distribution objectives**: know other mix
    objectives, state objectives explicitly (Apple "Apple experience"
    via big chains; Coca-Cola exclusive campus deals), check congruence.

7.  **Specify distribution tasks** (QB: **True**: the manager specifies
    the nature of tasks to meet objectives). Cricket-bat example: info,
    promotion, inventory, order processing, transport, credit, warranty,
    repair, returns.

8.  **Develop alternative structures**: (a) **number of levels**, (b)
    **intensity** at each level, (c) **types of intermediaries** (incl.
    auction sites).

9.  **Evaluate variables** affecting structure (below).

10. **Choose the "best" structure**: desired effectiveness at lowest
    cost; mostly managerial judgement.

**Intensity / coverage**

| **Type**      | **Meaning**                                                                            | **Example** |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------|
| **Intensive** | As many outlets as possible; everyday, high-demand items                               | Parle       |
| **Selective** | Not all intermediaries; needs expertise, service, image; balances coverage and control | Appliances  |
| **Exclusive** | Very few chosen outlets; luxury/specialised; high control, limited reach               | Rolex       |

> **Trap:** *SN KC2: "Intensive distribution restricts availability to a
> few chosen outlets" = **False** (that is exclusive).*

**Variables affecting channel structure (Ebook)**

| **Variable**      | **Key points**                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|-------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Market**        | Based on the **marketing concept**: take cues from the market (QB: **True**). Geography, size, density, behaviour                                                                                                                            |
| **Product**       | Bulky/heavy → **ship in large lots to few points** (QB: **True**), short channel · perishable → rapid, short · **low unit value → longer** channel · standardised → longer; customised → direct · technical → direct · new product → shorter |
| **Company**       | Size, financial capacity (more capital, less dependence on intermediaries), managerial expertise, objectives and strategies                                                                                                                  |
| **Intermediary**  | Availability (Dell built direct mail order), cost, services offered                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Environmental** | Uncontrollable macro forces: socio-cultural, economic, technological, legal                                                                                                                                                                  |

**6. Channel management, VMS, conflict (SN)**

- Management = **communication** (EDI, collaborative software),
  **relationship building** (support, training), **conflict
  resolution**.

| **VMS type**     | **Basis**                                                 | **Example**                     |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Corporate**    | One entity **owns** several levels (vertical integration) | Maker owns wholesale and retail |
| **Contractual**  | Independent firms bound by **formal agreements**          | **Franchises**                  |
| **Administered** | **Dominant member's power**, no formal contract           | Large maker or retailer         |

| **Conflict**   | **Between**                                                                                            |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Horizontal** | Same level (two retailers, two wholesalers): customers, territory, resources                           |
| **Vertical**   | Different levels (maker vs wholesaler, wholesaler vs retailer): pricing, positioning, responsibilities |

| **Resolution strategy**                | **Key feature**                                          |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **Mediation**                          | Neutral third party facilitates; **no imposed decision** |
| **Arbitration**                        | Neutral third party gives a **binding** decision; formal |
| **Collaborative problem-solving**      | **Mutual benefit and shared goals**; joint ownership     |
| **Clear agreements**                   | Proactive prevention via contracts                       |
| **Regular communication and feedback** | Spots conflict early                                     |
| **Negotiation and compromise**         | Concessions to find common ground                        |

- Misconception: conflict is inherent and **not always detrimental**;
  managed well, it improves relationships.

**7. Evaluating channel performance (SN)**

| **Metric**                       | **Measures**                                                        |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Sales**                        | Revenue, growth, market share, by channel                           |
| **Customer satisfaction**        | Surveys, feedback, reviews                                          |
| **Distribution efficiency**      | Order fulfilment time, **inventory turnover**, order accuracy       |
| **Cost**                         | Distribution, marketing, cost per customer acquired; ROI            |
| **Channel profitability**        | Net income per channel                                              |
| **Market reach and penetration** | **Geographic and demographic coverage**: outlets, regions, segments |

- **Continuous improvement**: regular performance reviews · **feedback
  loops** (improve performance) · technology integration · adaptability
  · training and development · **benchmarking** (comparison with
  industry standards).

**8. Retailers**

- Ebook: "retail" from French **retailer = to cut a piece**. Retailing =
  one of the largest sectors globally (US: ~23 million jobs, ~\$3
  trillion; Walmart biggest retailer). QB: "**Retail is the buzzword
  today**" = **True**.

> **QB trap:** *QB says "**Wholesaling** is one of the largest sectors
> in the global economy". The Ebook sentence is about **retailing**, so
> the best answer is **False**.*

- **India** (Ebook): second largest industry after agriculture, **~11%
  of GDP**, **8% of workforce**, **12 million outlets** (largest retail
  density), only **~2% organised**; highly fragmented.

**Retail functions**

| **Ebook (4 activities)**                                                          | **Study Notes (3 functions)**                            |
|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **Arranging assortment** (Subhiksha: only fast-moving SKUs; Food World: one-stop) | **Merchandising**: select, buy, display, price           |
| **Breaking bulk** (repack into consumer sizes)                                    | **Sales**: understand needs, build relationships         |
| **Holding stock** (software vs day-to-day)                                        | **Customer service**: training, problem-solving, loyalty |
| **Extending service** (credit, after-sales; Titan Time Zone)                      |                                                          |

- Retailer roles (SN): **connect products with consumers** · give
  manufacturers **direct access to end consumers** (market access) ·
  brand representation · consumer insights · influence purchases ·
  **economic impact (jobs)**.

**Retailer types (SN)**

| **Type**             | **Focus**                                                           | **Examples**                     |
|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Department store** | Wide range, one-stop, immersive                                     | Macy's, Nordstrom, Kohl's        |
| **Supermarket**      | **Everyday needs**, groceries, convenience                          | Walmart, Kroger, Tesco           |
| **Specialty store**  | **One category or niche**                                           | Electronics, pet supplies, books |
| **E-commerce**       | **Virtual space, digital platforms**; **broadens geographic reach** | Amazon, Alibaba                  |
| **Discount store**   | Low prices via bulk buying                                          | Walmart, Target, Dollar General  |

- Format significance: consumer experience · target audience · brand
  image and positioning · channel integration (omnichannel) ·
  adaptability (AR try-ons).

**Non-store retailing (Ebook)**

- **Direct selling** (cosmetics; India 60-65% annual growth) ·
  **telemarketing** (outbound and toll-free inbound; junk calls annoy) ·
  **mail order** (speciality, narrow lines) · **TV shopping** (Asian Sky
  Shop) · **electronic shopping** (fastest growing; eBay, Rediff).

**9. Wholesalers**

- Ebook: wholesaling = all activities selling to those who **buy for
  resale**; excludes producers. Two types: **merchants** (take
  **title**, bear ownership risk) and **agents** (do not take title).

| **Function (SN)**           | **Meaning**                                                                                                        |
|-----------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Bulk purchasing**         | Economies of scale; lower prices for retailers                                                                     |
| **Bulk breaking**           | Large quantities → smaller units to **meet specific retailer demands** (KC2: small → large = **False**)            |
| **Warehousing**             | **Storing and managing large stocks**; buffer against demand swings; cuts cost (KC2: "increases cost" = **False**) |
| Transport, order fulfilment | Let retailers focus on customers                                                                                   |

**Retailer-wholesaler relationship (SN)**

- **Cooperation** (willingness, communication) + **coordination**
  (synchronised flow of goods and data) → **enhanced efficiency ·
  optimised inventory · market responsiveness · cost efficiency · risk
  mitigation** (wholesalers carry bulk, storage, transport risk).

**10. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Myth**                                               | **Fact**                                       |
|--------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Channels are only about selling                        | A network of intermediaries and processes      |
| Direct always gives more control (so is always better) | Indirect can give reach and efficiency         |
| Retailers alone make products available                | Wholesalers and agents also play roles         |
| Online and offline are mutually exclusive              | Hybrid / omnichannel                           |
| Channel conflict is always harmful                     | Inherent; mediation and negotiation help       |
| Retailers only sell                                    | Merchandising, service, logistics, brand image |
| Wholesalers only facilitate transactions / only bulk   | Storage, transport, inventory, bulk breaking   |
| All retailers serve the same purpose                   | Formats differ in purpose                      |
