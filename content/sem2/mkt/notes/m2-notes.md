**1. Marketing mix and the service Ps**

- 4Ps framework suggested by **McCarthy**; originated from goods
  manufacturers.

| **P**             | **Elements (Ebook)**                                                                                                  |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Product           | Service core, levels, additional services, branding                                                                   |
| Price             | Price, discounts, terms of payment                                                                                    |
| Place             | Location, channels, coverage                                                                                          |
| Promotion         | **Advertising, sales promotion, personal selling, publicity** (QB: these are activities of **promotion**)             |
| People            | Customer-provider relationship, training, culture, skills, attitudes                                                  |
| Physical evidence | Ambience, appearance, equipment, buildings, facilities                                                                |
| Process           | Activity sequence, quality management, customer participation, delivery (QB: **Process** is one of the additional Ps) |
| Productivity      | Efficiency of human and non-human capital (Ebook's 8th P)                                                             |

**2. Concept of a product**

- **Alderson**: product = a **bundle of utilities** (features +
  accompanying services). **Schwarte**: something a firm markets to
  satisfy a personal want or a business need. Service product = activity
  performed for a target customer (hair styling by a barber).

- Buying depends on tangible **and** psychological attributes (service,
  brand, package, warranty, image). "We don't sell cars, we sell
  safety."

**Ebook: product levels (generic → potential)**

| **Level**          | **Meaning**                                                                                                             |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Core benefit**   | What the product means to the customer (car → convenience in travel)                                                    |
| **Generic**        | **Unbranded, undifferentiated** commodity (rice, bread, flour). QB: "generic = branded and differentiated" is **False** |
| **Branded**        | Identity through a name (Modern bread)                                                                                  |
| **Differentiated** | Real (ingredient, quality) or psychological distinction                                                                 |
| **Customised**     | Built to a specific customer's needs; common in industrial marketing                                                    |
| **Augmented**      | Voluntary improvements by the maker, **neither suggested nor expected** by customers                                    |
| **Potential**      | Tomorrow's product; limited only by the firm's technology and economic resources                                        |

**Study Notes: 3 layers (LMS)**

| **Layer**     | **Meaning**                                          | **Smartphone example**              |
|---------------|------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| **Core**      | Fundamental benefit / solution                       | Communication, connectivity         |
| **Actual**    | Tangible features: design, specs, brand, packaging   | The handset itself                  |
| **Augmented** | Extra layers: after-sales service, warranty, support | Warranty, software updates, service |

> **Source error:** *SN Knowledge Check marks "augmented product
> includes only tangible features" as True. That contradicts the SN text
> itself: augmented = additional services (warranty, support). Treat the
> statement as False.*

**Differentiation and strategy stances (Ebook)**

- Differentiation = designing meaningful differences from competitors.
  **Levitt**: there is no such thing as a commodity; everything is
  differentiable.

| **BCG industry type** | **Advantages**              |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Volume**            | Few, but large              |
| **Stalemated**        | Few, and each small         |
| **Fragmented**        | Many, each small            |
| **Specialised**       | Many, each with high payoff |

- Strategy stances: **offensive** (challenger attacks the leader) ·
  **defensive** (leader protects position) · **niche** (small segment
  with unique products; needs reasonable size, profit and growth
  potential).

**3. Product classification**

| **By durability/tangibility**                                                  | **Consumer goods**                                                                 | **Industrial goods**                                                                     |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Non-durable** (soap, salt, biscuits)                                         | **Convenience** (frequent, minimal effort): staple, **impulse**, emergency         | **Materials and parts** (raw material, manufactured parts; enter the product completely) |
| **Durable** (TV, fridge)                                                       | **Shopping** (compare suitability, quality, price: furniture, appliances, gadgets) | **Capital items** (installations, equipment; long-lasting)                               |
| **Services** (airline, banking: intangible, inseparable, variable, perishable) | **Specialty** (unique / brand identity; special effort: cars, designer watches)    | **Supplies and business services** (short-lasting)                                       |
|                                                                                | **Unsought** (not known or not thought of: **life insurance**, funeral services)   |                                                                                          |

- SN: specialty products are also listed as a third broad category
  (designer clothing, luxury cars, artisanal goods) with high brand
  loyalty.

**4. Product management**

- Ebook: 3 factors in product decisions = nature of market and
  competition · assistance needed (production, engineering, finance,
  staffing) · broader strategic requirements.

- Product manager checklist (Ebook): line control, PLC analysis, NPD
  success, communication, sourcing, market information, centralised
  planning, coordination, planned profits, cost and profit control.

- SN product manager role: **strategic planning · cross-functional
  collaboration · market analysis · development oversight · go-to-market
  strategy · lifecycle management** (whole life, not just launch).

- SN **portfolio management**: balancing the portfolio, resource
  allocation (focus on high performers), risk management, strategic
  alignment, new product introductions. Relevant to firms of all sizes.

- SN obsolescence/discontinuation factors: performance, market
  relevance, lifecycle stage, resource allocation, customer impact,
  communication. Discontinuation is a strategic move, not a sign of
  failure.

- SN product attributes: quality and performance · branding and
  labelling · packaging and presentation · **warranty** (repair/replace
  in a period) vs **guarantee** (satisfaction or refund).

- SN design importance: customer appeal, functionality, brand image,
  competitive advantage, user experience (not just aesthetics).
  Innovation: differentiation, adaptation, technology, sustainability,
  customer experience.

**5. Product line and product mix**

| **Term**             | **Meaning**                                                                                            |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Product item**     | A single product                                                                                       |
| **Product line**     | Group of closely related products (similar use, customers, channels). HLL detergents: Rin, Wheel, Surf |
| **Product mix**      | All products an organisation markets (product assortment / portfolio)                                  |
| **Breadth / width**  | Number of **different product lines**                                                                  |
| **Depth**            | Number of **items within each line** (models, sizes, colours)                                          |
| **Consistency** (SN) | Fit of lines with brand image and target market                                                        |

- HLL expanded **breadth** by entering agro-chemicals and **depth** by
  buying TOMCO.

- Why many products (Ebook): counter the PLC, even out seasonal sales,
  use resources better, ride on middlemen/consumer acceptance, spread
  costs, become better known.

- Modify a line via: composition, expansion/contraction, value addition,
  brand, packaging, physical characteristics, positioning.

- **Line length**: too long if dropping an item **raises** profit; too
  short if adding an item raises profit. Market-share objective → longer
  line; profit objective → shorter line. Cycle of lengthening then
  pruning.

| **Line stretching** | **Meaning and risks**                                                                                                                         |
|---------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Upward**          | Low-end firm enters high end (higher margins); risks: leader counter-attacks downward, credibility, infrastructure                            |
| **Downward**        | High-end firm adds low-price items (Parker pens at Rs 50; Videocon 14" TV); risks: **cannibalisation**, brand dilution, provoking competitors |
| **Two-way**         | Middle-market firm stretches both ways (ITC Ashoka group: 5-star → Ashoka Yatri Niwas)                                                        |

- **Cannibalisation** = a new product **reduces sales of the firm's
  existing products** (QB). Example: Hyundai Santro → Santro Xing. Avoid
  by targeting new segments with new appeals; acceptable when the new
  product has higher margins (Videocon trade-up).

- British Airways ran 8 sub-brands (Concorde, Club World, World
  Traveller, Super Shuttle...) each with a brand management team.

**Study Notes: extensions vs pruning**

|        | **Line extension**                                                                                             | **Line pruning**                                                                       |
|--------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| What   | New items within an **existing line**                                                                          | Eliminate or reduce items                                                              |
| Why    | Evolving preferences, brand loyalty, cost efficiency, saturated markets                                        | Poor performance, market change, core focus, cost rationalisation, brand image         |
| Result | Pros: low risk, cost savings, trust, expansion. Cons: **brand dilution, cannibalisation**, quality, saturation | Streamlined portfolio, higher profit, sharper positioning, freed resources, lower risk |

- SN importance of product mix: market coverage · **risk management** ·
  competitive advantage · brand image · revenue generation.

**6. Branding**

- Brand = name, term, sign, symbol or design that identifies a seller's
  products and **differentiates** them. **Walter Landor**: a brand is a
  **promise**. **David Aaker**: brand = "mental box"; brand equity =
  assets (or liabilities) linked to a brand's name and symbol. Best
  definition: a brand is a **collection of perceptions in the consumer's
  mind**.

- **Brand mark** = symbol/design (Air India Maharaja). **Trademark** =
  brand given **legal protection** for exclusive use. **Generic name
  brand** = brand that lost exclusive claim because it became the
  category name.

| **Term**                   | **Meaning**                                         |
|----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| Brand recognition          | Customers remember the brand                        |
| Brand preference / loyalty | Choose this brand over others                       |
| Brand insistence           | Will search for it; **no substitute** accepted      |
| Brand equity               | Value of the brand's overall strength in the market |
| Brand awareness            | First brand to come to mind in a category           |

- **Aaker's 5 levels of attitude**: switcher (price, no loyalty) →
  satisfied (no reason to change) → satisfied with switching cost →
  values brand as a friend → devoted.

- **Heidi and Don Schultz** (new economy): build corporate brand from
  values; measure by perceived value, satisfaction, share of wallet,
  retention, advocacy (not just awareness and recall).

- Brands are tradable assets: Coca-Cola bought Thums Up, Limca, Citra,
  Gold Spot (1993, ~Rs 175 cr); HLL paid Rs 110 cr for Lakme brands vs
  Rs 29 cr for its plants; Marico bought Parachute and Saffola (1999).
  Buying a brand = ready-made market.

**Study Notes: brand types and equity (LMS)**

| **Brand type**              | **Meaning**                                    | **Example**                   |
|-----------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Manufacturer (national)** | Owned by the producer                          | Apple, Nike                   |
| **Private label (store)**   | Owned by the retailer, sold only in its stores | House brands                  |
| **Family**                  | **One brand name across related products**     | Toyota: Camry, Corolla, Prius |
| **Individual**              | Each product has its own name                  | P&G: Tide, Pampers, Gillette  |

- Brand positioning = a **distinct space** in consumers' minds. Brand
  equity measures: awareness, association, perceived quality, loyalty,
  sentiment, market share. Equity → purchase intent, loyalty, **premium
  price**, advocacy, easier extensions.

- Naming and logo (Nike swoosh, McDonald's arches). Global branding:
  cultural sensitivity, **adaptation vs standardisation**, global
  positioning, localisation, global brand management.

**7. Packaging and labelling**

- Packaging once a pure expense (some toiletries: pack cost \>
  contents); now aids branding and loyalty and can cut marketing cost.

- Good package: attractive, convenient to store and display, protects
  contents, describes product. Basic functions = **protection and
  presentation**; also handling, consistent quality, transport economy,
  recognisable, eye appeal.

- Role of packaging: increases sales, adds to use, promotes, safety,
  storage, **differentiation**. Decisions: design (refill packs),
  colour, family packaging.

- Labelling: statutory needs (date of manufacture, expiry, composition,
  storage, method of use).

**8. New product development**

**Ebook**

- Indian launches: **marketing innovations** (Maggi, tetra-pack drinks:
  packaging, branding, availability) · **product improvements** (100cc
  bikes: TVS Suzuki then Hero Honda) · **technological innovations**
  (PCs, photocopiers, colour TV).

| **Technology / market matrix** | **Present or improved tech**              | **New tech**                          |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| **Current market**             | A: improved products (market development) | B: line extension                     |
| **New market**                 | C: new use / market extension             | D: **diversification** (highest risk) |

- Routes: transfer of technology · diversification (ITC into hotels) ·
  additions to lines (Videocon, Godrej) · improvements · **cost
  reductions** (Nirma, Tata 407) · relaunch (Good Day, Tata Tea).

- Idea sources: customers, competitors, distributors, creative
  techniques (brainstorming), external world, R&D. Concept questions:
  **need, trust, communicability, usage, perceived value**. Then
  evaluation (heart of the process) → commercialisation → review.

- **Abraham Koshy** study: 53% successes overall; only **36%** of new
  brands survived; **71%** of line extensions and **63%** of brand
  extensions succeeded. US success 2-10%. Failure causes: poor research
  use, poor opportunity identification, no concept testing (44% skip
  it), poor product testing.

- P&G **Corporate New Ventures** (volunteer team). Tom Peters: "design
  mindfulness". Product characteristics (designer, physical) vs
  attributes (consumer perceptions).

**Study Notes NPD sequence (LMS)**

1.  **Idea generation** (customers, research, competitors,
    brainstorming, partners, regulation, R&D) and **screening**
    (evaluate and filter by market potential, feasibility, fit)

2.  **Concept development and testing** (surveys, focus groups,
    prototype tests; iterative)

3.  **Product design and prototyping** (validate design, test
    functionality, find issues)

4.  **Market testing / test marketing** (limited segment or region;
    pricing and channel checks)

5.  **Launch and post-launch evaluation** (timing, campaigns, KPIs,
    feedback, iteration)

- Idea techniques: brainstorming · mind mapping · **SCAMPER**
  (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate,
  Reverse) · role playing · reverse engineering · crowdsourcing ·
  prototypes · hackathons. Screening uses cross-functional teams, risk
  assessment, prioritisation.

**9. Product life cycle**

- Products show "**perishable distinctiveness**": distinct when new,
  degenerate into commodities.

|              | **Introduction**     | **Growth**             | **Maturity**                 | **Decline**              |
|--------------|----------------------|------------------------|------------------------------|--------------------------|
| Sales        | Low                  | Fast growth            | Slow growth                  | Declining                |
| Profits      | Negligible           | **Peak**               | Declining                    | Low                      |
| Cash inflow  | Negative             | Moderate               | **High**                     | Low                      |
| Competitors  | Few                  | Growing                | Many                         | Declining                |
| Customers    | Innovators           | Mass market            | Mass market                  | Laggards                 |
| Focus        | Market establishment | Penetration            | Brand loyalty                | Renewal                  |
| Distribution | Selective            | Intensive              | Intensive                    | Selective                |
| Advertising  | Early adopters       | Brand benefits to mass | Differentiate similar brands | Low price, minimum spend |

- Source table: **Peter Doyle**, "The Realities of the Product Life
  Cycle" (1976). Innovative products: primary demand; imitative
  products: secondary (brand) demand. Ebook also names a **saturation**
  stage after maturity.

**Study Notes stage strategies (LMS)**

| **Stage**        | **Strategies**                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Introduction** | Heavy promotion for awareness and **trial** (samples, intro offers), USP positioning, **penetration or skimming** price, selective distribution, PR, influencers, educational content; market feedback checks positioning   |
| **Growth**       | Market expansion, product enhancements, more marketing, channel optimisation, competitive pricing; manage competition by differentiation, retention, partnerships                                                           |
| **Maturity**     | **Market saturation**; differentiation, price adjustments, CRM, cost control; extend life via innovation, new markets, diversification, partnerships                                                                        |
| **Decline**      | Cost reduction, niche segments, **harvesting**, after-sales support, inventory clearance; maintain / harvest / divest; phase-out: **phased withdrawal** (gradual reduction in availability), replace, license/sell, recycle |

- Case: **Kellogg's India** moved from "healthy breakfast" to
  round-the-clock office snacking (cereal dispensers in ~120 Delhi
  offices).

**10. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Myth**                                              | **Fact**                                             |
|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Design = aesthetics only                              | Aesthetics + functionality + user experience         |
| Product managers only handle launch                   | Whole lifecycle                                      |
| Innovation = only new products                        | Also adapting and improving existing ones            |
| Discontinuing = failure                               | Strategic reallocation                               |
| Ideas only from inside                                | Customers, trends, competitors, partners, regulation |
| Concept testing is one-time; prototypes only physical | Iterative; digital prototypes too                    |
| Post-launch = sales figures only                      | Feedback, trends, operations, brand perception       |
