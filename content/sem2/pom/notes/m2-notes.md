**1. Objectives of designing**

| **Ebook: main focus**             | **Ebook: secondary focus**                                      |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| Customer satisfaction             | Function · cost · profit · quality · appearance                 |
| Understanding what customer needs | Ease of production / assembly · ease of maintenance / servicing |

- Quick Notes: objectives go beyond aesthetics: **functionality and
  performance**, **aesthetics and user experience**, **cost efficiency
  and affordability**, **innovation and competitive advantage**,
  **sustainability**. Balance function with performance without
  inflating cost.

> **Misconceptions:** *Product design is not only aesthetics ·
> innovation is a strategic necessity, not optional · standardisation
> does not limit diversity · sustainability can improve cost efficiency
> · delayed differentiation **simplifies** inventory management.*

**2. Legal and environmental issues**

| **Issue**                   | **Meaning (Ebook)**                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Patent**                  | Grant of property rights by the government to an inventor; can be sold, licensed, used as collateral. Covers any new and useful process, machine, manufacture, composition or improvement |
| **Trademark**               | Word, name, symbol, device identifying a maker's goods (Coke, Apple's bitten apple, Ford mustang)                                                                                         |
| **Copyright**               | Protects authors, composers, artists; protects the **form of expression**, not the subject matter (spreadsheet concept not protected; the software is)                                    |
| **Product liability**       | Maker or vendor must compensate for injury from defective goods                                                                                                                           |
| **Uniform Commercial Code** | Created **1952**; uniform acts for sale of goods across the 50 US states; simplifies commercial paper (cheques); separates merchants from consumers; aims to remove need for lawyers      |

- Environment: **Environment Protection Act**, following the **UN
  Conference on the Human Environment, Stockholm, June 1972**. Central
  Government can take measures to protect environment; for a company
  offence, persons in charge are deemed guilty unless they prove no
  knowledge or due diligence.

- Quick Notes: protect IP (patents, trademarks, copyrights); use
  eco-friendly materials and processes; think through the life cycle of
  materials (extraction, production, use, disposal).

**3. Product life cycle**

| **Stage**                  | **Ebook features**                                                                 |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **1 Introduction**         | New; consumers must be motivated to try; low volumes; profit down                  |
| **2 Growth**               | Exponential volume growth; competitors enter; features change through improvements |
| **3 Maturity**             | Established; demand and quality stable; **cut-throat competition** on value        |
| **4 Decline / extinction** | New categories give better value, or needs change                                  |

- Introduction helped by: **relative advantage, compatibility,
  divisibility** (limited trial), **communicability**. Hurt by:
  **complexity, perceived risk**.

- Industrial products: diffusion depends on competitive intensity and
  reputation of the supplier industry and vertical co-ordination between
  supplier and adopter.

- Not all products pass all 4 stages (fads, consumer resistance,
  superior new product). PLC works for **product forms** (filter
  cigarettes) better than **brands** (Classic Mild).

> **QB trap:** *"Competition is most severe during the growth stage" =
> **False**. The Ebook places cut-throat competition in **maturity**.*

**Technology life cycle (Ebook, automobile example)**

| **Phase**                | **What happens**                                                                                            |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| I Technology development | 1887 (Daimler, first gasoline car) to 1902; weak competitors eliminated (69 US firms in 1909, half by 1916) |
| II Applications launch   | Experimentation until a standard design (Ford Model T); by 1923 only 8 firms with 99% share                 |
| III Applications growth  | Rapid penetration; then innovation slows and market peaks                                                   |
| IV Mature technology     | **Process innovations dominate**; price competition; by 1965 only GM, Ford, Chrysler, American Motors       |
| V                        | Substitute technologies overrun; market size set by demography, replacement, exports                        |

- Growth stage of **process** technology coincides with the **maturity**
  stage of the **product**.

- Quick Notes PLC management: introduction = careful launch planning;
  growth = scale up, optimise supply chain; maturity = manage
  saturation, extend life; decline = reassess features, modernise or
  exit.

**4. Product design and development**

- Edison: **1,300 inventions, 1,100 patents**; "Genius is 1% inspiration
  and 99% perspiration". Four components of his model still used:
  **lofty goals · right-to-left process** (start from customers) **·
  structure** (clear targets) **· fluidity** (talent, not hierarchy).

- Start from an evaluation of the organisation's capabilities and
  resources.

- **Fast to market**: (1) **fast to customization** = product for one
  customer's specific needs; (2) **fast to design** = product for a
  **cluster** of customers (usable in MTS, ATO, MTO). Nokia camera phone
  = first mover. Not always good: Mercedes buyers valued design over
  yearly models; the ad executive who waits a week.

**Ebook: 7 stages of the development process**

1.  **Clarification of the task**: ideas fitting the new-product
    strategy; customer needs; constraints; general specification.

2.  **Concept generation**: function structure, concept variants;
    **screening** (management process; **most ideas die here**, QB);
    business analysis.

3.  **Embodiment design**: preliminary configuration, refine,
    preliminary parts list.

4.  **Detailed engineering design**: components, materials, sizes;
    trade-offs; drawings; **CAD** (GM and IBM began in the sixties);
    virtual prototypes.

5.  **Physical evaluation**: working prototype, testing; computer
    simulation often first.

6.  **Product and process development**: plans for materials and
    production; suppliers involved.

7.  **Product introduction**: monitor satisfaction and field failures;
    customer-support intensive.

**Quick Notes: 4 phases**

| **Phase**                   | **Key activity**                                                            |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Idea generation**         | Brainstorm, explore customer needs; bedrock of innovation                   |
| **Concept development**     | Refine selected ideas; **feasibility studies, initial market assessment**   |
| **Prototyping and testing** | Physical prototypes; test functionality, **safety, reliability**            |
| **Design finalisation**     | Integrate prototype feedback; **detailed specifications for manufacturing** |

> **Two versions:** *Ebook lists 7 stages; Quick Notes list 4 phases.
> Misconception: design is **iterative**, not linear; prototyping also
> gives user feedback, not only technical checks.*

**5. Concurrent engineering**

- Ebook: based on **significant overlap** among phases; **parallel
  completion** of project phases; teams: **program management,
  technical, design-build**, with an integration team.

- Results: Chrysler, Ford, GM cut concept-to-production from **5 to 3
  years**; tank prototype by 14 engineers in **16 months** (vs 3 years,
  55 engineers); Northrop Grumman B2 mock-up in less than half the time.

- Quick Notes: **cross-functional collaboration** of design,
  manufacturing and other teams; reduces time-to-market. Misconception:
  it includes marketing, sales, customer support too, not only design
  and manufacturing.

**6. Standardisation, customisation, postponement**

- Standard parts: **Eli Whitney** (army rifle contract); Henry Ford's
  assembly lines. Standard products allow **make to stock**, decoupling
  manufacturing from market transactions.

| **Benefit of standardisation (Ebook)** | **Point**                                                            |
|----------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Lower product costs                    | Economies of scale; less skilled labour; but **reduced flexibility** |
| Quicker product design                 | Standard interfaces (PCs)                                            |
| Enhanced flexibility capabilities      | Add features within standard interfaces                              |
| Delivery                               | Transport economies; stock near customers                            |
| Simplified value comparisons           | 60-watt Philips vs Laxman and Sylvania bulb                          |

- Design checks for standard-part products: function, reliability,
  maintainability, safety, simple production process.

**Mass customisation**

- **Built on postponement**; the **extreme form** of postponement: final
  configuration once the customer's order is known; custom product at
  mass-production efficiency; turns the supply chain to **pull**.
  Example: **Dell** (Internet orders, direct shipment). BMW Mini:
  customers design their own roof.

- Quick Notes: technology such as **3D printing and CAD** enables mass
  customisation.

**Delayed differentiation (postponement)**

- First suggested by **Alderson in 1950**: add options or differentiate
  close to the time of purchase. Classic example: **Benetton**. Suits
  innovative products with short life cycle and high obsolescence risk.

- Does not eliminate inventory; shifts it **upstream**. Works because
  aggregate demand is more predictable than demand for each variant.

- Under competition, strategic effects can reduce its value; early
  differentiation can even dominate.

- Needs three design attributes: **commonality, modularity,
  standardisation**.

| **Concept**        | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                              |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Commonality**    | Common components across models → fewer parts in stock (mobile phone maker)                                                                              |
| **Recycling**      | Recover materials for future use. Benefits: **less** raw material use, lower environmental impact, cleaner surroundings, landfill space, money, energy   |
| **Modular design** | Pre-engineered sub-assemblies chosen to order (assemble to order); mix-and-match clothing; Dell; **Maruti 800, Omni, Gypsy (1984-85) from one platform** |

> **QB trap:** *"Waste recycling leads to **maximum** utilisation of raw
> materials" = **False**. Ebook: recycling leads to **less** utilisation
> of raw materials.*

- Quick Notes modular principles: **component modularity**
  (interchangeable modules), **scalability and flexibility** (design for
  future change), **supply chain integration** (inventory and production
  flexibility).

**Product architecture**

- Specifies (1) functional capabilities, features, post-sale
  servicing; (2) delivery system and support capabilities; (3) roles and
  risks of each supply-chain player. Issues: can MTS meet core needs,
  modules for ATO variety, module interfaces, technical risk, in-house
  vs contract.

**7. Design for manufacturability (DFM)**

- Design a product for efficient production at the **highest level of
  quality**; avoid complex, expensive designs; simplify assembly.
  Team-based. (QB: "less attention to quality in DFM" = **False**.)

- Guidelines: minimise parts · modular design · multi-use parts · avoid
  separate fasteners · eliminate adjustments · top-down assembly ·
  minimum handling · avoid tools · minimise sub-assemblies · standard
  parts · simplify operations · efficient testing · repeatable processes
  · analyse failures · assess value.

- US Navy Virginia-class submarine: standard parts list cut from
  **95,000 to 16,000**.

- **DFX** = special case of DFM focusing on one area "X". Escorts
  kettle-element holder: casting replaced by standard tube.

**8. Service design**

- Ebook: classify services by **degree of customer contact** = % of time
  the customer must be in the system relative to service time.

| **Contact** | **Ebook features**                                                                                                                  |
|-------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Low**     | Same stages as product design, but often no physical prototype                                                                      |
| **High**    | Hard to control: customer affects timing, nature and perceived quality; needs personalisation, speed, more capacity and flexibility |

**Product vs service design (Ebook, 3 differences)**

8.  Goods can be **inventoried** (extra freedom for designers).

9.  Goods need larger **up-front financial commitment** (more risk).

10. Goods innovation is often **supply-chain-wide dependent** (Intel
    needs software and equipment makers) (QB: True).

**Quick Notes Part 3**

- Service design = planning and organising how a business works to
  improve experience for **both employees and customers**; like a map or
  blueprint.

- Organises 3 resources: **People** (employees, customers, partners) ·
  **Props** (products, stores, social media, checkout software) ·
  **Processes** (steps by staff or customers).

- Product design = tangible things; starts with an idea, makes and tests
  it. Service design = experience; looks at the current state, imagines
  better, improves.

- Key considerations (5 rule sets): overall rules · how services work ·
  employees · handling information · using technology (**help services,
  not run them entirely**).

- General design principles include: understand purpose and
  capabilities; customer needs first; whole service, not bits; treat
  unusual events as normal; get user input; try ideas first; clear
  business model.

- Customer experience = feelings at every touchpoint; service design =
  behind-the-scenes working. Tools: stakeholder mapping, interviews,
  surveys, workshops, **journey mapping**.

- Examples of product + service design together: **Airbnb**, Spotify,
  Uber.

**10. Reliability and CAD**

- **Reliability** = a measure of quality of product and service design
  (patients expect competent staff). **Design for Reliability (DFR)**:
  set system reliability requirements first; "designed in"; models use
  **block diagrams and fault trees**.

- **Redundancy** = alternate success path (two brake-light bulbs); very
  effective but expensive, so limited to critical parts. Others:
  **physics of failure**, **component de-rating** (heavier gauge wire).

| **Improve reliability** | **How**                                   | **Cost**                             |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Fault avoidance**     | High-quality, high-reliability components | **Usually less expensive**           |
| **Fault tolerance**     | **Redundancy**                            | More complexity, weight, space, cost |

- Quick Notes: reliability standards from industry benchmarks; **Failure
  Mode Analysis** identifies failure modes, consequences, mitigation; it
  is **ongoing**, not a one-time event.

- **CAD** = computer-aided design / drafting: 2D and 3D virtual models
  for testing (airplane wing virtual stress tests). Business uses:
  **idea generation, augmentation, market testing**.

**11. Case: Maruti 800 (Ebook)**

- Nationalisation of Maruti in **1981**. Keywords: modern and
  contemporary, lower operating costs, commercially viable, modern
  process technology. Target scooter and motorcycle owners: a
  **sub-compact** rather than compact (less volume-sensitive), price
  under ₹40,000. "Competes not with other cars but two wheelers."
