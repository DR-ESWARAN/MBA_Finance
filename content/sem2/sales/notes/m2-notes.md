**1. Meaning of personal selling**

- **Ebook:** a promotional method in which one party (salesperson) uses
  skills and techniques to build **personal relationships** with another
  party, so that **both parties obtain value** (QB Q1 True).
  Salesperson's value = financial reward; customer's value = product
  benefits.

- Purchase is not always the objective; selling may simply **deliver
  information**. Face-to-face, telephone, video conferencing, online
  chat.

- US Department of Labor: **over 14 million (about 11%)** of the labour
  force in selling; worldwide closer to **100 million**. CEOs and COOs
  also "sell" the company to investors and officials.

| **Author**             | **Definition**                                                                                                      |
|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Richard Buskirk**    | Personal selling consists of **contacting prospective buyers personally**                                           |
| **AMA**                | "An **oral presentation in conversation** with one or more prospective purchasers for the purpose of making sales"  |
| **Cundiff and Still**  | Basically a **method of communication**; individual and social behaviour; each influences the other                 |
| **William J. Stanton** | Individual, personal communication, in contrast to mass impersonal communication of advertising and sales promotion |

- **SN:** a dynamic, **two-way** communication method delivered
  **orally**; exceptionally effective but **costly**. Success needs
  **unique (personal), product-specific and functional** competencies
  plus analytical and administrative work. Research the prospect's
  background, size, authority, needs first.

- **8 characteristics (Ebook):** direct selling; oral conversation;
  seller convinces buyer; sale personally; **most effective tool** to
  increase sales; **greatest freedom to adjust the message**; gives
  market information; essential for survival and growth. (QB Q2: direct
  method of selling through salesman = True.)

**2. Objectives of personal selling**

- **Qualitative (long-term) objectives (9):** service existing
  customers; get new customers; carry out the whole selling task if
  other promotion is absent; help the trade sell, stock and redress
  grievances; make customers aware of marketing strategy; act as
  **technical consultants** for complex products; feedback to the
  company; help middlemen's sales force; help the trade with
  administrative problems.

- Objectives are **qualitative in direction, quantitative in goals**.
  Quantitative objectives are **short-term**, keep changing and are
  **time bound**. Basic quantitative objective = **sales volume in
  rupees or units**; others derived from it: market share, profit, new
  accounts, expense level, % business from specified accounts.

- **Ebook end-of-chapter objectives (4):** obtain a specified sales
  volume; volume that contributes to **profit** (proper product mix);
  keep selling **expenses within limits**; secure and retain a specified
  **market share**.

- **Study Guide objectives:** prospecting (cold and warm calling,
  referrals), building awareness, sales, **building relationships** (the
  long-term objective: prevent attrition, repeat business, word of
  mouth).

**Importance (Ebook, 10)**

Helpful in selling · demonstrating the product · **time coordination** ·
finding prospective customers (not possible through advertising) ·
meeting specific objections · **non-selling functions** (marketing
research, repair, complaints, goodwill) · providing information ·
important to buyer · **important to society** (employment, standard of
living, balances demand and supply, reduces price fluctuation) ·
**effective selling** (stops wastage, unlike advertising that reaches
non-customers).

**3. Theories of personal selling (Ebook, 9)**

| **Theory**                          | **Core idea**                                                                                                                                                   | **Orientation / key words**                                                                                            |
|-------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. **AIDAS**                       | Five mental stages: Attention → Interest → Desire → Action → Satisfaction. Also called **formula-selling**                                                      | Seller. AIDA 1920s (**Strong 1925**); from **AIDR** (resolve) of **Arthur Sheldon, 1902**; AIDAS used since late 1950s |
| 2\. **Right set of circumstances**  | Also called **situation-response theory**. Situation is the psychological driver; salesperson creates a persuasive situation from internal and external factors | **Seller**-oriented. Five-star dinner example                                                                          |
| 3\. **Buying formula**              | Buyer's need/problem is the starting point; solution = **product/service + trade name**; adequacy + pleasant feeling → habit, repeat buying                     | Buyer-oriented. Every element highlighted in the presentation (QB Q7)                                                  |
| 4\. **Behavioural equation**        | **J. A. Howard (1963)** stimulus-response model; buying as learning: **drive, cue, response, reinforcement**; multiplicative equation                           | If any variable = 0, buying (B) = 0                                                                                    |
| 5\. **Need satisfaction**           | Need identification → need fulfilment → need satisfaction                                                                                                       | **Customer**-oriented. Payroll software, insurance examples                                                            |
| 6\. **Consultative**                | Salesperson acts as **consultant** to help customers achieve **strategic goals** (QB Q9)                                                                        | Diagnosis of needs first; 8 problems solved                                                                            |
| 7\. **Customer relationship-based** | Relationship over transaction; service is the link; customer buys a **set of benefits**                                                                         | Rapport building is key; social relationship overrides business                                                        |
| 8\. **Problem-solving**             | **Extension of need satisfaction**; starts with the **root of the problem**                                                                                     | "Intellectual part of selling". Group insurance, washing machine                                                       |
| 9\. **Team selling**                | Multiple personnel solve **complex buying problems**; national / key accounts, technical buying                                                                 | **Boress (2007)** ten rules                                                                                            |

**AIDAS detail**

- **Attention:** initial session is vital; conversational skill,
  openness, assertiveness, risk-taking, rapport, appearance.

- **Interest:** follows naturally; overt or covert (read body language);
  demonstration, samples, flipcharts, videos.

- **Desire:** handle objections and enquiries; a step closer to buying.

- **Action:** remove even minute doubts; complete control of the
  presentation.

- **Satisfaction:** first purchase is **no assurance** of repeat buying
  (QB Q4 False). Post-decision anxiety = **cognitive dissonance**.
  Actual \> expected = **delighted** customer. Satisfaction sets the
  tone for future buying (QB Q5 True).

> **QB trap:** *QB Q3 says "AIDAS stands on **attitude**, interest,
> desire, action, satisfaction" → **False**. The A is **Attention**.*

**Right set of circumstances: four problems**

- External factors fail if internal factors are under-emphasised ·
  internal factors are hard to identify · factors are not equally
  important · situation needs subtle handling.

- **SN version (different):** "if a salesperson garners attention,
  maintains interest and stimulates desire, a sale will result"; the
  salesperson's **control of the presentation** encourages the sale.

**Buying formula: seven implications**

- Buyer not concerned about the need → stress the product that satisfies
  it · knows the problem but not the product → link need and product ·
  knows need but not the trade name → link need and trade name · then
  facilitate purchase · **under competition stress adequacy and pleasant
  feelings** · highlight **every element** in the presentation ·
  **remind old customers** of every element.

**Behavioural equation (Howard)**

| **Element**       | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                                                         |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Drive (D)**     | Strong internal stimuli. **Innate** (hunger, thirst, pain) and **learned** (status, recognition)                                                                                    |
| **Cue**           | Weak stimuli. **Triggering** cues start the decision; **non-triggering** influence it: **product cues** (colour, size, price) and **informational cues** (advertising, salespeople) |
| **Response**      | The buying action                                                                                                                                                                   |
| **Reinforcement** | Psychological event that consolidates the response                                                                                                                                  |

- Corollaries: any variable zero → null response · unmotivated buyer
  (**D = 0**) → B = 0 regardless of habit (P) · satisfaction (**K**)
  raises P (predisposition) · greatest effect on P comes from **usage
  experience** · buyer seeking more information = salesperson motivated
  him · **triggering cues are more meaningful** · few sellers considered
  → stress brand merits (K).

**Consultative selling: eight problems solved**

Need problem · choice problem · deciding choice criteria · product
installation · product-use · product-maintenance · customer-feedback ·
quick redress of defects within and beyond warranty.

**Customer relationship benefits (8)**

Fullest information both ways · reduces doubts · removes communication
barriers · handles future problems · reduces post-purchase anxiety ·
**diminishes media expenditure** · staves off competition · meets future
requirements. A vacuum cleaner is a showpiece if it does not remove
dirt.

**Problem-solving: salesperson helps the prospect to**

Identify the genesis of the problem → define it → provide alternatives →
evaluate alternatives → select a solution (purchase).

**Boress (2007): ten rules of team selling**

Select members carefully · have an **orchestrator** · pre-briefing ·
**debriefing** · prepare questions · keep members to a **minimum** ·
answer succinctly · flexible agenda · same wavelength (no members who
dislike each other) · **sell something** every time. Team composition
changes post-sale (maintenance, service staff). Coordination is vital or
the team loses credibility.

**4. Market potential analysis**

- **Ebook:** market potential = **aggregate demand** for a product in a
  business environment; includes market size, growth, competition,
  profitability, consumer and product type. Do it **before launching** a
  new product.

- Key factors examined: **ability to compete** (price or cost
  advantage), **duration of the opportunity**, **growth potential**
  (long-term growth beats immediate reward), **risks and rewards**.

| **Element (5)**                   | **Key point**                                                                                                                                                               |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. **Market size**               | Total sales potential of all firms in the industry. Steps: define target customer → estimate number → **penetration rate** → volume and value → apply (best/worst/expected) |
| 2\. **Market growth**             | Use **past five years** of industry data                                                                                                                                    |
| 3\. **Profitability**             | Ratios: **ROI, ROS, RONA, ROCE**. Low profit → push volume; low volume → push margin                                                                                        |
| 4\. **Competition**               | High competition → **high entry barriers**; low competition → low awareness, differentiators dominate                                                                       |
| 5\. **Consumer and product type** | Repeat-buying or one-time sale; age of product vs adoption. Not applicable to **innovative** products                                                                       |

> **Formulas: *Market volume = number of target customers × penetration
> rate.*** ***Market value = market volume × average value (price
> expectation).** Penetration rate = % of target market that consumes
> the product; high if the product is vital, low if specialised.*

- Ebook example: **African cuisine restaurant** (200 people, mostly
  youth; new houses = growth; repeat business).

- **SN:** market potential analysis = strategic instrument that gauges
  **viability of the concept and existing demand**; starts with
  **identifying the target group**. Questions: problem resolution,
  beneficiary, financial capacity (**CAC vs Customer Lifetime Value**),
  brand positioning, buyer persona.

**5. Sales potential and sales forecasting**

- **Sales potential (Ebook):** estimated **market share** a company
  expects to capture in a stipulated time after entering. **Market
  potential** = total sales of **all players** in the industry in the
  area and period. Sales potential is generally a **percentage of market
  potential**; sales potential is the prospective market share, market
  potential the market size for the category.

- Brand X loyalists are **market potential** for Brand Y but **not its
  sales potential**. Sales potential estimates are usually calculated
  **from** market potential estimates.

- Advantages: right quantity at right time, expected revenue, raw
  materials and labour, decision start, planning. Disadvantages:
  unpredictable buying behaviour; inaccurate due to technological
  disruption and mega trends. **Coca-Cola**: weekly reviews adjust
  monthly forecasts; region → cities, towns, villages.

- **SN:** sales potential = company's envisioned **sales volume within a
  defined period** (number and monetary value; incorporates market
  share). A **sales forecast is the outcome of assessing sales
  potential**, often from historical data. Dual role: **alert**
  (underperformance vs quota) and **motivational tool**. Roles:
  strategic decisions, resource allocation, budgeting and goal setting,
  proactive problem solving.

- **Sales forecasting (Ebook):** estimating future sales from past data,
  industry comparisons and economic trends; new firms rely on market
  research and competitive intelligence. Lets firms predict revenue,
  allocate resources, plan growth.

- Importance (9): production volumes · **basis of sales and production
  budgets** · plant expansion and product mix · policies · extent of
  advertising · a **commitment** of the sales department · production
  and purchasing schedules · decision aid · guides activities.

- **Factors considered (4):** competition, **changes in technology**,
  **government action**, factors related to the concern itself
  (capacity, price, product mix).

**6. Personal selling methods**

**Ebook: five selling methods**

Marketing is long-run planning around customer wants; **selling is a
tool** in it. The salesperson is **bending demand to fit the company's
supply**.

| **Method**                   | **Essence**                                                                                             | **Steps**                                                                                                                                 |
|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Consultative**             | Problem solving that **improves the customer's profit**; long-term, mutually beneficial                 | 4: problem in **financial terms** → quantify profit-improvement solution (no product yet) → fit products → propose long-term relationship |
| **High octane / creative**   | Extraordinary, impress and challenge; original presentation remembered                                  | 6: preparation → **incubation** → **illumination** → evaluation → transformation → implementation                                         |
| **Non-manipulative**         | Also **collaborative / guilt-free**: "removing pressure and still getting the sale"                     | 4: define needs → find solution → implement → **track results**                                                                           |
| **Compelling / traditional** | "Selling is the art of communication for persuasion"; **salesperson-oriented, high pressure**, no trust | 6: sales plan → identify customer → preparation → approach → apply techniques → close. **Not recommended** long-term                      |
| **Cross selling**            | Referral from a **colleague within the company** to sell parallel products; "everyone's job"            | 5: nurture relationship → written plan → engage all → measure → positive feedback                                                         |

- It is **cheaper to maintain an ongoing relationship** than to create
  new ones (argument against compelling selling).

**SN: seven personal selling strategies**

Highlight the product's **advantages** (benefits) · showcase
**functionality** (home alarm demo) · foster engaging **dialogue** ·
serve as a knowledgeable **advisor** · prioritise **customer
satisfaction** (testimonials; real estate, automobiles) · engage through
**narratives** (stories, emotional appeal) · **adjust to customer
behaviour**. Best for intricate, high-value or customised purchases
(real estate, automobiles, insurance) and B2B.

**7. Sales-related marketing policies**

**Product policy**

- Defines the **type, volume and timing** of products offered. Product
  planning = search, screening, development and commercialisation of new
  products, modification of lines, and **discontinuance** of
  unprofitable items.

- **Exploration** (idea generation) is the first step. Internal sources:
  salesmen, employees, managers. External: customers, distributors, ad
  agencies, labs, trade associations, government.

| **Idea technique**                | **What it is**                                                                                                                   |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Gap analysis**                  | Find gaps of **unsatisfied demand**                                                                                              |
| **Attribute listing**             | List attributes and modify them into a new combination                                                                           |
| **Brainstorming**                 | **6 to 8** creative people, about **1.5 hours**; "the wilder the idea, the better"; quantity encouraged, **criticism ruled out** |
| **Forced relationships**          | List objects; consider each in relation to every other                                                                           |
| **Morphological analysis**        | Morphology = **structure**; identify structural dimensions of a problem                                                          |
| **Need / problem identification** | Starts with the **consumer**; rate problems for seriousness, incidence, cost of remedy                                           |

- Also named: synectics (listed only). **SN sales policy** covers target
  markets, key customers, pricing structures, sales quotas; marketing
  policy covers positioning and brand awareness.

**Distribution policy**

- Strategy for correct shipment from production to market positioning,
  within launch and replenishment times. Product journey: manufacturer →
  wholesaler → distributor/contractor → retailer → agents/reps →
  consumer.

| **Policy**    | **Meaning**                                                                                                                                  |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Direct**    | Single channel, own physical or online store                                                                                                 |
| **Indirect**  | Several intermediaries and channels                                                                                                          |
| **Intensive** | **Most wide-ranging**; many intermediaries; international, all-channel                                                                       |
| **Selective** | Limited channels or a small geographical network                                                                                             |
| **Exclusive** | Rights to **one channel/outlet**; luxury, prestige. Ebook: **Bulgari at Harrods, London**. SN: **Tanishq ("Tanshiq") in Delhi** for diamonds |

- Design factors: product type (perishable → faster; hazardous →
  insurance), where customers buy, need for in-person demos,
  customisation.

**Pricing policy**

- A pricing policy is "**a standing answer to a recurring question**".
  Pricing is often a patchwork of ad hoc decisions: "**price management
  by catastrophe**".

| **Considerations in formulating (Ebook, 8)** | **Key point**                                               |
|----------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Competitive situation                        | Pricing policy matters only under **imperfect competition** |
| Goal of profit and sales                     | Profitable combination sales                                |
| Long-range welfare                           | Avoid high prices that attract rivals                       |
| Flexibility                                  | Cyclical variations                                         |
| Government policy                            | Injects politics into price fixation                        |
| Overall goals                                | Broadest goal is **survival**                               |
| Price sensitivity                            | Businessmen **exaggerate** it                               |
| Routinisation                                | Conjectural data → mechanical formula                       |

- **Pricing objectives (10):** price-profit satisfaction (stable prices)
  · sales maximisation and growth · making money (premium) · preventing
  competition · market share · survival · **market penetration** (lowest
  price, price-sensitive market) · **market skimming** (high price;
  **DuPont**) · early cash recovery · satisfactory rate of return.

- **Factors (6):** cost data, demand, consumer psychology, competition,
  profit, government policy.

- **Cost:** short run must cover **variable cost**; long run all costs.
  Pricing is a **tripod**: cost, demand, competition. **Nickerson**:
  cost is an indicator and a **resistance point** to lowering price.
  Relevant costs = **directly traceable** to a product.

- **Demand:** inelastic → high price; elastic → lower price. Very short
  term, the chief influence is demand. **Psychology:** too low a price
  signals **inferior quality**; in affluent societies price indicates
  quality.

- **Competition:** perfect competition = no discretion; monopoly = high
  price. **Profit:** maximise where **MR = MC**; price rigidity =
  stable, not inflexible. **Government:** ceiling prices, minimum
  prices, **dual pricing**.

- **SN pricing strategy (6):** market research, **value-based**,
  **cost-plus**, **psychological** (charm, bundle, premium), **dynamic**
  (demand, season), discounting policies. **SN pricing factors (8):**
  competition, profit objectives, sales volume, financial health,
  adaptability, regulatory environment, pricing methodology (software),
  sales channels.
