**1. What is strategy?**

- **Origin (SN):** Greek **"strategos"** = "a general" / "the art of the
  general"; used in ancient Greek military science. **Study Guide:**
  "strategos" from **stratus (army) + ago (leading/moving)**. Ebook:
  "art of the general" covered tactics, siege craft, logistics.

- **Ebook definition:** a general plan to achieve one or more long-term
  goals **under conditions of uncertainty**. Strategy matters because
  **resources are limited**. It describes how the **ends (goals)** will
  be achieved by the **means (resources)**. Strategy can be **intended**
  or can **emerge** as a pattern of activity.

- **Chandler (1962):** "the determination of the basic **long-term goals
  and objectives** of an enterprise, and the adoption of **courses of
  action** and the **allocation of resources** necessary for carrying
  out these goals." Ebook names him **Alfred D. Chandler**; SN and FAQ
  say **Alfred P. Chandler**. Studied **DuPont, General Motors, Standard
  Oil, Sears Roebuck**.

- Chandler stressed 3 elements: (1) courses of action for attaining
  objectives; (2) the process of **seeking key ideas**; (3) **how**
  strategy is formulated, not just what it turns out to be.

- **Kenichi Ohmae (SN):** strategy = a company's attempt to
  **distinguish itself favourably** from competitors, using relative
  advantages to meet client demands.

- **QB:** "Which of these seeks to relate the goals of the organisation
  to the means of achieving them?" → **Strategy**.

**Nature of strategy (Ebook)**

- A major course of action relating the organisation to its
  **environment** (especially external factors).

- A **blend of internal and external factors**: internal factors are
  matched to external opportunities and threats.

- A combination of actions for a particular condition; actions differ by
  situation.

- May involve **contradictory actions** (close one business and expand
  another at the same time).

- **Future oriented**; needs systems and norms; gives an overall
  framework for enterprise thinking and action.

**Features of strategy**

| **Ebook (3 features)**                                                                                     | **Study Notes (10 features)**                                                          |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Significant because the future cannot be foreseen; must deal with uncertain events                         | **Winning** is the primary goal                                                        |
| Deals with **long-term developments**, not routine operations (innovation, new products, methods, markets) | Long-term priority · particular measures · **future-focused**                          |
| Takes into account probable behaviour of customers, competitors (and employees)                            | Set by **upper management** thinking · **dynamic** · **creative**                      |
|                                                                                                            | Best use of resources · handles **uncertainty** · covers **all organisational levels** |

**Scope of strategy (Ebook)**

- The products and services a company plans to offer over a period, and
  **where and to which target markets** they will be sold. One of
  **three sets of decisions** in strategic planning.

- Deciding scope helps: set profit targets from **existing vs new
  markets**; identify work priorities and volume; determine execution
  plans (structure, sourcing, manufacturing, marketing, sales, tools);
  establish **which markets to enter or exit**.

- **SN scope** has 3 parts: **analysing current position** · **changes
  made** · **future forecast**.

**4. Evolution of strategy**

- **Sun Tzu, The Art of War** (~5th century BC) = earliest root. Modern
  **corporate strategy became a practice in the 1960s** (SN).

- Global economy grew ~**4% a year for 50 years** (6× bigger).
  Consumerism gained acceptance in America in the **1920s**.

| **Phase (Richard Horwath, Strategic Thinking Institute)** | **Years**    | **Focus**                                              |
|-----------------------------------------------------------|--------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Budgetary planning                                    | 1950-1960    | Operating budgets, financial control                   |
| 2\. Corporate planning                                    | 1960-1968    | Plan for growth, earnings, visibility, brand           |
| 3\. Corporate strategy                                    | 1968-1975    | **Portfolio planning**; corporate parent and divisions |
| 4\. Industry & competitive analysis                       | 1975-1985    | Segments, markets, positioning                         |
| 5\. Internal sourcing of competitive advantage            | 1985-1995    | **Core competencies** (Hamel & Prahalad)               |
| 6\. Strategic innovation & execution                      | 1995-2001    | Sustained advantage no longer lasts; dynamic sources   |
| 7\. Strategic thinking & simplification                   | 2003 onwards | Practical strategic-thinking skills, simple frameworks |

> **Source difference:** *Ebook (Module 2) gives a **4-phase** evolution
> of strategic management (financial planning → forecast-based →
> externally oriented → strategic management). SN Module 1 gives
> Horwath's **7 phases**. Ebook M1 says only that strategic management
> emerged as a separate process **after World War II**.*

**5. Importance of strategy**

| **Ebook: a successful strategic plan…**       | **SN: 5 factors**                                                    | **Study Guide adds**                                   |
|-----------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Provides **direction and action plans**   | 1\. **Planning** (annual plan built on 3-5 year plan)                | Proper resource allocation                             |
| 2\. **Prioritises and aligns** activities     | 2\. **Strengths and weaknesses**                                     | Examines external environment                          |
| 3\. Defines **accountabilities**              | 3\. **Skills & knowledge**                                           | Profit maximisation · competitive advantage            |
| 4\. Enhances **communication and commitment** | 4\. **Resource allocation** (choosing is the foundation of strategy) | Vision accomplishment · dynamic leadership             |

**6. Purpose of business (SN Part 2)**

- Business = exchange, purchase, sale and production of goods and
  services. Main purpose (SN/FAQ) = **generate profit and satisfy client
  demands**. Purpose of a business = **offer value to clients** who pay
  with cash or alternatives; the margin left over can be reinvested,
  saved or donated.

- **Business plan** = formal document of goals, objectives, strategies;
  needed to **borrow money** to start up.

- Purpose is **timeless**: vision and mission may change, purpose does
  not.

- **Direct actions:** acquiring, maintaining and growing client
  business; also R&D (new value), marketing (demand), finance (cash
  flow). **Indirect:** workspace, tools, legal safeguards. Employees in
  **direct action** are valued more.

- **Evolution:** ancient = meet local needs → Industrial Revolution
  (18th-19th c.) = **scale and efficiency** → 20th c. =
  **stakeholder-centric** → recent = **CSR and sustainability**,
  **triple bottom line**.

| **Dimension**     | **Focus**                                                             |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Economic**      | Profit, growth, innovation, efficiency, financial sustainability      |
| **Social**        | Ethics, community, employee welfare, CSR, diversity                   |
| **Environmental** | Emissions, waste, renewable energy, recycling, sustainable production |

- **Stakeholder perception** is shaped by public image (most important),
  communication, **CSR activities**, leadership style and employee
  conduct (not communication alone).

| **Theory**                        | **Key point**                                                                           |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Stakeholder Theory**            | **R. Edward Freeman, 1984**; create value for all parties, not just shareholders        |
| **Triple Bottom Line**            | 3 Ps: **profit, people, planet**                                                        |
| **CSR**                           | Socially accountable; large firms use it as strategy                                    |
| **Purpose-Driven Business Model** | Purpose beyond monetary gain; customers, workers, community above shareholders          |
| **Conscious Capitalism**          | Free markets + value creation, cooperation, compassion, trust; does not restrict profit |
| **SDGs (Global Goals)**           | UN **2015**, **17 goals**, target **2030**; end poverty, hunger, AIDS, discrimination   |

**7. Goals vs objectives**

| **Point**        | **Goals**                                                 | **Objectives**                                  |
|------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Order            | Set to achieve the **mission**; higher                    | Set to achieve **goals**                        |
| Scope            | Broad; often **cannot be measured** in quantifiable units | Narrow; specific tasks                          |
| Tangibility      | Can be **intangible**                                     | **Tangible** targets                            |
| Time             | **Long** period                                           | Short (a **year or less**)                      |
| Nature (SN)      | Qualitative, inspirational, abstract, concept-based       | Quantitative, actionable, fact-based, **SMART** |
| Language (Ebook) | Conceptual thinking                                       | "More on the creative side"                     |

- **Objective examples (Ebook):** 15% ROI in a fiscal year · market
  share to 7% · cut operating cost 10% in two years · sales-inquiry
  response in 12 hours. **SN:** a business objective needs 4 elements:
  **growth intention, action(s), measurement, timeline**.

- **Benefits of goals:** direction · priorities · conviction · decision
  making · motivation · reach full potential. **Benefits of
  objectives:** measure progress · sense of achievement · confirm
  strategy · help difficult decisions · show what the company expects.

> **Source conflict:** *Ebook (Objectives and Goals section) also gives
> the opposite view: "objectives are **open-ended**, stated in general
> terms; when stated in specific terms they become **goals**", then adds
> that in strategic management goals are broad and objectives specific.
> The LMS Study Notes and the QB follow **goals = broad, objectives =
> specific**. Use that.*

- **QB:** "Which is true about goals and objectives?" → **Goals are
  incapable of being measured in quantifiable units**. (Goals, not
  objectives, give direction; goals can be intangible.)

**Objectives in strategic management (Ebook)**

- **Need:** yardstick for performance · motivating force · pursue vision
  and mission · define relationship with environment · basis for
  decision making.

- **Areas:** profit (most important), marketing, productivity
  (input-output ratio), product, social, financial (e.g. **collection
  period 30 days**), human resources (e.g. **absenteeism under 10%**).

- **Characteristics (7):** hierarchy · multiplicity · specific time
  horizon · attainable · understandable · concrete (quantified) · set
  within constraints.

**8. Strategic intent**

- Concept described in **Harvard Business Review, 1989**; term coined by
  **Hamel and Prahalad** (the Ebook prints "Hamed and Prahalad"). An
  **obsession** with winning, possibly **out of proportion to
  resources**; envisions a desired leadership position.

- **Ebook hierarchy (4):** creating and communicating a **vision** →
  designing a **mission** → **defining the business** → **setting
  objectives**.

- **SN hierarchy (5, pyramid):** **Vision → Mission → Business
  Definition → Business Model → Goals & Objectives**.

- **Stretch** = a **misfit between resources and aspirations**.
  **Leverage** concentrates, accumulates, conserves and recovers
  resources so a meagre base can be stretched. **Fit** matches resources
  and environment.

- SN: strategic intent turns strategy into a **"stretch" activity rather
  than a "fit" exercise**; focus on **winning** customers, competitors
  and the market. Study Guide: leveraging internal resources,
  capabilities and core competencies to achieve vision and mission.

- **QB:** "Something that doesn't fit between resources and aspirations"
  → **Stretch**. "Strategic intent involves" → **setting objectives**.

**Vision**

- **Top of the hierarchy**; what the firm ultimately wants to become. A
  **dream** more than an articulated idea; **forward thinking**. "Where"
  a business aspires to be. Study Guide: vision statement usually covers
  **5 to 10 years** or longer.

- **Envisioning** = process of creating a vision. A well-conceived
  vision has **core ideology** (core values + core purpose; unchanging)
  and **envisioned future** (long-term objectives).

- **Advantages:** foster experimentation, long-term thinking, risk
  taking; make firms competitive and unique; represent integrity;
  inspire.

- **SN characteristics (6):** precise language · drives a common goal ·
  attracts people · guides decision makers · sets a **standard of
  excellence** · bridges present to future.

- **Strategic vision** = roadmap of "where we are going"; a **mission
  statement is the result**.

**Mission**

- Relates the organisation to **society**; "**why**" the business
  exists. **Hunger & Wheelen:** "purpose or reason for the
  organisation's existence". **Thompson:** essential purpose, nature of
  business, customers served. **David F. Harvey:** sense of purpose,
  competitive environment, fit with capabilities and government
  opportunities.

- **Ebook characteristics (8):** realistic · neither too broad nor too
  narrow · not ambiguous · distinct · **societal linkage** · not static
  · motivating · indicates process.

- **SN characteristics:** broad in scope · **precise (usually not more
  than 250 words)** · inspiring · multi-component · distinctive ·
  components of strategy.

- **Examples (Ebook, "academically not accepted", called slogans):**
  India Today "The complete new magazine" · Bajaj Auto "Value for Money
  for Years" · HCL "To be a world class Competitor" · HMT "Timekeepers
  of the Nation". Ranbaxy "To become a research based international
  Pharmaceuticals Company".

- **Sources:** national priorities in plan documents · corporate
  philosophy · strategists' vision · consultants.

| **Mission**                                   | **Purpose**                        |
|-----------------------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Societal reasoning                            | Overall reason                     |
| **External**; relates to external environment | **Internal**; internal environment |
| For **outsiders**                             | For **own employees**              |

> **Misconception:** *Vision and mission are **not** interchangeable:
> vision = future aspiration ("where"); mission = current purpose and
> operations ("why"). Business model ≠ business definition: definition =
> how profits are created; model = how those elements are used in
> operations.*

**Business definition (Derek Abell)**

- Defines the business along **3 dimensions**: **customer groups**,
  **customer functions**, **alternative technologies**.

- **Watch example:** groups = individuals, commercial and sports
  organisations, educational institutions; functions = record time,
  alarm, gift; technologies = **manual, mechanical, automatic**.

**Values and purpose**

- **Values** = essential and enduring tenets (beliefs about employees,
  customers, shareholders). **Core purpose** = the "why"; internal;
  long-term rationale; linkage with own people.

**9. Core competencies**

- A company's **set of skills or experience** in some activity (not
  physical or financial assets); its **strategic strength**. Introduced
  by **C.K. Prahalad and Gary Hamel** in "The Core Competence of the
  Corporation", **HBR 1990**: "an area of specialised expertise from
  harmonising complex streams of technology and work activity".

| **3 tests (Ebook)**        | **Meaning**                                                   |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Access to **many markets** | Leads to new products and services                            |
| **Customer benefit**       | Significant contribution to perceived benefits of end product |
| **Hard to imitate**        | Difficult for competitors to copy                             |

- **Ebook examples:** Honda = engines (lawn mowers, snow blowers, cars)
  · Sony = **miniaturisation** · FedEx = logistics and customer service
  · Apple = product design (iPod).

- **SN examples:** Google = consistent high quality · QuickBooks =
  continuous innovation · Dropbox = incomparable value (file sharing) ·
  Amazon = client service · McDonald's = size and buying power · Target
  = marketing.

- **How to identify (SN, 6):** look at clients · turn to mission
  statement · discuss with team · check they are **real** core
  competencies · **outsource** as required · put them to the test.
