**1. Strategy evaluation**

- **Ebook:** as significant as formulation; shows efficiency and
  effectiveness of plans. Strategy evaluation is the **final phase** of
  strategic management. Needs an effective **computerised information
  system** for timely feedback.

- Significance: develop inputs for new planning · urge for feedback ·
  appraisal and reward · develop the process · judge validity of
  strategic choice.

| **4 steps (Ebook)**                   | **Detail**                                                                                                                                   |
|---------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. **Fix benchmarks** of performance | **Quantitative** (net profit, ROI, EPS, cost of production, employee turnover) + **qualitative** (skills, risk taking, flexibility)          |
| 2\. **Measure** performance           | Reporting and communication system; financial statements **annually**; manager's contribution and divisional performance are hard to measure |
| 3\. **Analyse variance**              | Tolerance limits; **negative deviation** = shortfall, find causes                                                                            |
| 4\. **Take corrective action**        | Analyse causes; **lower standards** if potential does not match; rare and drastic = **reformulate** the strategy                             |

**2. Strategic control**

- **Ebook:** the process used to control the formation and execution of
  strategic plans; a **specialised form of management control** that
  must handle **uncertainty and ambiguity**. Ensures the organisation is
  achieving its objectives, and what corrective action is needed.

- **SN:** strategic evaluation and control = evaluating a strategy's
  ability to reach objectives and taking corrective measures; e.g. a
  **crisis management team** and **contingency plans**.

- **SN control categories (2):** **operational control** (allocate and
  use resources by assessing how divisions, units, SBUs perform) and
  **strategic control** (assess strategy as it is used, against shifting
  presumptions).

| **4 types of strategic control** | **Ebook**                                                                                                                                           | **SN**                                                                                                |
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Premise control**              | Strategy rests on **assumptions** (government policy, competition); track changes in key postulations                                               | Checks the central premise; usually handled by the **corporate planning department**                  |
| **Implementation control**       | Are plans, programmes and projects guiding the firm to goals? "strategic rethinking"; monitor **strategic thrusts / throb points**                  | Step-by-step; 2 subtypes: **monitoring strategic projects (thrusts)** and **reviewing milestones**    |
| **Strategic surveillance**       | **Generalised and overriding**; monitors a broad range of events inside and outside                                                                 | Universal control; data from **knowledge management and organisational learning** systems             |
| **Special alert control**        | **Trigger mechanism** for rapid response to sudden events: fall of government, **terrorist attacks**, industrial disaster, earthquake, floods, fire | Quick reaction; **crisis management team**, backup plans; e.g. competitor acquisition, product recall |

- **QB:** "Sudden change in government, natural calamities, terrorist
  attacks, industrial disasters" → **Special alert control**. "How many
  types of strategic control?" → **Four**.

> **Misconceptions:** *Strategic and operational control have
> **different** goals · special alert ≠ surveillance · strategic control
> is also **continuous**, not just long-term planning · implementation
> control is more than budgets and schedules · premise control is
> **broader** than the corporate planning department.*

**3. Strategic vs operational control**

| **Point**            | **Strategic control**                               | **Operational control**                              |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Data sources (Ebook) | **More sources**, more **external**                 | Few sources, internal                                |
| Orientation          | **Future**; accuracy of the **decision premise**    | Immediate; quantitative outcomes                     |
| Standards            | Based on **external** factors                       | Past performance                                     |
| Reporting interval   | **Variable**; over months                           | Fixed (pieces/week, profit/quarter); daily or weekly |
| Corrections          | Slower (needs analysis)                             | **Faster**, on the spot                              |
| Type of control (SN) | **Feedback + steering**                             | **Feedback**; **post-action**                        |
| Who (SN)             | **Top executives**                                  | Functional-level officers                            |
| Aim (SN)             | **Effectiveness**; is the business moving properly? | **Efficiency**; how well is it doing?                |
| Time frame (SN)      | Long term, **5 years or more**                      | Short term, **1 month to 1 year**                    |
| Example (Ebook)      | The **sales process**                               | **Sales numbers**                                    |

- **Management control** (Ebook): "the set of measurement, analysis and
  action decisions required for the timely management of the continuing
  operation of a process". Control = set standards, measure, correct.
  **Planning and control = "Siamese twins" of management.**

- Strategic management is ambiguous, complex, organisation-wide,
  critical to survival, long term; operational management is the
  opposite. Performance measures via models such as the **BSC**;
  compliance measures via **COSO, Sarbanes-Oxley, BIS** frameworks.

**4. Synergy**

- **Ebook:** the whole is worth **more than the sum of the parts**; a
  driving force behind **M&A**. **SN:** two elements together create
  something more powerful than either alone.

| **Ebook type**        | **Meaning and example**                                                                                                                                                                      |
|-----------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Operating synergy** | Higher operating income and growth: economies of scale, pricing power, combined functional strengths. **Tata Steel–Corus, 2007, \$12.02 billion** → world's **fifth-largest** steel producer |
| **Financial synergy** | Revenue, **debt capacity**, **cost of capital**, cash flows, tax benefits                                                                                                                    |
| **Marketing synergy** | Marketing mix used for overall effectiveness. **Hero Honda** (Hero Cycles + Honda), **1985**, lasted **26 years**                                                                            |

- **Study Guide example:** **Kraft–Heinz (2015)**, about **\$1.5
  billion** annual cost savings by end-2017 (~5% of \$28 billion
  revenue).

- **SN 7 synergy types:** complementary geographies and clients ·
  complementary products · patents · supply chain efficiencies · shared
  IT · R&D · sales and marketing.

- **Why synergy matters (SN):** better outcomes, meets goals, stronger
  solutions; can raise market share, staff and customer satisfaction,
  cash flows and growth, and lower capital costs.

**5. Stakeholder expectations**

- **Ebook:** stakeholder involvement is now **active**, not passive, and
  starts at project initiation. PMBOK added **Stakeholder Management**
  as a knowledge area. Map stakeholders on a **power-influence grid**.
  Up to **50 metrics** per project, of which about **8-10 are KPIs**.
  Too many metrics = information overload.

- **SN:** expectations = criteria by which a stakeholder judges the
  project (on time, on budget, quality). Not every stakeholder is a
  client.

| **6 steps to manage expectations (SN)**                                                |                                             |
|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Recognise your stakeholders                                                        | 4\. Scope document approved by stakeholders |
| 2\. **Map** them (4 quadrants: keep satisfied, manage closely, monitor, keep informed) | 5\. Stakeholder management plan             |
| 3\. Obtain their expectations (interviews)                                             | 6\. Communicate during execution            |

- **Guidelines (SN):** document stakeholders · enforce process (change
  control) · regular progress updates · ask questions · dispel myths.
  Study Guide: deliver value every **3-4 weeks** to keep stakeholders
  engaged.

**6. Strategic alliances**

- **Ebook:** two or more businesses working together for a **win-win**
  (e.g. shared distribution). A strategic alliance is a joint venture
  that **bolsters core strategy**, creates **competitive advantage** and
  blocks competitors: **"coopetition"**. Example: **Starbucks + Barnes &
  Noble**.

- **SN:** an agreement to collaborate while **preserving each company's
  individuality**; **less complex and less legally binding than a joint
  venture**; short or long term.

| **Type**                          | **Meaning**                                                                                       | **Example**                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Joint venture**                 | **Child company of two parents**; shared equity and resources; profits split                      | **Caradigm** (Microsoft + GE Healthcare, 2012); Alphabet + GSK (**Galvani Bioelectronics**, 2016) |
| **Equity strategic alliance**     | One firm buys equity in another (partial acquisition) or **cross-equity**                         | **Panasonic → Tesla** (2010; battery plant in Nevada)                                             |
| **Non-equity strategic alliance** | Share resources by **agreement**; no new entity, no equity; looser; **the majority** of alliances | **Starbucks + Barnes & Noble**; Project Baseline                                                  |

- **5 components of a successful alliance (Ivey Business Journal):**
  critical to a core goal · critical to a core competency · **blocks a
  competitive threat** · creates or keeps strategic choices · mitigates
  significant risk.

- **Advantages:** share resources and expertise · new-market penetration
  · expanded production · drive innovation. **SN value:** short-term
  finances · remove **barriers to entry** · business insights ("test
  run") · share financial risk · innovate.

- **Disadvantages:** loss of control · increased liability
  (**Tesla–Panasonic** battery delays). Accenture: **76%** of leaders
  expect business models to be unrecognisable in **5 years**.

- **Finding a partner (SN):** brainstorm → outline proposals → determine
  goals → devise the plan (legal documents).

| **Decision**           | **Nature**                                                                                                     |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Tactical / routine** | Repetitive, rule-based, quick; delegated to **lower levels**                                                   |
| **Strategic**          | Non-repetitive, huge funds, careful analysis; **higher level** (objectives, capital expenditure, plant layout) |

- **Problems of alliances (Ebook):** differences in cognisance, values,
  aims and resources; one-sided transactions damage trust; partners may
  turn **rivals**. **9 conditions** include: know your capability needs,
  study the partner, avoid **over-dependence**, run it as an independent
  enterprise, **complete mutual trust**, leader sets the example.

- **SN 6 problems:** cultural and language barriers · uneven alliances ·
  **lack of trust** (most important and difficult) · damage to goodwill
  · different management styles · potential for conflicts.
