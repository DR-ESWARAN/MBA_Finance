**1. Concept of job design**

- Specifies the **contents, methods and relationships of jobs** to
  satisfy **technological and organisational** requirements and the
  **social and personal** needs of the job holder.

- Work arrangement (or rearrangement) to reduce **job dissatisfaction
  and employee alienation** from repetitive, mechanistic tasks.

- Raises productivity through **non-monetary rewards** (sense of
  achievement, challenge, responsibility).

- **Michael Armstrong**: deciding the **contents** of a job (duties,
  responsibilities), the **methods** (techniques, systems, procedures)
  and the **relationships** with superiors, subordinates and colleagues.

**Job design is the process of (Ebook, 6)**

1.  Deciding the **contents** of the job.

2.  Deciding **methods & processes** to carry it out.

3.  **Optimum use of work-time** ("time is money").

4.  **Avoiding manual tasks** that machines can do.

5.  **Synchronisation** of work; no conflict with other jobs.

6.  Deciding the **relationships** in the organisation.

- **Job design follows job analysis.** Job analysis supplies job data
  and the skills/knowledge needed; job design organises tasks, duties
  and responsibilities into a unit of work.

- Main objective: **integrate individual needs** (interest, challenge,
  achievement) **with organisational requirements** (productivity,
  technical efficiency, quality).

- Study Notes: a strategic process, more than **outlining
  responsibilities**; creates well-defined roles, prevents ambiguity,
  balances workload, promotes flexibility.

**Historical evolution (Study Notes)**

| **Period**         | **Milestone**                                                                                |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Late 18th century  | **Industrial Revolution**: mechanisation and efficiency                                      |
| Early 20th century | **Henry Ford’s assembly line**; **F.W. Taylor** scientific management, time & motion studies |
| 1920s–1930s        | **Hawthorne studies (Elton Mayo)**: social and psychological side of work                    |
| Mid-20th century   | **Socio-technical systems** theory: social + technical interaction                           |
| 1960s              | **Herzberg’s Two-Factor Theory**: intrinsic vs extrinsic factors                             |
| 1970s              | **Hackman & Oldham’s Job Characteristics Model**                                             |
| Recent decades     | **Job crafting** and **agile job design**                                                    |

**2. Approaches to job design (Ebook: 3)**

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"The three approaches are engineering, human
> and **practical**" = **False**. The third is the **Job
> Characteristics** approach.*

**A. Engineering approach (F.W. Taylor)**

- Management plans each worker’s task **a day in advance**; detailed
  instructions on what, how, when, deadlines.

- Scientific analysis; work **fragmented into logical tasks**; logical
  sequence; compensation and continuous training.

- **Specialisation of labour is the hallmark.** It lets employees learn
  fast, permits **short work cycles** (almost automatic), makes hiring
  of **low-skilled, low-wage** people easy, and reduces supervision.

| **Principles (7)**         | **Meaning**                                  |
|----------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Task fragmentation         | Break task into small manageable units       |
| Optimisation of technology | Through scientific study and analysis        |
| Standardisation            | Through **time and motion study**            |
| Specialisation             | Select workers by specialisation             |
| Training                   | Train selected workers to work efficiently   |
| Responsibility             | Each worker individually responsible         |
| Incentives                 | **Monetary** incentives for best performance |

**Problems (Walker & Guest, 5):** **Repetition** (boredom) ·
**Mechanical pacing** (fixed assembly-line pace) · **No end product**
(no pride) · **Little social interaction** · **No input** (no say in
methods/tools).

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"No difficulties were found after applying
> the engineering approach" = **False**. Walker and Guest listed 5
> problems.*

**B. Human approach (Herzberg)**

- Design jobs **around people**, not organisational processes; rewarding
  (financially and otherwise) and interesting.

- Jobs should satisfy needs for **recognition, respect, growth,
  responsibility**.

- **Job enrichment**, popularised by **Herzberg**, is the main
  human-approach tool.

| **Herzberg factor**                            | **Examples**                                                                          | **Effect**                                         |
|------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| **Motivators** (satisfiers, intrinsic)         | Achievement, work itself, responsibility, learning & growth, recognition, advancement | Drive better performance                           |
| **Hygiene factors** (dissatisfiers, extrinsic) | Working conditions, company policies, salary                                          | Don’t motivate; **absence causes dissatisfaction** |

> **Clinical analogy:** *Hygiene factors are like a normal potassium.
> Nobody gets better because their K+ is 4.2, but a low one makes
> everything go wrong. Motivators are the actual therapy.*

**C. Job Characteristics approach (Hackman & Oldham, 1970s)**

- **Direct relationship between job satisfaction and rewards.** Five
  core dimensions describe any job:

| **Core dimension**    | **Meaning**                                             |
|-----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **Skill variety**     | Use all skills and develop new ones                     |
| **Task identity**     | Complete a **whole, identifiable** piece of work        |
| **Task significance** | Impact of the job on **other people’s lives** (or work) |
| **Autonomy**          | Freedom and independence                                |
| **Feedback**          | Clear information about performance                     |

- **Motivating Potential Score (MPS)** = (Skill variety + Task
  identity + Task significance) ÷ 3 × Autonomy × Feedback. (The Ebook
  prints it without brackets.)

- **Critical psychological states**: core dimensions reward more when
  employees experience **three** psychological states.

- High-MPS jobs must be high in **at least one** of the three
  meaningfulness factors and **high in both autonomy and feedback**.

> **Trap:** *The Study Guide slide lists only 4 dimensions (it drops
> Autonomy). There are **five**. Study Notes Knowledge Check: JCM has
> **Five** core dimensions.*
>
> **Trading analogy:** *MPS multiplies by autonomy and feedback, so a
> zero in either kills the score, like position size × win rate. You
> can’t average your way out of a zero.*

**Study Notes: other approaches**

| **Approach**                      | **Key points**                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Scientific Management**         | Taylor, "father of scientific management"; **Taylorism**, time-motion studies; eliminate wastage; **Bethlehem Steel** (ideal dig size, technique, rest). Criticised as mechanistic                                                   |
| **Job Characteristics Model**     | Psychological states, unlike mechanistic approaches. Strategies: enrichment of tasks, rotation & job crafting, training & development, feedback mechanisms                                                                           |
| **Socio-Technical Systems (STS)** | Mid-20th century; organisations are **social systems**, not only technical. Balance via team collaboration, communication channels, leadership practices. Technology side: human-centred design, training, continuous feedback       |
| **Motivational approaches**       | Individual differences, skill development, clear career pathways, feedback & recognition; apply **Herzberg** (hygiene vs motivators)                                                                                                 |
| **Contemporary approaches**       | Job rotation, enrichment, **job crafting** (employees shape own tasks, relationships, perceptions to fit strengths and **career goals**). Adapting: flexibility & remote work, well-being, continuous learning, **agile job design** |

**3. Factors affecting job design**

**Ebook version: 3 groups**

| **Group**          | **Factors**                                                                | **Key points**                                                                                                                    |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Organisational** | Work nature · **Ergonomics** · Workflow · Culture (practices)              | Ergonomics = fit job to physical abilities and traits; workflow set by product/service; practices may clash with unions           |
| **Environmental**  | Employee abilities & availability · Socio-economic & cultural expectations | **Henry Ford** kept assembly jobs simple because workers lacked car-making experience; jobs now employee-centred, literacy rising |
| **Behavioural**    | Autonomy · Feedback · Diversity · Use of skills & abilities                | Autonomy promotes creativity, independence, efficiency; diversity prevents monotony                                               |

**Study Notes version: 6 factors**

| **Factor**                        | **Key points**                                                                                                                                                                          |
|-----------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Organisational goals & strategies | Innovation = creativity, collaboration; cost-efficiency = streamlined. Balance **efficiency vs well-being**                                                                             |
| Task characteristics              | Nature of tasks; **complexity**, skill variety, task significance                                                                                                                       |
| Employee preferences              | Skills, preferences, abilities; skill utilisation, autonomy & flexibility, career development, **job crafting**                                                                         |
| Technological advancements        | Task automation, communication & collaboration, data analysis, remote work. Automation effects: routine task automation, skill shifts, job redefinition, human-automation collaboration |
| Work environment                  | **Ergonomics** and **safety**; risk assessment, training, feedback, audits                                                                                                              |
| Legal & ethical                   | Non-discrimination, fair compensation, work-life balance, occupational health & safety; legal audits, HR policies, legal counsel, training, adapting to legal change                    |

> **Traps (Knowledge Check):** *Goals and strategies **do** affect job
> design · efficiency-focused jobs have **low** (not high) task variety

> organisation alone · remote work is driven by **technology**, not
> ergonomics. All four statements = False.*

**4. Techniques of job design (Ebook: 4)**

**A. Job rotation**

- Shift employee from **one job to another within a group** for variety
  and relief from boredom.

| **Advantages (8)**                            | **Disadvantages (5)**                   |
|-----------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| ↑ **Intrinsic reward** potential              | May not affect efficiency or enthusiasm |
| Competent in **several jobs**                 | Interrupts routine work                 |
| **Interdepartmental cooperation**             | Employee feels **alienated**            |
| Understand each other’s problems              | Specialists may feel **demotivated**    |
| Relieves boredom and monotony                 | ↑ Cost, ↓ productivity                  |
| Broadens knowledge and skills                 |                                         |
| Variety of work, workplace, peer groups       |                                         |
| Flexibility; ready for greater responsibility |                                         |

- Study Notes: linked to **talent development and succession planning**.
  Employee benefits: skill development, career development, motivation,
  networking, succession planning. Organisation benefits: talent
  retention, flexibility, knowledge transfer, performance, identifying
  talent.

> **Clinical analogy:** *Residency rotations. Cardiology, ICU, cath lab:
> broad competence and succession readiness, at the cost of some
> efficiency each time you move.*

**B. Job simplification**

- Job **broken into small subparts**, each given to one employee; needs
  **no special training**. Uses **time and motion studies**.

- Popularised by **Frederick Taylor**; first implemented by **Henry
  Ford** (cheap mass-produced cars). Low skill, cheaper, fewer errors;
  workers become **replaceable cogs**. Called the **engineering
  approach**.

| **5 steps**            | **What happens**                                    |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|
| 1 Select the job       | Start with a time-consuming or inefficient activity |
| 2 Collect data         | All facts; list every step                          |
| 3 Question everything  | **Why, where, when, who, how**                      |
| 4 Develop improvements | **Eliminate, Combine, Rearrange, Simplify** (ECRS)  |
| 5 Evaluate & implement | Test, adjust, implement                             |

- Published in the **1950s**; similar to today’s **design thinking**.

**C. Job enlargement (horizontal)**

- **Expanding the number of related tasks**; the **opposite of
  simplification**. Horizontal: same job, larger scale. Popular in the
  **1960s–70s**.

| **Advantages (5)**                            | **Disadvantages (4)**                                  |
|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| Wider range of activities                     | Lower efficiency (less specialisation)                 |
| Reduces monotony                              | Lower quality (disputed)                               |
| Variety of skills, career growth              | **Job creep**: workload keeps rising → stress, burnout |
| Higher wage                                   | ↑ **Training levels and costs**                        |
| More autonomy, accountability, responsibility |                                                        |

- Study Notes: increases **breadth**; tasks at the **same level of
  complexity**; cross-training; flexible workforce.

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *Job enlargement consists of **expanding**
> (not reducing) the number of tasks.*

**D. Job enrichment (vertical)**

- Goal: a **motivating job** by adding responsibilities that make it
  meaningful. Part of job design and redesign.

- Most relevant for **highly skilled, white-collar** jobs. **Fein
  (1986)**: less important for blue-collar workers, whose concerns are
  pay, job security, workplace rules.

- Study Notes: **vertical** expansion; introduced by **Herzberg**
  (intrinsic motivators); adds **depth and complexity**. Redesign via
  task variety, skill development, autonomy, feedback & recognition,
  task significance.

| **Enrichment interventions (11)** | **Key point**                                                                                               |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Natural work units                | Group interrelated tasks → ownership, task identity                                                         |
| Combining tasks                   | **Corning Glass Works** hotplates: productivity **+84%**, rejects **23% → \<1%**, absenteeism **8% → \<1%** |
| Quality circles                   | = **Kaizen groups**; meet regularly to solve problems                                                       |
| Suggestion programmes             | ESP, usually overseen by HR; cash awards                                                                    |
| Task teams                        | Unit for a single task; originated in the **US Navy**                                                       |
| Feedback                          | Easiest intervention; incl. 360-degree                                                                      |
| Autonomy                          | E.g., choose when to take a break                                                                           |
| Purpose                           | **Deci & Ryan** self-determination: autonomy, competence, relatedness (Daniel Pink, *Drive*)                |
| Client relationships              | Cummings & Worley 3 steps: identify client, direct contact, relay client judgement                          |
| **Vertical loading**              | Most crucial; control once reserved for management. Often lost after a mistake                              |
| Horizontal loading                | = job enlargement                                                                                           |

| **Advantages (8)**             | **Disadvantages (7)**               |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Learning new skills            | Not all employees welcome it        |
| Reduces boredom, boosts energy | Responsibilities don’t match skills |
| Positive work environment      | Overburden on employees             |
| Recognition and reward         | Poor outcomes                       |
| Motivation for advancement     | Increased monitoring                |
| Sense of accomplishment        | Lack of preparation                 |
| Reduces absenteeism            | Clash of employees                  |
| Professional approach          |                                     |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"Direct feedback" is a feature of job
> **enrichment**.*

**Enlargement vs Enrichment (high-yield)**

| **Point**      | **Job enlargement**              | **Job enrichment**                           |
|----------------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------------------|
| What           | More **number** of tasks         | More **decision-making power** and authority |
| Purpose        | Reduce boredom and monotony      | Make job **challenging**                     |
| Skills         | May not need higher-level skills | Uses and develops **higher-level skills**    |
| Direction      | **Horizontal**                   | **Vertical**                                 |
| Responsibility | Not affected                     | **Increased**                                |
| Supervision    | **More** external supervision    | **Less**; self-directed                      |
| Study Notes    | **Breadth**                      | **Depth**                                    |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"Job enlargement and job enrichment are
> similar on many levels" = **False**. They are distinct.*

**5. Study Notes extras**

**Self-managed work teams**

- Characteristics (8): **autonomy** · **shared leadership** ·
  decision-making authority · cross-functional skills ·
  **accountability** (individual + team) · collaborative culture · goal
  alignment · continuous improvement.

- Contributions: job satisfaction, skill development, problem-solving,
  flexibility, motivation, collaborative innovation, **inherent job
  enrichment**, organisational performance.

**Task significance and task identity**

- **Significance** = meaningful impact on others’ lives (inside or
  outside org) → intrinsic motivation, purpose.

- **Identity** = complete a **whole, identifiable** work → ownership,
  holistic experience.

- Enhance via: communicating impact, recognition & feedback, task
  rotation, empowerment, goal alignment, skill development, transparent
  processes.

**Ergonomics (human factors engineering)**

- **Fit the job to the human**, not the human to the job. Covers
  **physical, cognitive and organisational** aspects (not physical
  comfort only).

| **Type**           | **Focus**                                                      | **Tools**                                                                                                   |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Physical**       | Posture, movement                                              | Monitor at eye level, lumbar support, ergonomic tools, task rotation & breaks, adjustable furniture, layout |
| **Cognitive**      | Mental workload, decision-making, **information presentation** | Avoid overload/underload, clear decision pathways, dashboards, skill match, prioritisation                  |
| **Organisational** | = **sociotechnical systems design**                            | Collaborative structures, flexibility, strategic alignment, values, KPIs, employee involvement              |

- **Safety & health**: risk assessment (physical, chemical, biological,
  ergonomic, psychosocial), PPE, safety protocols, wellness, **EAPs**,
  audits.

- **Human-machine interface**: usability testing, intuitive design,
  clear information, customisation, feedback, accessibility, HMI
  standards, usability metrics.

> **Clinical analogy:** *Cognitive ergonomics is alarm fatigue in the
> ICU. Too many monitor alarms (poor information presentation) and the
> nurse misses the real VF.*

**6. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Wrong belief**                               | **Correct**                                                 |
|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Job design only outlines responsibilities      | Strategic organisation of tasks and relationships           |
| Job design ignores social/psychological side   | Human Relations Movement added it                           |
| Scientific management only aims for efficiency | Aims at efficiency; critics say it neglects well-being      |
| Job design is static                           | Dynamic: rotation, enrichment, crafting                     |
| Automation only causes job losses              | Redefines roles, needs new skills                           |
| Enrichment = enlargement                       | Enrichment = depth; enlargement = breadth                   |
| Job rotation only helps individuals            | Also succession planning, flexible workforce, collaboration |
| Self-managed teams lack accountability         | **High** accountability                                     |
| Enlargement only adds tasks                    | Also reduces monotony, builds skills                        |
| Ergonomics = physical comfort only             | Physical + cognitive + organisational                       |
