**HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT**

**Module 1 — Introduction & Scope**

*Notes · Source: Module 1 Ebook (LMS) only*

1\. Concept of HRM

- HRM = managing people of an organisation with a **human approach**
  (“management of man”).

- Binds **people + organisation** together so **both** their goals are
  achieved.

- A management function: **recruiting → selecting → training →
  developing** human resources.

- Covers the **“people dimension”** of management — not material or
  financial resources.

**Key definitions (remember author + keyword):**

| **Author**                                      | **Keyword to remember**                                                                     |
|-------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Leon C. Megginson                               | HR = knowledge, skills, creative abilities, talents, aptitudes (national + enterprise view) |
| Michael Armstrong                               | “Strategic and coherent approach” to most valued assets (people)                            |
| Edwin B. Flippo                                 | Planning, organizing, directing, controlling of P-D-C-I-M-S\*                               |
| Indian Institute of Personnel Management (IIPM) | Responsibility of all who manage people; “people at work + their relationships”             |

\*Flippo’s functions = **P**rocurement, **D**evelopment,
**C**ompensation, **I**ntegration, **M**aintenance, **S**eparation.

**Clinical analogy:** *HRM is the physiology of an organisation — the
body has organs (departments), but HRM keeps the people (cells) healthy,
coordinated, and performing.*

2\. Characteristics of HRM (6)

- **Universal force & integral part** — present in all organisations,
  every level; tied to strategic decisions (Dessler: getting results
  through people is “the name of the game”).

- **People-Oriented** — manages human, not material/financial,
  resources; best fit between individuals.

- **Staff Function** — supportive/service function; assists line
  managers (hire, train, reward, counsel). “Service to line management
  is the bread and butter.”

- **Comprehensive function** — covers all people, all levels (workers →
  managers; skilled, technical, clerical, etc.).

- **Line Responsibility** — HR manager also exerts line authority within
  his own HR department.

- **Pervasive Responsibility** — all-pervasive; every manager does HR
  through people.

**Memory hook:** *“U-P-S-C-L-P” → Universal, People, Staff,
Comprehensive, Line, Pervasive.*

3\. Scope of HRM

**IIPM’s 3 core aspects:**

| **Aspect**           | **Covers**                                                                                                                                                      |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Personnel Aspect     | Manpower planning, recruitment, selection, placement, transfer, promotion, training & development, layoffs/retrenchment, remuneration, incentives, productivity |
| Welfare Aspect       | Working conditions & amenities — canteen, crèche, rest/lunch room, medical assistance, education, health, safety, recreation                                    |
| Industrial Relations | Union–management relations, joint consultation, collective bargaining, grievance & disciplinary systems, settlement of disputes                                 |

**Added over the years:** HR Planning · Employee Assistance · HR
Information System (HRIS) · Organizational Development (the “synergetic
effect”) · Selection & Staffing · Employee Training & Development.

4\. Objectives of HRM (10)

Goal = attain organisational goals **economically + effectively** while
promoting employee welfare.

1.  **Procurement** of personnel (get capable people).

2.  **Effective utilisation** of human resources.

3.  Build **desirable working relationships**.

4.  **Raise morale** of employees.

5.  **Cost containment**.

6.  **Integration of goals** (individual/group ↔ organisation).

7.  **Maximum individual development**.

8.  **Satisfy individual needs & group goals** (fair remuneration,
    security).

9.  **Healthy employer–employee relations** (“minimum cost, maximum
    profit”).

10. **Disciplined atmosphere** at work.

5\. Importance of HRM — 4 Levels

| **Level**    | **Why it matters**                                                                                              |
|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Enterprise   | Attract/retain talent, develop skills & attitudes, secure cooperation, utilise HR, ensure future competent team |
| Professional | Improves quality of work life, teamwork, personal development, healthy relationships, proper work allocation    |
| Social       | Enhances dignity of labour, suitable employment, balances jobs ↔ jobseekers, conserves physical/mental health   |
| National     | Skilled committed manpower drives economic growth → higher living standards & fuller employment                 |

**Trading analogy:** *Human capital is the compounding asset — two firms
with identical resources diverge in returns purely on quality of people
(like two traders with the same capital but different skill).*

6\. Functions of HRM

Two broad classes: **Managerial** + **Operative**.

A. Managerial Functions (P-O-D-C)

- **Planning** — predetermined course of action; forecast HR needs,
  labour-market & wage trends; bridges “where we are → where we want to
  go.”

- **Organizing** — allocate tasks, set authority–responsibility,
  integrate activities; the structure that is the foundation of
  effective management.

- **Directing** — motivating, activating, leading, supervising; the
  **“heart”** of management (initiates action).

- **Controlling** — checking, verifying, regulating; audit training,
  analyse labour turnover, morale surveys, separation interviews.

B. Operative Functions (5)

| **Function**     | **Activities**                                                                                                                                                           |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Procurement  | Job Analysis → HR Planning → Recruitment → Selection → Placement → Induction/Orientation                                                                                 |
| 2\. Development  | Performance & Potential Appraisal, Training, Executive Development, Career Planning & Development                                                                        |
| 3\. Compensation | Job Evaluation, Wage & Salary Administration, Bonus (Payment of Bonus Act, 1965)                                                                                         |
| 4\. Integration  | Reconcile org ↔ member goals: motivation, job satisfaction, grievance handling, collective bargaining, workers’ participation, counselling, QWL                          |
| 5\. Maintenance  | Protect physical/mental health: fringe benefits (housing, medical, conveyance), social security (PF, pension, gratuity, maternity, insurance), safety + records/research |

**Watch the difference (common trap):**

- **Recruitment** = searching & attracting applicants (positive
  process).

- **Selection** = judging suitability & choosing the best
  (negative/screening process).

- **Job Analysis** → output = **Job Description** + **Job
  Specification**.

7\. Role of HRM (8 roles)

- **Specialist** — advises functional heads on planning, recruitment,
  training, appraisal, compensation.

- **Humanitarian** — moral & ethical obligations to employees.

- **Counsellor** — marital, health, mental, physical, career problems.

- **Mediator** — peacemaker in disputes/conflicts.

- **Spokesman** — represents company in media/forums.

- **Problem Solver** — overall HRM & long-term planning.

- **Change Agent** — introduces institutional change & OD programs.

- **Management of Manpower Resources** — leadership + labour-management
  relations.

8\. Origin, Evolution & Development of HRM

Seeds in **Industrial Revolution (1850s)**, Western Europe & USA →
reached India early 20th century.

| **Era**                   | **Key person / event**                                                | **Core idea**                                                                                                 |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1\. Trade Union Movement  | Royal Commission of Labour India 1911 (Whitley); Trade Union Act 1926 | Abolish ‘jobber’ system; protect workers → birth of collective bargaining, grievance handling, arbitration    |
| 2\. Social Responsibility | Robert Owen (British industrialist)                                   | Paternalistic — owner = father, worker = child; shower baths, model villages, ↑ min wage, ↓ hours 12→10       |
| 3\. Scientific Management | F.W. Taylor                                                           | 4 principles: scientific work methods, scientific selection/training, division of work, harmony & cooperation |
| 4\. Human Relations       | Hugo Munsterberg; Elton Mayo — Hawthorne Experiments (1924–32)        | A happy worker is a productive worker; social/psychological needs \> physical environment                     |
| 5\. Behavioural Science   | Sociology/psychology/anthropology scholars                            | Human behaviour = means to efficiency; motivation, leadership, group dynamics; people enjoy work              |
| 6\. Contingency Approach  | Mary Parker Follett (“Law of the Situation”), Taylor, Fayol           | No single best way — match management to the situation                                                        |
| 7\. Systems Approach      | —                                                                     | Org = interdependent sub-systems (Technical, Social, Power); inputs→transform→outputs→feedback; MIS at core   |

**Hawthorne findings (high-yield, 5):**

11. Physical environment had no material impact on efficiency.

12. Favourable worker/team attitudes mattered more.

13. Meeting social & psychological needs ↑ morale & efficiency.

14. Informal groups strongly influenced performance.

15. Money alone doesn’t motivate — security, recognition, voice do.

**Clinical analogy (Human Relations Era):** *Like the placebo effect —
the psychosocial context (attention, being observed/cared for) changed
outcomes more than the physical intervention itself. That’s literally
what Hawthorne showed.*
