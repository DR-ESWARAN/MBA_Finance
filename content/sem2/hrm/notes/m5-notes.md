**HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT**

**Module 5 — Human Resource Procurement**

*Notes · Source: Module 5 Ebook (primary) + LMS Study Notes (Parts 1–3),
Study Guide, Misconceptions, FAQ, Question Banks only*

> **Read this first:** *Module 5 has three parts: **recruitment**,
> **selection and placement**, and **induction**. The **Ebook** gives
> the 5-step recruitment process, internal vs external sources,
> direct/indirect/third-party methods, an 8-step selection procedure, 6
> types of selection tests and named Indian induction examples. The
> **Study Notes** give a different recruitment process (job analysis →
> onboarding), a 7-stage selection process and modern assessment types
> (MBTI, SJT, EQ). Both are below.*

**1. Concept of recruitment**

- Ebook: identifying, attracting, interviewing, selecting, hiring and
  on-boarding employees; everything from **identifying a staffing need
  to filling it**.

- A **linking function**: joins those with jobs to fill and those
  seeking jobs.

- Two aims: (i) attract **many qualified** applicants; (ii) give enough
  information for unqualified people to **self-select out** (e.g.,
  foreign bank asking for CAs who cleared in the first attempt).

- Two aspects: find number of vacancies and type of applicants; then
  appeal to them through **novel advertising**. Both depend on the
  economic, social, technological and political environment.

**Definitions**

| **Author**          | **Keyword to remember**                                                                                                      |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Dale Yoder**      | Discover **sources of manpower** to meet the **staffing schedule**, attract them in adequate numbers for effective selection |
| **Edwin B. Flippo** | **Searching** for prospective employees and **stimulating them to apply**                                                    |
| **Dale S. Beach**   | Development and maintenance of adequate manpower; creating a **pool of available labour**                                    |

> **Watch out:** *Yoder and Flippo also defined **job analysis** in
> Module 4. Read the keyword in the option: "sources of manpower /
> stimulating to apply" = recruitment.*

- Importance (Ebook): poor recruitment causes future problems; needs
  detailed **manpower planning**; "Recruiting is the process of
  generating a **pool of qualified applicants**."

- Study Notes: recruitment is the **gateway** to a competent workforce.
  Importance = talent acquisition, enhancing diversity, **reducing
  turnover** (cultural fit), long-term success.

| **Recruitment**                                  | **Selection**                                                 |
|--------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------|
| Identify, attract, encourage candidates to apply | Choose the right one from the pool                            |
| Builds a pool (positive)                         | Rejects many (a "rejection exercise" with a positive outcome) |
| Comes first                                      | Starts after screening and shortlisting                       |

**2. Recruitment process**

**Ebook: 4 stages (general)**

1.  Search out the **sources** (e.g., B-schools for young managers).

2.  Develop techniques to attract (goodwill, reputation, "professional
    employer").

3.  Use good techniques (attractive salary, development facilities).

4.  Stimulate as many candidates as possible to apply.

**Ebook: 5 steps (exam favourite)**

| **\#** | **Step**                 | **What happens**                                                                                                                                    |
|--------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1      | **Recruitment planning** | Comprehensive **job specification**: duties, skills, pay grade, start date, temporary/permanent, special conditions                                 |
| 2      | **Strategy development** | Make or buy; method; geographical area; source; sequence of activities                                                                              |
| 3      | **Searching**            | Attract job seekers from two sources: internal and external                                                                                         |
| 4      | **Screening**            | Treated as part of **recruitment**, not selection. Selection starts only after shortlisting (e.g., professor posts: screen, then seminar/interview) |
| 5      | **Evaluation & control** | Because recruitment is costly                                                                                                                       |

- Recruitment costs: recruiters' salary · time for job analysis and
  advertisement · administrative costs · outsourcing/overtime while
  vacancies stay unfilled · cost of recruiting **unsuitable**
  candidates.

**Study Notes version**

- Job analysis & job description → sourcing & attracting (compelling job
  postings) → screening & selection → offer & negotiation →
  **onboarding**.

> **Traps (Knowledge Check):** *Job analysis is an optional step =
> **False** · a job description is more about attracting than setting
> expectations = **False**.*

**3. Sources of recruitment**

**Internal (4)**

| **Source**              | **Key point**                                                                                                                     |
|-------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Present employees**   | **Promotion** (higher status, pay, responsibility) and **transfer** (same status, different job). Job rotation = lateral transfer |
| **Former employees**    | Retired/retrenched may return part-time; performance already known                                                                |
| **Employee referrals**  | One of the most effective; referred people tend to be similar (race, sex) to existing staff                                       |
| **Previous applicants** | Applications already on file; used for unskilled/semi-skilled jobs                                                                |

| **Advantages (5)**             | **Disadvantages (4)**                 |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| Familiarity with own employees | **Limited choice** (and "inbreeding") |
| Better use of talent           | Discourages competition               |
| Economical                     | **Stagnation of skills**              |
| Improves morale                | Creates conflicts                     |
| A motivator                    |                                       |

> **Traps:** *Skills can get stunted with internal recruitment =
> **True** (Objective QB) · employee referrals are an **internal**
> source (Knowledge Check: external = False).*

- Study Notes internal forms: promotions, transfers, job rotation;
  advantages include faster integration.

**External (8): outnumber internal sources**

| **Source**                    | **Key point**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|-------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Employment exchanges**      | National Commission on Labour (1969) · **Compulsory Notification of Vacancies Act 1959** (operative 1960) · Sec 4: establishments with **25+ workers** must notify · report outcome within **15 days** · useful for **blue-collar, white-collar and technical**; Gopalji (31 firms): most preferred for **clerical** |
| **Employment agencies**       | Private (ABC Consultants, Datamatics, Ferguson, S B Billimoria); supervisory and higher levels; cheaper, saves time, company identity hidden; risk of losing good applicants in screening                                                                                                                            |
| **Advertisement**             | Most widely used; highest reach; **AIDA** = Attention, Interest, Desire, Action. Study of 496 ads in The Hindu (1981): few gave full details                                                                                                                                                                         |
| **Professional associations** | Called "**headhunters**"; Institute of Engineers, IMA, AIMA; for highly skilled staff; uncommon in India                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **Campus recruitment**        | IIMs, IITs; HLL, HCL, L&T, Citibank; all candidates at one place, short notice, meet faculty, sell the firm. Limited to **entry** positions                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Deputation**                | 2–3 years to another organisation; government/PSU; ready expertise, no induction cost; too short to build commitment                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Word-of-mouth**             | Includes "**employee pinching**"; no cost; candidate may not be available, small choice                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Raiding / poaching**        | Rival offers better terms (HMT → Titan; Indian Airlines pilots); seen as **unethical**                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |

- Others: walk-ins, contractors, radio/TV, acquisitions and mergers.

| **Advantages (4)**                | **Disadvantages (3)**            |
|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Open process                      | Expensive and time consuming     |
| Talented candidates ("new blood") | Unfamiliar with the organisation |
| Select the best                   | Discourages existing employees   |
| Healthy competition               |                                  |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *Employment exchanges are useful **only** for
> blue-collar = **False** (also white-collar and technical).*
>
> **Trading analogy:** *Internal recruitment is adding to a position you
> already know: cheap and familiar, but concentration risk (inbreeding).
> External is a new stock: wider choice and fresh upside, with higher
> entry cost and an unknown track record.*

**4. Methods of recruitment (Ebook, 3)**

| **Method**      | **Examples**                                                                                |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Direct**      | Campus recruitment · internship · **walk-in interview**                                     |
| **Indirect**    | Print media: "**Situation Vacant**" ads (paid)                                              |
| **Third party** | Placement firms, employment agencies, management consultants · **e-recruitment** (internet) |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *Direct recruitment method = **Walk-in
> interview** (not employment agencies or e-recruitment, which are third
> party).*

**5. Employee selection**

- Picking the **right candidate** most suitable for the vacancy. Ebook
  definitions: right applicant on the right job · choosing qualified
  applicants · identifying and hiring · **matching org requirements with
  individual skills**.

- Principle: "**right man for the right job**". Selection is long and
  tedious, a **rejection exercise** with a positive outcome.

**Ebook selection procedure (8 steps)**

1.  **Preliminary interview**: eliminate the obviously unfit.

2.  **Blank application**: written record; tests expression and
    handwriting; no standard form; simple and brief.

3.  **Selection tests**: assume behaviour can be predicted by sampling
    it.

4.  **Selection interview**: checks application and test data; quiet
    setting, in depth.

5.  **Checking references**: ask **3 to 5** persons; limits: no one
    cites an unfavourable referee, referee may not reply, prejudice.

6.  **Medical examination**: protects against compensation claims; not
    to be used unfairly to reject.

7.  **Final approval**: appointment letter or service agreement.

8.  **Employment**: made permanent after a satisfactory **probation**.

**Selection tests (Ebook, 6 types)**

| **Type**        | **Sub-types / point**                                                                                                                                                                             |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Aptitude**    | Potential to learn; "most promising indices". Intelligence (IQ; criticised as against deprived sections) · mechanical · **psychomotor** (dexterity, eye-hand: packing, watch assembly) · clerical |
| **Achievement** | For experienced candidates: **job knowledge** test · **work sample** test                                                                                                                         |
| **Situational** | Real-life situation: **group discussion** · **in-basket**                                                                                                                                         |
| **Proficiency** | Skills already possessed: trade or skill tests                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Interest**    | Patterns of interest; which jobs will satisfy                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Personality** | Emotional balance, maturity, temperament; **can be faked**, may not predict success                                                                                                               |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *Group discussion is a **situational** test.*
>
> **Clinical analogy:** *Aptitude test = potential to learn (like a
> residency entrance exam). Achievement/work sample = what you can
> already do (like a supervised procedure log). Personality test is the
> weakest predictor because answers can be gamed.*

**Study Notes version**

- 7 stages: application review → **screening interviews** → assessment
  tests → **in-depth interviews** → reference checks → job offer &
  negotiation → onboarding.

- Assessment types: aptitude · personality (**MBTI, DISC, Big Five**) ·
  skills · **situational judgement tests (SJTs)** · behavioural ·
  emotional intelligence (**EQ-i 2.0, MSCEIT**).

| **Structured interview**                  | **Unstructured interview**  |
|-------------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Same preset questions for all             | Conversational, flexible    |
| Consistency, objectivity, easy comparison | Depth, flexibility, rapport |

> **Traps (Knowledge Check):** *Selection begins with application review
> = **True** · screening interviews are in-depth talks with department
> heads = **False** · onboarding includes reference checks = **False**.*

**6. Placement**

- Assigning the selected candidate to the job; finally assigning rank
  and responsibility.

- **Pigors & Myers**: determination of the job to which an accepted
  candidate is assigned; a **matching** of what he can do with job
  demands, and of what the job imposes with what it offers.

- Proper placement reduces absenteeism and turnover, raises morale.
  Misplacement → re-placement or training. **Probation = "trial
  period"**.

- Placement does (5): improves morale · reduces turnover · decreases
  accidents · **enhances labour productivity** · clarifies expectations.

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *Proper placement does not affect labour
> productivity = **False**.*

- Study Notes: onboarding & orientation · role clarity · evaluation &
  feedback · long-term retention, commitment, engagement.

**7. Induction and orientation**

| **Induction**                                       | **Orientation**                                             |
|-----------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|
| Introduces newcomer to company and work environment | Accustoms employee to department, job role and work culture |
| Day one; few hours to a day (PPT, videos)           | 1 day to several days                                       |

- First step towards gaining **commitment**; "who is who"; sense of
  belonging for permanent staff, temporary staff and trainees.

- **John M. Ivancevich**: orientation = organisationally sponsored,
  formalised activities for **socialisation**.

- **Billimoria**: orientation = new employee is **rehabilitated** into
  changed surroundings and introduced to practices, policies, purpose.

**Indian examples (Ebook)**

| **Company**   | **Remember**                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Citibank**  | 2.5 weeks on 3 business levels → simulation (overdraft cheque role play) → job in 3rd week with a **mentor**, no fixed hours → after 2 months, Asia Pacific Banking Institute, **Singapore** |
| **KPMG**      | Experienced consultants perform immediately; others get leeway; **6-month probation** for all                                                                                                |
| **Yes Bank**  | **Drum-beating** session, experiential learning                                                                                                                                              |
| **NTPC**      | **1 year**: 6 months classroom + 6 months on-the-job; 2-week theatre workshop by **National School of Drama**                                                                                |
| **BCG India** | **Layered** induction; informal dinners                                                                                                                                                      |

**Induction programme**

- Contents (8): history · mission, vision · policies & procedures ·
  rules · structure & authority · terms of job (pay, hours, holidays,
  promotion) · welfare · safety.

| **Phase**                 | **By whom**                  | **Purpose**                                             |
|---------------------------|------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| **General** orientation   | Staff                        | History and operations; pride and interest              |
| **Specific** orientation  | **Job supervisor**           | Department, workplace, customs; adjust to job           |
| **Follow-up** orientation | Personnel dept or supervisor | **1 week to 6 months** later; satisfaction, counselling |

- Effective induction (4): prepare a **checklist** · cover training ·
  encourage social interaction · ask for **feedback**.

- Study Notes: cultural context (one-size-fits-all fails in India) ·
  legal compliance (minimum wages, hours, contracts, **safety**) · HR
  designs, managers integrate · personalisation · feedback · continuous
  improvement.

> **Traps (Knowledge Check):** *Induction has no impact on engagement =
> **False** · induction negatively influences performance = **False** ·
> legal compliance includes workplace **safety** · tailoring fosters
> **inclusivity**.*

**8. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Wrong belief**                           | **Correct**                                     |
|--------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Recruitment only fills job openings        | Strategic: goals, diverse talent, relationships |
| Recruitment is only about technical skills | Also cultural fit and values                    |
| Recruitment is one-time                    | Ongoing, adaptive                               |
| External is always better than internal    | Depends on goals; internal boosts morale        |
| Selection only matches skills              | Also culture and long-term fit                  |
| Selection is one-size-fits-all             | Tailored to role                                |
| Onboarding is just paperwork               | Welcome, introductions, culture                 |
| Induction is just a warm welcome           | Systematic: culture, policies, procedures       |
| Legal compliance is not part of induction  | Integral (Indian labour laws)                   |
| Induction is one-time                      | Needs continuous improvement                    |
