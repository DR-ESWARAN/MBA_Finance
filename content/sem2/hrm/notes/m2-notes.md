**HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT**

**Module 2 — Human Resource Planning (HRP)**

*Notes · Source: Module 2 Ebook (primary) + LMS Study Notes, Study
Guide, Quick Notes, Question Banks only*

> **Read this first:** *The LMS gives HRP in two versions. The **Ebook**
> (also the Study Guide and Subjective QB) uses a 6-step process, 3
> external + 5 internal factors and 11 forecasting techniques. The
> **Study Notes / Quick Notes** use an 8-step process and add

> labelled.*

**1. Concept of HRP**

- **Continuous process** of systematic planning ahead to get **optimum
  use of the most valuable asset: quality employees**.

- Ensures **best fit between employees and jobs**; avoids manpower
  **shortages or surpluses**.

- **Bridges the gap** between organisational plans and HRM.

- A conscious determination of the **direction and composition** of
  human resources.

- Lays the groundwork for **manpower procurement**. Organisational
  objectives decide the number, type and kind of people needed, and HRP
  begins from there.

- Translates the organisation’s objectives and plans into the **number
  of people needed**.

**Definitions (author + keyword)**

| **Source**            | **Keyword to remember**                                                                                                                                                                 |
|-----------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Dale S. Beach**     | Determining and assuring an **adequate number of qualified persons**, available at **proper times**, doing jobs that meet enterprise needs **and give satisfaction to the individuals** |
| **Decenzo & Robbins** | **Right number and kind** of people, at the **right place**, at the **right time**, completing tasks that achieve overall objectives                                                    |
| Study Notes           | **Proactive** process of finding, hiring and making best use of HR to meet **current and future** business needs; not just filling jobs                                                 |

> **Clinical analogy:** *HRP is cath lab rota planning. You forecast
> next quarter’s case load (demand), count who is on staff and who is
> leaving (supply), then hire, cross-train or adjust shifts to close the
> gap.*

**2. Types of HRP (two levels)**

| **Level**           | **What it does**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|---------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Macro-level HRP** | Aligns HR administration with the **mission and overall strategic plan**. Also called **HR strategic planning** or **organisational design & development**. Examines HR policies and procedures; objectives touch recruitment, appraisal, pay & benefits, legal compliance, labour relations, safety. |
| **Micro-level HRP** | **Tactics** that deliver the strategic objectives. Ensures the right **number** of employees with the right **mix of knowledge, skills and abilities** in the right departments.                                                                                                                      |

- **Macro drives micro.**

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"There are three types of HR planning" =
> **False**. Only two levels: macro and micro.*

**3. Objectives of HRP**

**Ebook list (12)**

1.  Forecast **future HR needs**.

2.  Ensure **fuller and effective utilisation** of existing HR.

3.  Make necessary manpower **available as and when required**.

4.  **Relate HRP with organisational planning**.

5.  Estimate **surplus or shortage** at any given time.

6.  Know how current staff are **deployed**, skills needed for jobs, and
    manpower needs over a period.

7.  Anticipate the **impact of technology** on jobs and HR.

8.  Determine **levels of recruitment and training**.

9.  Estimate **cost of labour force and its housing** requirements.

10. Provide a basis for **management development programmes**.

11. Meet needs of **expansion and diversification**.

12. Increase **labour productivity** and so reduce labour costs.

**Study Notes list (5 key objectives)**

| **Objective**                         | **Core idea**                                                                                                       |
|---------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Forecasting workforce requirements    | Predict demand and supply from historical data, industry trends, growth; right people, right skills, **right time** |
| Aligning HR strategies with org goals | Translate business objectives into specific HR actions                                                              |
| Anticipating skill gaps               | Compare current skills with future roles; close via training, development or recruitment                            |
| Enhancing workforce flexibility       | Adaptable workforce that responds to **change** (market shifts, technology); continuous learning                    |
| Optimising resource utilisation       | Prevent over- or under-staffing; productivity and cost-effectiveness                                                |

**4. Factors affecting HRP**

**Ebook version: External (3) + Internal (5)**

| **Type** | **Factor**                        | **Key points**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|----------|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| External | **Economic**                      | Suppliers, customers, competitors, **globalisation**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| External | **Government policies**           | HRP, recruitment, selection, pay, terminations governed by constitutional provisions and central/state laws; HR cannot act unilaterally                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| External | **Technological**                 | New skills or upskilling; demand for sophisticated skills (telecom, biotech); automation and M&A cause **downsizing**; power tilts from management to **technical/knowledge workers**; team-based incentives; robots take risky and repetitive jobs; demand for **multi-skilled** people; risk of worker **alienation** |
| Internal | **Company policies**              | Guide maps. **Objectives show the destination; policies show the route**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| Internal | **Time horizons**                 | Short-term **6 months–1 year**; long-term **3–20 years**. **More uncertainty = shorter horizon**. Unstable (computers) = short; stable (university) = long                                                                                                                                                              |
| Internal | **Type & quality of information** | Quality depends on clarity of strategy, structure, budgets, schedules; needs good job-analysis data and **HRIS**. Stable environments get better information                                                                                                                                                            |
| Internal | **Trade unions**                  | Influence working hours per week, recruitment sources                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| Internal | **HR systems**                    | HRD departments, participative management, **quality circles**, upward communication, better employee status                                                                                                                                                                                                            |

> **Trap (Objective QB):** *"Type and quality of information is an
> **external** factor" = **False**. It is **internal**. Time horizons is
> also internal.*

**Study Notes version: Internal / External / Organisational**

| **Group**                 | **Factors**                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|---------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Internal determinants** | Current workforce (size, skill sets, diversity) · Organisational culture · Strategic objectives                                                                                                                                       |
| **External**              | Labour market conditions · Economic factors · Technological advancements · Government regulations (labour laws, immigration) · Globalisation                                                                                          |
| **Organisational**        | Alignment with strategic objectives · Innovation & technological integration · Market position & competitive advantage (differentiation vs cost leadership) · Organisational structure & culture (flat structure = decentralised HRP) |

**5. Process of HRP**

**Ebook version: 6 steps**

| **\#** | **Step**                           | **What happens**                                                                                                          |
|--------|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1      | **Environmental scanning**         | First stage; where HRM and **strategic planning** first interact; scan external + internal environment ("global scanner") |
| 2      | **Demand forecasting**             | Future HR needs in **quantity and quality**; year-by-year for every significant level and type                            |
| 3      | **Supply forecasting**             | Future availability. **Internal**: promotion, transfer, job enlargement, job enrichment. **External**: fresh recruitment  |
| 4      | **Matching demand & supply**       | Bring to **equilibrium**. Shortage = hire; overstaffing = reduce employment                                               |
| 5      | **Action plan**                    | Execute via recruitment, selection, placement, T&D, socialisation; then control and evaluation; update with time          |
| 6      | **Monitoring, control & feedback** | Allocate HR, update inventories, find deficiencies; compare plan vs actual                                                |

**Study Notes version: 8 steps**

1.  **Assessment of current workforce** (skills, competencies,
    demographics, turnover).

2.  **Environmental scanning** (economy, technology, labour market
    trends).

3.  **Forecasting future workforce needs**.

4.  **Identifying skill gaps** (required vs present).

5.  **Developing HRP strategies** (recruitment, training, succession
    planning).

6.  **Implementation** of strategies (with clear communication).

7.  **Monitoring and evaluation** (KPIs, adjust).

8.  **Feedback and adaptation** (feedback loops, continuous
    improvement).

- The process is **iterative and continuous**, not one-time.

> **Trap (LMS Knowledge Check 2):** *The LMS key marks "The HRP process
> begins with forecasting future workforce needs" as **True**, although
> the Ebook’s first step is environmental scanning and the Study Notes’
> first step is assessing the current workforce. If you see this exact
> statement, the LMS answer is True.*
>
> **Trap:** *Demand forecasting is **not based solely on historical
> data** (False). Continuous monitoring is needed **during and after**
> implementation (False if it says "only during").*

**6. Demand forecasting techniques**

**Ebook list (11)**

| **Technique**                      | **One-line meaning**                                                                                |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Bottom-up**                      | Each level, **starting from the lowest**, forecasts its needs; summed into one total                |
| **Top-down**                       | Starts with **high-level market data** and works down to the company                                |
| **Delphi technique**               | Judgements of a **selected group of experts** solicited and summarised                              |
| **Nominal Group Technique (NGT)**  | **Structured group brainstorming**; everyone contributes; quick agreement on priorities             |
| **Productivity & staffing ratios** | Ratio of a business variable to employees needed; good for **growth-driven** needs                  |
| **Trend analysis**                 | Historic employment patterns projected to future demand                                             |
| **Regression analysis**            | Predicts a **dependent** variable from an **independent** one (e.g., executive pay vs company size) |
| **Mathematical models**            | Variables + equations linking them                                                                  |
| **Workload analysis**              | Future work and skill needs from **historical performance** baseline                                |
| **Group brainstorming**            | Usable in any **DMAIC** phase; mostly **Analyze and Improve**                                       |
| **Sales force estimate**           | Salespeople’s collective opinion predicts future sales                                              |

**Study Notes split: Quantitative vs Qualitative**

| **Quantitative (4)**                          | **Qualitative (4)**                                     |
|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| Trend analysis                                | Expert opinion                                          |
| Ratio analysis (employees vs revenue/output)  | Delphi method (iterative rounds, opinions **converge**) |
| Workload analysis (best for predictable work) | Scenario planning                                       |
| Regression analysis                           | Focus groups                                            |

- Study Notes: demand assessment also considers organisational goals,
  historical data, external factors, **industry benchmarks** and the
  **employee lifecycle** (retirements, promotions).

> **Clinical analogy:** *Delphi is how consensus documents get made: an
> expert panel answers anonymously in rounds until opinions converge.
> Regression is the risk score: predict outcome (dependent) from a risk
> factor (independent).*

**7. HR flows and supply forecasting**

- **Inflow**: new recruits, promotions, transfers.

- **Outflow**: retirements, terminations, resignations, deaths,
  discharge & layoff, downsizing, exit policy.

**Internal supply methods (Ebook, 6)**

| **Method**                      | **Key point**                                                                                                                                |
|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Simple staffing tables**      | Graphic list of all jobs, current headcount and future requirements                                                                          |
| **Skills inventories**          | Compilation of skills, education, experience of current staff                                                                                |
| **HRIS**                        | Faster, better-quality HR decisions; higher productivity                                                                                     |
| **Succession planning**         | Count people per job category/skill; adjust for retirements, promotions, transfers, turnover, terminations                                   |
| **Manpower replacement charts** | Used in succession planning; map **key roles, current holders, potential replacements**, promotion potential                                 |
| **Renewal analysis**            | Flows in/out at each level. **Start at the top**, work across, then down; promotion moves upward. Vacancy at a level = promoted out + losses |

**External supply sources (Ebook, 4)**

- **Labour markets**: area within which employers compete for labour
  (includes self-employed and unemployed with relevant skills).

- **Demographic trends**: working-age population, participation rate,
  employment rate, gender.

- **Contingent workforce**: hired **on demand**; freelancers,
  independent contractors, consultants; **not on payroll**.

- **Industry & occupational trends**: track industries that may fail.

**Study Notes view of supply**

| **Area**                     | **Items**                                                                                                                                                   |
|------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Evaluating current workforce | Skills & competency inventory (**explicit + tacit**) · Performance assessments · Succession planning · Employee development programmes · Surveys & feedback |
| Internal sources             | Promotions & transfers · Succession planning · Skills development programmes · Cross-functional training                                                    |
| External sources             | Recruitment & hiring · Outsourcing & contingent workforce · Consultants & freelancers · Partnerships & collaborations                                       |

**8. Gap analysis and action plans**

| **Condition**    | **Meaning**  |
|------------------|--------------|
| Supply = Demand  | Balance      |
| Supply \< Demand | **Shortage** |
| Supply \> Demand | **Surplus**  |

**Action plans (Ebook, 7)**

| **Action**             | **Key point**                                                                                                          |
|------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Layoffs**            | Cut salary costs, raise shareholder value; due to strategy change, falling revenue, automation, offshoring/outsourcing |
| **Downsizing**         | Narrow the demand–supply gap, work more efficiently                                                                    |
| **Attrition**          | **Gradual, deliberate** reduction as people retire/resign and are **not replaced**                                     |
| **Early retirement**   | Cut labour cost of an ageing workforce; avoids large compensation on dismissal                                         |
| **Reduced work hours** | Keep all employees despite shortage or surplus                                                                         |
| **Outplacement**       | **Terminating with dignity**: resume reviews, job search advice, interview training, coaching, career assessment       |
| **Hiring**             | When projected **supply is short** of demand                                                                           |

**Study Notes: gap analysis + strategies**

- Gap analysis covers: demand vs supply **alignment** · **quantitative**
  analysis · **qualitative** assessment · **scenario** analysis.

| **Strategy group**         | **Tools**                                                                                                                             |
|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Recruitment                | Targeted recruitment · Campus hiring & early talent (internships, apprenticeships) · Diversity & inclusion                            |
| Training & development     | Skill development · Leadership development · Cross-functional training (handles **both** shortage and surplus)                        |
| Downsizing & restructuring | Strategic downsizing · Outplacement services · Flexible work (reduced hours, job-sharing, remote) as an **alternative to downsizing** |

> **Trading analogy:** *Gap analysis is portfolio rebalancing. Target
> allocation = demand, current holdings = supply. Underweight = buy
> (hire, train); overweight = trim (attrition, early retirement,
> outplacement).*

**9. Implementation, evaluation, feedback (Study Notes)**

| **Stage**               | **Elements**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|-------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Strategic execution     | Clear communication · T&D initiatives · Recruitment & onboarding · Downsizing with sensitivity                                                                                                                                                                         |
| Leadership involvement  | Leadership support (buy-in) · Change management · Continuous communication                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| Evaluation criteria (4) | Alignment with org goals · Impact on workforce capability · **Cost-effectiveness** · Employee satisfaction & engagement                                                                                                                                                |
| Analysis                | Quantitative metrics + qualitative assessments (interviews, focus groups, surveys)                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| Feedback mechanisms     | Employee: surveys, focus groups, **anonymous** feedback · Leadership: executive briefings & workshops, strategic alignment discussions · Iterative improvement: actionable recommendations, iterative planning · Communication: transparent, **feedback loop closure** |

**10. Common misconceptions (LMS)**

| **Wrong belief**                          | **Correct**                                                                |
|-------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| HRP is only about filling vacancies       | It is strategic forecasting: right people, right skills, now and in future |
| HRP ignores employee satisfaction         | It aligns employee skills and goals with organisational goals              |
| Talent acquisition = external hiring only | Includes **retaining and developing existing** staff                       |
| HRP is one-time                           | **Ongoing, iterative cycle**                                               |
| HRP is only for large firms               | Applies to organisations of **all sizes**                                  |
